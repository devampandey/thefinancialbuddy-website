US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Tuesday that India and the United States are within weeks of concluding their long-running trade agreement. He described the negotiations as reduced to a handful of technical points, and said the two leaders could sign the pact once it is announced.

Rubio made the remarks in Mumbai on the first day of his India visit, in an interview with Network18 and in comments to reporters. He did not give a date and did not say which issues were still open.

## What Rubio actually said

Rubio's message was that the deal is effectively done in substance and is now being checked word by word. He said the two sides were about as close as they could be without a signed agreement, with a small number of items left to close. The remaining work, he said, is making sure both governments read the same language the same way. In a pact this large, a phrase that each side interprets differently can turn into a dispute later.

He also explained why Washington has not announced anything yet. Every detail has to be agreed before a public announcement, he said, because declaring a deal that later falls apart would damage a relationship of this importance. That is a notable caution after months in which both capitals repeatedly said a deal was near.

Rubio was careful about his own role. The negotiations are run by the US Trade Representative, not the State Department, but he said he had followed them closely and expected an outcome in weeks, not months. Asked how India's terms would compare with those of other countries, he said the bulk of the agreement would work in India's favour, without giving figures.

## Why the timing matters

For Indian exporters, the most useful signal is the shift in tone from Washington. Earlier in the month, Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said New Delhi expected to finalise the first phase of the agreement soon. Rubio's comments are the clearest US statement yet that the gap has narrowed to drafting.

The trade track has run alongside a tense energy dispute. Days ago, President Donald Trump waived Russia-linked tariffs that could have hit India with a 100% levy, as we reported in [our earlier coverage](/politics/trump-waives-graham-act-russia-tariffs-sparing-india-from-100-percent-levy-for-now). A signed trade agreement would give both sides a firmer base than the waivers and threats that have shaped the relationship this year.

Rubio also argued that senior US officials do not visit India often enough. He pointed to recent trips by the US Vice President, Commerce Secretary, Attorney General and FBI Director, noted that External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar had travelled to the United States four times this year, and called India a key Quad member and a major defence and commercial partner.

## What to watch next

Rubio's visit continues on Wednesday with a stop at Reliance Industries' Jamnagar refinery in Gujarat, where Venezuelan crude supplies are expected to be on the agenda. On trade, the next milestone is a formal announcement from the US Trade Representative and India's Commerce Ministry. Rubio's suggestion of a signing by the two leaders means a summit-level event could follow.

Until the text is published, the key questions remain open: the final tariff rates on Indian goods, how farm and dairy access is handled, and whether energy purchases are tied to the deal. Rubio's caution about announcing too early suggests those details will come out all at once rather than piece by piece.
