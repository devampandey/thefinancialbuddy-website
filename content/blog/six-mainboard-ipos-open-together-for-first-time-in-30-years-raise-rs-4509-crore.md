Indian primary markets set an unusual record on Wednesday as six mainboard initial public offerings opened for subscription on the same day, a feat last seen roughly three decades ago. According to Prime Database, the previous instance of exactly six IPOs launching on a single day was on October 14, 1996, when the offerings collectively raised just Rs 22 crore. By contrast, Wednesday's six issues are together seeking to raise approximately Rs 4,509 crore, underscoring how dramatically the scale of India's IPO market has grown over the past thirty years.

The companies launching offers include RentoMojo, Asset Reconstruction Company of India (Arcil), Karamtara Engineering, Manipal Payment & Identity Solutions, LCC Projects and Steamhouse India, spanning sectors from consumer rental services and payments technology to engineering and asset reconstruction. RentoMojo's Rs 1,256 crore issue, priced in a band of Rs 384 to Rs 404 per share, is the largest of the six and drew strong early demand, with the offering subscribed around 69 percent within the first few hours of bidding, led by strong interest from non-institutional investors.

## A Broader Wave of Listings

The clustering of offerings is being read by market participants as a sign of continued issuer confidence in India's primary markets, even as broader indices have wobbled in recent sessions on the back of rising crude oil prices and global uncertainty. Grey market premiums across the six issues varied widely, with Karamtara Engineering and LCC Projects both commanding premiums of roughly 27 percent over their upper price bands, while Manipal Payment & Identity Solutions traded at a comparatively modest premium near 11 percent, reflecting differing investor appetite across sectors.

Arcil's offering, entirely a sale of existing shares rather than fresh capital, drew interest partly on the back of the asset reconstruction sector's steady role in India's banking cleanup story, while Steamhouse India and LCC Projects offered exposure to infrastructure-linked engineering demand. All six issues are scheduled to close on Friday, September 11, with most tentatively expected to list around September 17.

## Why the Timing Matters

Investment bankers said the bunching of offerings reflects issuers racing to complete listings before the year-end window narrows, alongside a broader queue of companies waiting for regulatory clearances. The single-day record also arrives just as market attention is separately focused on the much-anticipated initial public offering of the National Stock Exchange itself, which recently received regulatory approval and is expected to open later in September, adding to what is shaping up to be one of the busiest stretches for India's IPO market in years.

For retail investors, the simultaneous opening of six offerings means having to prioritise limited capital across issues with meaningfully different risk profiles, price bands and grey market signals, a dynamic that bankers say is likely to sharpen scrutiny of each company's fundamentals over the remaining bidding days.
