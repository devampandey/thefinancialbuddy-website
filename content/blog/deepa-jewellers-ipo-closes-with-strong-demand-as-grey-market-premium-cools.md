The initial public offering of Deepa Jewellers Limited closed for subscription on September 3, capping a three-day bidding window that drew solid participation from retail and non-institutional investors even as enthusiasm in the unofficial grey market noticeably cooled.

## Strong Retail and NII Response

The Rs 460-crore issue was subscribed well over five times its offered size by the time bidding wrapped up, according to exchange data compiled by market trackers. Retail investors bid for more than six times their allotted quota, while the non-institutional investor (NII) category, which includes high-net-worth individuals, saw demand run close to nine times the shares on offer. Within that segment, smaller NII applicants bidding up to Rs 10 lakh were the most enthusiastic, subscribing more than ten times their portion.

The qualified institutional buyer (QIB) segment, typically the last to fill in as institutions place bids on the final day, lagged behind at under half of its allotted quota through most of the window, a pattern common for jewellery and consumer-facing IPOs where institutional interest tends to firm up only in the closing hours.

## Grey Market Premium Slides

While subscription numbers painted a healthy picture, the stock's grey market premium (GMP) told a more cautious story. The unofficial premium, which had touched Rs 55 on August 31, implying a listing gain of over 31 percent, slid to around Rs 28 by the time the issue closed. At that level, the shares were being indicated to list near Rs 205 against the upper end of the price band, a gain of roughly 16 percent over the issue price of Rs 177.

Market watchers noted that grey market premiums can swing quickly in the days before listing and are not a reliable predictor of actual listing performance, but the pullback suggests investor appetite, while still positive, had moderated from the highs seen earlier in the subscription window.

## Issue Details

Deepa Jewellers had set a price band of Rs 168 to Rs 177 per share for the offer, which combined a fresh issue with an offer for sale. The company is expected to finalise allotment on September 4, with refunds and demat credits to follow before shares make their debut on the BSE and NSE on September 8.

The jewellery retailer's IPO arrives amid a broader wave of primary market activity in India this year, with several mainboard and SME issues jostling for investor attention in quick succession. Gold and jewellery-sector listings have drawn mixed reception from the market this cycle, with valuations and post-listing performance varying widely depending on regional footprint and margin profile.

## What Investors Should Watch

Analysts tracking the offer said the final allotment ratios, particularly in the retail and small-NII categories where oversubscription was heaviest, would determine how many first-time applicants actually receive shares. With the GMP now well off its peak, some market participants cautioned that listing-day gains could be more modest than initially anticipated when the issue opened.
