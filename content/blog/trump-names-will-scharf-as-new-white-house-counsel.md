US President Donald Trump announced on Sunday that Will Scharf, currently serving as White House staff secretary, will become his new White House counsel from September 1, succeeding David Warrington, who is leaving the administration for the private sector.

## Who Is Will Scharf

Scharf is a familiar face in Trump's inner circle, having served as a federal prosecutor before representing Trump personally in a number of legal matters, including proceedings before the US Supreme Court. As staff secretary, he has been responsible for managing the flow of documents to the president's desk and has frequently been seen handing Trump executive orders for signature in the Oval Office, giving him a high public profile within the West Wing.

Announcing the move on his Truth Social platform, Trump described Scharf as "Tough, Strong, and Smart," adding that he "loves our Country, and respects the Law." Trump said Scharf would "do a great job as White House Counsel."

Beyond his staff secretary duties, Scharf has also chaired the National Capital Planning Commission, the federal agency overseeing major construction and renovation projects on government buildings in Washington. In that capacity, he helped secure approval for Trump's proposed $400 million White House ballroom project, which won final clearance from the commission in April. However, a divided federal appeals court ruled last week that construction must be paused because Congress had not separately approved the project, a decision Trump has vowed to challenge at the Supreme Court. Construction on the ballroom has already progressed on the east side of the White House complex.

## What the Counsel's Office Does

The White House counsel's office serves as the president's primary in-house legal team, advising on legal and policy matters tied to the presidency. It functions as the main point of contact with the Department of Justice, oversees the vetting of judicial nominees, manages presidential pardons and reviews pending legislation. The office also plays a central role in responding to congressional investigations into the administration and in coordinating the president's defence in lawsuits filed against him in his official capacity.

## Timing Ahead of the Midterms

The leadership change in the counsel's office comes just months before the US midterm elections, in which Trump's Republican Party will be defending its control of Congress. Democratic leaders have signalled that, should they retake either chamber, they intend to pursue fresh investigations into the Trump administration, making the counsel's office's role in managing congressional oversight especially consequential in the months ahead.

Warrington, who has held the counsel position since Trump's inauguration and previously served as personal counsel and a campaign lawyer for Trump, is expected to formally exit before Scharf takes over next month. The transition adds to a broader pattern of senior personnel moves within the administration this year as it navigates ongoing legal and political battles, including recurring congressional friction, court challenges to executive actions and the fallout from separate initiatives such as the White House ballroom project.

The appointment was reported by multiple US outlets over the weekend, with wire agencies noting that Scharf's elevation reflects Trump's preference for loyalists with direct legal advocacy experience in his most sensitive legal roles.
