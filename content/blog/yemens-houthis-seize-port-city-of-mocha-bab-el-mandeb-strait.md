Yemen's Houthi rebels seized control of the historic Red Sea port city of Mocha on Thursday, dealing a significant blow to Saudi-backed government forces and tightening the Iran-aligned group's grip on the approaches to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, one of the world's busiest shipping corridors.

Government troops withdrew from Mocha on Thursday morning after hours of intense fighting, retreating south toward Dhubab, a coastal town that sits between Mocha and the strait itself. Mocha lies less than 50 miles from Bab el-Mandeb, and its capture hands the Houthis greater leverage over a waterway that links the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and carries a substantial share of global trade between Asia, Europe and the Middle East.

## A Rapidly Shifting Front Line

The fall of Mocha caps a week of sharply intensifying combat along Yemen's western coast, in what regional observers are describing as one of the most serious escalations since a United Nations-brokered truce in 2022 largely paused major hostilities between the warring sides. The Houthis launched a large-scale offensive against Yemeni government forces roughly a week ago, and fighting in and around Mocha has already displaced thousands of families from their homes, according to reports from the area.

In response to the offensive, Saudi Arabia has reportedly carried out a fresh salvo of dozens of air strikes targeting Houthi positions, a move the group says followed its own attacks on oil facilities and other infrastructure deep inside Saudi territory. The exchange marks a sharp deterioration from the relative calm that had held in Yemen for much of the past two years, even as the broader Middle East has been consumed by a separate and expanding confrontation between the United States and Iran.

## Why Bab El-Mandeb Matters

The Bab el-Mandeb Strait is a chokepoint through which a large volume of container traffic, energy shipments and other cargo moves between Asia and Europe via the Suez Canal. Any disruption to vessel movement through the strait tends to ripple quickly through global shipping costs and insurance premiums, and further Houthi advances toward Dhubab and the strait's approaches are likely to unsettle shipping companies already navigating a volatile security environment across the wider region, including the parallel crisis around the Strait of Hormuz to the east.

## Regional And International Stakes

Yemen's internationally recognised government, headquartered in Aden and backed by a Saudi-led coalition, has struggled to hold territory against the better-organised Houthi movement for much of the past decade. The loss of Mocha is likely to raise fresh questions in Riyadh and among Yemen's other regional backers about the durability of the current military balance, particularly as attention and resources across the Gulf remain heavily focused on the ongoing US-Iran conflict further north.

For now, the immediate humanitarian toll is mounting, with aid groups warning that displaced families from Mocha and surrounding areas require urgent shelter and support. Whether the current truce framework can be salvaged, or whether Yemen is sliding back into a fuller-scale civil conflict, is likely to depend heavily on how far the Houthis push their advantage in the coming days.
