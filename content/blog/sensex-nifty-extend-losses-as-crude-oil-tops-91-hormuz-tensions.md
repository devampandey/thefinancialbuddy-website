Indian equity benchmarks extended their recent losing streak on Wednesday, with the Sensex and Nifty 50 sliding through the morning and afternoon session as elevated crude oil prices and escalating tensions around the Strait of Hormuz kept investors on edge.

## The Numbers

The 30-share BSE Sensex opened weak, slipping about 17 points to 77,218.05, while the Nifty 50 opened down roughly 3 points at 24,152.05. Selling pressure built steadily through the session: by around 1 PM, the Sensex was down over 340 points, or 0.44%, at 76,893, while the Nifty 50 had shed about 102 points, or 0.42%, to trade near 24,053. The declines marked a continuation of the pressure seen through the current week, with the indices struggling to find a sustained bounce.

## What's Driving The Selloff

Crude oil has been the dominant theme weighing on Indian markets this week. Brent crude has climbed to around the $91-$92 a barrel range, a level that raises concerns for India as one of the world's largest oil importers, given its impact on the trade deficit, the rupee and domestic inflation. The rise has been driven largely by the expiry of a US-brokered interim understanding on Strait of Hormuz shipping, along with a fresh flare-up between the UAE and Iran that has added another layer of geopolitical risk to an already jittery energy market.

Elevated global bond yields added to the cautious mood, with investors also digesting weakness in global technology and artificial intelligence-linked stocks after Wall Street pulled back from recent highs. Asian markets broadly traded lower in sympathy, reinforcing the risk-off tone that carried into Indian trading hours.

## Sector And Stock Moves

Heavyweight names including Power Grid and Bajaj Finance were among the notable drags on the Sensex through the session, while some pockets of relative strength emerged, with IT stocks attracting buying interest even as the broader market stayed under pressure, reflecting a degree of sector rotation rather than uniform selling.

## Outlook

Market participants said the near-term direction for Indian equities will likely continue to hinge on how the Strait of Hormuz standoff evolves, along with any fresh developments in US-Iran relations and the broader Gulf security situation. A sustained move in crude oil beyond current levels could further pressure oil-sensitive sectors and add to inflation concerns just as the Reserve Bank of India continues to monitor price stability. Investors are also watching global bond markets, where yields have climbed to multi-year highs, for signs of how much further risk appetite could deteriorate in the sessions ahead.
