US President Donald Trump has announced a one-time payment of $90 each to more than 20 million Medicare Part B enrollees. The money is meant to offset their monthly premiums and will be drawn from a fund that no administration has used since Congress created it in 2008. Trump posted the plan on Truth Social late on Friday night, Washington time, and the White House followed with a fact sheet.

The timing is the political point. The payments are due to arrive in early October, roughly a month before the November 3 midterm elections.

## Who gets the money, and how

Most eligible beneficiaries will receive the $90 by direct deposit in early October, according to the White House. People who do not get federal payments electronically will be sent a paper check at the address Medicare has on file.

Not everyone on Part B qualifies. Two groups are excluded:

- Enrollees whose premiums are already paid by Medicaid, which covers many low-income seniors and people with disabilities.
- Higher-income beneficiaries who pay the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA), a surcharge on top of the standard premium.

The administration has asked seniors to call Medicare's helpline to check whether they qualify, and the Social Security Administration to check on a payment's status.

## Tapping a dormant fund

The money comes from the Medicare Improvement Fund. Congress set it up in 2008 to pay for improvements to traditional fee-for-service Medicare, and lawmakers have provided it with $2 billion. Paying $90 to 20 million people would cost around $1.8 billion, which would use up most of that balance.

In his post, Trump called the fund a "slush fund" and said earlier Congresses had spent it on special interests. He described the payments as checks of nearly $100. The White House said this is the first time any president has used the fund to lower seniors' costs directly. It also pointed to a separate plan, announced in September, to refund about $500 each to roughly one million people it says were overcharged for Affordable Care Act coverage.

## The midterm backdrop

Trump tied the announcement to his campaign message. In the same post, he again promised a $5,000 "Trump Dividend" for every US citizen if Republicans win the midterms. Critics have questioned whether he could pay that without approval from Congress, and why it would depend on an election result.

Republicans are defending narrow majorities in both chambers. Polls have pointed to a difficult November for the party, especially in the House. A direct payment to tens of millions of older voters, who turn out in large numbers in midterm elections, will be widely seen as an attempt to shift that picture.

## Why the method matters

For most Medicare enrollees, $90 covers well under one month of the standard Part B premium. The amount is small next to household healthcare costs, but the method matters. The administration is using money already set aside for Medicare to make direct payments without a new vote in Congress. If more payments of this kind follow, they are likely to add to the debate over how much federal spending a president can direct without Congress.
