Godrej Consumer Products (GCPL) is a reminder that in a high-inflation quarter, the profit headline is not what the market is pricing. The maker of Good Knight, Cinthol and Hit reported double-digit growth in both revenue and profit for the September quarter, yet its shares fell as much as 7.1% to Rs 777.20 on the NSE on Friday morning, a 52-week low. By 10 am the stock had pared some losses to trade about 3.6% lower at Rs 806.50, still the worst performer on the Nifty FMCG index.

## Growth on the surface

On paper the quarter was solid. Consolidated net profit rose 13.8% year on year to Rs 522.92 crore, up from Rs 459.34 crore. Revenue from operations rose 19% to Rs 4,413 crore, and the company said underlying volume growth was 8%, with India, Indonesia and its African businesses all contributing. The board also declared an interim dividend of Rs 5 per share.

## Where it went wrong

The problem sat one line below sales. Gross margin contracted by about 300 basis points to 47.6%, from 50.6% a year earlier, as the cost of crude-linked derivatives, palm oil and other raw materials climbed. EBITDA still grew 12% to Rs 820 crore, but the EBITDA margin slipped to 18.6% from 19.8%.

GCPL described the period as one of the highest inflationary impacts it has seen in many years, putting the hit at roughly high single digits. Crude oil, a key input for many household and personal care products, is trading above $116 a barrel, and the company named palm oil, the main input for soaps, among the costs that rose.

More worrying for investors was the outlook. Management said it expects input cost inflation to intensify further in the December quarter. It has already raised prices and plans more calibrated increases, especially in soaps and liquid detergents, where it said margins are below normal levels.

## Guidance intact, estimates trimmed

The company kept its FY27 guidance of high-single-digit underlying volume growth in the standalone business and double-digit revenue and EBITDA growth at the consolidated level. That suggests demand is not the issue. The concern is how much of the growth turns into profit.

Brokerages largely stayed constructive but trimmed expectations. Motilal Oswal kept its Buy rating with a Rs 1,050 target, saying pricing and cost savings should support absolute EBITDA growth, though margin recovery will depend on how prices and commodity costs move. Jefferies retained Buy but cut its earnings estimates by 2% to 3% and lowered its target sharply to Rs 1,075 from Rs 1,400, citing higher input costs and lower valuation multiples across the sector. HSBC said the company is on track to meet its annual guidance but called inflation a continuing headwind, particularly for India margins.

## What it says about FMCG

GCPL's results land in the middle of a difficult season for consumer companies. With fuel, edible oil and packaging costs elevated, the sector faces a choice between protecting volumes and protecting margins. Price increases help margins but risk slowing demand in rural and price-sensitive markets, where soaps and insecticides are everyday purchases.

The contrast with Trent, which widened its gross margin in the same quarter and rallied on Friday, shows that investors are rewarding pricing power and punishing companies exposed to raw material swings. For GCPL, the next two quarters will test whether its price increases can catch up with costs before volume growth starts to slip.
