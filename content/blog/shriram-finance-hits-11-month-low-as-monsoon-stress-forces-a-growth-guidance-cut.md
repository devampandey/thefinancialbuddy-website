Shriram Finance has delivered one of the strongest headline profit numbers of this results season, and the market sold it anyway. The vehicle and MSME lender's shares fell as much as 6.3% on Monday to Rs 810.20 on the NSE, their lowest level since November 2025, as investors looked past a 59% jump in quarterly profit and focused on what management said about the next six months.

By late morning the stock had recovered only slightly, trading about 5% lower near Rs 821. It has now lost roughly 10% in two sessions, about 18% so far in October, and close to 30% from its 52-week high of Rs 1,153.65 set on August 6. The company's market value stands at about Rs 1.93 lakh crore.

## Strong quarter, weaker outlook

The September-quarter numbers themselves were robust. Standalone profit after tax rose to Rs 3,677 crore from Rs 2,307 crore a year earlier. Net interest income climbed 38% to Rs 8,661 crore, and assets under management grew 17% year on year to Rs 3.29 lakh crore. Net interest margin widened sharply to 9.52% from 9.04% in the June quarter, helped by putting to work the equity capital raised from Japan's MUFG and by lower funding costs. The board also declared an interim dividend of Rs 6.50 per share, with October 29 as the record date.

The problem was the guidance. Management lowered its FY27 AUM growth target to 15-17% from 18%, citing the West Asia conflict and, more specifically, the risk that a weak and erratic monsoon will squeeze rural cash flows. It also said credit costs could run 30-40 basis points above its normalised level of about 2% for the next one or two quarters, as slippages and write-offs rise in drought-affected geographies. Credit cost for the quarter came in at 2.03%, up from 1.68% a year earlier.

Asset quality on paper barely moved: gross NPAs edged up one basis point to 4.65% and net NPAs eased slightly to 2.31%. But analysts read the commentary as a signal that the stress has not yet fully shown up in the reported ratios.

## How brokerages read it

The reaction among analysts was a mix of target cuts and continued conviction. JP Morgan said operating metrics beat its estimates but provisions disappointed, and that the company now looks likely to miss its 2% credit cost guidance for the year. HSBC flagged rising stage-2 and stage-3 assets across several segments. Motilal Oswal said the stress was concentrated in specific regions and products rather than spread across the book, and still expects FY27 credit costs below 2.3%.

Jefferies, Citi and Motilal Oswal all lowered their target prices but kept Buy ratings, arguing that the margin cushion from fresh equity and the recent AAA rating upgrade should help absorb a rate-hike cycle. ICICI Securities said the valuation correction now offers upside despite the near-term concerns.

## Why it matters beyond one stock

Shriram's book is heavily exposed to used commercial vehicles, small businesses and rural borrowers, which makes its commentary an early read on how the deficient monsoon is filtering into household finances. A lender of this size openly guiding for higher bad loans in farm-dependent districts is a warning that other rural-focused NBFCs and microfinance players may report similar pressure over the coming quarters.
