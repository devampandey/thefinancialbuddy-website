The biggest privatisation deal in Indian banking produced an awkward first reaction on Dalal Street. Shares of IDBI Bank fell as much as 7.5% to Rs 83.62 on the BSE on Thursday morning, a day after the government named Canada's Fairfax Financial Holdings as the buyer for a controlling 60.72% stake in the lender.

The drop has little to do with doubts about the deal itself. It is about the price.

## The gap between Rs 81 and the market

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs on Wednesday cleared a Fairfax subsidiary as the strategic buyer at Rs 81 per share. That values the combined block at roughly Rs 52,878 crore (about $5.5 billion), split between the Centre's 30.48% holding, worth around Rs 26,620 crore, and the 30.24% held by Life Insurance Corporation of India, worth around Rs 26,440 crore.

The government said the price is above the Rs 75.99 fair value set by two independent valuers. But the market had already run well past both numbers. IDBI Bank shares jumped about 9% on Wednesday before the announcement, adding more than Rs 8,000 crore to its market value and closing at about Rs 90.44. Some traders had been betting on a deal price in the Rs 100-110 range.

With the stock trading above the price a strategic buyer agreed to pay, Thursday's fall looks largely like the speculative premium coming out. On the NSE, the stock hit an intraday low of Rs 84.97, down about 6%, and was still among the weakest names in the Nifty PSU Bank pack late in the morning, even as the Sensex rallied more than 1,000 points.

## Why the deal still matters

For the Centre, the sale ends a privatisation effort that has dragged on since 2022, when the process was formally restarted after earlier attempts stalled. It is also being described as the largest foreign investment ever made in an Indian bank.

For LIC, which became IDBI Bank's majority owner in 2019 under a rescue plan, the exit frees up a large chunk of capital that has been tied to a non-core banking holding for years.

## Hurdles still ahead

The transaction is not complete. The Reserve Bank of India still has to carry out a fit-and-proper assessment of the buyer before the shares change hands.

There is also a structural wrinkle. Fairfax, led by billionaire Prem Watsa, already controls Kerala-based CSB Bank. Indian rules do not allow a single promoter to control two banks, which means Fairfax is expected to either merge the two lenders or reduce its stake in one of them within a set period, widely reported to be about two years. How it resolves this will shape what happens to minority shareholders of both banks.

## What investors are watching

- **RBI clearance:** The timing of the central bank's approval will decide when the transfer happens.
- **Open offer:** A change in control normally triggers an open offer to public shareholders under SEBI's takeover rules, and its pricing will be closely tracked given where the stock trades today.
- **CSB Bank's future:** Any signal on a merger or stake sale will move both stocks.

For now, Thursday's trade is a reminder that a good deal for the seller and a good entry point for a late buyer are not always the same thing.
