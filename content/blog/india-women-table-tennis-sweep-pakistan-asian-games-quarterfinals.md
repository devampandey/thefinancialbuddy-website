India's women's table tennis team stormed into the Asian Games 2026 quarterfinals on Monday with a commanding 3-0 win over Pakistan, completing a perfect run through the group stage at Aichi-Nagoya.

Sutirtha Mukherjee got India off to a dominant start, beating Aleena Khan 11-3, 11-3, 11-4 in straight games. Asian Games debutant Syndrela Das followed up with a 11-6, 11-7, 11-5 win over Kalsoom Khan, before Sreeja Akula sealed the clean sweep by beating Fatima Khan 11-4, 11-4, 11-5. The result meant India finished Group F unbeaten with three wins from three matches, sending the team through to the knockout stage.

## A clinical group-stage campaign

India's build-up to the Pakistan tie was equally emphatic. The team opened its campaign with a straightforward 3-0 win over Indonesia before edging past Singapore 3-2 in a hard-fought comeback in its second group match. Against Pakistan, team management opted to rotate personnel, bringing in Sutirtha and Syndrela in place of Diya Chitale and Yashaswini Ghorpade — a change that did little to disrupt India's rhythm, with the team recording another straight-games victory to close out the group phase.

The win sets up a quarterfinal clash in the knockout rounds as India looks to build on a campaign that has already yielded an early medal boost for the country's shooting contingent on the Games' opening day.

## Men's team faces tougher road

India's men's table tennis team has had a more mixed start to its own campaign. After opening with a 3-0 win over Indonesia — featuring victories for Sathiyan Gnanasekaran, Manav Thakkar and Manush Shah — the side suffered a 0-3 defeat to Iran in its second group match, with Manush Shah, Manav Thakkar and Harmeet Desai all beaten in straight matches. The men's team will next face Pakistan in its final group fixture, a result that could prove decisive for their own knockout-stage prospects.

## Chasing a first table tennis medal beyond bronze

India has won three table tennis medals at the Asian Games to date, all of them bronze. The country's first came at Jakarta 2018, when the men's team took bronze, followed by a mixed doubles bronze for Sharath Kamal and Manika Batra at the same edition. At Hangzhou 2023, Sutirtha Mukherjee and Ayhika Mukherjee added a women's doubles bronze — India's first Asian Games medal in that event.

With the women's team now through to the last eight at Aichi-Nagoya unbeaten, India will fancy its chances of going one better than bronze this time, in what has so far been a strong start to the Games for the country's table tennis programme. The team's next assignment in the knockout stage will determine whether India can convert its group-stage dominance into the country's first non-bronze table tennis medal at the Asian Games.
