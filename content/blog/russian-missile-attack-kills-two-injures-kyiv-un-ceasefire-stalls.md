Russian forces launched a fresh barrage of ballistic missiles at Kyiv in the early hours of Thursday, killing at least two people and injuring several others, according to Ukrainian officials, in an attack that struck just hours after Russia signalled at the United Nations that it had no intention of pausing its war.

Kyiv's Air Force had warned residents of incoming ballistic missiles targeting the capital, the wider Kyiv region and Chernihiv region to the north shortly before explosions were heard across the city. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said falling debris partially destroyed a private house in the Dniprovskyi district, where rescue crews feared people may have been trapped under the rubble, while a nearby 16-storey apartment block and an eight-storey commercial building also sustained damage.

## Maternity Hospital Among Sites Hit

In the Podilskyi district, missile debris shattered windows and damaged part of the facade of a maternity hospital, and a nearby diagnostic centre was also hit. One person was killed in a parking area beside the hospital, where several vehicles caught fire. Klitschko's preliminary assessment found no one inside the maternity ward itself was hurt, and in a striking detail from the night, a baby boy was reportedly born in the facility's shelter as the attack unfolded. Emergency crews also tackled warehouse fires in the Solomianskyi and Sviatoshynskyi districts as they responded to strikes across several parts of the capital.

Thursday's strike followed a Russian drone barrage just a day earlier that killed two people and injured dozens more in Kyiv, underscoring the intensity of attacks on the capital even as diplomatic efforts to end the war continue on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York.

## Diplomacy Falters As Attacks Continue

The timing of the strike drew attention because it came only hours after Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov told the UN Security Council that Moscow would not halt its military campaign "in any format" while negotiations were under way. That statement undercut recent efforts by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, who had offered a reciprocal energy ceasefire during talks with US President Donald Trump, under which Kyiv would stop targeting Russian energy infrastructure if Moscow halted strikes on Ukraine's power, heating and water systems.

Trump had told the General Assembly he expected progress toward ending the war sooner than many observers anticipated, but no agreement had been reached by the time the missiles hit Kyiv. Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha condemned Russia for obstructing a ceasefire, and more than fifty countries backed a joint statement at the UN urging Moscow to halt its attacks and engage in substantive negotiations.

The renewed strikes highlight the widening gap between the diplomatic overtures being exchanged in New York and the situation on the ground in Ukraine, where civilian infrastructure continues to come under regular attack even as world leaders discuss pathways to peace. With no ceasefire agreement in place and both sides continuing military operations, the human cost in cities like Kyiv shows little sign of easing in the near term.
