EAAA India Alternatives, the alternative investments business of the Edelweiss group, opened its initial public offering for subscription on Monday. The Rs 1,200 crore issue is priced in a band of Rs 1,585 to Rs 1,665 per share and closes on Wednesday, October 28. Allotment is expected on October 29 and the shares are scheduled to list on the NSE and BSE on November 2.

## What is on offer

The issue is entirely an offer for sale of about 72.07 lakh shares by promoter Edelweiss Securities and Investments. That means EAAA itself will not receive a rupee; the money goes to the selling shareholder. The size was cut from the Rs 1,500 crore proposed in the draft papers. Half the net offer is reserved for qualified institutional buyers, 15% for non-institutional investors and 35% for retail investors, who can bid for a minimum of nine shares, or just under Rs 15,000 at the upper end.

Ahead of the opening, the company raised roughly Rs 356-360 crore from 26 anchor investors at Rs 1,665 a share, the top of the band. Domestic mutual funds took the largest slice at about 60% of the anchor book, with foreign portfolio investors including Goldman Sachs and BNP Paribas, and alternative investment funds making up the rest.

## The business

Set up in 2008, EAAA manages private funds focused on two areas: real assets such as infrastructure, including two InvITs, and private credit lending to companies. It runs 10 active real assets funds and 16 private credit funds. Fee-paying assets under management stood at about Rs 48,600 crore as of June 2026, split roughly Rs 28,040 crore in real assets and Rs 19,272 crore in private credit. Institutions account for around half of its AUM, alongside wealthy individuals and family offices.

In FY26, the company reported revenue of Rs 963.76 crore and net profit of Rs 265.20 crore. In the June 2026 quarter alone, it earned Rs 80.71 crore on revenue of Rs 336.13 crore.

## Valuation: our read

At the upper end of the band, EAAA is valued at about Rs 10,765 crore, or roughly 40.6 times FY26 earnings per share of Rs 41.02 and 33.2 times EV/EBITDA. That is a full price for a business whose earnings depend partly on performance-linked fees and carried interest, which can swing sharply from year to year as funds exit investments. Listed asset and wealth managers trade across a wide band of about 21 to 80 times earnings, so EAAA sits in the middle, without the predictable recurring fee base that justifies the top end.

The positives are real. India's private credit and infrastructure funds market is still small relative to the economy, regulators have been supportive, and management fees in alternatives are far richer than in mutual funds. The concerns are equally specific: brokerages point to a rising cost-to-income ratio, margins that slipped to about 24% in the June quarter, declining return on equity, and the fact that a pure OFS gives the company no fresh capital to grow.

## How brokers see it

Opinion is split. Anand Rathi and Ventura recommend subscribing, mainly for the long term. SBI Securities, SMC Global and Religare are neutral, while BP Equities and Marwadi Financial advise avoiding the issue on valuation. The grey market premium of about Rs 45-50 implies a listing gain of only 2-3%, signalling limited enthusiasm for a quick flip.

For investors, this looks more like a long-duration bet on the growth of India's alternatives industry than a listing-day trade.
