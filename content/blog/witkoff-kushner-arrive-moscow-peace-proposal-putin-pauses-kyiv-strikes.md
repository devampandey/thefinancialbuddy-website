US President Donald Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff and his son-in-law Jared Kushner touched down in Moscow on Saturday, carrying what the White House has described as a proposal to end Russia's war in Ukraine, now grinding into its fifth year. The visit marks the pair's first trip to the Russian capital together on this mission, and they are expected to travel on to Kyiv afterward for their first-ever visit there as a team.

Kirill Dmitriev, a special representative of President Vladimir Putin, greeted the American delegation on arrival at Vnukovo airport, and Russian state media reported that Putin himself was scheduled to meet the envoys later in the day. Trump, speaking ahead of the trip, struck an optimistic note, suggesting there was "a good chance" a deal could come together, though he stopped short of offering specifics on what the proposal contains.

## A Fragile Pause Over Kyiv

In a gesture timed to the envoys' arrival, the Kremlin announced that Putin had instructed Russian forces to halt strikes on Kyiv for three days, starting at midnight Saturday. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky responded by saying Ukraine would also suspend its own air attacks for the duration of the visit, while calling on Moscow to extend the same restraint beyond the capital. The de-escalation, however limited and temporary, comes just a day after Russian drones and missiles struck Kyiv and the nearby Boryspil area overnight, damaging a residential building and injuring at least two people, underscoring how quickly the fighting has continued even as diplomacy resumes.

## Moscow Signals It Won't Bend Easily

Despite the choreographed goodwill around the visit, Russian officials have been careful to temper expectations. Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov said the United States needs to reckon with what he called shifting "realities on the ground," arguing that the battlefield situation has moved in Russia's favor rather than Ukraine's. That framing suggests Moscow may use the talks to press for concessions favorable to its territorial and security demands rather than simply accept a US-brokered framework.

The trip is the latest in a string of American efforts this year to find an opening for a negotiated end to the conflict, following earlier rounds of shuttle diplomacy between Washington, Kyiv and Moscow that have so far failed to produce a lasting breakthrough. What is different this time, according to officials familiar with the planning, is that Witkoff and Kushner intend to carry the same proposal to both capitals in immediate succession, rather than negotiating with each side separately over an extended period.

## What Happens Next

Neither the Kremlin nor the White House has detailed the substance of the proposal, and it remains unclear whether it addresses the toughest sticking points in the conflict, including territorial control in eastern Ukraine, security guarantees for Kyiv, and the pace of any sanctions relief for Russia. The envoys' onward trip to Kyiv will be watched closely for whether Zelensky's government finds the terms workable, particularly given Ryabkov's signal that Moscow sees little reason to soften its position.

For now, the three-day strike pause offers a rare, if narrow, window of calm for residents of Kyiv, even as both sides continue to trade accusations over who bears responsibility for the war's continuation. Markets and diplomats alike are likely to treat the visit as a test of whether renewed high-level engagement can succeed where previous rounds of talks have stalled.
