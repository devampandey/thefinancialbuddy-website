Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Russian President Vladimir Putin held bilateral talks in New Delhi on Friday, hours ahead of the formal start of the BRICS Summit that India is chairing this year. The meeting, held at Bharat Mandapam, marks Putin's first in-person appearance at a BRICS gathering outside Russian soil since the war in Ukraine began, lending the visit added diplomatic weight beyond the routine agenda of a bilateral summit.

## A Broad Agenda

According to a statement from India's Ministry of External Affairs, the two leaders exchanged views on India-Russia engagement in the multilateral sphere, particularly within BRICS under India's chairship for 2026. Discussions also touched on regional and global flashpoints of mutual concern, including the ongoing conflicts in West Asia and the Black Sea region, both of which have disrupted maritime trade routes and raised safety concerns for Indian seafarers operating in affected waters.

Beyond geopolitics, the talks reportedly covered a wide sweep of economic and strategic cooperation, spanning trade and investment, energy, defence, technology, pharmaceuticals, and people-to-people exchanges. Civil nuclear energy, fertilisers, critical minerals, manufacturing, railways, and steel were also flagged as areas the two governments intend to deepen collaboration on, building on the framework of the India-Russia Programme for Economic Cooperation to 2030. Officials on both sides pointed to an ambitious bilateral trade target of $100 billion by 2030 as a marker of how far the relationship still has to travel from current levels.

## Reaffirming A Strategic Partnership

In their joint statement, Modi and Putin reaffirmed their commitment to strengthening the "Special and Privileged Strategic Partnership" between the two countries, language both governments have used for years to describe a relationship built on decades of defence cooperation, energy trade, and diplomatic alignment on issues where India and Russia have historically found common ground, including at the United Nations Security Council.

Putin arrived in New Delhi in the early hours of Friday for a three-day visit, timed around the BRICS Summit, which formally opens this weekend. His decision to travel to India for an in-person summit, rather than participating virtually as he has done at several international gatherings since 2022, is being read by diplomatic observers as a signal of the continued importance Moscow places on its ties with New Delhi even as Western sanctions and the broader fallout from the Ukraine war continue to reshape Russia's global engagements.

## What Comes Next

With the bilateral talks concluded, attention now turns to the broader BRICS Summit proceedings, where Chinese President Xi Jinping is expected to hold separate talks with Modi in the coming day, alongside other leaders of the grouping. India's chairship of BRICS this year has put New Delhi at the centre of efforts to expand the bloc's economic and diplomatic footprint, even as member states navigate diverging positions on issues ranging from trade to regional conflicts.

For India, the Modi-Putin meeting offers a chance to advance concrete economic asks, including energy security and defence procurement, at a moment when global oil and shipping markets remain unsettled by tensions in West Asia. How much of Friday's talks translates into firm agreements is likely to become clearer as the BRICS Summit proceeds over the weekend.
