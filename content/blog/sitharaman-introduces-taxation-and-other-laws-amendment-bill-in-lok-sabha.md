Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman introduced the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, in the Lok Sabha this week, as Parliament's monsoon session took up a cluster of financial legislation. The bill seeks to further amend three pieces of existing law: the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the Finance Act, 2026, folding changes across payments regulation and direct taxation into a single legislative package.

## What the Bill Covers

The Payment and Settlement Systems Act governs how money moves through India's banking and digital payment infrastructure, including the rules underpinning systems like UPI, NEFT and RTGS as well as the oversight powers the RBI holds over payment operators. Amendments to this Act typically address either regulatory gaps that have emerged as digital payment volumes have grown or updates needed to keep pace with new payment technologies and business models. The bill's changes to the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the Finance Act, 2026, extend into direct tax administration, though the specific provisions will only become clear as the bill moves through debate and committee scrutiny.

Alongside the bill, Sitharaman is also expected to formally explain the Income-tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026, laying an explanatory statement before the House in both Hindi and English. Ordinances are emergency legislative instruments the government can issue when Parliament is not in session; when the House reconvenes, the government is constitutionally required to place the ordinance before lawmakers and eventually replace it with regular legislation, which appears to be part of the intent behind this bill.

## Part of a Busy Legislative Stretch

The introduction comes in the middle of an active few days for Parliament's monsoon session, which began on July 20 and is scheduled to run through August 13. In the days just before, the Lok Sabha passed a separate bill to expand the Supreme Court's judge strength, while the Rajya Sabha cleared the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (Amendment) Bill, aimed at strengthening support for smaller businesses. Taken together, the sequence suggests the government is using the back half of the session to push through a backlog of legislative priorities spanning the judiciary, industry support and financial regulation.

## Why It Matters for Markets and Businesses

Changes to payment-systems law and income-tax provisions carry direct relevance for businesses operating in India's financial and technology sectors, from banks and fintech firms to any company navigating digital payment compliance. Amendments folded into a Finance Act amendment bill can also carry retrospective or clarificatory tax implications, which is typically why markets and corporate tax teams watch this category of legislation closely even when the headline coverage focuses on the political theatre of the session. As the bill moves toward further debate, more detail is expected to emerge on the specific clauses being amended and their effective dates.
