The initial public offering of Ardee Industries Limited saw a dramatic jump in investor demand on its second day of bidding on Thursday, with overall subscription climbing well past 14 times by late afternoon, according to exchange data. The sharp acceleration marks a significant step-up from the company's opening day, when the issue had crossed roughly two times subscription.

## What's Driving the Surge

The Rs 425.87 crore mainboard offering, priced in a band of Rs 50 to Rs 53 per share, is being led higher by an aggressive push from non-institutional investors (NIIs), a category that typically includes high-net-worth individuals and corporate bidders making large-ticket applications. Exchange bidding data through the day showed NII demand climbing well above 17 times its allotted quota, comfortably outpacing both the retail and institutional segments.

Retail investor interest also picked up meaningfully as the day progressed, with that portion of the book moving into double-digit subscription territory. The qualified institutional buyer (QIB) segment, which typically books the bulk of its demand only in the final hours of an issue, remained more measured through Thursday's session, a pattern common for IPOs that still have a full day of bidding left.

## About the Offer

Ardee Industries operates in the secondary metals and circular economy space, an industry tied to recycling and reprocessing metal for reuse in manufacturing. The company's IPO opened for subscription on Tuesday and is scheduled to remain open through Friday, giving investors one more full trading session to place bids before the book closes. Shares are tentatively expected to debut on the BSE and NSE around August 12, pending finalisation of allotment.

## Why the Jump Matters

A sharp swing from roughly 2x subscription on day one to well over 14x on day two is a notable signal in India's primary market, where bidding momentum in the closing stretch of an issue often determines listing-day sentiment. Heavy NII participation in particular is often watched closely by market observers, since this category tends to use leveraged funding to bid for shares, and strong demand here can sometimes translate into elevated grey market activity ahead of listing. Retail investors and analysts tracking the offer will likely be watching Friday's final subscription numbers, along with any movement in the unofficial grey market premium, for cues on how the stock might perform once it lists.

As with any IPO, investors are typically advised to weigh subscription enthusiasm alongside the company's underlying fundamentals, sector outlook, and valuation before making allotment or listing-day decisions, rather than relying on subscription numbers alone.
