Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Saturday inaugurated Param Pragya, a new AI-powered high-performance supercomputing facility at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi's Sonepat campus, timing the launch to coincide with the institute's 57th convocation ceremony.

## A New Computing Backbone for Research

Param Pragya is designed to significantly boost IIT Delhi's capabilities in artificial intelligence, data science, and advanced computational research, giving faculty and students access to high-performance computing infrastructure for work that would otherwise require external or international resources. The facility adds to India's broader Param series of supercomputers, which the government has used over the past several years to expand indigenous high-performance computing capacity at premier research institutions.

By locating the facility on IIT Delhi's newer Sonepat campus, the institute is signalling that advanced computing and AI research will be a central pillar of its expansion plans, rather than an add-on to existing departments.

## Convocation Backdrop

The inauguration took place alongside IIT Delhi's 57th convocation, where Modi served as chief guest and conferred degrees on more than 3,000 graduating students, including 587 PhD scholars. He also presented top honours such as the President's Gold Medal and the Director's Gold Medal to the institute's highest-performing graduates.

In his address, Modi told students that the pace of technological change makes continuous learning essential, urging graduates to keep their curiosity alive rather than treat their degree as a finishing point. He framed the ability to adapt to fast-moving fields such as AI as a defining skill for the next generation of engineers and researchers, and encouraged students to see emerging challenges as opportunities rather than obstacles.

## Part of a Wider AI Push

The Param Pragya launch fits into a broader pattern of the government backing AI and high-performance computing infrastructure at India's top technical institutions. It follows earlier rollouts of Param Rudra supercomputing systems at other centres, part of a sustained push to build indigenous computing capacity rather than relying solely on cloud infrastructure from overseas providers.

For IIT Delhi specifically, the addition of a dedicated AI supercomputing facility comes at a time when Indian engineering institutions are competing globally for AI research talent and trying to expand their footprint in fields like large language models, computer vision, and scientific computing, all of which are compute-intensive and benefit directly from dedicated infrastructure of this kind.

## Why It Matters

For India's technology and research ecosystem, facilities like Param Pragya matter less as one-off announcements and more as an indicator of where public investment in AI infrastructure is headed. As Indian startups and research labs increasingly compete on access to compute power, having elite institutions equipped with dedicated high-performance systems could help retain research talent domestically and support more ambitious AI projects without full dependence on foreign cloud providers.
