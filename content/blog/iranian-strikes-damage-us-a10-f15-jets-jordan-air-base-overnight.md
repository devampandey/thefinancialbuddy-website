Multiple United States military aircraft were damaged overnight when Iranian missiles struck the Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan, marking one of the most direct hits on American hardware since the US-Iran conflict entered its seventh month. The strike has intensified concerns that the war, which began with US and Israeli operations against Iranian nuclear and military sites earlier this year, is now inflicting tangible damage on US assets stationed across the region.

## What Happened

According to multiple reports citing people familiar with the matter, an A-10 Thunderbolt II, commonly known as the Warthog, lost a wing after being struck during the barrage. Roughly eight F-15 fighter jets also sustained light damage but were reportedly returned to service. US and Jordanian air defenses, including Patriot missile batteries, intercepted the majority of the incoming projectiles, but the scale of the barrage meant several missiles got through.

The attack followed a pattern of escalating retaliatory strikes. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps had earlier claimed responsibility for targeting US-related sites in Jordan after American forces destroyed five Iranian oil tankers on September 8, which the Pentagon said was a response to repeated Iranian attempts to strike a US Navy warship. Jordanian officials said air defense systems intercepted the bulk of the roughly 20 ballistic missiles fired toward the kingdom, with no loss of life reported among Jordanian personnel.

## A Widening Conflict

The damage to American aircraft is a notable escalation in a conflict that has largely been fought through strikes on shipping, tankers, and military infrastructure rather than direct hits on frontline US hardware. Iran's Revolutionary Guard has separately claimed it targeted US Navy destroyers and additional tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, though US Central Command has publicly rejected those specific claims as false, saying all attempted attacks on its warships failed.

Muwaffaq Salti Air Base has served as a key staging ground for American air operations in the region throughout the conflict. Analysts tracking the war note that any sustained damage to US air assets there could affect the tempo of American operations, even as officials in Washington have downplayed the overall impact and stressed that no US personnel were killed in the latest exchange.

## Why It Matters

The incident lands at a moment when the broader war is already reshaping regional dynamics, from surging global oil prices to a fresh push by Western powers to refer Iran's nuclear program to the United Nations Security Council. With both sides now trading direct strikes on military hardware rather than solely on shipping and infrastructure, the risk of further escalation, and of a longer, more costly conflict than either side initially anticipated, appears to be rising. US officials have reportedly cautioned that the war could extend well beyond the current political calendar in Washington, underscoring how difficult it may be to find an offramp in the near term.
