India's women's singles badminton contingent produced its best day of the Korea Masters 2026 on Friday, as Ashmita Chaliha and Rakshitha Ramraj both won their quarter-final matches to set up an all-Indian semifinal at the Yi Sun-sin Gymnasium in Asan, South Korea. The result guarantees India at least one finalist in the women's singles draw of the BWF Super 300 event and keeps alive the country's hopes of another title on the international circuit.

## Chaliha Pulls Off the Tournament's Biggest Upset

The standout result of the day came from World No. 50 Ashmita Chaliha, who knocked out top seed Hina Akechi of Japan in a gritty three-game battle. Chaliha lost a tight opening game 20-22 before finding her rhythm to take the second game comfortably 21-15, and then held her nerve through a tense decider to close it out 21-19. The win over Akechi, ranked well inside the world's top 25, stands as Chaliha's biggest scalp of the tournament and a significant confidence boost as she continues to build momentum on the senior international circuit.

## Ramraj Overcomes Sharma in an All-Indian Battle

In the other quarter-final, unseeded Rakshitha Ramraj came from behind to beat third seed and compatriot Tanvi Sharma 18-21, 21-19, 21-18. Sharma, who had arrived at the tournament fresh off her maiden BWF Super 300 title win in Taipei and was seen as one of India's brightest prospects this week, took the first game but could not hold off Ramraj's fightback over the next two. The win marked Ramraj's fourth consecutive victory over Sharma in their head-to-head record, underlining a rivalry that has increasingly tilted in Ramraj's favour.

## What It Means for India

With both semifinalists now Indian, the country is assured of at least one player in Saturday's final, regardless of how the last-four clash between Chaliha and Ramraj plays out. For Indian badminton, which has leaned heavily on a small group of established stars in recent years, the emergence of depth in the women's singles category, with multiple players capable of going deep in Super 300-level events in the same week, is a notable development. It also continues a strong stretch for the women's contingent at this year's Korea Masters, following a tournament in which higher-profile names like Anmol Kharb bowed out earlier in the draw while the likes of Chaliha and Ramraj kept advancing.

## Contrast With the Men's Draw

The women's singles success stood in contrast to a quieter week for India in the men's draw, where Kidambi Srikanth had already exited in the first round. That split outcome, a disappointing week for the men and a breakthrough one for the women, has been a recurring theme in Indian badminton's results through 2026, as a new generation of women's singles players continues to close the gap on the sport's traditional powerhouses from East and Southeast Asia.

Saturday's all-Indian semifinal between Chaliha and Ramraj will not only decide who reaches the final but will also add another chapter to a head-to-head series that Ramraj has increasingly dominated. Either way, India's campaign in Asan has already delivered one of its most encouraging results of the badminton season so far.
