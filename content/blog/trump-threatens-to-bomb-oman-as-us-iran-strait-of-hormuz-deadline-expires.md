US President Donald Trump issued a blunt warning to Oman on Monday, saying Washington would strike the Gulf nation if it obstructed ongoing efforts to resolve navigation disputes in the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most critical oil shipping corridors. The remarks came in an interview with Fox News correspondent Trey Yingst, during which Trump was asked about the status of talks between Iran and Oman over future maritime arrangements through the strait.

The comments landed on the same day that a 60-day memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran, aimed at resolving the broader standoff between the two countries, formally expired without a follow-on agreement in place. Trump told Yingst he was "not in a hurry" to finalize a permanent deal with Tehran, even as the deadline lapsed.

## Oman-Iran Talks Continue

Despite the expiration of the US-Iran memorandum, Oman and Iran have reportedly made some headway in their own separate negotiations. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson said earlier in the day that the two countries had reached an understanding on a proposed map for a transit route through the strait, though officials on both sides have yet to finalize the specifics of new shipping lanes.

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the most strategically significant waterways in global trade, having carried roughly a third of the world's seaborne crude oil in recent years, according to International Energy Agency estimates. Shipping through the passage has been severely disrupted for months amid the wider tensions between Washington and Tehran, with traffic reduced to a fraction of normal levels.

## A Volatile Negotiating Style

Trump's threat against Oman, a longtime US partner in the region that has often served as a diplomatic intermediary with Iran, underscores the unpredictable nature of the administration's approach to the standoff. Even as his own memorandum with Iran expired without resolution, Trump directed his most pointed remarks at Oman, a country that has largely played a facilitating role rather than a combative one in the dispute.

The White House's rapid response team amplified a clip of Yingst recounting the exchange, suggesting the administration was comfortable with the threat becoming public. Trump has previously argued, controversially, that the United States effectively "owns" the Strait of Hormuz, despite the waterway running through the territorial waters of both Iran and Oman.

## What It Means for Markets

The renewed uncertainty over the strait has kept global oil markets on edge, with crude prices holding near multi-month highs as traders weigh the risk of further disruption to one of the busiest oil-transit routes on the planet. Any escalation involving Oman, a stable and relatively low-friction actor in the standoff until now, could complicate efforts to fully restore normal shipping traffic through the corridor.

For now, officials in Muscat have not issued a public response to Trump's remarks. Analysts tracking the region say the comments are unlikely to derail the technical talks between Oman and Iran in the near term, but they add another layer of unpredictability to a negotiation that already has significant implications for global energy prices and trade flows.
