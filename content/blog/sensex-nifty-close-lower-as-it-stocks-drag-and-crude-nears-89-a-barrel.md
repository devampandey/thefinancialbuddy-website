India's benchmark equity indices closed lower on Monday, extending a cautious tone from last week as weakness in information technology stocks and persistent worries over elevated crude oil prices weighed on sentiment. The Sensex slipped 281.09 points, or 0.36 percent, to settle at 77,728.16, while the Nifty 50 declined 78.35 points, or 0.32 percent, to close at 24,287.65.

## IT Stocks Lead the Decline

The technology sector was the single biggest drag on both benchmarks, with the Nifty IT index falling nearly 2 percent over the session. Heavyweight IT names bore the brunt of the selling: HCL Technologies dropped roughly 2.6 percent, Infosys fell about 2.5 percent, and Tata Consultancy Services declined around 2 percent. Tech Mahindra and Wipro also ended in negative territory. Other laggards included Sun Pharmaceutical, Nestle India, ITC, Cipla and Bharti Airtel, all of which slipped between roughly 1 and 2.5 percent.

Not every sector moved lower, however. Metals and select banking names bucked the trend, with Hindalco Industries gaining close to 2 percent and Tata Steel and JSW Steel also finishing higher. Axis Bank added close to 1 percent, while HDFC Life Insurance, Larsen & Toubro, ONGC, Reliance Industries and Bharat Electronics all closed in positive territory, helping cushion the broader market's losses.

## Crude Oil and Geopolitics Weigh on Sentiment

Much of the day's caution stemmed from global crude oil prices, which hovered near $89 a barrel after climbing roughly 4.6 percent over the previous week. The rise has been driven in large part by uncertainty tied to the standoff between the United States and Iran over the Strait of Hormuz, a critical corridor for global oil shipments that has seen disrupted traffic for months. With a US-Iran memorandum of understanding on the issue lapsing on Monday, and fresh rhetoric from Washington directed at Oman over its role in the talks, traders have grown warier of further supply-side shocks.

Elevated crude prices are a particular concern for India, which imports the vast majority of its oil needs. Higher energy import costs tend to widen the country's trade deficit and add pressure to the rupee, which has also been under strain in recent sessions, hovering near record-weak levels against the US dollar.

## Broader Market Context

Foreign fund flows remain a key swing factor for Indian markets in the current environment, with elevated oil prices and currency weakness typically discouraging overseas investors from adding to their India positions. Domestic institutional buying has helped offset some of this pressure in recent sessions, but market participants say sentiment is likely to stay choppy until there is more clarity on both the geopolitical situation in the Gulf and the trajectory of global interest rates.

Monday's session also came against the backdrop of a busy corporate calendar, with several companies reporting quarterly results and at least one high-profile stock market debut adding to overall trading volumes. Analysts said investors are likely to keep a close watch on crude oil prices, the rupee, and incoming corporate earnings in the sessions ahead for cues on the market's near-term direction.
