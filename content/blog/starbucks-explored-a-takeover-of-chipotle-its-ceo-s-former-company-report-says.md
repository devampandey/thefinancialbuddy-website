Starbucks has explored a takeover of Chipotle Mexican Grill, the Financial Times reported on Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter. If a deal were struck, it would bring together two of America's best-known restaurant brands and would rank as the largest acquisition the restaurant industry has seen.

According to the report, the coffee chain has worked with advisers in recent months on a possible proposal. No formal offer has been confirmed, and neither company immediately responded to requests for comment. Such talks often fall apart, so it is possible no deal will ever be announced.

## The Niccol connection

What makes the idea plausible is the person running Starbucks. Brian Niccol took over as chief executive in September 2024 after six years at the helm of Chipotle, where he is widely credited with turning the burrito chain into one of the strongest growth stories in US dining. He sped up digital ordering, expanded drive-through pickup lanes and steadied a brand still recovering from food safety scares.

At Starbucks he has been running a turnaround plan built on simplifying menus, speeding up service and restoring a cafe experience that many customers felt had been lost to mobile-order crowding. A move to buy his old company would be an unusual bet: Starbucks would be taking on a large new business while its own recovery is still a work in progress.

## How markets reacted

Investors gave a quick verdict. Chipotle shares rose about 4% in early New York trading after the report, while Starbucks fell about 3%. That pattern is typical when a potential buyer is expected to pay a premium, and when its shareholders worry about the price and the distraction of a large integration.

Any deal would also draw antitrust attention, though the two chains overlap less than it might seem: one sells coffee and breakfast, the other fast-casual Mexican lunches and dinners. The bigger questions would be financing, the premium Chipotle's board would demand, and whether Starbucks investors would back such a bold bet.

## Why it matters beyond the US

Both companies are bellwethers for consumer spending, and a combination would create a global restaurant group with a much larger footprint. Starbucks operates in India through Tata Starbucks, a joint venture with Tata Consumer Products, which runs stores across dozens of Indian cities. Chipotle has a limited international presence, mainly in Canada and Europe, and has been pushing into the Middle East through franchise partners.

A combined company could, in theory, use Starbucks' global partnerships to take Chipotle into new markets faster. For now, though, the report describes exploration rather than negotiation, and the next signal will be whether either company confirms or denies talks.
