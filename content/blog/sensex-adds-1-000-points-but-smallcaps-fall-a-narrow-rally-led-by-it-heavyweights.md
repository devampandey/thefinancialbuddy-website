The headline numbers on Thursday morning looked like a broad relief rally. They were not.

By 11 am, the Sensex had climbed 1,062 points, or 1.46%, to 73,844, and the Nifty 50 was up 310 points at 22,932, reclaiming the 22,900 mark and more than wiping out Wednesday's losses. Yet over the same stretch, the Nifty Smallcap 100 was down more than 1%, and the Nifty Next 50 had also spent the early part of the session in the red. The gains were concentrated in a handful of large, index-heavy stocks, while a big part of the market went the other way.

## Where the points came from

Information technology did most of the lifting. The Nifty IT index rose about 2.1% to 30,805 by mid-morning, its third straight session of gains, with Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, HCLTech and Tech Mahindra among the leaders. The sector has been running on two supports this week: HCLTech's better-than-expected September-quarter profit growth, and positioning ahead of results from Wipro, due later on Thursday, and Tech Mahindra, whose board meets on October 15 and 16 and will also consider its first bonus issue in more than a decade.

Because IT majors carry heavy weights in both benchmarks, a 2% move in the sector translates into a large points gain for the Sensex even when most other stocks are flat or lower. Auto shares such as Hero MotoCorp, Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra added support.

## The oil cue

Crude offered the backdrop. Brent's December futures slipped about 1.7% to around $104 a barrel in early trade, retreating from the near-$107 level seen earlier in the week. For an economy that imports most of its oil, every dollar off the price eases pressure on inflation, which is why rate-sensitive and consumption names often react to crude moves.

But Brent above $100 is still historically high. The pullback is a relief, not a resolution, and that helps explain why investors put money into large, liquid stocks with earnings visibility rather than spreading it across the market.

## What fell

Public sector banks were among the weakest pockets. The Nifty PSU Bank index slipped about 1%, dragged down by Canara Bank, which fell as much as 7% as investors focused on muted operating profit growth and a drop in treasury income in its September-quarter numbers, a sharp reversal from the gains that followed its [results on Wednesday](/business/canara-bank-q2-profit-rises-8-percent-to-rs-5-175-crore-as-lower-provisions-lift-shares). IDBI Bank shed 6-7% after the government agreed to sell a controlling stake to Fairfax at a price below where the stock had been trading. Bank of Maharashtra and Bank of India also slipped.

In smallcaps, names such as HFCL, MTAR Technologies, Netweb Technologies and refiners MRPL and Chennai Petroleum fell up to 5%.

## Why breadth matters

A rally led by a few heavyweights can lift index levels without improving the experience of most investors, especially those holding mid and smallcap portfolios. Durable recoveries usually see participation widen over time. Thursday's morning session showed the opposite: the benchmarks gained ground while the broader market lost it.

The next test arrives after market hours, when Wipro, Nestle India and dozens of other companies report earnings, with Tech Mahindra's numbers close behind. If the IT numbers disappoint, the sector that carried Thursday's move could quickly become the one that unwinds it.
