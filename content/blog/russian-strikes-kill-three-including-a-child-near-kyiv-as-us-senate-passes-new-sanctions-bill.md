Russian strikes killed three people, including a child, and wounded three others in the Kyiv region overnight into Saturday, as explosions and fires broke out across the Ukrainian capital in one of the latest bombardments in an escalating campaign against the country's cities.

## Attack Hits Kyiv's Outskirts

The strikes hit three locations in the Brovary district, just outside Kyiv, according to Tymur Tkachenko, head of the regional administration, who said the toll included a child among the dead and another among the injured. Ukraine's air force began warning residents of incoming drone waves shortly after midnight local time, with the first explosions reported in the capital roughly 45 minutes later, followed by further blasts and, by around 3 a.m., ballistic missile strikes as emergency crews were still working to extinguish fires in Brovary. Within Kyiv itself, fires broke out in the Holosiivskyi and Obolonskyi districts, while explosions were also reported in the city of Dnipro.

The attack struck a residential building, vehicles and outbuildings in Brovary, a suburb that was already reeling from a mass Russian strike just days earlier that killed eight people at a train station platform. Officials described the latest assault as part of a broader pattern in which Ukraine's depleted air defences have struggled to keep pace with the scale of Russian bombardment, leaving the country's cities increasingly exposed to ballistic missiles that travel at several times the speed of sound and give defenders only seconds to react. Patriot systems supplied by the United States remain the only weapons in Ukraine's arsenal capable of reliably intercepting such missiles, but officials in Kyiv say they lack anywhere near enough batteries or interceptors to shield the capital and surrounding towns properly.

## Sanctions Bill Clears the Senate

The overnight strikes came just hours after the US Senate passed a sweeping new sanctions package aimed at squeezing Russia's energy revenues, approving the measure by an 86-11 vote on Friday. The bill would impose steep tariffs, including a 100% levy, on countries that continue purchasing Russian oil and gas, and is named after the late Senator Lindsey Graham, a longtime advocate for tougher action against Moscow. It still needs approval from the House of Representatives and a signature from President Donald Trump before taking effect. Zelensky welcomed the vote, while European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the measure would help curtail Russia's ability to finance the war.

## A Broader Pattern of Strikes

The Brovary attack is the latest in a string of major strikes on the Kyiv region; a similar bombardment just 48 hours earlier killed 17 people and injured nearly 30 after Russia launched dozens of ballistic and hypersonic missiles that went entirely unintercepted. Ukraine's defence ministry has said the country managed to intercept only a fraction of the roughly 195 ballistic missiles Russia fired in July. Ukrainian officials have also continued to strike targets deep inside Russian territory, including oil refineries and logistics sites, as both sides press attacks even as diplomatic pressure over sanctions and Western military support intensifies.
