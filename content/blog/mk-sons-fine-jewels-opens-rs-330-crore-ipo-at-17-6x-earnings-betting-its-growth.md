MK Sons Fine Jewels, a regional jewellery retailer with five showrooms across Mumbai and Ahmedabad, opened its initial public offering on Wednesday, October 28. The issue is priced in a band of Rs 153 to Rs 162 per share and closes on Friday, October 30, with shares scheduled to list on the BSE and NSE on November 4.

## The offer at a glance

The total issue is worth up to about Rs 330 crore, made up of a fresh issue of roughly Rs 275 crore and an offer for sale of about Rs 55 crore by existing shareholders. Retail investors must apply for a minimum of 91 shares, which works out to Rs 14,742 at the top of the band. Half of the net offer is reserved for qualified institutional buyers, 35% for retail investors and 15% for non-institutional investors.

A day before opening, the company raised Rs 82.62 crore from nine anchor investors by allotting 51 lakh shares at Rs 162 each. The anchor book included Venus Investments VCC, Advent Global Fund PCC, Goldman Sachs Bank Europe and several smaller offshore funds. Aryaman Financial Services is the sole lead manager.

Fresh-issue proceeds will go towards a new showroom in Maharashtra, repayment of debt and general corporate purposes. Brokerage notes point to a planned Ulhasnagar store and an expansion of the Ahmedabad outlet as the main near-term growth projects.

## The business

Incorporated in 2012, MK Sons sells gold, diamond and cubic zirconia jewellery under the MK Jewels brand. Its five company-owned showrooms sit in high-footfall jewellery districts — Bandra, Andheri and Zaveri Bazaar in Mumbai, and CG Road and Sindhu Bhavan Road in Ahmedabad. Every rupee of revenue comes from these physical stores; there is no online channel. The company also does not manufacture, buying finished pieces from third-party makers and using a small team of in-house artisans for custom work.

## Growth has been rapid

In FY26, the company reported a net profit of Rs 51.8 crore on revenue of Rs 538.9 crore. For the June 2026 quarter, it earned Rs 15 crore on revenue of Rs 101.5 crore. Between FY24 and FY26, revenue grew at a compound annual rate of about 57%, while EBITDA and net profit each compounded at more than 150% a year — a sign that margins have expanded sharply, not just sales.

## Is the valuation reasonable?

At the top of the band, the company is valued at a little over Rs 910 crore. SBI Securities puts the issue at about 17.6 times earnings on post-issue capital, while BP Equities calculates around 13.4 times FY26 earnings per share of Rs 12.13. Either way, that is well below the multiples at which India's large listed jewellery chains have traded, which partly reflects the obvious gap in scale, brand reach and store count.

The discount looks justified by the risks. The business is heavily dependent on a single flagship store on CG Road in Ahmedabad, operates in only two cities, and carries high working-capital needs because jewellery inventory is expensive to hold. Kunvarji Wealth also flagged rising inventory and inconsistent operating cash flows. With gold prices volatile, a sharp correction could squeeze both inventory values and customer demand.

## What brokerages say

Most brokers are constructive. Anand Rathi recommends subscribing for the long term, and BP Equities, Ventura and Kunvarji have subscribe ratings. SBI Securities is neutral, preferring to track a few quarters of listed performance first. Grey-market indications suggest only a modest listing premium of around 5–6%, which is no guarantee of the actual debut price.

Early demand was thin on the first morning, as is typical for day one. For investors weighing the issue, the key question is whether a five-store regional jeweller can replicate its recent profit growth as it expands.
