Tata Chemicals was the standout loser on Dalal Street on Friday, sliding more than 8 percent intraday, as a widening boardroom rift at Tata Sons spilled over into the share prices of nearly every listed Tata Group company. Tata Consultancy Services and Tata Technologies each fell between 2 and 4 percent, while Tata Motors also came under pressure, reversing a rally the group had staged just a day earlier.

## What triggered the sell-off

The declines came a day after the Tata Sons board approved a fresh five-year term for chairman N Chandrasekaran and cleared the company's long-pending stock market listing, a step the Reserve Bank of India has effectively mandated after rejecting Tata Sons' bid to deregister itself as a core investment company. Both resolutions passed with Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata casting the lone dissenting vote.

Tata Trusts, which holds a 66 percent stake in Tata Sons and is by far its largest shareholder, escalated matters further on Thursday evening, calling the reappointment illegal. In a formal statement, the Trusts argued that the board's Articles of Association require unanimous support from its nominee directors for any chairman appointment or reappointment, and that Noel Tata's dissent therefore renders the resolution invalid. The Trusts also said they had separately submitted a legal opinion from former Chief Justice of India D Y Chandrachud supporting their position, though its contents were not made public.

Adding another layer to the standoff, Noel Tata has tabled a proposal from the Shapoorji Pallonji Group, the second-largest shareholder in Tata Sons at 18.4 percent, to monetise a portion of its holding, a move that could unlock at least Rs 25,000 crore in liquidity for the group but that further complicates an already tense governance dispute.

## A reversal from Thursday's optimism

The sell-off marks a sharp reversal from Thursday, when Tata Group stocks had initially rallied on news of Chandrasekaran's extension, before the Trusts' pointed rejection of both the reappointment and the listing plan soured sentiment. Investors appear to be pricing in the prospect of a prolonged legal and boardroom battle that could delay the long-awaited Tata Sons listing, a deal that has been closely watched given the holding company's roughly Rs 11.5 trillion portfolio of group businesses.

## Broader market held up better

The Tata-specific rout stood in contrast to the wider market, where the Nifty50 and Sensex traded largely range-bound through the morning session, supported by softer crude oil prices and a positive lead from Asian peers. Separately, foreign institutional investors were net sellers of Indian equities to the tune of roughly Rs 3,209 crore in the previous session, even as domestic institutional investors absorbed the selling with net purchases of about Rs 3,618 crore, cushioning the broader indices from the kind of sharp swings seen in individual Tata Group counters.

With Tata Trusts signalling it does not consider the board's decisions settled, investors will be watching closely for whether the dispute moves toward a shareholder vote, a legal challenge, or a negotiated resolution between the Trusts and the Tata Sons board in the coming weeks.
