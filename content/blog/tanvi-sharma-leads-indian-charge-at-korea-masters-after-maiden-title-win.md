India's badminton contingent has landed in Asan, South Korea, for the Korea Masters 2026, a BWF Super 300 event running from August 4 to 9, with 17-year-old Tanvi Sharma arriving as the standout name after claiming her first World Tour title just days earlier.

## A Breakthrough in Taipei

Sharma capped a rapid rise over the weekend by winning the Taipei Open, defeating Vietnam's sixth seed Nguyen Thuy Linh in the final to claim her maiden BWF Super 300 (or higher) title. The win places her in select company: she becomes only the fourth Indian woman to win a title at this level or above, following Saina Nehwal, P V Sindhu, and Devika Sihag, a lineage that underlines how significant the milestone is for a player who is still a teenager on the senior international circuit.

## Top Seed in Asan

With two-time Olympic medallist P V Sindhu taking a break from competition and Unnati Hooda not in the draw, Tanvi Sharma enters the Korea Masters as India's third seed and highest-ranked player in the women's singles field, currently sitting at world number 35. That positioning puts real weight on her shoulders to convert momentum from Taipei into another deep run, particularly with the tournament serving as a ranking and form marker ahead of the busier part of the BWF season.

## A Deep Indian Contingent

Sharma is far from India's only representative in Asan. The women's singles draw alone features a long list of Indian shuttlers, including Isharani Baruah, Malvika Bansod, Shriyanshi Valishetty, Anmol Kharb, Rakshitha Sree Santhosh Ramraj, Ashmita Chaliha, Tanya Hemanth, Aakarshi Kashyap, and Mansi Singh. On the men's side, veteran Kidambi Srikanth is also competing, giving India's challenge depth across both draws rather than resting on a single player's shoulders.

## Why Depth Matters Right Now

The scale of India's contingent at a mid-tier Super 300 event reflects a broader shift in Indian badminton over the past few years: the pipeline behind the sport's biggest stars has widened considerably, with multiple players now capable of threatening seeded opposition on any given day. That depth reduces India's reliance on any one headline name and gives selectors more options to manage workload and form heading into the next major continental and world events.

## What to Watch This Week

Attention through the week will center on how far Sharma can carry her Taipei form, particularly if she is drawn against higher-ranked opposition in the later rounds, while the rest of the Indian contingent will be looking to bank ranking points and match experience on the way to bigger tournaments later in the season. Live streaming of the event is available in India through the BWF TV YouTube channel for fans looking to follow the results as they come in.
