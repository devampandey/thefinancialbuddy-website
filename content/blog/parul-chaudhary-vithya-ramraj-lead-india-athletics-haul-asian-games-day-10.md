India's track and field team delivered its best single day of the Asian Games so far on Monday, with athletes accounting for seven of the country's eight medals in Aichi-Nagoya. The haul lifted India's overall tally to 42 medals: four gold, 18 silver and 20 bronze, leaving the country 12th in the standings. Athletics alone has now produced 16 of those medals.

## Parul Chaudhary's Second Podium In Two Days

The evening's standout performance came from Parul Chaudhary in the women's 5000m. The Olympian finished third in 15:14.61, holding off the chasing pack in a tight final. Bahrain's Winfred Yavi won gold in 15:10.18 and Japan's Nozomi Tanaka took silver in 15:11.65, just 1.46 seconds ahead of the Indian.

The result capped a demanding programme for Parul, who raced three finals on three consecutive days. She finished fourth in the 1500m on September 26, then won bronze in the 3000m steeplechase on Sunday in a national record of 9:08.67, before returning less than a day later to claim the 5000m medal. It is her fourth Asian Games medal overall.

## A National Record Falls In The Hurdles

Vithya Ramraj produced the other headline moment on the track. She won bronze in the women's 400m hurdles with a personal best of 54.75 seconds, breaking the national record she had shared with the legendary PT Usha, a mark that had stood for more than four decades. Vithya had already won silver earlier in the Games as part of India's mixed 4x400m relay team.

## Silver For Sreeshankar, Gulveer And Yashvir

Long jumper Murali Sreeshankar won silver with a leap of 8.07m despite an ankle problem that forced him to skip his final attempts, a medal that underlined both his class and his resilience.

In the men's 1500m, Gulveer Singh finished second in a personal best of 3:36.72 behind Japan's Kazuto Iizawa, who set a Games record. It was Gulveer's second silver of these Games.

The javelin produced a double podium. Yashvir Singh saved his best for last, launching a personal best of 85.72m on his final throw to take silver, while Rohit Yadav claimed bronze, giving India two athletes on the podium in an event the country has come to treat as its own.

## Shooting Adds To The Tally

Away from the track, Esha Singh won silver in the women's 25m pistol after equalling the Games record with 38 hits in the final, before losing a narrow shoot-off for gold. Kurash exponent Pincky Balhara, who had already secured a medal by reaching the semifinals ([see our earlier coverage](/sports/pincky-balhara-assures-india-a-kurash-medal-at-asian-games-2026-with-3-0-quarter)), finished with bronze.

## Why Monday Matters

India's gold count at these Games remains modest compared with its tally of silver and bronze. But Monday showed unusual depth in athletics, with medals spread across distance running, hurdles, jumps and throws. Several of the winners also set personal bests or national records, a sign that the squad is peaking at the right time rather than relying on one or two stars.

With several athletics finals still to come, the coming days will test whether that depth can be converted into gold.
