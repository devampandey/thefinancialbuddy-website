Retail investors moved first on Fusion CX's initial public offering, while large institutions held back entirely. By about 5 PM on Wednesday, the opening day of the ₹702 crore issue, the offer had drawn bids for 57.28 lakh shares against 1.34 crore shares on offer, an overall subscription of 0.43 times.

The Kolkata-based customer experience and outsourcing company is selling shares at ₹275 to ₹289 apiece. Details of the price band, business and financials are in our [earlier coverage](/ipo/fusion-cx-sets-rs-275-289-price-band-for-rs-702-crore-ipo-opening-october-14).

## A tale of three categories

The category-wise split shows how uneven demand was on day one:

- **Retail investors:** 1.63 times subscribed, with bids for 39.59 lakh shares against 24.29 lakh reserved.
- **Non-institutional investors:** 0.48 times, with bids for 17.54 lakh shares against 36.44 lakh. Smaller HNIs bidding up to ₹10 lakh were more active, at 0.66 times, than larger ones at 0.39 times.
- **Qualified institutional buyers:** effectively zero, with bids for just 15,300 shares against 72.87 lakh set aside, excluding the anchor portion.

About 68,700 applications had been lodged by the time of the update.

## Why the empty QIB book matters

Institutional bidders tend to arrive on the final day of an issue, so a blank QIB book after day one is not unusual on its own. The concern is the size of that quota. QIBs account for more than half of the shares on offer in the public tranche, which means the issue cannot be fully covered without them, no matter how strong retail interest is.

The company did line up institutional support before opening. It raised about ₹315 crore from anchor investors on October 13, which covers a large slice of the overall offer. Whether mutual funds and foreign investors return in the regular QIB book will be the key signal to watch on Friday.

Some brokerages have also flagged reservations. Reviews published ahead of the opening were mixed, with customer concentration cited as a central risk, since telecom and utility clients account for close to half of Fusion CX's revenue.

## Market backdrop is not helping

The issue opened on a weak day for Indian shares, with the Sensex closing 252 points lower after a sharp rise in wholesale inflation. Fusion CX is also competing for money with other offerings. HD Fire Protect's IPO crossed nine times subscription on its second day and closes on Thursday, while the much larger Zetwerk issue opens on October 15.

## Key dates ahead

Bidding closes on October 16. Share allotment is expected on October 19, and the stock is scheduled to list on the BSE and NSE on October 22. Retail investors need to bid for at least 51 shares, which costs ₹14,739 at the upper end of the price band.
