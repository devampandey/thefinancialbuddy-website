A panel of judges at the Kosovo Specialist Chambers, a European Union-backed tribunal sitting in The Hague, convicted former Kosovo President Hashim Thaci on Wednesday of multiple war crimes committed during his country's 1998-99 war of independence from Serbia, and sentenced him to 25 years in prison. The verdict closes out one of the most closely watched war crimes cases to emerge from the Balkan conflicts of the 1990s.

## The Charges and Verdict

Thaci, 58, was convicted on four counts, including murder, torture, cruel treatment and arbitrary detention, carried out by forces of the Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA) during the conflict. Judges found him responsible for the murder of 96 political opponents and people perceived to be collaborators with Serbian security forces. He was also held responsible for the arbitrary illegal detention of 385 people, the torture of 303 people, and the cruel treatment of roughly 50 others.

Thaci had served as Kosovo's president and, earlier, as a senior KLA political figure during the war. He resigned from the presidency nearly six years ago specifically to face the tribunal's charges, a move seen at the time as a significant test of the young Balkan state's willingness to hold its own wartime leadership accountable.

## Other Sentences Handed Down

Three other former senior KLA commanders were sentenced alongside Thaci. Jakup Krasniqi received 25 years, matching Thaci's term. Kadri Veseli was sentenced to 18 years, with time already spent in custody counted toward the term, and Rexhep Selimi received 13 years, also inclusive of time served.

## Why It Matters

The Kosovo Specialist Chambers was established with EU backing specifically to prosecute crimes linked to the KLA's conduct during the independence war, complementing earlier international tribunals that focused primarily on Serbian forces. The convictions are likely to reopen debate within Kosovo over the legacy of the KLA, which is widely celebrated domestically as a liberation movement but has faced years of scrutiny over allegations of abuses during and after the conflict.

The ruling also carries weight for the wider Balkans, a region where EU accession talks, ethnic tensions and unresolved wartime grievances continue to intersect. Analysts will be watching how the verdict affects Kosovo's own domestic politics, given Thaci's stature as a former head of state, as well as how it shapes Pristina's relations with Brussels at a time when several Balkan states are pushing for faster progress on EU membership.
