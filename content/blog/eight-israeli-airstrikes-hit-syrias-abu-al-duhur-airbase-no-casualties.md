Israeli fighter jets carried out eight airstrikes on the Abu al-Duhur military airbase in northwestern Syria in the early hours of Tuesday, targeting the installation's runway and storage facilities, according to Syria's state-run broadcaster Ekhbariya, which cited a military source. Both a military source and a civilian living near the base confirmed that there were no reported casualties from the strikes.

## Details Of The Strike

The Abu al-Duhur airbase sits roughly 70 kilometres from the Turkish border and has a long and turbulent history. It ceased functioning as a dedicated military airfield in 2013 and changed hands repeatedly during Syria's 14-year civil war, as forces loyal to former President Bashar al-Assad and various opposition factions fought for control of the country. Since Assad's ouster in late 2024, the facility has been repurposed as a training centre for Syria's newly formed national army, according to the military source cited by Syrian state media.

The Israeli military declined to comment on the operation when approached, while Syria's defence ministry did not respond to requests for comment. This pattern of official silence has become familiar in the wake of previous Israeli strikes on Syrian military infrastructure since the fall of the Assad government.

## Rising Tension Between Israel And Turkey

The strikes land against a backdrop of increasingly strained relations between Israel and Turkey over the future of Syria. Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan used a speech earlier this month to accuse Israel of continuing to threaten Syria's security through repeated military action, calling on the international community to pressure Israel into respecting Syria's territorial integrity. Ankara has positioned itself as a key backer of Syria's post-Assad government and has been expanding its own military and economic footprint in the country.

Adding to the friction, Syria conducted air exercises in the north of the country in early August using Russian-made MiG-21 fighter jets, marking the first such exercise since Assad's fall. Israeli public broadcaster Kan reported around the same time that Israeli security officials had detected signs that Syria was working to rebuild its air force, including recruiting new pilots, conducting flight training and holding a first exercise involving fighter aircraft. An Israeli security source quoted by Kan said the country was closely monitoring these developments and had already shared its concerns with the United States.

## A Recurring Pattern

Israel has carried out repeated strikes on military sites inside Syria since the collapse of the Assad regime, citing concerns about weapons stockpiles and the emergence of hostile forces near its borders. The latest strikes on Abu al-Duhur fit that broader pattern, coming as Syria's new leadership works to reconstitute its armed forces and as regional powers, including Turkey and Israel, jockey for influence over the country's security architecture.

For now, neither side has escalated rhetoric beyond the current exchange of strikes and statements, but the combination of Syria's rebuilding military ambitions, Turkey's growing involvement, and Israel's stated wariness suggests northern Syria is likely to remain a flashpoint in the months ahead.
