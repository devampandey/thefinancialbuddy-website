The death toll from the devastating flash flood that tore through the Nepal-Tibet border region has climbed to 1,252, with an additional 4,216 people still believed to be missing, Nepal's National Disaster Management Authority said in an update issued Thursday.

The figures mark a sharp rise from the previous day's count of 1,114 dead and 3,916 missing, underscoring how rescue teams continue to recover bodies and account for the missing more than a week after the disaster struck. Chinese state media separately reported 16 deaths and 546 people missing on the Tibetan side of the border, pushing the combined cross-border toll to 1,268 dead and 4,762 missing.

## What caused the disaster

The catastrophe was triggered by the failure of part of a glacier near the Nepal-Tibet frontier, which unleashed a massive deluge of ice, rock, mud and debris that swept through settlements and villages in the mountainous border region. The flooding has wiped out entire communities and severely damaged infrastructure, including roads, bridges and communication towers across the affected districts.

The scale of destruction has revived global conversations about the role of climate change in accelerating glacial instability in the Himalayas, a region increasingly vulnerable to what scientists call glacial lake outburst floods.

## Rescue and relief efforts

Nepali authorities said almost 21,500 security personnel have been mobilised for search and rescue operations, with nearly 12,000 people rescued so far through a combination of domestic and international assistance. More than 5,000 people have received treatment for injuries sustained in the disaster.

Communication towers in the affected districts have largely been restored, aiding coordination between rescue teams operating across difficult mountainous terrain. In Nepal's Nuwakot district, army personnel have been working to construct a temporary bridge across the Trishuli river at Devighat to reconnect communities cut off by the flooding.

Nepal's Prime Minister said earlier this week that the government's focus was beginning to shift toward rehabilitation even as search operations continue, reflecting the scale of the humanitarian task ahead. Officials have indicated that roughly 20,000 houses will need to be rebuilt in the flood-affected areas, a reconstruction effort expected to stretch on for months.

## Regional and global attention

The disaster has drawn attention well beyond Nepal's borders, with international rescue and aid contributions playing a role in the recovery effort. The rising death toll and persistently high number of missing persons highlight the difficulty of search operations in remote, debris-choked mountain terrain where access remains limited even days after the initial event.

Meteorological officials have also flagged a forecast for a "very strong" El Nino pattern this year, raising concerns about the potential for further extreme weather events in South Asia in the months ahead, even as the region works through the aftermath of this disaster.
