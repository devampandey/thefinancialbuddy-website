The head of India's largest lender has put a number on what many in the bond market have been quietly pricing in. State Bank of India Chairman CS Setty said on Monday that the Reserve Bank of India could raise its policy rate by another 50 basis points at its December meeting, and then hold rates steady until the end of the financial year on March 31.

Setty was speaking at a banking and financial services summit in Mumbai. If his expectation plays out, the repo rate would rise to 6% from 5.5%, a cumulative 75 basis points of tightening in two meetings.

## Who feels it first

Setty's more useful message was about which borrowers will feel the pinch. He said the effect of higher rates on both the size and the mix of credit demand would not be immediate but would become visible over the coming months. If December brings another hike, he expects rate-sensitive segments tied to discretionary spending, such as personal and consumption loans, to slow.

He was notably more relaxed about lending to businesses. India is entering this tightening cycle with credit growing strongly and the economy projected to expand 7.1% this fiscal year, he said, so demand for loans that fund production and investment should not weaken even after one or two more hikes.

That distinction matters for how banks plan their books. Unsecured retail credit has been the fastest-growing and highest-yielding part of many lenders' portfolios. A sustained slowdown there, combined with higher deposit costs, would compress margins unless corporate and MSME lending picks up the slack, which is essentially the bet Setty is describing for SBI.

## Why a 50 bps call is plausible

The RBI ended a long pause in October, raising the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5% and switching its stance from neutral to calibrated tightening, as we reported in our [coverage of the October policy](/business/rbi-raises-repo-rate-to-5-5-in-first-hike-since-2023-shifts-to-calibrated-tighten). The Monetary Policy Committee vote was unanimous.

Since then the inflation data has given the hawks more ammunition. September retail inflation climbed to a 23-month high of 5.54%, with food prices rising sharply and rural inflation crossing 6% ([details here](/business/retail-inflation-hits-23-month-high-of-5-54-as-onion-prices-more-than-double)). The RBI itself raised its FY27 inflation forecast to 5.2% in October, pointing to a deficient monsoon, strong El Nino conditions, higher fuel and food costs and a pickup in core inflation. Governor Sanjay Malhotra has said future moves will depend on how far price pressures broaden.

SBI's own economics team had already argued last week that the RBI would choose between a straight 50 basis point increase and a smaller 25 basis point hike paired with a change to its liquidity corridor. Setty's remarks lean firmly toward the larger option.

## What it means for borrowers and savers

For households, a December hike of that size would push up floating-rate home and car loan EMIs once banks reset their external benchmark rates, typically within a quarter. Someone with a Rs 50 lakh, 20-year home loan would see the EMI rise by roughly Rs 1,600 a month for each 50 basis point increase in the lending rate. Our [EMI calculator](/tools/emi-calculator) can show the effect on a specific loan.

Depositors may have to wait longer for their share. Banks have historically raised fixed deposit rates more slowly than loan rates in the early months of a tightening cycle, so the gap between what borrowers pay and what savers earn tends to widen before it narrows.

The RBI's next policy decision is due in early December.
