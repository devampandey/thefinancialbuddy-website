Russia's first parliamentary election since its full-scale invasion of Ukraine began wrapped up on Sunday, with President Vladimir Putin's United Russia party all but certain to retain its dominant hold on the 450-seat State Duma. Voting ran across three days starting Friday, with polling stations closing through the evening as Russia's eleven time zones reported results in sequence, and preliminary numbers expected within hours of the final polls shutting in the country's west.

## Turnout Climbs, Opposition Sidelined

Russia's Central Election Commission said turnout stood at nearly 45 percent by Sunday morning, up from roughly 41 percent a day earlier, as authorities leaned on online balloting to draw out the country's 111 million eligible voters. United Russia has won every Duma election since Putin first took power and was polling close to 40 percent support heading into the vote, with a handful of smaller "systemic opposition" parties that broadly align with the Kremlin expected to fill out the remaining seats.

Genuine opposition was largely absent from the ballot. The liberal Yabloko party, the only registered group to openly criticize the war, was struck off the ballot by Russia's Supreme Court last month and fielded only a small number of candidates in individual constituencies. Several anti-war politicians were jailed or forced abroad ahead of the campaign, and Yabloko said many of its poll observers were turned away from voting stations over the weekend. The Kremlin also declined to invite monitors from the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe, dismissing the body's past criticism as biased.

## Irregularities and a First Vote in Annexed Territory

Independent observers flagged a string of irregularities, including ballot boxes left unsealed overnight, polling stations refusing to issue paper ballots, and reported cyberattacks targeting Moscow's electronic voting system, which the election commission's chief acknowledged on Sunday. This was also the first Duma election to include voting in Crimea and four Ukrainian regions that Russia illegally annexed after 2022 but does not fully control, a move Kyiv and the European Union have both condemned as unlawful.

## Drone Barrage and Protests Abroad

The vote's final day unfolded against a major escalation on the battlefield. Russia's Defense Ministry said its forces intercepted more than 1,100 Ukrainian drones overnight across the country, occupied Crimea and the Black Sea, with strikes in the Moscow region killing two people and wounding twenty, according to local officials. The barrage followed a summer of Ukrainian long-range attacks on refineries and logistics hubs that have deepened Russia's fuel shortages and brought the economic cost of the war home to ordinary Russians.

Abroad, exiled opposition figures used the election to organize protest. Yulia Navalnaya, the widow of the late Kremlin critic Alexei Navalny, called on anti-war Russians to turn out at polling stations at noon local time as a show of dissent. Demonstrations followed outside Russian diplomatic missions in Yerevan and Berlin, drawing dissidents and expatriates opposed to both the war and the vote's legitimacy.

With the outcome barely in doubt, the election is being watched less for suspense than for what it signals: a Kremlin moving to lock in political control at home even as the war grinds into its fifth year and its economic toll becomes harder to obscure.
