Devdutt Padikkal struck a career-best 167 to steer India to 460 for 9 at stumps on the second day of the opening Test against Sri Lanka in Galle, an innings that dominated a session drastically shortened by persistent rain.

## A day defined by the weather

Rain washed out the entire morning session and much of the second session as well, leaving only a little over three hours of playing time across the day. Despite the interruptions, India made full use of the overs available, adding to the platform Padikkal had built with his century on the opening day to push the total well past 450 by the close.

## Padikkal's big day

Resuming with his overnight century intact, Padikkal continued to bat with control against the Sri Lankan spin attack before eventually falling for 167 — his highest score in Test cricket — stumped down the leg side after venturing down the pitch too early against a quick, flat delivery. His innings anchored the middle order through the shortened session and gave India the platform to push well past the 400-run mark despite the lost time.

Support came from further down the order. Rishabh Pant fell relatively early in the day for 39, but Dhruv Jurel chipped in with a composed half-century to keep the innings moving, while other contributions from the middle and lower order helped India reach 116 overs at the close of play. Left-arm spinner Prabath Jayasuriya was the pick of the Sri Lankan bowlers, finding turn and bounce on a wearing surface, with debutant off-spinner Keshara Nuwantha also among the wickets as Sri Lanka's spin duo shared the bulk of the workload through the innings.

## Where the match stands

India go into Day 3 in a strong position at 460 for 9, with No. 11 Prasidh Krishna unbeaten and Kuldeep Yadav also not out at the crease when bad light and the close of play intervened. The pair will look to add quick runs on the third morning before India turn their attention to bowling Sri Lanka out twice on a pitch that has increasingly favoured the spinners as the match has progressed.

The match is the first of India's Test series in Sri Lanka and comes at a landmark moment for the touring side, being led by captain Shubman Gill in a milestone Test appearance for Indian cricket. Rain has been a persistent disruptor through the opening two days, and the forecast for the remainder of the match will be closely watched by both camps as they assess how much playing time remains to force a result across the final three days.

For Sri Lanka, containing India's tail quickly on the third morning will be key to limiting the deficit before their own batters get a chance to respond on a surface that has already shown signs of assisting the spinners considerably more than seamers through the first two days.
