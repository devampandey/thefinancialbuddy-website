The scale of India's spam-call problem — and the technology now being deployed against it — came into sharper focus this week with fresh data from the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI). For the first quarter of FY27 (April-June 2026), AI-based detection systems deployed across telecom networks flagged 24.43 billion suspected spam calls and SMSs, giving consumers the option to either ignore such communication or approach it with caution.

The figures, compiled from telecom operators and released by TRAI, also show that regulators took direct action against nearly 1,83,839 telecom resources during the quarter. Of these, 1,37,053 resources were barred for 15 days following a first violation, while repeat offenders faced a full year of disconnection. On the messaging side, 2,873 SMS headers were blocked and 263 senders were blacklisted for a year.

## Promotional Calls and DND Enforcement

Beyond flagging suspicious traffic, telecom networks also blocked 1.48 billion promotional calls and 7.3 billion promotional SMSs after checking them against subscribers' registered Do Not Disturb (DND) preferences. This enforcement layer is separate from AI-based spam detection and works by cross-referencing outbound commercial communication with each user's consent settings before it ever reaches their phone.

## Consumers Are Reporting More, and Using the App to Do It

TRAI's data also points to growing consumer participation in spam enforcement. During the quarter, 10.85 lakh Unsolicited Commercial Communication (UCC) complaints were registered, of which 5.53 lakh were classified as actionable. Notably, the TRAI DND app — which crossed 33.16 lakh cumulative downloads by the end of June 2026 — accounted for roughly 89 percent of all UCC complaints filed, with 9.66 lakh of the 10.85 lakh complaints submitted through the app rather than through calls or web forms.

## Why This Matters

Spam and fraud calls have been a persistent irritant for Indian telecom users, and complaints about pesky calls and SMSs routinely rank among the top grievances raised with regulators. The Q1 FY27 numbers suggest that the layered approach — AI flagging at the network level, DND-based blocking of promotional traffic, and an increasingly used consumer complaint app — is processing spam at a scale that would be impossible to manage manually. Whether this translates into a noticeably quieter phone for the average subscriber is a separate question, since spammers have historically adapted their tactics as soon as one channel gets tightened.

For now, the data offers one of the clearer pictures yet of how much unwanted communication is moving through India's networks every quarter, and how much of it is being intercepted before it reaches a ringing phone. With over three million downloads of the DND app and a rising share of complaints now coming through it, TRAI appears to be betting that giving consumers a direct, low-friction reporting tool is as important as the AI filtering happening upstream.
