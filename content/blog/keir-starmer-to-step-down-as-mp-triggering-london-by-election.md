Former British Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer announced on Tuesday that he is standing down as the Member of Parliament for Holborn and St Pancras, the north London seat he has represented for eleven years. The decision, confirmed in a statement to his local constituency party and shared on social media, will trigger a by-election that is already shaping up to be a significant test for the Labour Party under his successor as Prime Minister, Andy Burnham.

## From party leader to backbencher to exit

Starmer took over Labour's leadership in 2020 after the party's worst general election defeat since 1935, and steered it to a landslide victory in 2024. He served as Prime Minister until earlier this summer, when he stepped aside as both party leader and premier after his standing within the party eroded following a difficult run of local election results. Burnham, the former mayor of Greater Manchester, succeeded him in Downing Street.

In his resignation statement, Starmer said he had used the summer to reflect on his future and concluded it was "the right time" to hand the Holborn and St Pancras seat to a successor. He said he intends to focus on international affairs, including defence, security, trade and technology policy, as well as continuing long-running work on tackling violence against women and girls, a cause he has championed since before entering Parliament.

## A by-election with real stakes

The upcoming contest will be the seventh by-election of the current Parliament and the first in London since a former Prime Minister vacated a seat in the capital more than a decade ago. Early positioning suggests it could be a tightly fought race between Labour and the Green Party, which made notable gains in Camden's local council elections earlier this year. Green Party leader Zack Polanski has been named by some in his party as a potential candidate, and observers expect the contest to be read nationally as a barometer of Labour's standing under Burnham.

Reaction from within Labour has been largely warm. Former colleagues credited Starmer with rebuilding the party from its lowest point and delivering a decisive election win, while acknowledging his premiership ended amid criticism over policy reversals and controversial appointments. The opposition Conservative Party was more pointed, with its chairman calling the resignation "another U-turn" and arguing it would saddle taxpayers with the cost of an unplanned by-election.

## What comes next

A selection process for Labour's candidate is expected to move quickly given the seat's high profile, and the timing of the by-election itself will depend on procedural steps in the House of Commons in the coming weeks. For Starmer personally, departing the Commons closes the electoral chapter of a career that took him from human rights lawyer and former Director of Public Prosecutions to the country's highest office in under a decade. Whether his successor as MP can hold the seat may say as much about the broader political mood in Britain as it does about local politics in north London.
