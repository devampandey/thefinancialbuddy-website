The Union Cabinet has approved a revamped Khelo India Scheme along with enhanced Assistance to National Sports Federations, together carrying a combined outlay of Rs 36,441 crore for the period from 2026-27 to 2030-31. The government has described the package as the country's most ambitious sports development programme since Independence, underlining the scale of ambition attached to India's push to build a deeper athletic talent base over the next five years.

## How the Money Is Split

Of the total outlay, Rs 29,054 crore has been earmarked for the Khelo India Scheme itself, which is focused on identifying and nurturing talented athletes from the grassroots level upward. The remaining Rs 7,387 crore is allocated to Assistance to National Sports Federations, which funds the operational and competitive needs of the bodies governing individual sports across the country, including coaching support, sports science, modern equipment, international exposure, training camps, foreign expert engagement and participation in major international events.

## New Elements in the Revamp

The updated scheme introduces several new components rather than simply extending the previous version at a larger budget. Among them are Khelo India Feeder Schools and what the government has termed Utkrishta Vidyalayas, both aimed at embedding structured athletic development earlier into the schooling pipeline. The scheme also expands its athlete pool substantially through a new Emerging Khelo India Athletes category, which the government says will grow the pool of supported athletes nearly tenfold compared with the current programme's reach.

That expansion in scale reflects a deliberate shift toward casting a wider net at the grassroots level, rather than concentrating resources narrowly on athletes who have already reached elite or near-elite levels. The logic is that a broader base of supported young athletes increases the odds of unearthing competitive talent across a wider range of sports and regions, though it also raises the execution challenge of maintaining consistent coaching and infrastructure quality across a much larger cohort.

## The Timing: 2030 and 2036 Ambitions

The scale and timing of the approval are tied directly to India's broader sporting ambitions over the coming decade. The country is set to host the Commonwealth Games in 2030, and is also pursuing a bid to host the Olympic Games in 2036. Both events require not just infrastructure investment but a credible pipeline of competitive athletes, which is where the revamped Khelo India framework is meant to make its biggest difference. Building that pipeline over a five-year runway, from FY27 through FY31, gives the programme a reasonably long horizon to show results before those hosting milestones arrive.

## What Success Will Look Like

The real test for the revamped scheme will not be the size of the outlay but whether the expanded athlete pool and new feeder-school structures translate into measurable improvements in India's medal performance at major multi-sport events over the next several years. India's recent showing at the Commonwealth Games, where the country closed out its campaign with dozens of medals before handing over hosting duties for 2030, offers a baseline against which the impact of this fresh investment will eventually be measured. Sports administrators and federations will now be watching how quickly the funding translates into on-the-ground programmes at the school and district level, where the scheme's grassroots ambitions will ultimately be tested.
