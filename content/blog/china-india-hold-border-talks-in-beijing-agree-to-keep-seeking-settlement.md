China and India have agreed to continue working toward a "fair, reasonable and mutually acceptable settlement" of their long-running border dispute, following talks between senior diplomats in Beijing this week that both sides described as candid and constructive.

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi and India's National Security Advisor Ajit Doval met on Tuesday for what China's foreign ministry called "comprehensive, in-depth, friendly and candid" discussions covering both border affairs and the broader bilateral relationship. The Indian Embassy in China confirmed the meeting in a post on social media, saying the two sides had reviewed bilateral ties and discussed ways to expand cooperation while maintaining stability and peace along the border.

## What Was Discussed

Wang told Doval that China-India relations were no longer a matter of bilateral importance alone but now carried "global and strategic significance," and urged both countries to strengthen communication while narrowing their differences. "Both sides should learn from historical experience, place the border issue in an appropriate position in bilateral relations, and move forward without stagnation, regression, or deviation," Wang was quoted as saying.

The two sides agreed to hold the next round of formal border talks in India in 2027, according to China's official statement on the meeting.

## A Fragile but Improving Relationship

The talks unfold against the backdrop of a slow but steady thaw between the world's two most populous nations, both of which are nuclear-armed and share a roughly 3,800-kilometre, largely undemarcated border through the Himalayas that has been a source of friction for decades. Relations deteriorated sharply after a deadly clash in 2020 triggered a four-year military standoff along the frontier, before a disengagement agreement was finally reached in October 2024.

Since then, ties have gradually improved. Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi travelled to China last year for the first time in seven years, a visit widely seen as a signal that both governments were ready to move past the worst of the standoff. That said, the relationship remains far from settled — a fresh border-related dispute emerged more recently and triggered sharp diplomatic exchanges between the two countries, underscoring how quickly tensions can resurface even amid dialogue.

## Why It Matters

For India, keeping a functioning diplomatic channel open with Beijing carries significance well beyond the immediate question of where the border lies. Trade and economic ties between the two countries remain substantial even as strategic rivalry persists, and periodic flare-ups along the Line of Actual Control have historically had knock-on effects for everything from bilateral trade flows to India's broader foreign policy posture toward China's regional partners.

The latest round of talks does not resolve the underlying territorial dispute — an issue that has defied full settlement since a war between the two countries in 1962 — but it does extend a diplomatic track that had been at risk of stalling amid recent friction. With the next formal round not scheduled until 2027, the working-level relationship in the interim will likely continue to be shaped by how effectively both sides manage the border quietly, away from the negotiating table.
