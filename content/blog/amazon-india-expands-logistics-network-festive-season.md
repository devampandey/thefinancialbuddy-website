Amazon India announced today that it is expanding its operations network across the country with 150 new last-mile delivery stations, 20 fulfilment centres and six sort centres, as the e-commerce giant gears up for what is typically its busiest stretch of the year.

## The Scale of the Expansion

The buildout is part of Amazon's previously announced investment of more than INR 2,800 crore to strengthen its infrastructure and operations in India. Once complete, the company's overall storage capacity in the country will rise to 64 million cubic feet, a 50% jump from current levels. The six new sort centres are coming up in Chikkaballapur, Hassan, Meerut, Ramanagar, Raipur and Varanasi, while seven of the 20 new fulfilment centres are being built in non-metropolitan cities, underlining how much of the incremental demand Amazon is chasing now comes from smaller towns rather than the traditional metro strongholds. Third-party logistics partners will run and manage much of the new infrastructure.

Notably, the very first Amazon last-mile delivery stations are opening in twelve smaller cities, including Bhojpur, Bokajan, Jalaun, Jehanabad, Namsai, Puruliya, Ribhoi, Senapati, Sivasagar, Udalguri, Ukhrul and Wokha, spread across 118 cities in total for this rollout. For shoppers in these areas, that typically means shorter delivery windows and wider access to Amazon's full catalogue during the festive rush, a period that has historically seen delivery slowdowns in less-connected regions.

## Jobs and Shopper Impact

The infrastructure push comes alongside Amazon's previously announced addition of more than 1.6 lakh seasonal jobs across roughly 400 locations, spanning both major metros and smaller towns, as the company braces for a spike in order volumes through the festive period. Abhinav Singh, Amazon's vice-president of operations for India, APAC, the Middle East, Turkey and Africa, said the new facilities were geared toward serving customers across India ahead of the festive season while supporting economic activity in the communities where they are located.

## Sellers Face a Mixed Picture

The expansion lands at a moment when sellers on Amazon's platform are simultaneously adjusting to revised fee structures. Since mid-August, Amazon has changed how it calculates costs for merchants using its Easy Ship and Self Ship services, shifting from category-specific referral charges to a percentage of order value. Flipkart has made similar changes to its own fee and penalty regime around the same time. Several small and medium sellers have said the changes add pressure to already thin margins just as festive-season volumes are expected to peak, a tension that is likely to shape how aggressively vendors discount their listings over the coming weeks even as logistics capacity improves.

For consumers, the net effect of a larger delivery network should be faster shipping and fewer stockouts during India's biggest shopping season, even as the seller-side economics behind those deliveries continue to shift.
