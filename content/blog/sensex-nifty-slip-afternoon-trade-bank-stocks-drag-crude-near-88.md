Indian equity benchmarks stayed under pressure through Tuesday afternoon, with the Sensex and Nifty extending their morning losses as banking stocks led the retreat and rising crude oil prices added to investor caution.

## Banks Weigh on the Index

The BSE Sensex was trading down around 0.55 percent by the afternoon session, hovering near the 78,110 level, while the Nifty 50 slipped roughly 0.4 percent. The sectoral banking gauge, Nifty Bank, bore the brunt of the selling, falling close to 1 percent to touch levels near its morning lows. Heavyweight private lenders including Axis Bank, IndusInd Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank and public sector major Bank of Baroda were among the biggest drags on the index, with traders citing profit-booking after recent gains and caution ahead of more quarterly earnings this week.

## IT Stocks Offer Some Cushion

Information technology stocks bucked the broader trend and traded higher during the session, offering partial support to the indices. However, the gains in IT counters were not enough to offset the weakness in financials, auto and oil-linked stocks, leaving the overall market in negative territory through the afternoon.

## Crude Oil Climbs Past $88

A key driver of the day's cautious mood was crude oil, which surged past $88 a barrel amid signs that talks aimed at easing tensions in West Asia had stalled. Higher crude prices are a persistent concern for Indian markets given the country's heavy reliance on imported oil, as they raise the prospect of wider current account and inflation pressures. Uncertainty around the broader US-Iran standoff over shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has kept energy markets on edge for much of August, and Tuesday's price move reinforced investor nervousness.

## Broader Market Mood

Traders pointed to a mix of global and domestic cues weighing on sentiment, including elevated crude prices, a packed earnings calendar with more than 300 Indian companies reporting first-quarter results this week, and continued watchfulness over global risk appetite. Midcap and smallcap indices showed a more mixed picture during the session, with some segments managing modest gains even as the headline indices stayed in the red, suggesting the selling pressure was concentrated in large-cap financials rather than broad-based across the market.

Market participants said they expect volatility to persist through the rest of the week as companies continue to report June-quarter earnings and as clarity on the West Asia situation and its impact on oil prices remains awaited. Any further escalation in crude prices or a delay in a resolution to the regional tensions could keep pressure on rate-sensitive and import-dependent sectors, analysts said, while a swift de-escalation could help indices claw back some of the day's losses before the closing bell.

For now, investors are treating the afternoon dip as part of a broader pattern of choppy, news-driven trading that has characterised the Indian market through much of August, with earnings reactions on individual stocks likely to drive short-term moves even as the headline indices track global crude and geopolitical cues.
