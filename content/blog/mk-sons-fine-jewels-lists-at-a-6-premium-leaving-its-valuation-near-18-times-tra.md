Shares of MK Sons Fine Jewels began trading on Wednesday at a modest premium to their issue price. The stock opened at Rs 171.60 on the BSE, 5.9% above the Rs 162 IPO price, and at Rs 171 on the NSE, a gain of about 5.6%. The debut closely matched grey-market indications, which had pointed to a listing price of around Rs 172-174 in the days before. Our [coverage of the IPO launch](/ipo/mk-sons-fine-jewels-opens-rs-330-crore-ipo-at-17-6x-earnings-betting-its-growth) set out the company's business and the offer terms.

## How the issue was finally sized

The offer ran from October 28 to October 30 and was priced at the top of its Rs 153-162 band. According to the final issue terms, it raised about Rs 275.4 crore, made up of a fresh issue of 1.36 crore shares worth about Rs 220.3 crore and an offer for sale of 34 lakh shares worth Rs 55.1 crore by promoter Ramchand Murlidhar Raimalani. Bids came in for about 5.09 times the shares on offer, solid but well short of the heavy oversubscription seen in some recent mainboard issues.

Most of the fresh money, about Rs 152.8 crore, is earmarked for inventory for a new showroom in Maharashtra and the expansion of an existing store in Gujarat. Another Rs 30 crore will repay borrowings.

## What the debut price implies

At the listing price, MK Sons carries a market value of roughly Rs 960 crore. Set against FY26 net profit of Rs 51.8 crore, that works out to about 18.5 times trailing earnings. The figure looks undemanding next to India's large listed jewellery chains, which have typically traded at far higher multiples, but the gap reflects real differences in scale and reach.

The company's growth has been striking. Revenue rose from Rs 217 crore in FY24 to Rs 539 crore in FY26, while net profit jumped from about Rs 8 crore to Rs 51.8 crore. In the June 2026 quarter it earned Rs 15 crore on revenue of Rs 101 crore. Average order value climbed from about Rs 1.4 lakh to Rs 1.9 lakh over the same two years, and the number of unique customers roughly doubled to over 24,000.

## Why the market was cautious

The muted pop suggests investors were weighing those numbers against clear concentration risks. MK Sons runs just five company-owned showrooms, three in Mumbai and two in Ahmedabad, and earns all its revenue offline. Gold makes up more than 87% of sales, leaving margins and demand exposed to swings in bullion prices. The company also buys most of its jewellery from outside manufacturers rather than making it, which keeps the model asset-light but limits control over design and cost.

Working capital is the other watchpoint. Jewellery retailers must carry expensive inventory, and new stores take time to reach full productivity. Much of the IPO money is going into stock for a single new showroom, so the next few quarters will show whether the expansion can match the growth of the past two years.

For investors who got an allotment, the listing delivered a small but quick gain. For those considering buying now, the case rests on whether a regional jeweller can scale beyond two cities without diluting the margins that justified its rapid profit growth.
