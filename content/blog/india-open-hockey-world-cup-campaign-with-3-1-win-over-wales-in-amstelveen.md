India began their campaign at the FIH Men's Hockey World Cup 2026 with a 3-1 win over Wales in Amstelveen on Saturday, giving Harmanpreet Singh's side the winning start they were looking for as they chase a first World Cup title since 1975.

## How the Match Unfolded

India drew first blood through a penalty corner conversion inside the opening minutes, before captain Harmanpreet Singh added to the lead with a well-struck drag-flick from another penalty corner. Harmanpreet was at the centre of India's dominance throughout the match, doubling his personal tally in the second half to complete a brace and put the contest firmly out of Wales' reach. Wales pulled a goal back late in the game through Sam Welsh, following a lengthy video review, but could not find a way back into a contest India controlled for long stretches.

The result means India collect maximum points from their opening fixture of the tournament, an assignment considered important given the competitiveness of the group stage in a World Cup year.

## Why the Win Matters

India have not lifted the Hockey World Cup since 1975, and every edition since has added to the weight of expectation on the team heading into the tournament. A clean opening win, without needing to rely on a nervy finish, will be seen as a confidence-building result for a squad that has spent recent years working to close the gap with the sport's traditional powerhouses in Europe and Australia.

Harmanpreet Singh's continued reliability from set pieces remains one of India's most important attacking weapons at major tournaments, and his performance against Wales extends a run of penalty-corner conversions that have often been the difference in tight matches for India in recent years.

## What's Next

With three points on the board, India will look to build on the win as the group stage progresses, with the team's next matches expected to test their depth against tougher opposition. Hockey has been drawing heightened attention in India this month, with both the men's and women's World Cups underway alongside the ongoing badminton World Championships, giving Indian sports fans a packed few weeks of international action to follow.

For now, though, the takeaway from Amstelveen is straightforward: a composed, largely comfortable win to start the campaign, built on early goals and a disciplined defensive performance that limited Wales to a single, late consolation strike.
