India's junior women's hockey team claimed a third consecutive Junior Asia Cup title on Sunday, edging out host nation China 1-0 in a tightly fought final at the Moqi Training Base in Hulunbuir. The result also secures India a direct berth in the next FIH Junior Women's World Cup, giving the young side a marquee assignment to build towards over the coming year.

## A Tense, Low-Scoring Final

Unlike India's group-stage matches, which were often one-sided, the final against China was a cagey, closely contested affair. The opening quarter passed without a goal as both defences held firm, but India broke the deadlock in the 17th minute through forward Sanika Chandrakant Mane, whose finish proved to be the only goal the match would produce. India carried that slender advantage into half-time and then spent the third quarter absorbing pressure from a home crowd willing China back into the contest, before shutting the game down in the final quarter to seal a 1-0 win.

## An Unbeaten Run To The Title

India's route to the final underlined the squad's depth and firepower. The team topped the Elite Pool without dropping a match, finishing the group phase with ten points from three wins and a draw, and advanced on goal difference. Along the way, India put together one of the most emphatic scorelines of the tournament, a 19-0 demolition of Pakistan in the quarterfinal, before following it up with a composed 5-1 win over South Korea in the semifinal, a contest in which drag-flick specialist Supriya was among the standout performers. That semifinal victory over the Koreans had already been covered as India booked its final berth; Sunday's result completes that story with the trophy itself.

## Hat-Trick Of Titles Under A New Coach

With the win, India's junior women have now lifted the AHF Junior Asia Cup in three straight editions, an achievement made more notable given the squad has been through a change in coaching leadership this year. The team has operated under a restructured training programme since the appointment of coach Tim White, who took charge earlier in 2026, and Sunday's triumph marked his first major title with the group. For a young core of players who will likely form the backbone of India's senior program in years to come, retaining continental dominance at the junior level is being viewed by Hockey India as a strong signal of the pipeline's health.

## Rewards And What Comes Next

Hockey India moved quickly to recognise the achievement, announcing cash incentives for the title-winning squad: Rs 3 lakh for each player and Rs 1.5 lakh for every member of the support staff. Beyond the financial reward, the more significant prize is the confirmed slot at the FIH Junior World Cup, where India will get a chance to test itself against the best junior sides globally rather than only regional rivals. For Indian hockey more broadly, which has invested heavily in age-group programmes in recent years, a third successive Asia Cup crown offers fresh evidence that the results are beginning to show at the top of the continental ladder, even as the senior women's team continues its own separate campaign at the Asia Cup in the Gulf.
