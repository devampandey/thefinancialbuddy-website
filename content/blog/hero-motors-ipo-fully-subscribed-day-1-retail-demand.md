Hero Motors Limited's initial public offering was fully subscribed on its opening day, Wednesday, with retail investors driving most of the early demand even as institutional buyers stayed on the sidelines for now. The Rs 1,000-crore issue is one of three IPOs that opened simultaneously on the mainboard this week, alongside Jindal Supreme and SS Retail, underscoring a busy primary market ahead of the festive season.

## Subscription Numbers So Far

As of 2:25 pm on Wednesday, the issue had received bids covering 1.05 times the shares on offer. The retail individual investor category was the standout, subscribed 1.75 times, reflecting healthy appetite from smaller investors for the auto-components maker's shares. The non-institutional investor portion, which typically includes high-net-worth individuals, was subscribed 0.81 times.

Qualified institutional buyers, however, showed muted interest so far, with that category subscribed just 0.01 times as of the same time. QIB participation traditionally picks up sharply on the final day of bidding, as institutions tend to place the bulk of their orders closer to the subscription deadline, so the current figure is not unusual for an opening day and will be watched closely as the issue progresses.

## Issue Details

Hero Motors has priced the IPO in a band of Rs 79 to Rs 84 per share. The offer, comprising a mix of fresh issue and an offer for sale, aims to raise close to Rs 1,000 crore at the top end of the price band. The issue opened for public subscription on Wednesday and will close on September 18, with the shares expected to list on both the BSE and the NSE on September 23.

## Context

Hero Motors' listing comes amid a wave of primary-market activity in India this month, with the National Stock Exchange's own much-larger IPO also opening for subscription this week and drawing strong anchor demand. The clustering of large and mid-sized IPOs reflects continued investor appetite for new listings even as secondary markets have seen bouts of volatility, with companies across sectors -- from steel pipes to auto components to market infrastructure -- moving to tap public capital while sentiment remains supportive.

Analysts tracking the auto-ancillary space will be watching how institutional demand for Hero Motors builds over the remaining two days of bidding, particularly given the relatively soft QIB numbers on day one compared with the strong retail response.
