Chennai-based software company Zoho on Wednesday unveiled Zia Chat, a unified conversational AI interface for businesses, alongside a broader push into vertical SaaS with seven new industry-specific applications, at its annual Zoholics user conference.

## What Zia Chat Does

Zia Chat is built natively into Zoho's existing ecosystem rather than sitting alongside it as a separate product, giving users a single conversational interface to search for information, generate leads, resolve customer support issues and analyse data across sales, marketing, support, HR and finance functions. The company said the tool is designed to reduce token consumption for users compared with other AI assistants, and will be available for early access within a month, initially in English only.

## Entering Vertical SaaS

Alongside Zia Chat, Zoho announced its entry into vertical SaaS with seven applications built around the specific regulatory, operational and workflow needs of individual industries: retail, automotive, BFSI, healthcare, education, real estate and restaurants. The move marks a shift for Zoho, which has historically built broad, horizontal productivity and business software used across sectors, toward products tailored to the particular compliance and workflow demands of specific industries.

## Vembu's Pitch: "Commoditise Intelligence"

Speaking at a briefing on the sidelines of Zoholics in Chennai, Zoho co-founder and chief scientist Sridhar Vembu framed the announcements around what he called "ubiquitous intelligence" — the idea that AI should be embedded across products and everyday workflows rather than treated as a standalone add-on. "Our goal is to commoditise intelligence so customers can get work done, without incurring high costs on tokens," Vembu said, arguing that the next meaningful shift in AI adoption would come from rethinking user interfaces around conversational AI rather than simply layering chatbots onto existing software.

## Why It Matters

Zoho's move comes as software vendors globally race to bundle generative AI into their core products, but the company's emphasis on lower token costs and vertical-specific applications sets it apart from rivals pursuing broader, more generic AI assistants. For India's software industry, a homegrown SaaS major building both a general-purpose AI interface and industry-specific applications in-house — rather than relying solely on foundation models from US labs — is a notable data point in the country's push to build more of its own AI-native enterprise software stack.
