The United States has temporarily halted the scheduling of visa appointments at its embassies and consulates around the world, a move that could affect thousands of applicants, including large numbers of Indian students, skilled workers and travellers seeking entry into the country.

## What is happening

A US State Department spokesperson confirmed the pause is linked to a newly launched global training programme for consular officers, with appointment schedules being adjusted to accommodate the sessions. Applicants who already had interview slots booked have reportedly received notices that their appointments were cancelled and would be rescheduled at a later, unspecified date. The State Department said the training is intended to help officers screen out applicants who might become dependent on US public benefits and to ensure visa decisions are made through what it called a "comprehensive and consistent" review process. Officials have not disclosed how long the pause or the training programme will last.

## Why it matters for India

India is among the largest sources of US visa applicants globally, sending hundreds of thousands of students, skilled professionals and visitors to the country each year, making it one of the nations most exposed to any slowdown in consular processing. The pause lands amid a broader tightening of US immigration pathways under the current administration. Separately, the Department of Homeland Security has proposed an additional fee of roughly $103,265 for H-1B cap-subject petitions, including those filed under the advanced degree exemption, a change that could significantly raise hiring costs for companies that sponsor foreign technology workers, a group in which Indian professionals make up a substantial share.

The visa appointment pause also follows reports that US authorities are examining a large-scale review of business and tourism visas. According to documents cited by wire reports, the State Department is looking at the possible revocation of up to 200,000 visitor visas issued between 2016 and 2026 to individuals who subsequently applied for asylum in the US. Officials have clarified that such a review would not automatically trigger deportation but could strip affected individuals of their existing visa privileges.

## Part of a wider pattern

The pause comes days after a federal judge struck down a separate administration policy that had suspended immigrant visa issuance for applicants from 75 countries, ruling that the measure exceeded the legal authority granted to the Secretary of State. That ruling has not stopped the administration from pursuing other avenues to tighten screening, including the newly announced training-linked pause.

Human rights organisations have criticised the broader immigration crackdown as discriminatory, arguing it raises due process concerns and risks racial profiling in visa decisions. The administration has defended the measures as necessary to strengthen national security and prevent misuse of the immigration system.

For now, the practical impact on Indian applicants remains unclear, with consular posts yet to indicate when normal appointment scheduling will resume. Immigration lawyers and education consultants are likely to watch closely in the coming days, particularly with the US academic year already underway and peak H-1B and work-visa processing periods on the horizon.
