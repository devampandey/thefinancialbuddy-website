India put themselves firmly in control of the first Test against Sri Lanka on Monday, with spinners Manav Suthar and Ravindra Jadeja combining for seven wickets to bowl the hosts out for 284 on day three at the Galle International Stadium. The result handed India a substantial 178-run first-innings lead in a match already shaping up as a statement performance for the touring side.

Left-arm spinner Suthar was the standout bowler, finishing with figures of 4 for 76, striking with the very first ball he bowled in the innings and consistently extracting turn and bounce on a wearing surface. Jadeja was nearly as effective, picking up 3 for 57 to complement Suthar's effort. Persistent rain interrupted play through the day and eventually forced an early end to the session, but not before India had wrapped up the Sri Lankan innings.

## Dinusha's Fighting Century

Sri Lanka's innings was given late respectability by all-rounder Sonal Dinusha, who compiled a maiden Test century in a rearguard effort that briefly threatened to trim India's advantage. Dinusha put on 146 runs for the sixth wicket alongside wicketkeeper-batter Niroshan Dickwella before the partnership was finally broken by fast bowler Prasidh Krishna, who dismissed Dickwella for 80. Suthar sealed the innings soon after, stumping last-man Lahiru Kumara as he charged down the pitch in an attempt to counter-attack.

## India's Batting Foundation

The bowlers built on a strong platform laid by India's batting unit earlier in the match. India had posted 462 in their first innings, anchored by a commanding 167 from Devdutt Padikkal and a composed 82 from KL Rahul. That total always looked likely to put Sri Lanka under pressure, and the eventual 178-run lead reflects the gap in first-innings performance between the two sides through the opening three days.

## A Milestone for Jadeja

Beyond the team's position in the match, Monday's play also delivered a personal milestone for Jadeja. His third wicket of the innings was also his 350th in Test cricket, a landmark that places him alongside Kapil Dev as the only Indian to have combined more than 4,000 Test runs with more than 350 wickets. The achievement makes Jadeja just the fourth player in the history of the format to reach that dual milestone, joining an elite group that includes Kapil Dev, England's Ian Botham and New Zealand's Daniel Vettori.

## Match Situation

With a healthy first-innings lead already banked and two days of play still to come, India will look to extend their advantage further when Sri Lanka begin their second innings, weather permitting. The first Test runs from August 15 to 19, with the two sides set to move on to Colombo for the second match of the series later in the month. India's bowling attack, led by the spin duo of Suthar and Jadeja alongside pace support from Prasidh Krishna, will be aiming to press home their advantage and push for an outright result before time and rain intervene further.
