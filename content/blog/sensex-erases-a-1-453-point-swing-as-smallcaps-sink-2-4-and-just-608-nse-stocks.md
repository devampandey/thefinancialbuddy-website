Look only at the headline numbers and Wednesday seems like a quiet session. The BSE Sensex ended 143.21 points, or 0.19%, lower at 73,709.69, and the NSE Nifty 50 slipped 77.60 points, or 0.34%, to 22,811.35. Look a level deeper and the picture changes sharply: on the NSE, 2,988 stocks closed lower and only 608 closed higher, roughly five losers for every gainer.

That gap between a small index loss and a broad sell-off is the most useful thing to understand about the session.

## A Morning Rally That Did Not Last

The day began with optimism. Stocks opened strongly after the Dussehra holiday on comments from Washington that an India-US trade deal was close ([see our earlier coverage of the open](/markets/nifty-it-leads-post-dussehra-rebound-as-sensex-adds-570-points-with-brent-at-110)). The Nifty touched 23,120 early in the day.

From there it went one way. The Sensex fell as much as 1,453 points from its intraday peak and the Nifty dropped to 22,650 before recovering some ground late in the session. At 3 pm the Sensex was down about 580 points; buying in the final half hour roughly halved that loss by the close.

## Oil Is Doing the Damage Again

The main pressure came from crude. Brent futures for December delivery rose as much as 2.3% to around $111 a barrel, close to a five-month high, after fresh attacks on Gulf shipping and reports that Iran had ruled out talks with Washington until its demands are met. Falling US crude inventories added to the move.

For India, which imports most of its oil, every sustained move above $110 feeds into inflation worries, company margins and pressure on the rupee. That explains why the selling landed hardest on rate- and cost-sensitive pockets. Nifty Consumer Durables was the worst sector, down 1.9%, while metal, bank, financial services, auto, FMCG and realty indices lost between 0.5% and 1.7%. Persistent foreign selling and higher US bond yields did not help.

Fear gauges reacted too. India VIX, which tracks expected volatility, rose about 6% on the day.

## IT Was the Only Place to Hide

The Nifty IT index was the lone major sector to finish in the green. Infosys, HCL Technologies, Tech Mahindra and TCS were among the top Nifty gainers, helped by September-quarter results that have broadly come in better than feared. L&T Technology Services extended its post-results rally and closed about 10% higher at Rs 3,982.

Without that support from software heavyweights, the benchmark loss would have been considerably larger.

## Where the Losses Landed

Bajaj Finance was the weakest Nifty stock, falling 3.27% to Rs 926 as analysts focused on margin pressure despite the lender's 29% profit growth. Zomato and Blinkit owner Eternal, Eicher Motors, Trent, Maruti Suzuki, Adani Enterprises, Shriram Finance and Axis Bank each lost between 1.5% and 2.6%.

The broader market fared worse. The Nifty Midcap 100 fell 1.58% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 dropped 2.37%, more than seven times the Nifty's decline. For investors whose portfolios lean towards smaller companies, Wednesday hurt far more than the Sensex suggests.

## The Levels Traders Are Watching

Technically, the Nifty closed below its 20-day exponential moving average, a sign that short-term momentum is cooling. Sudeep Shah of SBI Securities identified 22,630 to 22,650, the day's low, as immediate support, with a break below it opening the way to 22,500. On the upside, he sees resistance around 22,960 to 22,980.

With Q2 earnings still coming in and Diwali Muhurat trading on November 8, the near-term direction will likely depend on whether crude cools and whether the trade-deal optimism turns into signed terms.
