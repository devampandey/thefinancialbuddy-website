Indian equity benchmarks closed lower on Tuesday, snapping the previous session's gains, as a sharp jump in crude oil prices and renewed uncertainty over a potential US-Iran agreement on the Strait of Hormuz rattled investor sentiment.

## How the Indices Fared

The BSE Sensex declined roughly 388 points, or about 0.49%, to close near 78,154, while the NSE Nifty 50 shed about 112 points, or 0.46%, to settle just under the 24,472 mark. Market breadth stayed weak through the session, with roughly as many stocks declining as advancing on the BSE, though the broader mid-cap space held relatively steady and small-caps managed modest gains, suggesting the sell-off was concentrated in large-cap, rate- and crude-sensitive names rather than a broad-based flight from equities.

Sectorally, FMCG and metal stocks were the biggest drags on the day, while pharmaceutical and healthcare counters attracted buying interest, offering some cushion to the broader indices. Heavyweights including Mahindra & Mahindra, Bharti Airtel and Axis Bank were among the top drags on the Nifty.

## Crude Oil Back in the Driver's Seat

The proximate trigger for the day's weakness was crude oil, which jumped roughly 2.5% to push Brent futures toward the $90-a-barrel mark, its highest level in more than a week. The move came after US President Donald Trump responded to Iran's conditions for reopening the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz by demanding compensation for people killed in regional conflicts, a stance that appears to have set back hopes of an imminent breakthrough. Iran, for its part, has been pressing Washington to compensate for damage caused by earlier US and Israeli strikes on its territory.

For India, which imports the vast majority of its crude requirements, a sustained rise in oil prices carries direct implications for the import bill, the rupee and inflation expectations, all of which factored into Tuesday's cautious trading. The rupee itself edged weaker against the dollar, extending its recent softness.

## Bonds, Volatility and Global Cues

The yield on India's 10-year benchmark government bond rose to just under 6.8%, reflecting some repricing of inflation risk tied to costlier crude. The India VIX, a gauge of expected near-term volatility, actually eased over 3%, indicating traders were not pricing in outsized swings despite the day's decline. Overseas, most European markets slipped alongside weaker UK retail sales data, while Asian markets broadly ended higher. On Wall Street overnight, the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Dow Jones Industrial Average all closed marginally lower, weighed down in part by weakness in chipmaking stocks.

## What's Ahead

Investors are now turning their attention to the US consumer price inflation report due Wednesday, which could shape expectations around the Federal Reserve's next policy move; a stronger-than-expected reading could revive talk of a rate hike, adding another layer of uncertainty for emerging markets like India. Closer to home, the ongoing June-quarter earnings season continues to drive individual stock moves, with several mid-cap names posting sharp swings on the back of results even as the broader index narrative remained dominated by crude oil and geopolitics.
