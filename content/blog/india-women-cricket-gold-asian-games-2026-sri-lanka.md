India's women's cricket team delivered a dominant performance on Tuesday to win gold at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, thrashing Sri Lanka by a massive 147 runs in the final and successfully defending the title the team first won at the Hangzhou Games in 2023. The result gave India their first gold medal of this edition of the Games.

Batting first, India put up a formidable total of 216 for 3, built around a blistering innings from opener Smriti Mandhana, who smashed 79 runs off just 45 balls, an innings studded with five boundaries and seven sixes. Mandhana's aggressive start set the tone for the rest of the innings, allowing India's middle order to accelerate further and post a total that always looked out of Sri Lanka's reach on a used Korogi Athletic Park surface.

Sri Lanka's chase never got going. India's bowlers, led by Deepti Sharma with three wickets, alongside Shafali Verma and pacer Shree Charani with two apiece, dismantled the Sri Lankan top and middle order in quick succession, bowling the opposition out for a paltry 69 runs. The margin of victory, 147 runs, ranks among the most emphatic in the short history of women's T20 cricket at the Asian Games, and left little doubt about the gap between the two sides on the day.

The win carries extra significance because it was a straight rematch of the 2023 Hangzhou final, where India had also beaten Sri Lanka to claim the inaugural women's cricket gold at the Games. Successfully defending that title against the same opponent underlines the depth India's women's team has built across formats over the past three years, with several players from the Hangzhou-winning squad again central to Tuesday's win.

The gold medal also arrives at a good moment for Indian women's cricket more broadly, with the team having spent the build-up to these Games rebuilding momentum through white-ball series at home and abroad. A commanding campaign through the group stage and knockouts at Aichi-Nagoya, capped by Tuesday's one-sided final, will further strengthen the squad's confidence heading into a packed international calendar over the coming months.

For the broader Indian contingent at these Games, the cricket team's gold adds to a campaign that had earlier seen the country's shooters and other athletes chip in with medals across a range of disciplines. With India's cricketers now with their second successive Asian Games gold, wrapped up, attention at the Games shifts to India's other medal contenders across hockey, badminton and athletics as the competition moves into its concluding days.
