India and Switzerland signed two agreements meant to make it easier for Indian students, researchers and young workers to live and work in Switzerland, after Prime Minister Narendra Modi held delegation-level talks with Swiss President Guy Parmelin at Hyderabad House in New Delhi on Monday. The two sides also exchanged several memoranda of understanding and said they had agreed on steps to widen market access for Indian goods in Switzerland.

The talks were the centrepiece of the first day of Parmelin's three-day state visit, which [we previewed earlier](/politics/swiss-president-parmelin-begins-first-state-visit-to-india-as-efta-trade-pact-tur).

## The mobility deals

The two pacts signed on Monday are a Migration and Mobility Partnership Agreement and a Young Professionals Agreement. Speaking at a joint press appearance, Modi described people-to-people ties as the strongest part of the relationship and said the agreements would open new opportunities in Switzerland for Indian students, researchers, professionals and young people.

Young professionals schemes typically allow a fixed annual quota of early-career workers from each country to take up jobs in the other for a limited period without going through the usual work-permit tests. The full text and any quota figures had not been published by Monday afternoon, so the practical scale of the arrangement remains to be seen. For Indian graduates, though, the deal adds another European destination to a growing list of bilateral mobility arrangements New Delhi has been negotiating with wealthy economies facing labour shortages.

## Trade and investment

On the economic side, Modi said the two countries had taken several decisions to improve market access for Indian exports and to make cross-border investment smoother. He named agriculture, pharmaceuticals, textiles and engineering goods as the Indian sectors expected to benefit, and listed biotech, life sciences, banking, insurance, food processing and sustainability as areas where India hopes to draw fresh Swiss capital.

Modi also restated the headline promise attached to the India-EFTA Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement, which entered into force a year ago: $100 billion of investment into India and one million direct jobs over 15 years. Switzerland accounts for the largest share of India's trade and investment with the four-nation EFTA bloc, so how quickly Swiss companies commit money will largely decide whether that target is met.

## Defence, nuclear energy and global reform

Beyond trade, Modi said the leaders had identified new openings for military exchanges and defence manufacturing, and agreed to expand cooperation in nuclear energy. Switzerland's neutrality has traditionally made it a cautious defence partner, so any concrete follow-through there would be notable.

On geopolitics, Modi said both countries share a commitment to democracy, the rule of law and diplomacy, and that India has backed every meaningful peace effort on Ukraine and West Asia. He added that the two governments agree that global institutions need reform to deal with today's challenges, a familiar Indian position that gains weight when a European partner signs on.

## What comes next

Parmelin is scheduled to meet President Droupadi Murmu, who will host a state banquet in his honour, and to attend the second India-EFTA Prosperity Summit on October 7. That summit is likely to be where investment announcements from Swiss businesses travelling with him are made public, offering the first real measure of how much capital the trade pact is pulling in.
