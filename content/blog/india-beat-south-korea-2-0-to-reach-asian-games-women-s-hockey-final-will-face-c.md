India's women's hockey team is one win away from Asian Games gold. Playing South Korea in the semifinal in Aichi-Nagoya on Wednesday afternoon, India kept a clean sheet and struck twice in quick succession after the break to win 2-0, setting up a final against China.

The result extends a campaign in which India have looked sharper with each round, following a 3-1 win over hosts Japan in the [quarterfinal](/sports/india-women-hockey-beat-japan-asian-games-semifinal-2026).

## A patient first half

South Korea have long been one of Asia's most disciplined defensive sides, and the first two quarters reflected that. Neither team found a way through, and the match went into half-time goalless. For India, the priority was not to concede on the counter, and the back line held its shape against Korean pressure.

## Two goals in four minutes

The breakthrough came from a set piece. In the 43rd minute, India won a penalty corner and Ishika converted it to give her side the lead.

The second goal followed soon after. Captain Salima Tete drove a cross into the circle, and the ball took a touch off a South Korean defender's stick before finding the net. The goal was credited to Tete in some reports and treated as a deflection in others, but either way it doubled India's advantage in the space of about four minutes.

That burst effectively decided the tie. Korea pushed forward in search of a way back, but India's defence and goalkeeper saw out the final quarter without major alarm.

## Why this final matters

India's only Asian Games women's hockey title came back in 1982, when the Games were held in New Delhi. Since then the team has come close without finishing the job, and a second gold would rank among the biggest results for the women's programme in more than four decades.

China will be the toughest opponent India have faced in Aichi-Nagoya. They are a physical, well-organised team with a strong record at this event, and the two sides know each other well from continental and international tournaments. The Indian women also opened their recent World Cup campaign with a 2-2 draw against China, which suggests there is little to separate the teams on current form.

## What to watch in the final

A few threads from the semifinal will carry into the gold medal match:

- **Penalty corners:** India's opener came from a set piece, and conversion rates in a tight final often decide the result.
- **Defensive discipline:** Keeping South Korea scoreless is a strong signal. Doing the same against China's direct style will be a different test.
- **Game management:** India protected a two-goal lead for more than a quarter without panic, a skill that tends to matter in finals.

The win also lifts the mood across the Indian contingent on a busy Day 12, which earlier brought gold medals in compound archery and trap shooting. A hockey gold in the final would add one of the most prized titles on the Asian Games programme to that haul.
