SRIT India made its stock market debut on Tuesday, opening at Rs 148 per share on the National Stock Exchange, a premium of 13.85% over its issue price of Rs 130. The opening on the BSE was noticeably more muted, at close to Rs 140, or roughly 7.5% above the issue price. By around 10 am the stock was trading near Rs 148.

For investors who received an allotment, the NSE opening translated into a gain of about Rs 2,070 on each lot of 115 shares, which had cost Rs 14,950. That took the value of a single lot to roughly Rs 17,020.

## A solid debut, but not a blowout

The listing comes after one of the more heavily bid issues of the season. As [we reported when the offer closed](/ipo/srit-india-ipo-closes-125-times-subscribed-as-institutions-pour-in-during-the-fi), the Rs 218.40 crore IPO was subscribed about 125 times overall. Non-institutional investors subscribed their portion nearly 313 times, qualified institutional buyers about 92 times and retail investors close to 64 times.

Demand of that intensity often raises expectations of a far larger first-day pop, so a mid-teens gain on the NSE, and a single-digit one on the BSE, points to a measured debut rather than a frenzy. That is in line with the wider mood in the market, where the Sensex is down sharply over the past quarter and investors have become more selective about paying up for new listings, even as Tuesday's session opened on a firmer note.

## What the company does

SRIT India is a Bengaluru-based IT and IT-enabled services firm that builds and runs digital platforms, mainly for government bodies and domestic enterprises, with some overseas work. Its offerings span digital health platforms and hospital information systems, e-governance solutions, cybersecurity, enterprise software, networking, fibre-optic infrastructure, automation and managed services.

The issue was entirely a fresh sale of about 1.68 crore new shares, with no offer for sale by existing shareholders, so all of the money goes to the company. SRIT has said it will use the proceeds for capital spending on modernising and redeveloping its products, working capital, acquisitions that have not yet been identified, and general corporate purposes. Ahead of the issue, it raised about Rs 65.5 crore from anchor investors including Abakkus Venture Opportunities Fund 2, Taurus Mutual Fund and Saint Capital Fund.

## What investors should weigh

A heavy dependence on government contracts can mean lumpy revenue and long payment cycles, which typically show up in working-capital needs, one reason the company has earmarked part of the proceeds for that purpose. The use of funds for unspecified acquisitions also leaves some uncertainty about how capital will be deployed. Those who received shares will be watching whether the stock can hold its NSE listing price once early trading interest fades, and how the company's first results as a listed entity compare with its pre-IPO track record.
