Zetwerk Manufacturing Businesses made a quiet but better-than-feared start on the stock market on Friday. The Bengaluru-based manufacturing platform opened at Rs 159.25 on the NSE, 5.4% above its issue price of Rs 151, and at Rs 159 on the BSE, a 5.3% premium. Ahead of the debut, the grey market had priced the shares at about Rs 152, signalling a gain of just Re 1.

For an allottee holding a single 99-share lot, the opening price works out to a paper gain of about Rs 817 on an investment of Rs 14,949. That is modest, but it is a gain at a time when the benchmark indices are on course for a fourth straight losing session.

## Demand never really caught fire

The listing matched the tone of the bidding. The Rs 5,012 crore issue, which ran from October 15 to October 19, was subscribed 3.06 times overall, with bids for about 56.6 crore shares against 18.5 crore on offer. Qualified institutional buyers took up their quota 4.73 times and non-institutional investors 1.40 times, while the retail portion was left undersubscribed at 0.73 times, even though retail investors were offered only 10% of the issue because the company is loss-making. We covered the issue structure and anchor book [when bidding opened](/ipo/zetwerk-s-rs-5-012-crore-ipo-opens-to-thin-bids-as-loss-making-platform-tests-in).

Friday's other listings show how loosely subscription numbers and debut gains are linked. Tonbo Imaging, which drew more than 130 times bids, opened 66% higher. Sahajanand Medical Technologies listed about 8% up. Bonfiglioli Transmissions opened flat despite roughly 46 times subscription. Against that backdrop, a 5% pop on a 3 times book is a reasonable outcome for Zetwerk.

## The numbers that will decide the next move

Zetwerk's pitch is scale. It runs a hybrid network of its own factories and third-party suppliers through its in-house software, Zetwerk OS, and serves sectors ranging from renewable energy and utilities to consumer electronics, AI infrastructure, aerospace and defence. Since incorporation it has delivered goods worth Rs 74,374 crore in gross merchandise value, and its client list includes Siemens Gamesa, CG Power, NTPC Renewable Energy, DRDO, Indian Oil and boAt.

The problem is the bottom line. Revenue has grown at about 13.4% a year and adjusted operating profit much faster, but the company posted a consolidated loss of Rs 1,606 crore in FY26, including discontinued operations. Its adjusted EBITDA of about Rs 421 crore on total income of over Rs 16,000 crore implies an operating margin of under 3%, which leaves little cushion if order flow slows or input costs rise.

## What the IPO money changes

Of the Rs 5,012 crore raised, Rs 2,830 crore is fresh capital, and a large part is earmarked for repaying borrowings, which should lower interest costs and narrow losses. The remaining Rs 2,182 crore went to selling shareholders through the offer for sale and does not reach the company.

## Our take

The muted grey market had set a low bar, and Zetwerk cleared it. But a small listing premium on thin retail demand suggests the market is pricing the company as a turnaround story rather than a proven compounder. The next real test comes with its first quarterly results as a listed company, when investors will look for progress on margins and a clear path to net profit. Long-term holders will want evidence that the debt reduction is narrowing the gap between revenue growth and profit.
