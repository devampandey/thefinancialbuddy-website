India's men's badminton team settled for a bronze medal at the Asian Games 2026 after going down 3-1 to defending champions China in the team event semifinal in Aichi, Japan, on Wednesday, ending the team's bid to reach back-to-back finals at the Games.

## How the Tie Went

China took the opening rubber when world No. 1 doubles pair Liang Weikeng and Wang Chang were expected to test India, but it was India's Lakshya Sen who opened the tie, going down 21-16, 21-18 to reigning world champion Shi Yuqi. China stretched their lead when Ayush Shetty lost 21-19, 21-13 to world No. 12 Li Shi Feng in the second singles rubber, putting India firmly on the back foot.

India's only bright spot came in the men's doubles, where Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty produced the standout performance of the tie, beating Liang and Wang — the reigning world champions in the discipline — 21-11, 22-20 to keep India's hopes alive. But the recovery was short-lived: the second doubles pairing of Hariharan Amsakarunan and MR Arjun lost a hard-fought four-game battle 21-16, 17-21, 21-9 to China, sealing the tie 3-1 in China's favour and confirming India's exit to the bronze medal position.

## Context

India had entered the semifinal on the back of a strong run through the group and knockout stages, and a team-event medal continues an encouraging run for Indian badminton at the Asian Games, even as the squad falls short of retaining the higher finish it managed at the previous edition. The Satwik-Chirag pairing's win over the world champions was a rare bright spot against a Chinese team that remains the dominant force in Asian team badminton.

## Why It Matters

A team bronze keeps Indian badminton's medal tally ticking over at an Asian Games where the country's contingent has already picked up medals across shooting, weightlifting and other disciplines through the week. With individual events still to come at these Games, Wednesday's result also offers a form guide: Satwik-Chirag's win over the top-ranked Chinese pair will be seen as a significant confidence boost heading into the individual doubles draw, even as the singles players will look to address the gaps exposed against China's top-ranked competitors.
