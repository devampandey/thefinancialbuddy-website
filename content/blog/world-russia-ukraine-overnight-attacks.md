Russian forces launched a fresh wave of overnight strikes across Ukraine on Friday, killing at least three people and wounding several others, even as diplomatic efforts to end the four-and-a-half-year war showed tentative signs of movement.

## Deadly Strikes Across Multiple Regions

A large-scale Russian attack on the Odesa region killed one person and injured at least five others, including a child, according to the regional head. The strikes damaged several residential buildings, including two multi-storey blocks. Separately, overnight strikes killed two police officers in the city of Dnipro, while a drone attack on Kyiv injured three people after hitting a residential building.

A Russian drone also struck the headquarters of Ukraine's security service in central Kyiv, close to a historic church and several international hotels. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he had been in contact with the agency's acting chief following the strike. The security service is among the Ukrainian agencies known to carry out long-range strike operations inside Russian territory, and officials have noted that Russian attacks, once concentrated at night, are increasingly occurring during daylight hours as well.

Russia was not spared retaliation. Ukrainian drones struck the Black Sea resort city of Sochi and the nearby district of Sirius, sparking fires at oil storage facilities, regional officials said. Ukraine has repeatedly targeted refineries and fuel depots that underpin Russia's energy-dependent economy, contributing to mounting public frustration and economic strain inside Russia.

## Diplomatic Track Gains Cautious Momentum

The violence unfolded even as both sides signalled openness to renewed negotiations. In his nightly address, Zelenskyy said preliminary dates had been set for meetings in both Moscow and Kyiv, describing continued American engagement as essential. Ukraine's negotiating team, he said, remains in near-constant contact with US counterparts and is preparing for substantive talks.

Russian state media reported that American negotiators could travel to Moscow first before proceeding to Kyiv over the weekend, although this could not be independently confirmed and the Kremlin's spokesperson declined to confirm the itinerary. Any Kyiv visit by the American team would mark its first, following an earlier trip to Moscow at the start of the year that Russian officials described as constructive, while cautioning that a durable settlement would require resolving territorial disputes.

Momentum on the diplomatic front has been uneven in recent months, with Washington's attention divided by its separate confrontation with Iran. Russian President Vladimir Putin, in comments earlier in the week, described the state of negotiations as "frozen," even as he later acknowledged a continued chance of reaching an agreement, with support from other governments including the United States and China.

## A War Still Testing Global Stability

The renewed exchange of strikes underscores how far the two sides remain from a ceasefire, even as external actors push for talks. Elsewhere, Finnish authorities said they were investigating how a Russian-made surveillance drone washed ashore on a rocky coastline east of Helsinki, adding to a string of incidents that have unsettled countries along Europe's eastern flank.

For now, front lines in eastern and southern Ukraine remain largely static, even as both militaries escalate long-range strikes deep inside each other's territory. Whether the reported plans for talks in Moscow and Kyiv translate into a genuine breakthrough is likely to become clearer in the days ahead.
