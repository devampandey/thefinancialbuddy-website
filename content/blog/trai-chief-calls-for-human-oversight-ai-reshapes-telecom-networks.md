The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India has waded into the global debate over how much autonomy to give artificial intelligence systems, with chairman Anil Kumar Lahoti telling an industry forum in New Delhi on Tuesday that telecom operators and technology vendors need to build stronger safeguards into AI deployments before handing over greater decision-making power to machines.

## Autonomy Needs Accountability

Speaking at the CO.AI 2026 forum, Lahoti said AI can help telecom operators build networks that are more self-managing and efficient, automatically detecting faults, balancing traffic loads and optimising performance in ways that would be impractical for human engineers to manage manually at scale. But he cautioned that as networks become more autonomous, particularly in areas where AI systems can take decisions with significant consequences for users, that autonomy has to be matched by clear lines of responsibility, meaningful human intervention, and protections for consumers.

Lahoti's remarks set out a practical checklist for operators: assess risks before any AI system goes live, test models rigorously under real-world conditions, document important AI-led decisions so they can be audited later, and retain the ability to review or override outcomes when something goes wrong. The message was less a warning against adopting AI than an argument for adopting it deliberately, with governance built in from the start rather than bolted on after problems emerge.

## Part of a Wider Pattern

The comments land at a moment when questions about how fast to push AI adoption are dominating conversations well beyond India's telecom sector. Just this week, prominent global AI executives called for a more measured pace of AI model development, citing safety concerns, a debate that has already rippled into Indian markets, with IT services stocks rallying on the view that enterprises may lean more on careful AI integration support rather than aggressive in-house deployment. TRAI's intervention effectively extends that same caution to the infrastructure layer that carries India's mobile and broadband traffic, an area regulators globally have been slower to address compared with consumer-facing AI applications like chatbots or content generation.

## Why It Matters for the Industry

For telecom operators such as Reliance Jio, Bharti Airtel and Vodafone Idea, all of whom have been investing in AI-driven network management to cut costs and improve service quality, Lahoti's comments signal that future regulatory expectations are likely to include documentation and override requirements rather than a free hand to automate. That could mean additional compliance overhead in the near term, but industry watchers argue it may also reduce the risk of costly network failures or consumer complaints stemming from opaque automated decisions, an outcome that ultimately protects both operators and subscribers as India's networks lean further into AI-driven management over the coming years.
