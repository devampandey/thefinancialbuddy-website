Star Indian javelin thrower Neeraj Chopra has confirmed his place in this year's Diamond League final, set to be held in Brussels in early September, even after opting out of the Zurich leg of the circuit that concluded on Thursday.

## A season built on limited but strong outings

Chopra has competed sparingly on the Diamond League circuit in 2026, taking part in only two legs of the season rather than the full slate most rivals contested. His most recent outing came at the Lausanne meet, where he finished runner-up with a season-best throw of 88.05 metres, trailing Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage. That performance, combined with his points tally from earlier in the season, proved sufficient for him to finish sixth in the overall qualifying standings and secure automatic qualification for the season-ending final, even without adding to his points in Zurich.

Pathirage, who has emerged as one of the standout performers on this year's circuit, topped the Zurich leg with a throw of 92.07 metres, his second-best of the season, and finished the qualifying phase at the top of the standings. His consistency across multiple meets this year has made him one of the athletes to watch heading into Brussels, alongside Chopra and the rest of the finalists who have punched their tickets over the course of the Diamond League season.

## What it means for Chopra

For Chopra, reaching the final without a full competitive season is itself a reflection of the depth of his point tally from a smaller number of high-quality performances, rather than the volume-based approach some of his competitors have taken. Fans and analysts of Indian athletics will be watching closely to see whether the two-time Olympic medallist can find his best form on the day in Brussels, an event that traditionally serves as the season's showpiece and draws the sport's top throwers into a single high-stakes competition.

Chopra's presence in the final also matters beyond his individual result. As one of the most recognisable faces of Indian athletics, his participation in marquee international meets continues to draw domestic attention to track and field in India, a sport that has historically operated in the shadow of cricket in terms of media coverage and sponsorship. A strong showing in Brussels would cap what has, by his own recent standards, been a more selective and lower-volume season, while a disappointing outing would raise questions about his form heading into next year's global championships.

## The road to Brussels

The Diamond League final will be held on September 4 and 5 in the Belgian capital, bringing together the season's top qualifiers across all disciplines. For India, Chopra's participation remains the primary point of interest in the field events, with the country continuing to build broader depth in throwing disciplines but still relying heavily on his individual performances for podium contention on the world stage.
