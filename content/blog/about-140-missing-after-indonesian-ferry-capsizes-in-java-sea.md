A major search and rescue operation is underway in Indonesia's Java Sea after a passenger ferry carrying more than 240 people capsized and sank in rough weather early on Sunday, leaving dozens dead or missing and triggering anguished scenes at the port where relatives gathered for news.

## What Happened

The vessel, Virgo Transport 8, departed Surabaya on the island of Java on Saturday, bound for Banjarmasin, a port city on Borneo roughly 150 kilometres away. According to Indonesia's national search and rescue agency, Basarnas, the ship's captain reported rough seas with waves reaching up to three metres before the vessel began listing and ultimately capsized. Contact with the ship was lost in the early hours of Sunday, at around 2 a.m. local time, with a distress signal reaching authorities roughly two hours later.

The ship's manifest recorded 243 people on board, comprising 213 passengers and 30 crew members, though officials noted that actual headcounts on Indonesian ferries often differ from official manifests. By Sunday afternoon, search teams working with tugboats, a navy vessel and a helicopter had rescued more than 100 people and recovered six bodies, leaving close to 130 to 140 people still unaccounted for as the operation continued into the evening.

## A Difficult Search In Rough Waters

Officials coordinating the rescue described conditions at sea as hazardous even for the search vessels themselves, with waves of up to 2.5 metres complicating efforts to locate survivors and wreckage near the ferry's last known position, roughly 150 kilometres from Banjarmasin. Basarnas has deployed close to 300 personnel, drawing on the navy, maritime police and its own rescue teams, in an operation that authorities say will likely stretch over multiple days given the scale of those still missing.

At Banjarmasin's Trisakti port, family members of those aboard gathered at a makeshift command centre, scanning passenger manifests and rescue lists for names of loved ones. Accounts from relatives underscored both the anxiety and occasional relief of the unfolding search, as some learned their family members had been pulled from the water safely while others continued to wait.

## Part Of A Troubling Pattern

Sunday's disaster is the latest in a string of maritime accidents to hit Indonesia's ferry network in recent months, renewing scrutiny of safety standards across the archipelago nation's vast inter-island transport system. Just last month, a fire aboard a ferry travelling between Bali and Lombok left one person dead and several others missing, while July saw another vessel carrying more than 70 people sink near Selayar Island. Weak enforcement of safety regulations has repeatedly been cited as a contributing factor in Indonesia's recurring ferry disasters, given the country's reliance on maritime transport across more than 17,000 islands.

The Virgo Transport 8 disaster also comes just days after a separate ferry tragedy in the Philippines, where a fire aboard a passenger vessel killed at least 76 people, adding to a difficult few weeks for maritime safety across Southeast Asia. Indonesian authorities have not yet indicated when the search operation is expected to conclude, and the death toll is likely to rise as recovery efforts continue in the challenging conditions off Borneo's coast.
