A tanker making its way into the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unidentified projectile on Monday, leaving two crew members with minor injuries, Britain's United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) agency said, in the latest sign that the waterway remains a flashpoint months into the standoff between Iran and the United States.

UKMTO said military authorities had informed it that the vessel, which was on an inbound transit, was hit by the projectile but remained under its own power and continued toward its next port of call. No oil spill or other environmental damage has been reported so far, and authorities are investigating the incident while urging vessels transiting the strait to exercise caution and report any suspicious activity.

## Fourth Strike In 11 Days

Monday's incident marks the fourth attack on commercial shipping passing through the Strait of Hormuz in just 11 days, and the first this month to leave anyone injured. The narrow waterway, through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil trade passes, has been targeted repeatedly since Iran began striking merchant vessels following US and Israeli strikes on Iranian territory in late February. Maritime trackers say the campaign has now killed 14 seafarers and a port worker.

Iran's leadership has separately signalled it has no intention of easing pressure on the strait, with a senior Iranian official reiterating in recent days that it would stay effectively closed until Tehran's conditions are met.

## Sanctions Squeeze Tightens

The attack came on a day when the standoff between Washington and Tehran deepened on another front. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said all Iranian airlines would be shut out of international operations from September 23, warning that any company providing them fuel, landing services or ticket sales risked being cut off from the US dollar financial system. The move builds on a broader Treasury campaign, dubbed Operation Economic Outcast, that has already sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian carriers and several service providers this month.

Iranian officials have responded with their own warnings. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it would retaliate harshly and strike new targets if the US launched a fresh attack, while Iran's military leadership has said it possesses intelligence suggesting an attack is being prepared. President Trump, for his part, has said Iran's economy would collapse, or its leadership face being wiped out, if Tehran does not agree to a deal.

Gulf officials have pushed back on some of Washington's framing of the standoff. Qatar's energy minister this week dismissed a suggestion from Bessent that the Strait of Hormuz could become commercially irrelevant within two years, arguing the waterway's importance extends well beyond oil and gas shipments.

With diplomatic efforts largely stalled and both sides trading threats ahead of this week's United Nations General Assembly session in New York, shipping companies are left weighing the risks of transiting one of the world's most strategically vital, and increasingly dangerous, chokepoints.
