PV Sindhu's campaign for a sixth World Championships medal came to an end on Thursday, as the two-time Olympic medallist lost an 88-minute pre-quarterfinal battle to third seed Wang Zhi Yi of China at the BWF World Championships being held in New Delhi.

## How The Match Unfolded

Sindhu dropped the opening game comfortably before finding her attacking rhythm to level the contest, carrying that momentum into a tense decider at the Indira Gandhi International Stadium. She led at multiple points in the final game and drew level with Wang deep into the contest, only for a string of unforced errors at the most critical stage to hand the Chinese shuttler the closing points and the match. The final scoreline reflected just how tightly contested the encounter was, with Sindhu managing to push a player ranked above her to the wire before the match slipped away in the closing exchanges. Speaking afterward, Sindhu pointed to missed opportunities in the decider as the difference between advancing and going out of the tournament she was hoping would add to her extensive medal collection.

## A Mixed Day For India At Home

Sindhu's exit came on a day of contrasting fortunes for the Indian contingent at a tournament being hosted in the country for the first time since 2009. Young talent Ayush Shetty, who had impressed through the earlier rounds with a comfortable straight-games win over Dumindu Abeywickrama, also fell in his own pre-quarterfinal, extending the day's disappointments in singles. Unnati Hooda was eliminated as well despite arriving at the tournament in good recent form. Not every Indian pairing suffered the same fate, however, with the doubles combination of Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty continuing to progress and keeping alive India's hopes of a medal from the championships being staged on home soil.

## Why The Home Tournament Matters

New Delhi hosting the World Championships for the first time in 17 years was seen as a significant moment for Indian badminton, both as a reward for the sport's growing profile in the country and as an opportunity for Indian players to compete in front of large home crowds at a marquee global event. Sindhu, who has anchored Indian women's badminton for close to a decade and already holds five World Championships medals including a gold from 2019, had been viewed as one of the host nation's best chances for another podium finish. Her early exit means the pressure now shifts to India's remaining players, particularly the doubles pairings, to deliver a home medal before the tournament concludes.

## What's Next

With several of India's singles hopes eliminated in the pre-quarterfinal round, attention at the championships turns to the remaining doubles events over the following days, as India looks to salvage a strong overall showing from a tournament it had hoped would be a career highlight for its singles stars on home soil.
