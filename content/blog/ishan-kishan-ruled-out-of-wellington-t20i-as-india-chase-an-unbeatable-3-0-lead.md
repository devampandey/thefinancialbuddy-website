India will go into Tuesday's third T20I against New Zealand in Wellington without Ishan Kishan, and with a chance to wrap up the five-match series with two games to spare.

The BCCI said on Monday that the wicketkeeper-batter is recovering well from a low-grade strain to his right adductor, picked up during the first T20I in Christchurch, but has not yet regained match fitness. He sat out the second game for the same reason. The board's medical team will continue to monitor him, and uncapped Jharkhand wicketkeeper Kumar Kushagra, who was called up as cover, will stay with the squad. Bowling coach Morne Morkel said the team management did not want to rush Kishan back before he was fully fit.

## A record on hold

The injury has paused one of the more remarkable individual seasons in Indian T20 cricket. Kishan has scored 1,159 runs in 35 T20Is this calendar year at an average of 36.21 and a strike rate above 171, with a century and seven half-centuries. That leaves him just six runs short of Suryakumar Yadav's record of 1,164, the most T20I runs by an Indian batter in a single calendar year.

With two more matches after Wellington, he still has time to get there in this series. But his absence also takes away India's most prolific top-order batter of the year at a point where the side has been winning ugly rather than dominating.

## Winning without fluency

India lead 2-0, but neither win came easily. In the opener at Hagley Oval they chased down a total of more than 220 to win by four wickets. Two days later, at the same venue, they slipped to 70 for 5 chasing 181 before Tilak Varma, Nitish Kumar Reddy and Naman Dhir rebuilt the innings, helped by several dropped chances from New Zealand, and India got home by four wickets with one ball to spare.

That second chase exposed the question the team management now faces. Without Kishan, teenager Vaibhav Sooryavanshi is the most likely option to bat at No. 3. He has 250 runs in nine T20Is at a strike rate close to 179, but made only four off eight balls when tried in that slot on Saturday. The alternative is to hand a debut to Kushagra, who arrives in strong form, with 641 runs in his last 14 innings across formats at an average above 53, including two centuries.

## What is at stake in Wellington

For New Zealand, Sky Stadium is now a must-win venue. Another loss would hand India the series and leave the hosts playing only for pride in the last two matches, after two games in which sloppy fielding cost them dearly.

For India, a 3-0 lead would give the team management room to rotate the squad and give Kishan the time he needs to return for the closing games, where that calendar-year record will still be waiting. It would also offer a chance to settle the No. 3 question before the batting order is tested again.
