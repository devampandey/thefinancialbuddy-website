Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the fifth edition of SEMICON India 2026 in New Delhi on Thursday, opening a three-day event that organisers say has drawn its largest turnout yet and coincided with fresh investment commitments worth billions of dollars to India's young semiconductor industry.

## A Bigger Stage For India's Chip Ambitions

The event, held at the Yashobhoomi convention centre from September 17 to 19 under the theme Silicon to Systems: Building the Ecosystem, is hosting more than 600 companies and delegates from 52 countries, with organisers expecting roughly 50,000 attendees across the exhibition and conference sessions. The scale marks a shift in tone from earlier editions, when much of the discussion centred on announcements and intent; officials and industry executives said several projects unveiled in previous years are now moving into actual commercial production. In his remarks, Modi said India should aim to be among the early leaders in emerging technology areas such as artificial intelligence and quantum computing, and called for greater industry involvement in skilling programmes tied to the chip sector, building on a government target of training roughly one lakh engineering students for semiconductor-related roles.

## Billions In New Investment Commitments

India's semiconductor policy, launched under a roughly $13.5 billion mission with 12 chip-related projects already approved, has drawn about $12 billion in fresh investment pledges from global and domestic players within just months of its rollout, according to figures cited at the event. Adding to that momentum, US-based chip equipment maker Applied Materials announced separately on Thursday that it would invest up to $5 billion in India over the next decade, one of the largest single commitments to date from a global semiconductor equipment supplier. Modi also pointed to tangible progress on the ground, noting that two more facilities, in Mohali and Surat, have begun commercial chip production this week, adding to a small but growing list of Indian sites now shipping finished semiconductor products rather than just breaking ground on new plants.

## Building Beyond Individual Factories

Industry participants at the event said the bigger story this year is less about any single factory and more about India assembling the surrounding ecosystem, from raw material suppliers and equipment makers to packaging and testing firms, that a durable chip industry requires. That shift matters because semiconductor manufacturing has historically clustered in a handful of countries precisely because the supply chain around a fab is as important as the fab itself. With SEMICON India 2026 running through Saturday, more corporate announcements and state-level incentive packages are expected before the event closes, and analysts will be watching whether the investment pledges made this week translate into the same pace of construction and hiring that organisers highlighted from earlier editions of the show.
