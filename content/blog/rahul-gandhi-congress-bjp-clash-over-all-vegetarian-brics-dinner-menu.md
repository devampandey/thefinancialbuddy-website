A menu card from the BRICS Summit's gala dinner has become the unlikely centre of a political row in Delhi, with Congress leaders accusing the Modi government of hiding India's culinary diversity from visiting world leaders, and the BJP dismissing the criticism as politics over a state banquet.

## The Menu That Started It

The controversy began after Congress leader Pawan Khera shared a photograph of the official banquet menu served to BRICS heads of state and government during their gala dinner in New Delhi. The spread was entirely vegetarian, featuring starters such as Khandvi Mille-Feuille and Khumb Galouti, a main course lineup including Paneer Lababdar, Himalayan morel mushrooms and Carrot Bean Poriyal, and desserts like Old Delhi Fruit Cream with Jalebi. Khera questioned why the government chose an exclusively vegetarian spread for a summit meant to showcase India to the world, framing the choice as an attempt by the ruling party to impose its own dietary preferences on both Indian citizens and foreign dignitaries.

## Rahul Gandhi Joins In

Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, Rahul Gandhi, amplified the criticism a day after the dinner, posting on social media that eighty per cent of Indians are non-vegetarian and that the country's non-vegetarian cuisine deserved a place at the table. He shared a photograph of chole bhature prepared by his sister, using the moment to argue that the government's menu choices did not reflect the diversity of Indian food culture. The remarks quickly drew reactions from both sides of the political aisle, with several BJP leaders accusing Gandhi of trivialising a diplomatic occasion for a talking point.

## BJP's Response

BJP leaders pushed back sharply, framing the Congress criticism as an unnecessary politicisation of hospitality arrangements for foreign heads of state. Party spokespersons argued that menu choices at state banquets are routine matters of protocol rather than ideological statements, and accused the Congress leadership of manufacturing controversy on a day when the summit itself had delivered genuine diplomatic wins for India, including the unanimous adoption of the New Delhi Declaration.

## Bigger Picture

The exchange played out against the backdrop of the 18th BRICS Summit's closing day in New Delhi, where Prime Minister Narendra Modi hosted leaders from the eleven-nation bloc for two days of talks on trade, technology and global governance reform. While the menu dispute is unlikely to have any lasting policy consequence, it reflects a recurring pattern in Indian politics where questions of food, culture and identity are quickly drawn into partisan battles, particularly around high-profile events with significant media coverage. Both the Congress and BJP used the moment to reinforce familiar narratives, with the Opposition portraying the government as out of touch with India's pluralism and the ruling party portraying its critics as opportunistic. The episode is likely to remain a talking point in the news cycle for a few more days even as the substantive outcomes of the BRICS Summit, including its declaration on West Asia and calls for United Nations Security Council reform, continue to be analysed by policymakers and commentators.
