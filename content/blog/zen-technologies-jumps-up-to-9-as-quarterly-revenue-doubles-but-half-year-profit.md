Shares of Zen Technologies, the Hyderabad-based maker of combat training simulators and anti-drone systems, climbed as much as about 9% on Monday to around Rs 1,726, as investors got their first trading session to react to the company's September-quarter results, which were filed with the exchanges on Saturday.

## A big quarter after a weak one

Revenue from operations rose 98.8% year on year to Rs 345.1 crore, from Rs 173.6 crore a year earlier. Total consolidated profit came in at Rs 101.9 crore, up from Rs 61.9 crore. Of that, Rs 89.5 crore belongs to Zen's own shareholders, a 50.7% increase, with the remaining Rs 12.3 crore attributable to minority partners in its subsidiaries. Profit before tax rose about 66% to Rs 137.7 crore, and earnings per share improved to Rs 9.97 from Rs 6.61.

The sequential swing was even larger. Revenue jumped about 144% from Rs 141.6 crore in the June quarter, and profit for shareholders rose from Rs 34.5 crore to Rs 89.5 crore. The June quarter had disappointed the market, and this rebound appears to be what the stock is rewarding.

## Why the half-year view matters

Defence equipment makers book revenue when they deliver, and deliveries tend to bunch up around government acceptance cycles. That makes single quarters a poor guide. Looking at the first six months of the financial year together, Zen's revenue rose 46.7% to Rs 486.8 crore, which is strong, but profit attributable to shareholders grew a more modest 15.7% to Rs 124 crore. In other words, much of the growth in the September quarter made up for a soft start to the year.

The order book offers some visibility. Zen reported outstanding orders of about Rs 1,375.7 crore as of September 30, after new inflows of roughly Rs 482 crore during the quarter. Set against first-half revenue, that backlog covers around 1.4 years of sales at the current pace. Execution speed, rather than order wins alone, will decide whether the coming quarters look more like September or more like June.

## An AI bet and a factory fire

Zen's board has approved buying a 51.98% stake in Cogknit Semantics for Rs 135.15 crore, a move aimed at adding artificial intelligence and machine learning capability to its training and simulation products. The deal remains subject to completion conditions. Simulation software that adapts to a trainee's behaviour is where much of the value in defence training is heading, so the acquisition fits the company's strategy, though integration of a small AI firm carries its own risks.

The company also disclosed a fire on October 18 at the facility of its subsidiary Unistring Tech Solutions, with a preliminary estimated loss of about Rs 4 crore. The assets are insured and the final assessment is pending, so the financial impact should be limited, but it is worth tracking for any delivery delays.

## What investors should weigh

Zen trades at a rich multiple of book value, and defence stocks as a group have been swinging sharply on order news and brokerage notes. The September quarter shows what the business can do when deliveries line up, but the half-year numbers are a reminder that its earnings are uneven. The next two quarters, which typically carry heavy government spending ahead of the March year-end, will show whether this momentum holds.
