Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Sunday inaugurated a new garment manufacturing plant built by Ahmedabad-based Arvind Ltd at Khochwan in the Mirzamurad area of Varanasi, his parliamentary constituency. The Rs 150 crore facility is the company's first large manufacturing unit in eastern Uttar Pradesh, a region better known for handloom silk than for modern, export-oriented apparel factories.

## What the plant will make

The unit sits on roughly 7 to 8 acres and will produce sportswear, innerwear and other ready-made garments. Products for brands including Jockey, US Polo and Arrow are part of the planned output, and the facility also showed khadi-based clothing during the visit. Because Arvind supplies a global client base, garments stitched in Varanasi are expected to be shipped to international markets as well as sold in India.

The project is planned in two phases once production is fully under way. The company and state officials have pitched it as a large source of local jobs: more than 300 women have already been hired and are being trained, local women are being given priority in recruitment, and the plant aims to employ about 1,000 women in its first stage. Officials say the second phase could take employment into the thousands across Purvanchal.

Modi toured the shop floor, spoke with women trainees about their work and the problems they face, and visited an exhibition of Arvind products. Uttar Pradesh Governor Anandiben Patel and Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath accompanied him.

## Why the location matters

Garment making is one of the most labour-intensive parts of manufacturing, which is why governments court it for job creation. It has, however, tended to cluster in established hubs such as Tiruppur, Bengaluru, the National Capital Region and parts of Gujarat. Eastern Uttar Pradesh offers a large pool of workers and lower land costs, but has struggled to attract organised factories because of weaker infrastructure and supply chains.

A plant from a nearly century-old textile group gives the region a test case. Arvind, founded in 1931, works across the textile chain, from yarn and fabric, including denim, cotton, linen and suiting, to finished garments and technical textiles. If the Varanasi unit hits its staffing and output targets, it would strengthen the case that apparel capacity can move east, closer to where much of India's surplus labour lives.

## The numbers to watch

For investors, a Rs 150 crore plant is modest next to Arvind's overall business, so the near-term financial impact is likely to be limited. What will matter more is how fast the unit ramps up, how much of its output goes to export orders, and what it costs to train a largely first-time workforce. Garment exporters are also working through high freight and energy costs this year, with Brent crude well above $100 a barrel, which squeezes margins on price-sensitive orders.

The opening was part of a wider Varanasi visit by the Prime Minister, who also attended the Indian Air Force's Wings Over Ganges aerial display at Namo Ghat to mark the force's 94th anniversary.
