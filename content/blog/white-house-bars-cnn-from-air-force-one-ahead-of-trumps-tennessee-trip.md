CNN has been left off the presidential press pool for President Trump's Saturday trip to Tennessee, just days after a federal judge ordered the White House to restore the network's access following an earlier ban, reigniting a running dispute over press freedom at the White House.

## What happened

Trump traveled to Knoxville on Saturday to attend a University of Tennessee versus University of Texas football game scheduled for around midday on the US East Coast. For the trip, the White House's official schedule for the day simply did not list CNN as the television network in the small rotating press pool that travels aboard Air Force One and files shared reports for the wider press corps. Conservative outlet Real America's Voice was named in CNN's usual slot instead.

## The backstory

The exclusion follows a chain of events that began when the administration announced it was barring CNN, along with MS NOW and Politico, from White House premises, accusing the outlets of biased coverage. A federal judge intervened last Thursday, ruling that the administration had to restore the credentials of the affected journalists for a two-week period. Regular pool coverage briefly resumed on Friday, when a CNN correspondent took part in covering Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping's joint appearance at the National Archives.

Saturday's pool list suggests that restoration has not extended to guaranteeing CNN's place in the travel rotation specifically. CNN's chief national security correspondent, Jim Sciutto, pushed back publicly, arguing that presidents of both parties have historically respected the tradition of pool access regardless of friction with individual outlets, and suggesting Trump was breaking with that norm because of coverage he personally dislikes.

## Why the press pool matters

The White House press pool system exists so that a small rotating group of reporters and camera crews can travel in tight quarters with the president and then share their footage, photos and notes with every other outlet that could not fit on the trip. Legal commentators, including analyst Elie Honig, have argued that removing CNN from that rotation, even while nominally restoring its credentials, could run against the spirit of the court's order, potentially setting up further legal wrangling.

The episode also revives memories of an earlier standoff in which other major networks, including Fox, NBC, ABC and CBS, had briefly declined pool assignments themselves in solidarity when CNN was excluded, arguing that the rotation only works if no outlet can be arbitrarily dropped for its coverage choices. Whether that kind of pushback repeats this time, or whether the dispute heads back to court, will likely become clear in the coming days as the credential restoration window continues to run.
