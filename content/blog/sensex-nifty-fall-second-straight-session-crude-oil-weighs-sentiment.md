Indian equity benchmarks extended their losing streak to a second consecutive session on Tuesday, as elevated crude oil prices continued to weigh on investor sentiment and dragged banking heavyweights lower through the trading day.

## How the Indices Moved

The BSE Sensex fell 555.23 points, or 0.73 per cent, to settle at 75,577.58, while the NSE Nifty50 dropped 144.05 points, or 0.61 per cent, to close at 23,635.10. The decline followed a similarly weak session a day earlier, marking back-to-back losses for both benchmarks after a period of relative stability.

Banking stocks were among the biggest drags on the indices through the session. SBI Life Insurance Company, ICICI Bank and Axis Bank emerged as the top losers within the Nifty50 basket, with ICICI Bank alone shedding around 2 per cent on the day. The weakness in financials outweighed gains elsewhere in the market, pulling both headline indices into the red by the closing bell.

## Crude Oil Remains the Key Overhang

Market participants pointed to persistently high crude oil prices as the primary source of pressure on sentiment. Brent crude has been trading close to the $100-a-barrel mark in recent sessions, driven higher by escalating tensions in the Middle East, including renewed hostilities between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthi rebels and the continuing confrontation between the United States, Israel and Iran. For India, which imports the vast majority of its crude requirements, sustained high oil prices raise concerns about the trade deficit, inflation and corporate input costs, all of which tend to weigh on equity valuations.

## A Cautious Mood Across the Market

The session's losses were broad enough to offset any support from sectors that might otherwise have provided cushioning, reflecting a market that remains wary of external shocks even as domestic economic indicators have, at various points this year, offered reasons for optimism. Traders said the combination of rising energy costs and global geopolitical uncertainty has made investors more cautious about adding fresh positions, particularly in rate-sensitive sectors such as banking and financial services, which bore the brunt of Tuesday's selling.

## What It Means Going Forward

With crude oil prices showing little sign of easing amid ongoing tensions in the Gulf, market watchers expect volatility to persist in the near term, particularly for sectors most exposed to input cost pressures and interest rate expectations. Investors are likely to keep a close watch on global oil benchmarks and any fresh developments in the Middle East, given how directly these have translated into swings in Indian equity sentiment over the past several sessions. Barring a de-escalation in geopolitical tensions or a meaningful pullback in crude prices, analysts expect the current cautious, range-bound mood to continue weighing on the Sensex and Nifty in the sessions ahead.
