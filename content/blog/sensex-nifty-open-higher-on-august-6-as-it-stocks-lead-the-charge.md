Indian equity benchmarks opened Thursday's session, August 6, on a firmer note, with the BSE Sensex gaining around 201 points, or 0.25 percent, to start at 78,782.43, while the Nifty 50 added about 16 points to open near 24,641. The move followed a session in which the Sensex had closed at 78,581 and the Nifty at 24,624.65, and came against a backdrop of easing crude oil prices and cautious optimism around global cues.

## Technology Stocks Set the Pace

The standout theme in early trade was strength in information technology stocks, which helped the Nifty IT index extend a recovery that has been building through recent weeks. Frontline names including TCS, Infosys, HCLTech and Wipro featured among the gainers, continuing a pattern seen through late July and early August in which IT counters bounced back from a weaker stretch earlier in the year. The sector's rebound has been attributed to a mix of stabilizing earnings commentary and renewed interest in AI-related deal announcements from Indian IT majors, though the pace of gains has varied considerably from session to session.

## Broader Market Was Mixed

Even as the headline indices advanced, the picture underneath was less uniform. The BSE Midcap Select Index slipped modestly in early trade, while the BSE Smallcap Select Index added around 0.45 percent to trade near 9,008. That divergence is a reminder that a positive Sensex and Nifty print does not always mean broad-based buying — in this case, gains were concentrated more in large-cap technology names than spread evenly across market capitalizations.

## Foreign Investors Stayed Cautious

One notable undercurrent was the continued gap between foreign and domestic investor behavior. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers on August 5, offloading equities worth roughly Rs 943.42 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) went the other way, buying equities worth about Rs 2,883.17 crore on the same day. This pattern — domestic funds absorbing the supply that foreign investors are stepping back from — has been a recurring feature of Indian markets through much of 2026, and it has helped cushion the market against sharper foreign outflows.

## What It Means for Investors

Thursday's gap-up open extends a broadly constructive tone for Indian equities this week, but the underlying details are worth watching closely. A rally led narrowly by IT stocks, alongside continued FII selling offset by DII buying, suggests the market's resilience currently depends heavily on domestic liquidity rather than a uniform improvement in sentiment across all investor classes and sectors. Traders will likely watch whether the IT-led momentum broadens into other sectors, or whether the divergence between large-caps and the broader market persists through the rest of the week.
