Indian pole vaulter Baranica Elangovan created history on Friday at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, winning a bronze medal that makes her the country's first-ever Asian Games medallist in the event, across both men's and women's competition.

## How The Medal Was Won

Baranica cleared a height of 4.15 metres, matching Kazakhstan's Polina Ivanova, with both athletes then failing to clear the next height of 4.25 metres. Since the two were level on both their best height and number of attempts, they were awarded a shared bronze medal. Notably, Baranica's medal-winning height was below her personal best of 4.23m, suggesting there may be more to come from the 25-year-old as she continues to compete on the continental stage.

Japan's Misaki Morota won gold with a personal-best clearance of 4.55m, while China's Chunge Niu took silver with 4.50m, underlining the gap Indian pole vault still has to close against the region's established powers even as Baranica's medal marks a genuine breakthrough.

## Why This Medal Matters

Pole vault has historically been one of the weaker events for India at continental and global meets, with the technical demands of the discipline and limited specialised training infrastructure in the country often cited as barriers to producing competitive vaulters. A first-ever Asian Games medal in the event, achieved by a woman, is being seen within Indian athletics circles as a signal that grassroots investment in technical field events may be starting to pay off, alongside the country's more visible successes in track sprints, javelin and middle-distance running.

## Part Of A Strong Day For India

Baranica's medal came on a broader Day 6 of competition for India at the Games, which also featured a gold-medal-winning performance from the mixed 10m air pistol team of Kamaljeet and Suruchi Inder Singh, and a silver for distance runner Gulveer Singh in the men's 10,000m. India's shooting contingent has been especially productive through the Games, contributing the largest share of the country's medal tally across the ten sports it has featured in so far.

## What's Next

With the Asian Games still underway, Indian athletics officials will be watching to see whether Baranica can build on this result in future continental and world-level pole vault competitions, where the qualifying heights required for medals are considerably higher than what she cleared in Aichi-Nagoya. For now, though, the historic first stands as one of the more meaningful individual breakthroughs of India's campaign at these Games.
