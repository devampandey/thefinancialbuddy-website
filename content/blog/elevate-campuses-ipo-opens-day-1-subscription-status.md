Elevate Campuses, a Hillhouse Investment-backed operator of student housing and managed campus accommodation, opened its Rs 2,100-crore initial public offering on Wednesday, though early demand from investors was muted on the first day of bidding.

## Issue Details

The IPO has been priced in a band of Rs 343 to Rs 362 per share and will remain open for subscription through September 25. Based on the searches conducted, the basis of allotment is expected to be finalised on September 28, with shares tentatively scheduled to list on both the BSE and NSE on September 30. The size of the offer, at Rs 2,100 crore, places it among the more significant mid-sized issues to hit Indian primary markets this year, though it arrives in a period when the market's attention has been dominated by larger, more high-profile listings.

## Day One: Subscribed 0.21 Times

By 5:06 pm on the opening day, the issue had been subscribed 0.21 times overall, with bids received for about 67.95 lakh shares against roughly 3.19 crore shares on offer. Non-institutional investors led the demand at 0.32 times subscription, followed by qualified institutional buyers at 0.19 times, while the retail portion lagged at 0.14 times.

A slow first day is not unusual for IPOs of this size, since institutional investors and high-net-worth individuals frequently place the bulk of their bids closer to the closing date once they have a clearer read on overall demand trends and grey market activity. Retail investors, too, often wait until the final day to commit, which means day-one subscription figures are typically a weak predictor of how an issue ultimately closes.

## What Elevate Campuses Does

The company operates in the managed student housing and campus accommodation space, a segment that has drawn growing investor interest in India as higher education enrolment rises and organised, professionally managed housing options expand beyond traditional hostels and unregulated paying-guest accommodations. Backing from Hillhouse Investment, one of Asia's larger private equity and venture investors, lends the company a degree of institutional credibility as it seeks to convert that backing into public market support.

## What to Watch Next

With two more days of bidding left before the issue closes on September 25, the key question for the market is whether non-institutional and retail demand picks up meaningfully in the final stretch, and how qualified institutional buyers, who typically bid late, respond in the last day of the offer. A stronger finish would help gauge how appetite for newer, less widely known consumer-adjacent businesses compares with the demand seen in recent large-cap listings that have dominated headlines this IPO season.
