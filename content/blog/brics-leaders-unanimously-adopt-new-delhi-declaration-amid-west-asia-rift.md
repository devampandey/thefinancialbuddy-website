Leaders of the BRICS bloc unanimously adopted the New Delhi Declaration 2026 on Saturday, capping the first day of the 18th BRICS Summit with a consensus document that negotiators had struggled for days to finalize. Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the adoption at Bharat Mandapam, calling it a significant outcome for India's year-long chairship of the grouping.

## Bridging A Deep Divide

The path to consensus was not straightforward. Diplomats from the bloc's eleven members, including Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the UAE, spent the days leading up to the summit working to reconcile sharply different positions on West Asia, particularly between Iran and the United Arab Emirates over regional tensions. According to officials familiar with the drafting process, the final compromise text condemns unilateral warfare and stresses the need for conflict prevention without naming any specific country, a formulation designed to hold together a bloc whose members often sit on opposite sides of the same disputes.

## What The Declaration Says

The document, themed around India's 2026 chairship slogan of "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability," reiterates the group's commitment to resolving international disputes through dialogue, consultation and diplomacy rather than force. Beyond geopolitics, the declaration also touches on strengthening trade, energy and supply-chain resilience among member states, along with deeper cooperation on high technology and cross-border payment mechanisms, themes that have featured prominently in parallel discussions at the summit's business forum.

## A Diplomatic Win For India

For New Delhi, securing full consensus mattered as much as the content itself. Getting eleven governments with competing alignments and rival regional interests to sign onto a single text, especially one touching on live conflicts, was seen as a genuine test of India's ability to steer the bloc after its recent expansion beyond the original five members. Indian officials and commentators framed the adoption as evidence that BRICS can still function as a platform for dialogue even as skeptics have periodically questioned whether the grouping's growing and more divergent membership would make consensus impossible.

## The Wider Context

The declaration's adoption came on a day already dominated by summit diplomacy in the capital, with Chinese President Xi Jinping arriving for his first visit to India in nearly seven years and Russian President Vladimir Putin using the sidelines to warn European governments against deploying troops to Ukraine. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal separately told the BRICS Business Forum that the bloc's combined exports have surged to nearly six trillion dollars, now accounting for close to a quarter of global exports, underlining the economic weight member states hope the grouping can eventually translate into more unified positions on trade and technology as well as geopolitics.
