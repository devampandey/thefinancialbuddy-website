The single biggest input into Indian equities this week is not an earnings report or a rate decision. It is the price of a barrel of Brent crude, and on Monday morning it fell. That was enough to lift the benchmarks more than 1% in the first hour of trade, even after a punishing Friday on Wall Street.

## Oil gives back part of last week's spike

Brent crude dropped about 2.7% to roughly $110.4 a barrel in early Asian trade, and US benchmark WTI slipped to around $101. Brent had climbed above $118 last week as the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz deepened. The relief came from the diplomatic track: Iran's foreign minister has outlined a two-day plan, discussed with Qatari and Pakistani mediators, under which Washington would meet a list of conditions on day one and the strait would reopen on day two. US President Donald Trump also repeated that the conflict would end soon.

The pullback is fragile. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday evening that two tankers, one of them a supertanker, struck mines while using a route Tehran had not approved, a claim that has not been independently confirmed. Crude is still up sharply over the past three months, and for India, which imports most of its oil, every $5 swing changes the outlook for inflation, the rupee and company margins.

## The numbers at the open

The Nifty 50 opened at 23,222.30, up 169 points from Friday's close of 23,053.10, while the Sensex opened 719 points higher at 75,178.24. Buying built from there. By 9:32 am the Nifty was up 1.07% at 23,302.75, and by mid-morning the Sensex was about 1,050 points higher near 75,500, with the Nifty around 23,340.

Participation among large-caps was broad. Forty-one of the 50 Nifty stocks were in the green in early trade. Reliance Industries led with a gain of around 5% to 6% as investors reacted to its quarterly numbers ([see our earlier coverage](/business/reliance-q2-profit-jumps-41-to-rs-25-548-crore-as-refining-carries-the-quarter-a)), followed by NTPC and Tata Steel, each up about 3.3%, Max Healthcare up 3.1%, Maruti Suzuki up 2.2% and Coal India up about 2%. Apollo Hospitals and Sun Pharma were the main drags, each down a little over 1%.

## Where the rally ran thin

The more telling detail sat beneath the headline indices. The Nifty Next 50 rose about 1.2%, but the Nifty Smallcap 100 was up only around 0.2% and the Smallcap 50 barely 0.16% in the first hour. Money went to the largest, most liquid names first, which is typical of a relief rally driven by one macro variable rather than a broad change in risk appetite.

India VIX, the market's fear gauge, also rose rather than fell, climbing above 15.5 even as the Nifty gained more than 1%. A rising VIX on an up day suggests traders were buying protection into the rally, not chasing it. With the US midterm elections due on Tuesday, a Federal Reserve that has kept a December hike on the table, and the 10-year US Treasury yield still near 4.9%, there are several events this week that could reverse Monday's mood quickly.

Friday's US session adds to the caution. The Nasdaq fell 2.86% and the S&P 500 lost 1.82% in a technology-led sell-off, yet Indian IT stocks held up, with Nifty IT up about 0.6% early on Monday.

## What to watch

For the rest of the session, the key test is whether the Nifty can hold above 23,300 if crude stabilises, and whether smallcaps join in. A rally that stays concentrated in a handful of heavyweights while volatility rises tends to be fragile. If Hormuz talks stall and Brent heads back toward $115, oil-sensitive sectors such as oil marketing companies, airlines and paint makers would be the first to feel it.
