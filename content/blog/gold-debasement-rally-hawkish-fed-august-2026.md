August 2026 was a month of two markets. For its first three weeks, a revived "debasement trade" — built on worries about America's fiscal trajectory and the Treasury's decision to double its long-bond buyback operations — sent gold and silver into their sharpest monthly rally since January. Then, in the final week, Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish turn at the Jackson Hole symposium collided with a fresh flare-up between the US and Iran near the Strait of Hormuz, and the story flipped: rate-hike odds jumped, oil climbed, and much of the metals rally unwound. Almost every market covered below — from the dollar to Indian equities — traces back to that single pivot.

## Global Markets

US equities spent most of August grinding higher, with the S&P 500 touching a closing high near 7,799 around mid-month before Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks introduced two-way risk into the final week. The index still finished the month up roughly 2.5%, one of its steadier months of the year, even as trading turned choppier once markets began pricing in a near coin-flip chance of a September rate hike, up from about one-third before his speech.

Japan's Nikkei 225 had a rockier ride, climbing through the middle weeks before tumbling more than 2% in a single session on August 30 as the same rate-hike repricing hit risk assets broadly. It recovered most of that loss within a day, ending the month essentially flat. In Europe, the FTSE 100 and Germany's DAX both drifted modestly higher through August, comparatively insulated from the Fed-driven swings that dominated US and Japanese trading in the final week.

## Currency

The US Dollar Index spent the first half of August sliding as the debasement trade took hold — investors reasoning that a Treasury actively intervening in the bond market to cap long-term yields was, in effect, a form of currency debasement. That logic reversed sharply after Jackson Hole: the dollar climbed back through the last ten days of the month as rate-hike bets firmed, ending August roughly half a percent higher than where it started, well off its intra-month peak above 101.

The Indian rupee was comparatively insulated from the swings, trading in a narrow band through most of the month. The Reserve Bank of India's intervention, together with softer crude prices in the first half of August, helped offset the pressure from a stronger dollar later on. The rupee touched a multi-week high in the final days of the month as crude eased slightly off its highs.

## Commodities

Precious metals were the month's defining story. Gold gained roughly 9% and silver surged more than 15% through most of August, with silver briefly touching $68.63 an ounce in the final week — both moves attributed by analysts to a resurgence of the "debasement trade" after the Treasury announced it would more than double the size of its liquidity-support buybacks for longer-dated bonds. As TD Securities' Bart Melek put it, "market participants believe the government bond market interference may get even more aggressive."

That rally hit a wall in the final week of August. Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks, followed almost immediately by a fresh US military strike near the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian retaliation against targets in the UAE and Jordan, pushed Brent crude up roughly 9% for the month and revived fears that energy-driven inflation could force the Fed's hand. Gold slipped back from its highs to close the month up about 9% rather than the 15%-plus it had touched mid-month, while silver gave back a similar share of its gains. As MKS PAMP's Nicky Shiels noted, the dynamic pit "a tightening bias" against "a loosening bias, aimed at the same curve" — a tension that is likely to keep defining precious metals trading into September.

## Indian Indices & Flows

The Nifty 50 and Sensex snapped a two-month winning streak, falling 1.2% and 1.5% respectively in August as high crude prices and renewed geopolitical tensions weighed on sentiment. The index traded in its tightest monthly range in nearly a year, with banks and IT holding up relatively better than metals, telecom and FMCG. Sectoral divergence was sharp beneath the surface: names like Eternal, Jio Financial Services and Trent posted double-digit monthly gains, while Asian Paints, NTPC and Kotak Mahindra Bank were among the index's biggest laggards.

Institutional flows stayed a bright spot even as the index fell. Foreign portfolio investors were net buyers for a second consecutive month, deploying roughly ₹20,000 crore through primary and secondary market investments, while domestic institutional investors continued their steady buying throughout August. The primary market was unusually busy alongside this: August saw 21 mainboard IPOs raise about ₹21,000 crore, the highest monthly deal count of 2026 so far. India VIX stayed contained for most of the month, dipping below 11 in the third week, before edging back up as Jackson Hole and Hormuz-driven volatility crept back in.

On the policy front, the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee held the repo rate at 5.25% for a fourth consecutive review while raising its FY27 growth forecast to 6.7%, and July retail inflation cooled to a comfortable 4.45%. The NSE also rolled out a new closing auction session during the month, a mechanism that came under scrutiny after producing unusually sharp swings during the Sensex's August derivatives expiry.

## What to Watch Next Month

The central question heading into September is whether Warsh's hawkish framing at Jackson Hole marks a genuine shift toward tightening, or whether softer incoming data pulls the Fed back toward its more familiar cutting path — the answer will likely set the tone for gold, the dollar, and risk assets broadly. Watch whether the Strait of Hormuz situation escalates further or cools, since Brent crude's direction from here is now doing double duty as both an inflation signal and a geopolitical risk gauge. Domestically, keep an eye on whether FII buying can be sustained through any global volatility, and whether the NSE's new closing auction mechanism draws further regulatory attention after its bumpy debut month.
