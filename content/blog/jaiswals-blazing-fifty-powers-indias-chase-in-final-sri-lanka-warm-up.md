India's red-ball squad wrapped up its final hit-out before the Test series against Sri Lanka with an aggressive batting display on the third and final day of the three-day practice match at the Nondescripts Cricket Club Ground in Colombo on Sunday. Opener Yashasvi Jaiswal set the tone with a blistering 61 off just 46 balls before retiring hurt, propelling India to a rapid start in their chase of a 201-run target set by Sri Lanka Cricket XI.

## A Tune-Up Built Around Padikkal and Jadeja

The warm-up game had already produced plenty of talking points before Sunday's play. Sri Lanka XI posted 363 for 8 on day one, with India's spin trio of Ravindra Jadeja, Kuldeep Yadav and Manav Suthar sharing the wickets. India's reply was built around an outstanding unbeaten 142 from Devdutt Padikkal, who anchored the innings after Jaiswal fell for a golden duck early in the chase for parity. Jadeja's own half-century added depth to India's total as the tourists closed their first innings just short of Sri Lanka XI's score, keeping the contest finely balanced heading into the final day.

## Sri Lanka XI Set a Target, India Respond in a Hurry

Resuming their second innings on day three, Sri Lanka XI battled through the morning and post-lunch sessions before Ravindra Jadeja again made inroads with the ball, finishing with figures of 2 for 33. Nishan Madushka top-scored for the hosts with a well-made half-century, while contributions from the middle order helped Sri Lanka XI move to a declared total of 200 for 6 in 45 overs, leaving India needing 201 for an outright win in their final warm-up fixture.

India's top order responded aggressively. Jaiswal raced to fifty inside the first ten overs of the chase, finding the boundary rope repeatedly against the new ball, while captain Shubman Gill played the anchor role at the other end. Jaiswal eventually retired hurt after his 46-ball 61, with India well past the hundred mark and requiring just over a hundred more runs with all ten wickets in hand as the chase progressed through the afternoon session.

## Selection Headaches Before the Test Series

Beyond the runs on the board, the practice match doubled as an audition for places in India's Test line-up. Rishabh Pant and Dhruv Jurel, both in contention for the wicketkeeping role, had modest returns with the bat across the fixture, while Jaiswal and Gill's form at the top of the order will offer the team management reassurance ahead of the series opener. The tour comes at a sensitive time for India's bowling attack, with the squad already missing several frontline options through injury, prompting a fresh Test call-up for uncapped quick Auqib Nabi.

With the warm-up fixture serving its purpose of sharpening both batting and bowling combinations, India's focus now shifts fully to preparations for the opening Test against Sri Lanka, where the runs and rhythm found in Colombo this week will be closely watched for signs of the team's best available eleven.
