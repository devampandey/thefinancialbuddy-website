A senior adviser to the commander of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has signalled that Tehran is seriously considering a strategy of deliberately extending its ongoing conflict with the United States until President Donald Trump's term in office ends, in some of the most explicit comments yet on Iran's long-term war calculus.

## What Was Said

Mohammad Reza Naqdi, a senior IRGC adviser, told PBS NewsHour that prolonging the war and "causing attrition" was one option Tehran was actively weighing, arguing that a drawn-out conflict would send a message to any future US administration that attacking Iran carries a lasting cost. He framed the approach as a deterrence strategy rather than a purely military one, suggesting that inflicting sustained pain on the United States now would make future leaders "think twice" before launching similar action against Iran.

Naqdi also asserted that Iran currently views itself as prevailing in the conflict, even though it has not achieved what he described as its ultimate objective. He credited the war with giving Iranian forces valuable combat experience against the US military, which he characterised as less formidable in practice than widely assumed.

## Claims on Missile Production

Perhaps the most striking element of Naqdi's remarks concerned Iran's missile stockpile. He claimed that Iranian factories are producing more missiles each day than are currently being fired, with manufacturing deliberately spread across multiple sites to make the supply chain harder to disrupt through strikes. On that basis, he suggested Iran could sustain missile launches for years if the conflict continued at its current pace.

In a more ambiguous warning, Naqdi said that if Iran ever did exhaust its missile arsenal, the country would become "even more dangerous" for the United States, pointing to America's extensive economic interests around the world as potential points of leverage. He did not elaborate on specific targets or the methods Iran might use, leaving the remark open to interpretation but adding to the uncertainty already surrounding the conflict's trajectory.

## Why It Matters

The comments arrive as the broader US-Iran conflict continues to weigh on global energy markets. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most critical oil chokepoints, has remained sharply reduced for weeks, and the International Energy Agency has warned that the world is drawing down oil stockpiles at a rapid pace as a result. Brent crude has been trading in the high-$80s per barrel range, with prices ticking higher on repeated occasions as traders track the on-again, off-again diplomatic efforts between Washington, Tehran, and regional intermediaries including Oman.

Naqdi's remarks suggest Iranian officials do not see a near-term resolution as likely, and that Tehran may be prepared to treat the conflict as a long-run war of attrition rather than a short, containable exchange. For markets and governments watching the Gulf region, that raises the prospect of continued disruption to shipping lanes and sustained upward pressure on crude prices, even as diplomatic channels remain technically open.

The remarks have been picked up by multiple international outlets covering the conflict, underscoring how closely Iran's public messaging is being watched for signs of where the war may be headed next.
