Logistics services provider Delhivery reported a sharp decline in quarterly profit even as its top line kept growing, in results announced on Saturday for the first quarter of FY27.

## The Numbers

Consolidated net profit fell 64.9% year-on-year to Rs 32 crore, down from Rs 91 crore in the same quarter last year. The drop came despite healthy business momentum: revenue from operations rose 27.8% to Rs 2,931 crore for the April-June period, up from Rs 2,294 crore a year earlier.

Total expenses grew even faster than revenue, climbing 29% year-on-year to a little over Rs 3,011 crore, which squeezed the bottom line even as the top line expanded. On a more encouraging note, earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation rose to Rs 214 crore from Rs 119 crore a year ago, pushing the EBITDA margin up to 7.5% from 5.4% previously, suggesting the underlying operating business is becoming more efficient even if that hasn't yet translated into stronger net profit.

## Why the Profit Slipped

In a letter to shareholders accompanying the results, the company pointed to a bruising operating environment during the quarter. Delhivery cited volatile labour availability linked to state elections, climate-related disruptions that affected delivery networks, geopolitical uncertainty, and changes to labour codes as key headwinds. To keep its network running smoothly through these disruptions, the company said it had to deploy buffer staff and additional network capacity, both of which added to costs during the quarter.

## Market Reaction

Delhivery shares closed flat on the BSE on Friday ahead of the results, ending the session at Rs 471.10, up a modest 0.26%. The stock has had a rough stretch recently, down roughly 9% over the past month, though it remains up about 1% over the past year. Its 52-week range spans from a low of Rs 374.40 to a high of Rs 524.25, underlining how volatile sentiment around the logistics player has been even as its underlying revenue growth has stayed consistently strong.

## The Bigger Picture

Delhivery's results land at a moment when India's logistics sector is contending with rising e-commerce volumes on one hand and rising cost pressures, from fuel and labour to compliance, on the other. The company's ability to grow revenue by nearly 28% while still expanding EBITDA margin suggests its underlying delivery network is scaling efficiently, even though one-off disruptions during the quarter took a visible bite out of reported profit.

Investors and analysts will likely watch the September quarter closely to see whether the labour and weather-related disruptions that weighed on Q1 prove temporary, and whether the improved EBITDA margin can eventually flow through to net profit as the festive shopping season approaches later in the year.
