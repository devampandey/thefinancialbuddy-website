Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply lower on Friday, reversing the previous session's gains, as a fresh surge in crude oil prices and rising bond yields rattled investor sentiment across Dalal Street. The Sensex tumbled as much as 700 points in early trade before paring some losses, while the Nifty 50 slipped below the psychologically important 23,250 mark.

## The Numbers

By mid-morning trade, the Sensex was down more than 600 points, or roughly 0.8%, hovering near 74,280, while the Nifty 50 had shed close to 220 points, or about 0.9%, to trade around 23,260. The broader market bore the brunt of the selloff, with the Nifty MidCap 100 and Nifty SmallCap indices both falling more than 1.4%, signalling that the pullback was not confined to large-cap stocks alone. Metal and financial counters led the declines, with Hindalco Industries, Tata Steel and Bajaj Finance among the top laggards on the Nifty 50.

## What's Driving The Selloff

The proximate trigger was a sharp jump in crude oil prices, with Brent crude climbing to around $108 a barrel, its highest level in roughly four months, as escalating tensions in West Asia continued to disrupt global energy corridors and prompt fresh regional military mobilisation. For India, which imports the vast majority of its crude needs, a sustained rise in oil prices has an outsized impact on the trade deficit, inflation trajectory and, ultimately, corporate earnings across energy-intensive sectors.

Compounding the pressure, the yield on the benchmark 10-year government bond edged up to around 4.96%, inching closer to the widely watched 5% threshold that many market participants view as a potential inflection point for equity valuations globally. Higher yields tend to make fixed-income instruments relatively more attractive versus equities and raise borrowing costs for companies, both of which weigh on stock prices.

The rupee also came under pressure through the session, weakening against the US dollar before recovering some ground, as importers stepped up dollar demand to cover costlier energy bills. A weaker currency in turn adds to imported inflation risks, feeding back into the same set of concerns pressuring bond yields and equities.

## The Broader Backdrop

Friday's decline comes against a backdrop of already elevated geopolitical risk, with ongoing tensions in the Middle East continuing to reverberate through global energy and financial markets. Thursday's session had seen the Sensex and Nifty close modestly higher, with strength in financial stocks helping offset similar global headwinds, but Friday's price action suggests investors are growing warier as crude prices push toward levels last seen months ago.

Market participants will now be watching whether crude oil prices stabilise or extend their climb, given the direct read-through to India's macroeconomic outlook. A sustained period of elevated oil prices, combined with firming bond yields, would complicate the environment for Indian equities just as the economy heads into a busy earnings and events calendar, including this weekend's BRICS Summit in New Delhi.
