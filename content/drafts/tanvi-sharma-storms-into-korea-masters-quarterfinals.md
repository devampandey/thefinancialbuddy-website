India's campaign at the Korea Masters 2026 badminton tournament produced a mixed day on Thursday, with rising star Tanvi Sharma booking her place in the women's singles quarterfinals while compatriot Anmol Kharb was knocked out in the second round.

## Tanvi Sharma Wins All-Indian Battle

Sharma's route to the quarterfinals ran through an all-Indian pre-quarterfinal clash against Shriyanshi Valishetty, and it proved to be anything but straightforward. Sharma edged the contest 21-16, 17-21, 21-13, needing all three games to see off her compatriot. The decider was the most telling stretch of the match: Sharma built an 11-9 lead at the mid-game interval and then pulled away decisively, conceding just four more points as she closed out the win in 57 minutes.

The victory extends what has been a strong week for the young shuttler, who arrived in South Korea in good form and has now backed that up with results on court. With this win, Sharma joined two other Indians, Ashmita Chaliha and Rakshitha Ramraj, in the women's singles quarterfinals, giving India a solid presence in the last eight of the draw.

## Anmol Kharb's Campaign Ends

The day was less kind to Anmol Kharb, who lost her second-round match in three games to bow out of the tournament. Kharb, who has been one of the more closely watched young talents in Indian badminton over the past couple of seasons, will look to regroup and carry lessons from this defeat into her next tournaments as she continues building toward the bigger events later in the season.

Her exit means the Indian contingent's hopes in the women's singles draw now rest with Sharma, Chaliha and Ramraj as the tournament moves into its business end.

## Tournament Context

The Korea Masters, part of the BWF World Tour Super 300 circuit, is running from August 4 to 9 and has featured a strong showing from the Indian badminton contingent across categories through the week. Earlier in the tournament, India also saw exits and progressions across the men's and women's draws, reflecting the depth, and unpredictability, of the current Indian shuttler pool on the international circuit.

For Sharma, reaching the quarterfinals sets up a tougher test as she moves further into the draw against more experienced international opposition. Indian fans will be watching closely to see whether she can carry her current form deeper into the tournament, especially after having already shown the composure to close out a tricky three-game match against a fellow countrywoman under pressure.

The results also serve as a reminder of how competitive India's current crop of young women shuttlers has become, with several players now capable of pushing deep into the draw at international Super 300 and higher-tier events, even when that means facing each other in early rounds, as Sharma and Valishetty did on Thursday.
