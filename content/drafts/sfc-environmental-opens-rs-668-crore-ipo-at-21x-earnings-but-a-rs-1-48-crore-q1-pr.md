SFC Environmental Technologies, a Navi Mumbai company that designs, builds and runs wastewater and solid-waste treatment plants, opened its initial public offering on Tuesday. Shares are priced at Rs 313 to Rs 330, with a minimum lot of 45 shares, so a retail investor needs about Rs 14,850 to apply at the top of the band. Bidding closes on Thursday, October 29. Allotment is expected on October 30 and the shares are scheduled to list on the NSE and BSE on November 3.

The issue is worth about Rs 668 crore. Only Rs 150 crore of that is fresh capital. The remaining Rs 518 crore or so is an offer for sale of roughly 1.57 crore shares by existing holders, and that money goes to them, not to the company.

## Where the company's share of the money goes

SFC plans to use its Rs 150 crore mainly to repay debt held by the parent and a subsidiary, with the rest going to working capital and general corporate purposes. Nothing is set aside for new plants or acquisitions. That is a balance-sheet clean-up more than a growth raise, and the working capital line matters more than it looks, for reasons covered below.

On Monday the company raised a little over Rs 200 crore from anchor investors at Rs 330 a share. The anchor book included SBI Mutual Fund, Nippon India, ICICI Prudential, Sundaram, Quant and Societe Generale. Half of the net offer is reserved for qualified institutional buyers, 15% for non-institutional investors and 35% for retail.

## The valuation question

For FY26, SFC reported a net profit of about Rs 160 crore on revenue of about Rs 821 crore. That is a net margin of close to 20%, which is high for an engineering and contracting business. At the top of the price band the company is valued at around Rs 3,235 crore after the issue, or roughly 20 to 21 times FY26 earnings. SBI Securities called that a reasonable discount to listed peers. BP Equities put the multiple at about 19.9 times against an industry average of 18.2 times, so slightly richer than the sector.

The June 2026 quarter tells a different story. SFC earned just Rs 1.48 crore in profit on revenue of Rs 137.9 crore. Some of that reflects seasonality, since project work and billing in water infrastructure usually bunch up in the second half of the fiscal year. Even so, Religare Broking pointed out that if you annualise the latest quarter, the stock looks very expensive. Anyone applying is effectively betting that FY26 margins come back over the rest of FY27.

## Working capital is the real risk

The more lasting concern is cash. Religare noted that SFC's cash conversion cycle has stretched to about 242 days in FY26 from 156 days in FY24, meaning it now takes about eight months to turn its spending on a project into cash. SBI Securities flagged the same issue, linking high receivable days to the company's heavy reliance on government and municipal contracts.

On the positive side, SFC makes key parts of its treatment systems in-house and has exclusive technology arrangements with DuPont for ultrafiltration and other advanced treatment. Its newer water recycling and reuse business, which won its first order only in July 2025, had built an order book of about Rs 561 crore by August 31, 2026.

## What the grey market and brokers say

Unofficial grey market premiums have moved between about Rs 20 and Rs 32 over the past week, pointing to a modest listing gain of roughly 6% to 10%. SMIFS, BP Equities and Ventura recommend subscribing, while SBI Securities and Religare are neutral. BLS Polymers' smaller Rs 255 crore issue opens the same day and has drawn a much higher grey market premium, so the two will compete for the same pool of retail money this week.

For long-term investors the decision comes down to whether SFC can turn a strong order book into cash faster than it has so far.
