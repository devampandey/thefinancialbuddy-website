Indian equity benchmarks closed higher on Wednesday, with a sharp rally in metal stocks doing the heavy lifting even as IT counters continued to drag on sentiment for a second straight session.

## Metals Lead, IT Lags

The Nifty Metal index jumped over 2% during the session, making it the standout sector of the day. Tata Steel was among the biggest movers, gaining more than 3% on heavy trading volumes of over 4 crore shares, as steel and metal counters broadly outperformed the rest of the market. IT stocks told the opposite story: the Nifty IT index slipped over 1%, extending a recent stretch of underperformance in the sector that has been weighed down by concerns over demand trends and near-term earnings visibility at large exporters.

Pharma and FMCG stocks added modest support to the day's gains, while broader markets stayed constructive, with both the midcap and smallcap indices posting gains broadly in line with the benchmarks.

## Domestic Buyers Outweigh Foreign Selling

Foreign institutional investors were net sellers on the day, offloading close to Rs 3,810 crore worth of Indian equities, according to provisional data. Domestic institutional investors more than absorbed that selling, buying roughly Rs 4,120 crore worth of shares — a pattern that has repeated through much of September as domestic mutual funds and insurers continue to deploy steady inflows even when foreign flows turn negative.

## What's Driving the Rotation

The rotation out of IT and into metals reflects a broader theme playing out in Indian markets this month: investors have been favouring cyclical, domestically-driven sectors over export-heavy IT names, which remain sensitive to US demand signals and currency swings. Metal stocks, in particular, have benefited from firmer global commodity prices and expectations of steady domestic infrastructure and construction demand heading into the festive season.

## Why It Matters

Wednesday's session is a reminder that headline index moves can mask sharply divergent stories underneath — a trader looking only at the Sensex and Nifty closing numbers would miss that IT investors had a rough day while metal and steel investors had a strong one. With India's primary market also active this week through fresh IPO listings, sustained DII buying continues to act as a cushion against foreign outflows, a dynamic that has underpinned much of the market's resilience through 2026.
