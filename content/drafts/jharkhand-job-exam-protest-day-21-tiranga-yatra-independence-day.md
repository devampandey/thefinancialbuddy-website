The students' protest against alleged irregularities in Jharkhand's recruitment examinations entered its 21st day on Friday, with demonstrators announcing plans to take out a "Tiranga Yatra" on August 15 to mark the country's 80th Independence Day, signalling no early end to one of the state's longest-running youth agitations in recent years.

## A Movement That Has Outlasted Government Assurances

The protest, organised under the banner of the JPSC-JSSC Reforms Manch, began over allegations of large-scale irregularities in examinations conducted by the Jharkhand Public Service Commission and the Jharkhand Staff Selection Commission. Demonstrators are demanding greater transparency in the recruitment process, cancellation of several disputed tests, and an independent investigation either by the CBI or a panel of retired judges from outside the state.

Chief Minister Hemant Soren has repeatedly said his government is committed to reforming the examination process and has assured protesters that the 14th JPSC examination, at the centre of the irregularity allegations, will be cancelled. He has also accused the opposition BJP of trying to exploit the agitation for political gain. The BJP, in turn, has alleged the state government is attempting to suppress the movement, while the Congress, an ally in Jharkhand's ruling coalition, has accused the BJP of politicising the protest and bussing in outside workers to disrupt it.

## Friday's Flashpoint: The Independence Day March

Protesters said the planned Tiranga Yatra on August 15 is intended to inject fresh momentum into the movement and cautioned the Ranchi district administration against taking action over an FIR already lodged in connection with violence during an August 10 march toward the state assembly. That FIR, filed against roughly 300 unidentified people, relates to alleged breach of prohibitory orders, damage to government property and attacks on police personnel. Fourteen police personnel were injured in clashes that day, after which police used batons, water cannons and tear gas to disperse the crowd. Police said no arrests had been made as of Friday and that video footage was still being examined to identify those involved in the violence.

Manch leader Ravindra Paswan appealed to the state government not to target genuine students even as it pursues the case against unidentified individuals.

## Health Concerns and a Widening Probe

Concern has also grown over the health of Jharkhand Loktantrik Krantikari Morcha leader Devendra Nath Mahto, who has been on a hunger strike for 13 days and was hospitalised earlier in the week after his condition deteriorated. Four other protesters on hunger strike are also receiving treatment. Separately, the state's Criminal Investigation Department has carried out raids at multiple locations to execute arrest warrants in cases linked to alleged examination irregularities, with roughly 20 people, including a former JPSC chairperson, arrested so far in the broader probe.

Former chief minister Raghubar Das has threatened to begin his own hunger strike if the demand for a CBI-led investigation is not met within a week, while the RSS-affiliated Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad has announced protests at more than 1,200 universities nationwide, questioning the credibility of the state-appointed special investigation team over what it called repeated changes in its composition.

With Independence Day falling on Saturday and the protest movement showing no signs of losing steam three weeks in, the standoff has become a significant political flashpoint in Jharkhand, testing both the state government's handling of youth unemployment grievances and the opposition's ability to convert the unrest into broader political capital ahead of future elections.
