India's road to the 2027 men's ODI World Cup now has dates on it. The International Cricket Council released the full schedule on Thursday at a ceremony in Cape Town marking one year to go, and it hands India a heavyweight start: defending champions Australia at Newlands on October 7, 2027, followed by Pakistan at Johannesburg's Wanderers Stadium on October 10.

The opener is a rematch of the 2023 final in Ahmedabad, which Australia won. The India-Pakistan game, as usual, is expected to be the most sought-after ticket of the group stage.

## A longer, three-stage format

The tournament will be co-hosted by South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia and will run from October 2 to November 21, 2027. It features 14 teams, 57 matches and 12 venues: eight in South Africa, three in Zimbabwe and one in Namibia. It is the first men's World Cup in Africa since 2003 and the first time Namibia hosts matches at the senior event.

The format has three rounds before the knockouts:

- **Super Series (Windhoek, October 2, 4 and 6):** the three lowest-ranked qualifiers play a round-robin, and only the top side advances.
- **Group Stage:** two groups of six. Group A has India, Australia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, co-hosts Zimbabwe and a qualifier. Group B has New Zealand, South Africa, Sri Lanka, England, Bangladesh and a qualifier. The top three in each group, plus the better fourth-placed side, move on.
- **Super 7 (October 25 to November 14):** a full round-robin in which each team plays six more games. Points from the group stage are not carried forward.

The top four after the Super 7 reach the semi-finals in Cape Town (November 17) and Centurion (November 18), both with reserve days. The final is in Johannesburg on November 21, with November 22 kept in reserve.

## India's group-stage path

After Australia and Pakistan, India are scheduled to play Afghanistan in Tshwane on October 14 and the Group A qualifier in Bloemfontein on October 17. They close the group stage against Zimbabwe on October 24 in the first World Cup match at the new Mosi-oa-Tunya Stadium near Victoria Falls.

## Why seeding matters

To give fans and broadcasters more certainty, the ICC has pre-assigned Super 7 slots. India are A1, Australia A2, Pakistan A3, England AB4, New Zealand B1, South Africa B2 and Sri Lanka B3. If an unseeded team finishes above a seeded one, it simply takes over that slot and its fixtures.

On the current seeding, India and Pakistan would meet a second time in the Super 7, in Cape Town on November 7. India's Super 7 run would also include another meeting with Australia, at Victoria Falls on October 28.

## Tickets go to a ballot

Ticket registration opened on Thursday through a ballot rather than a first-come, first-served sale. Entry is free, fans can enter once per person per match, and the window stays open until 23:59 South African time on November 21, 2026. Where demand outstrips supply, buyers will be picked at random and contacted by email.

The ICC put prices at roughly $9.50 (about R158) at the entry level up to around $30 (about R514) for the biggest matches, with family bundles, student and pensioner rates and local early-bird allocations also planned. ICC chairman Jay Shah said the governing body wants the event to stay affordable for families and local communities.

## How qualification worked

The cut-off for direct qualification was September 30, 2026. Ten teams, including hosts South Africa and Zimbabwe, earned direct places. The remaining four spots will come from the World Cup Qualifier: its winner goes straight into the group stage, while the next three enter the Super Series in Windhoek.

For Indian fans planning travel, the opening week offers the clearest picture: two of the tournament's biggest games within four days, in Cape Town and Johannesburg.
