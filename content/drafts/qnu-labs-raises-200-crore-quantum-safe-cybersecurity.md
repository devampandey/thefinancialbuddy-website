QNu Labs, a Bengaluru-based deep-tech firm building quantum-safe cybersecurity technology, has raised Rs 200 crore in a Series A1 funding round led by India's National Quantum Mission and Speciale Invest, with participation from Sony Innovation Fund, Gaja Capital and Artha Ventures.

## Funding to Back R&D and Global Expansion

The round takes QNu Labs' total capital raised to Rs 375 crore and will be used to accelerate research and development, deepen the company's technology leadership, and scale its sales and go-to-market operations across India and international markets. A portion of the funds will support the company's contribution to a government-backed project building the technology backbone of India's Quantum Secure and Sensing Networks initiative. The company also plans to use the capital to expand into quantum sensing and artificial intelligence, extending its existing quantum-safe technology stack.

Incubated at IIT Madras in 2016 by Srinivasa Rao Aluri and Sunil Gupta, QNu Labs has grown into a team of around 160 engineers, physicists and mathematicians, and says it has already been deployed across government, critical infrastructure and banking, financial services and insurance (BFSI) clients. Co-founder Sunil Gupta said India has moved early in the quantum era, with the National Quantum Mission's IIT Madras hub laying the groundwork and 2026 implementation timelines now shifting the technology from policy discussion to commercial deployment.

## Investors Point to a Widening Threat Window

The investment lands as governments and enterprises worldwide brace for a transition to quantum-safe infrastructure, with India's National Quantum Mission setting an aggressive 2029 timeline. The concern driving the shift is that sufficiently powerful quantum computers, combined with advances in artificial intelligence, could eventually break the encryption standards that currently protect most digital communication and financial data, making early migration to quantum-resistant systems a priority for sensitive sectors.

Gaja Capital co-founder Gopal Jain described quantum-safe security as becoming "national infrastructure," comparing its importance to power and transport in a digital economy, while Speciale Invest's Vishesh Rajaram said the firm's conviction in the space, first backed five years ago, is now "playing out at scale" as governments and enterprises race to prepare. Sony Innovation Fund's India head, Yoko Fukata, said the fund remains focused on India's fast-growing deep-tech ecosystem.

## Building a Case for Sovereign Technology

Executives and investors alike framed the round as part of a broader argument for India to develop homegrown, globally competitive quantum technology rather than depend on foreign providers for critical security infrastructure. Roughly 80 percent of the new round came from investors participating in QNu Labs for the first time, according to the company, a signal it says reflects growing international confidence in India's quantum-safe capabilities as the sector moves from research labs toward real-world, revenue-generating deployment.
