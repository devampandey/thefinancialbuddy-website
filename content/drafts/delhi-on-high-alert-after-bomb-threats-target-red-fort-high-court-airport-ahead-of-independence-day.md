Delhi's security apparatus was thrown into high alert on Friday after a series of email bomb threats targeted some of the capital's most sensitive locations just a day before India marks its 80th Independence Day. The threats named the Red Fort, the Delhi High Court, Indira Gandhi International Airport's Terminal 3, Delhi Metro lines and trains arriving from Ambala, along with several government offices.

The Delhi High Court reported receiving a threatening email warning of a bomb blast timed for 2:11 PM on August 15 — the day Prime Minister Narendra Modi is scheduled to address the nation from the Red Fort ramparts. The message invoked "Khalistan" and referenced the 1984 anti-Sikh riots, alleging that the court had failed to deliver justice in cases connected to that violence.

## Security Agencies Move Quickly

Delhi Police activated bomb disposal squads, sniffer dog units and fire services within minutes of the threats surfacing, launching extensive search operations across the affected sites, including a sweep of the High Court complex. As of Friday evening, security agencies said they had found no explosives or suspicious objects at any of the locations named in the threats, and officials cautioned that there was no evidence so far confirming the threats were genuine.

Even so, authorities were taking no chances given the timing. Independence Day security in the capital had already been elevated in the days leading up to the event, with roughly 20,000 personnel deployed around Delhi, anti-drone shields installed near the Red Fort, and close to 1,000 CCTV cameras providing round-the-clock surveillance of the venue and its surroundings. More than 20,000 people are reported to have been screened as part of pre-event verification drives.

## Nationwide Vigilance

The alert in Delhi came alongside heightened security elsewhere in the country. In Jammu, police stepped up patrols and vehicle checks across border districts, highways and other sensitive locations as part of standard precautions ahead of the national holiday. Similar patterns of intensified checks at entry and exit points and increased vigilance at high-footfall public spaces were reported across multiple states.

This is not the first time threats have been directed at high-security sites in the capital around a major national occasion, and past experience has generally shown such email-based threats to be hoaxes intended to create panic rather than credible plots. Even so, security officials have stressed that every such threat is treated with full seriousness until search operations are conclusively completed, particularly given the scale of the public gathering expected at the Red Fort for Saturday's ceremony.

## What Happens Next

With Independence Day celebrations set to proceed as planned, all eyes will be on whether investigators can trace the origin of the threatening emails in the coming days. Cybercrime units are expected to examine the digital trail behind the messages, a process that in similar past cases has taken anywhere from a few days to several weeks. For now, the massive security deployment across Delhi is expected to remain in place through the Independence Day weekend, with officials indicating that vigilance will not be scaled back until celebrations conclude without incident.

The episode underscores the recurring challenge Indian security agencies face in balancing large-scale public celebrations with credible-sounding but often unverified threats, a dynamic that has become a near-annual feature of Independence Day and Republic Day security planning in the capital.
