Indian equity benchmarks opened lower on Tuesday, with the Sensex slipping around 290 points and the Nifty 50 dropping below the 24,500 mark, as fading hopes of an early resolution to US-Iran tensions pushed crude oil prices higher and dented investor sentiment.

The GIFT Nifty had signalled a negative start well before the opening bell, quoted roughly 40-45 points lower ahead of trade, as global cues turned mixed. Wall Street had closed marginally weaker overnight, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average and the S&P 500 both ending in the red and the Nasdaq Composite slipping about a third of a percent, as investors digested the latest developments around West Asia. Asian markets, by contrast, were broadly firmer in early trade, with Hong Kong's Hang Seng and South Korea's Kospi both in positive territory, suggesting the weakness in Indian indices had more to do with domestic and crude-specific concerns than a broader regional selloff.

## Crude Oil in Focus

The key driver behind Tuesday's cautious mood was oil. Prices firmed up amid growing concerns over future supply, with US petroleum reserves reportedly falling below 300 million barrels, their lowest level since 1983. That decline has coincided with signs of an impasse between Washington and Tehran over a proposed arrangement to ease shipping restrictions through the Strait of Hormuz, a route that carries a significant share of the world's seaborne oil trade. Any disruption or prolonged uncertainty around the strait tends to feed directly into crude prices, and by extension, into sentiment on Dalal Street, given India's heavy reliance on imported oil.

Precious metals moved in the opposite direction, with gold and silver futures both trading firmly higher in early trade, reflecting a flight to safer assets as geopolitical uncertainty persisted.

## Adani Group Stocks in Focus

Even as the broader market opened weak, Adani Group counters, including Adani Green Energy, Adani Power and Adani Enterprises, bucked the trend and advanced after a US court dismissed a long-running criminal indictment against Gautam Adani and two other group executives, removing a significant overhang that had weighed on the conglomerate's stocks for months.

## Earnings Season Continues

Tuesday also marked one of the busiest days of the ongoing June-quarter earnings season, with more than 340 companies scheduled to report results, including Siemens, MRF, Zydus Lifesciences and Rail Vikas Nigam. Investors are likely to stay selective, rotating into stocks with strong earnings beats while trimming exposure to names flagged for margin pressure or weak guidance, a pattern that has defined much of this reporting season so far.

## What to Watch

With the earnings calendar still heavy and the Hormuz situation unresolved, traders are expected to keep a close eye on crude oil movements and any fresh headlines out of West Asia through the day. A sustained rise in oil prices could keep pressure on rate-sensitive and oil-marketing stocks, while any signs of a breakthrough in US-Iran talks could quickly reverse the morning's cautious tone. For now, market participants appear to be treating Tuesday's dip as a pause rather than the start of a deeper correction, with domestic fundamentals, including the ongoing earnings season, still seen as broadly supportive.
