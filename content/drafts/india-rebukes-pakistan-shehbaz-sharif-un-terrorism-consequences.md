India delivered a sharp rebuttal to Pakistan at the United Nations General Assembly on Saturday, using its Right of Reply to reject Islamabad's renewed push on Jammu and Kashmir and warn that continued cross-border terrorism would carry consequences for the country.

The exchange followed an address by Pakistan Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif to the 81st session of the UN General Assembly in New York, in which he raised the Kashmir dispute, accused India of violating Pakistan's rights over the Indus river system, and alleged human rights violations against Kashmiris living under Indian administration. Sharif also described any Indian move to restrict Pakistan's share of Indus waters as an "act of war" and invoked US President Donald Trump's earlier intervention in easing tensions between the two nuclear-armed neighbours following last year's conflict.

## India's Response

Responding on India's behalf, First Secretary Petal Gahlot of India's Permanent Mission to the UN dismissed Sharif's speech as containing "bizarre remarks" and pointed to the fact that Osama bin Laden, mastermind of the September 11 attacks, had been found living close to the Pakistan Army's premier military academy. She argued that this context was essential to understanding the credibility of Pakistan's positions on security and terrorism.

Gahlot went further in criticising Pakistan's domestic power structure, describing its political leadership as governed "by the army, and for the army," and asserting that civilian leaders in Islamabad were replaceable figureheads while the military retained real control. She referenced Pakistan's 27th constitutional amendment, which she said had entrenched lifelong legal immunity for the country's military leadership, questioning Pakistan's standing to lecture others on democratic governance.

## Kashmir And Terrorism Take Centre Stage

On the core dispute, Gahlot firmly rejected Pakistan's claims over Jammu and Kashmir, calling it an "integral and inalienable part" of India, and turned the spotlight instead on Pakistan-occupied Jammu and Kashmir, urging the international community to examine reports of killings, violence and rights denials in areas such as Rawalakot.

She placed cross-border terrorism at the centre of India's response, stating that India had already demonstrated, in reference to last year's conflict, that "terrorists will have nowhere to run and nowhere to hide." Gahlot added that India would continue exercising its right to self-defence against terrorism and cautioned that any further terrorist activity originating from Pakistan would have consequences, potentially undermining any prospects for friendly ties between the two countries.

She did not directly address the legal arguments Sharif raised over the Indus Waters Treaty, choosing instead to concentrate India's reply on terrorism, Kashmir and Pakistan's internal political and human rights record.

## Accusations Of Hypocrisy

Gahlot closed her remarks by accusing Pakistan of hypocrisy on democracy, minority rights and human values, stating that the treatment of minorities within Pakistan was well known internationally regardless of the "pious platitudes" offered at the UN podium. She expressed confidence that the General Assembly would see through Pakistan's self-portrayal and recognise the country's record for what it truly represented, adding that repeating the same allegations year after year did not make them true.

The exchange is the latest in a long-running pattern of diplomatic confrontations between the two countries at the UN General Assembly, where India has repeatedly used its Right of Reply in recent years to counter Pakistani statements on Kashmir with pointed references to terrorism emanating from Pakistani soil.
