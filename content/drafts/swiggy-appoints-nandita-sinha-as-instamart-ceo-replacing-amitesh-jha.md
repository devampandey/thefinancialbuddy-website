Swiggy has appointed Nandita Sinha, the former chief executive of Myntra, as the new CEO of its quick-commerce arm Instamart, effective Monday. She takes over from Amitesh Jha, who tendered his resignation on July 28 and stepped down from Swiggy's senior management with immediate effect.

## A Veteran Consumer-Business Operator

Sinha brings more than two decades of experience across India's consumer businesses to the role. Before her stint leading Myntra, she held senior positions at Flipkart, Britannia Industries and Hindustan Unilever, giving her exposure across fashion e-commerce, packaged foods and fast-moving consumer goods. That breadth of experience across categories is being read as a signal that Swiggy wants Instamart to sharpen its merchandising and category strategy as it scales, rather than compete on delivery speed and discounting alone.

## Jha's Exit After a Formative Stretch

Jha's departure comes after a tenure in which he is credited with steering Instamart through a critical growth phase, building out its operating culture and expanding its dark-store network as quick commerce became one of the most competitive battlegrounds in Indian retail. Company statements characterised the transition as leaving the business in a "solid, well-run state," though leadership changes of this kind often prompt scrutiny of a unit's underlying performance and profitability trajectory, particularly for a business segment that has been central to Swiggy's growth narrative since its public listing.

## Why the Timing Matters

The leadership change lands at a pivotal moment for India's quick-commerce sector, where Instamart competes against Blinkit, owned by Eternal, and Zepto, among others, in a race to capture grocery and essentials delivery in under 10-15 minutes. All three players have been investing heavily in dark-store expansion and customer acquisition, compressing margins across the industry even as order volumes grow. Investors and analysts tracking Swiggy will be watching for signals on how Sinha plans to balance continued expansion with the path toward profitability that quick-commerce operators have promised for several quarters.

## What to Watch Next

Sinha's immediate priorities are likely to include category expansion, private-label strategy and deepening supply-chain efficiency, areas where her FMCG and e-commerce background could prove directly applicable. How quickly she moves to make her mark, and whether Swiggy's Instamart segment shows any shift in growth or margin trends in its next quarterly results, will offer early evidence of the strategic direction under the new leadership.
