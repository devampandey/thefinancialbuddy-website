The Government of India opened a much-anticipated Offer for Sale (OFS) in Life Insurance Corporation of India on August 4, giving non-retail investors their first window to bid for shares in what could become one of the largest disinvestment exercises in recent years. Retail investors and eligible employees get their turn on August 5, with a discount built into their allotment price.

## Deal Structure

The government has set a floor price of Rs 382 per share, a discount of roughly 10% to LIC's closing price of Rs 424.35 on the BSE the previous trading session. The offer is structured as a base sale of 2.5% equity, with an additional 4% available through a green shoe option, taking the total potential offer size to 6.5% of the company. If fully subscribed at the floor price, the sale would involve just over 82 crore shares and could raise approximately Rs 31,000 crore for the exchequer.

Retail investors and LIC employees participating in the OFS will receive an additional Rs 10 per share discount on the final cut-off price, a sweetener designed to encourage broader retail participation in a stock that has a substantial individual shareholder base built up since its 2022 listing.

## Government's Stake Coming Down

The Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM) currently holds 96.5% of LIC on behalf of the government. Should the full 6.5% offering be taken up, that holding would fall to roughly 90%. The move is part of a broader, previously signaled plan by the government to trim its stakes across public sector undertakings in tranches rather than through a single large sale, spreading out both market impact and investor absorption.

LIC shares came under pressure as the OFS news set in, falling roughly 6.7% as investors weighed the near-term supply overhang against the company's underlying business performance. Large stake sales via OFS routinely put temporary downward pressure on a stock's price, since the market must absorb a meaningful new supply of shares in a compressed window, even when the divestment does not change the company's operating fundamentals.

## Why It Matters

For the government, the sale advances its broader disinvestment target for the fiscal year and reduces its holding in a company where regulatory norms eventually require promoter stakes in listed public sector firms to come down toward levels more consistent with typical listed-company norms. LIC's public float remains thin relative to its market capitalization, and successive OFS tranches are widely expected as the government works toward compliance with minimum public shareholding requirements over the coming years.

For investors, the OFS offers an entry point into India's largest life insurer at a defined discount to recent market prices, though the near-term stock reaction shows the market is also pricing in the dilution effect of fresh supply. Analysts will be watching subscription levels across both the non-retail and retail tranches as a signal of investor appetite for further government stake sales in LIC and other public sector companies later in the year.
