On a morning when South Korea's KOSPI jumped more than 6% and Japan's Nikkei added 1.5%, Indian equities went the other way. By around 11 am on Wednesday, the BSE Sensex had shed roughly 886 points, or 1.2%, to about 72,713, and the Nifty 50 was hovering near 22,500 — a level technical analysts had singled out as the line in the sand for the session.

The split with the rest of Asia is the story. Wall Street had closed higher overnight on a tech-led rally, which helped lift regional markets. India, which imports most of its crude, had a more immediate problem: oil.

## Crude does the damage again

Brent crude was trading around $115.39 a barrel early in the session, up about 1.3%. The move followed reports that the United States could resume strikes on Iran once its mid-term elections are over, reviving fears about supply through the Gulf. For India, every sustained leg higher in crude feeds into the import bill, the rupee and, eventually, inflation — which is why domestic markets have repeatedly sold off on oil spikes this month while other Asian markets shrugged them off.

The Sensex had opened at 73,253 against its previous close of 73,598.67, then slid quickly; within minutes it was down about 691 points at 72,907. GIFT Nifty futures had already been signalling a gap-down of roughly 230 points before the bell.

## IT stocks take the heaviest hit

The selling was concentrated in technology. The Nifty IT index fell about 3.8% in early trade, by far the worst sectoral performer. Infosys dropped more than 4%, Tech Mahindra lost close to 3.9% and HCL Technologies fell around 3.5%. FMCG was the next weakest pocket, with its index down about 1.5%, while financial services, metal, pharma, media and PSU bank indices also traded lower.

The IT slide is notable because it came on a day when US tech stocks had rallied — a sign that domestic concerns, including persistent foreign portfolio selling and worries over demand from overseas clients, are outweighing the global cue.

## The few bright spots

Larsen & Toubro was the standout gainer, rising more than 5.7% a day after its [September-quarter results](/business/l-t-q2-profit-jumps-33-to-rs-5-202-crore-as-record-rs-1-48-lakh-crore-orders), and Bajaj Auto added about 2.3%. The Nifty Auto index managed a small gain of under 0.2%, the only major sector in the green early on.

Attention was also split by Jio Platforms' market debut, which listed at a premium of about 21% to its issue price of Rs 1,119 after the record-sized offer drew bids worth more than 22 times the shares on offer.

## Levels and what traders are watching

Analysts at Axis Direct identified 22,500 as immediate support for the Nifty, warning that a decisive break could open the way to 22,300. On the upside, a sustained move above 22,900 would be needed before the index could aim for the 23,000–23,100 zone.

Geojit's chief investment strategist V K Vijayakumar said any recovery would depend on crude cooling off and foreign investors switching from steady selling to buying. He also said a change in long-term capital gains tax looked increasingly likely given the scale of outflows — a policy question that has started to weigh on sentiment in its own right.

Beyond oil, investors are waiting for the US Federal Reserve's policy decision later this week, which could set the tone for global risk appetite and the dollar. For Indian markets, though, the more immediate signal remains the price of a barrel of Brent.
