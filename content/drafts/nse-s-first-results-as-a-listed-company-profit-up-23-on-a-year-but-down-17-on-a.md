The National Stock Exchange of India reported its first quarterly results as a listed company on Tuesday evening, and the numbers can be read two very different ways depending on which comparison an investor picks.

Consolidated net profit for the July-September quarter came in at Rs 2,578 crore. Against the same quarter last year, that is a rise of about 23% from Rs 2,098 crore. Against the June quarter, it is a fall of about 17% from Rs 3,120 crore. Earnings per share rose to Rs 10.41 from Rs 8.48 a year ago.

The results were released after market hours. NSE shares had closed at Rs 1,803.80 on the BSE, up 1.35% on the day, only slightly above the Rs 1,785 price at which the exchange sold shares in its IPO.

## The year-on-year jump has a one-off behind it

The headline growth looks stronger than the underlying business. Total expenses fell 51% to Rs 1,161 crore from Rs 2,354 crore, mainly because the year-ago quarter carried a Rs 1,297 crore provision for settlement fees paid to SEBI over the long-running colocation and dark fibre cases. Remove that charge from the comparison and much of the profit jump disappears.

Total income grew a more modest 9% to Rs 4,554 crore. Transaction charges, the exchange's biggest revenue line, also rose 9% to Rs 3,035 crore. Operating EBITDA more than doubled year on year to Rs 3,079 crore, again flattered by the provision, with a margin of about 76%.

## What volumes are saying

The volume data shows where the exchange's growth is and is not coming from. Average daily turnover in the cash market rose 24% to Rs 1,18,377 crore, a healthy sign of investor activity in a quarter when markets were under pressure. But average daily options turnover measured by premium, the segment that powered NSE's profits through the retail derivatives boom, grew only 3% to Rs 47,977 crore.

That slowdown is consistent with the tighter rules SEBI introduced to curb speculative trading in index options. For investors, it is the key question hanging over the stock: whether cash trading, listings and data services can grow fast enough to make up for a derivatives business that is no longer expanding at its earlier pace.

The smaller lines are moving in the right direction. Listing services revenue rose 70% to Rs 150 crore, helped by a busy IPO year, and income from data feeds and terminals rose 43% to Rs 165 crore. But together they are still a fraction of transaction income.

## The sequential slide

The quarter-on-quarter decline is the number markets may focus on first. Revenue from operations fell about 11% from the June quarter to Rs 4,067 crore, and the EBITDA margin narrowed to 75.7% from 77.9%. For the first half of the year, profit was up 13% at Rs 5,698 crore, with half-year EPS of Rs 23.02.

A rough annualisation of that first-half figure puts EPS near Rs 46, which values the stock at about 39 times earnings at Tuesday's close. That is a premium multiple for a business whose largest revenue stream is growing in single digits, and it explains why the shares have struggled to move far from their IPO price. Jefferies, which [started coverage last week with a Buy rating](/markets/jefferies-starts-nse-at-buy-with-rs-2-085-target-as-the-stock-hovers-near-its-ipo-p), argues the market is overstating the options risk.

## Other decisions

The board approved selling up to 40% of National Coal Exchange of India, currently a wholly owned subsidiary, through secondary share sales in one or more tranches. NSE also said it facilitated India's first tokenised bond issues during the quarter, totalling Rs 1,000 crore, and received approvals for derivatives on the Nifty India FPI 150 Index and corporate bond index futures. Over the first half, the exchange said it contributed Rs 41,234 crore to the government, most of it as securities and commodities transaction tax.
