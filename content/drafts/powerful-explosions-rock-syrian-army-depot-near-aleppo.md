A series of powerful explosions tore through a Syrian army munitions site south of Aleppo in the early hours of Monday, injuring at least four people and forcing authorities to shut down roads leading to the area, according to state media reports.

The blasts occurred at a military depot near the town of al-Eis, south of Aleppo, the state-run SANA news agency reported. Faisal Mohammad Ali, head of civil defence operations in Aleppo, said rescue teams managed to evacuate four injured people from the site, but that continuing detonations were preventing emergency crews from moving in further to assess the damage or search for additional casualties.

## Roads Shut, Residents Told to Stay Indoors

Aleppo's Internal Security Command closed roads leading to the explosion site, including the key Aleppo-Damascus motorway, citing civilian safety. Aleppo Governor Azzam al-Gharib urged residents in the surrounding area not to leave their homes for the time being, adding that hospitals, ambulance crews and emergency teams had been placed on full alert.

The precise cause of the explosions had not been established as of Monday morning, and officials stopped short of assigning blame. Authorities said the situation remained fluid, with intermittent blasts continuing to go off at the site hours after the first explosion was reported.

## Third Depot Blast in Under Two Weeks

Monday's incident is the third time in eleven days that a Syrian Defence Ministry arms depot has exploded, following similar incidents at facilities in Idlib and Deir Ezzor. The clustering of explosions at ammunition storage sites has raised questions among regional security analysts about whether the incidents are connected.

One theory circulating among security commentators is that Israeli aircraft may have targeted the depots to prevent Syria's reconstituted national army from gaining access to ammunition stockpiles left over from the previous government, though this has not been officially confirmed by Israeli, Syrian or international authorities. Israel has carried out repeated strikes on military infrastructure inside Syria over the past year, citing concerns about weapons falling into the hands of hostile groups during the country's ongoing political transition.

## Broader Context

Syria has experienced a turbulent stretch of military incidents in 2026 as the country's new leadership works to consolidate control over disparate armed factions and rebuild state institutions following years of civil war. Repeated depot explosions, whatever their cause, complicate that process by destroying stockpiles and raising fresh security concerns in areas the government is still working to stabilise.

Aleppo, Syria's largest city before the war and a frequent flashpoint during the conflict, has seen intermittent military activity in its surrounding countryside even as the city itself has been relatively calmer in recent months. Monday's explosions add to a growing list of unexplained or unattributed incidents at Syrian military sites this year.

Officials said further details, including a formal assessment of the cause and full casualty figures, were expected later in the day as civil defence teams gain fuller access to the site.
