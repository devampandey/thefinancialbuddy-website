Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told his cabinet on Sunday that Israel is rejecting a 15-point Gaza peace framework backed by US President Donald Trump, saying Israeli forces will not withdraw from the territory until Hamas has been "genuinely disarmed."

## What Netanyahu Said

Addressing ministers at the weekly cabinet meeting, Netanyahu said "Israel rejects the 15-point document," referring to the plan put forward by the US-led Board of Peace, the body Washington set up to oversee the implementation of a Gaza settlement. He said the Israeli military "will not carry out any withdrawal until Hamas is genuinely disarmed and will continue to thwart threats against our forces and our citizens," drawing a sharp distinction between what he called genuine disarmament and what he suggested would be a merely symbolic or partial process.

## Background to the Standoff

The 15-point plan had reportedly been endorsed by Hamas last month as part of a broader push, backed by Trump, to move from the fragile ceasefire that has held since last October toward a fuller end to the conflict, including provisions for Hamas to surrender weapons in stages. Despite the ceasefire technically remaining in place, Gaza has continued to see near-daily Israeli strikes in the months since, with rights groups and UN agencies tracking a steady toll of casualties, including children, over that period.

Last week, the Board of Peace — led by its director Nickolay Mladenov — had said publicly that any Israeli withdrawal from Gaza would only follow the complete disarmament of Hamas, a position that emerged after Mladenov met directly with Netanyahu. Sunday's cabinet statement effectively hardens that same line from the Israeli side, with Netanyahu setting a high bar for what would count as acceptable proof of disarmament before any troop pullback is considered.

## Why It Matters

The rejection puts Israel at odds with a plan that had been publicly framed by Trump as a "historic agreement" and a "monumental step toward lasting peace," and that had been negotiated with input from mediators including Egypt, Qatar, Turkey and the United States. Hamas, for its part, has previously said it will not move forward on its own obligations under any deal unless Israeli forces fulfil their side of the bargain, including a phased withdrawal — meaning Sunday's statement risks deepening a standoff in which each side is effectively waiting on the other to move first.

For a ceasefire that has already been marked by continued violence rather than a genuine cessation of hostilities, Netanyahu's comments signal that a durable resolution to the Gaza conflict remains distant, with the disagreement over the sequencing and verification of Hamas's disarmament now emerging as the central sticking point. Diplomatic attention is likely to turn next to how Washington and the other mediating parties respond to Israel's rejection of a plan they helped broker.
