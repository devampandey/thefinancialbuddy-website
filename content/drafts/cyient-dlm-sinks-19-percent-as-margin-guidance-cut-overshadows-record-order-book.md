On a morning when IT heavyweights were lifting the Sensex, one of India's electronics manufacturing names went the other way, and hard. Cyient DLM shares fell about 19% in Friday's session, sliding from Thursday's close of Rs 1,030.10 to an intraday low near Rs 830 on the NSE on unusually heavy volumes, after its September-quarter numbers and a guidance cut disappointed the Street.

## The numbers behind the sell-off

On the surface, the quarter was not bad. Revenue grew 26.5% year on year to about Rs 393 crore, and consolidated net profit rose roughly 6% to Rs 34 crore. But growth was not the problem; profitability was.

EBITDA margin fell 135 basis points to 8.7%. Antique Stock Broking had expected 10.4%. Gross margin dropped about 380 basis points to 37.4%, which analysts attributed to a weaker revenue mix and purchases tied to product development. The company has been spending on a larger sales organisation and expensing development costs for its own products, and those investments are now visible in the margin line.

The bigger blow was forward-looking. Management lowered its FY27 EBITDA margin guidance to 9-10%, from 10-11% earlier, and pushed the return to double-digit margins out to FY28. For a stock that trades on expectations of steady operating leverage, a one-percentage-point cut to the margin outlook mattered more than a quarter's revenue beat or miss.

## Brokers split sharply

The reaction from analysts shows just how wide the range of views has become:

- JPMorgan rated the stock Underweight with a target of Rs 530, implying almost 50% downside from Thursday's close.
- Kotak Institutional Equities called it a Sell with a target of Rs 790.
- Antique cut its FY27 and FY28 earnings estimates by 10% and 2% and downgraded the stock to Hold, with a target of Rs 1,025.
- Motilal Oswal held its Buy call and a Rs 1,320 target, arguing the company is entering a multi-year growth phase.

The bull case rests on visibility. The order book stands at a record Rs 2,810 crore, the book-to-bill ratio is about 1.6 times, and management expects revenue growth of more than 30% in the second half of FY27, with quarterly order intake staying above Rs 600 crore for the next two quarters. Aerospace demand, more box-build work and a ramp-up in semiconductor equipment are the main drivers. Motilal estimates revenue compounding at about 30% a year from FY26 to FY29.

## What the drop says about EMS valuations

The episode highlights a recurring tension in India's electronics manufacturing services space. Investors have paid premium multiples for growth stories, often 50 times forward earnings or more, on the assumption that margins expand as scale builds. When a company signals that it must spend more before profitability improves, there is little valuation cushion, and the stock reprices quickly.

For Cyient DLM, the coming two quarters will test management's claim that the investment phase is temporary. Delivery on the H2 revenue guidance and any sign that gross margins have stabilised will matter more to the stock than the order book alone.
