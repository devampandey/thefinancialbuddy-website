Play on the second morning of the opening Test between India and Sri Lanka in Galle was held up by persistent rain on Sunday, with covers rushed onto the square and both teams forced into an anxious wait in the pavilion before the delayed start to a match India already dominate.

## A Damp Start to Day 2

The Galle International Stadium woke up to overcast skies and a steady downpour on the second morning, pushing back the scheduled 10 a.m. local start and denying spectators an immediate look at India's push to extend their overnight advantage. Ground staff moved quickly to cover the pitch and square as the rain intensified, and both sides were left waiting for a break in the weather before any action could resume. Forecasts for the day had suggested a comparatively lower chance of rain than the opening day, but the early downpour meant the first session was already in jeopardy before a ball had been bowled.

## Padikkal's Landmark Day 1

The delay comes a day after India put together a commanding opening day with the bat. Put in to bat first by captain Shubman Gill, who won the toss, India closed the first day on 288 for 2, built on the back of a maiden Test century from Devdutt Padikkal. Batting at number three after an early run-out, Padikkal finished the day unbeaten on 131 off 178 balls, an innings laced with 12 boundaries and a six that also carried historical significance as only the second time an Indian batter has scored an international century on Independence Day.

Padikkal's innings was built around a productive stand with vice-captain KL Rahul, who anchored the middle session with a composed 77 before cramps forced him to retire hurt shortly after tea, ending a partnership worth 150 runs. Opener Yashasvi Jaiswal made a brisk 32 before being run out, while Gill himself fell for 16 to the day's lone successful bowler, left-arm spinner Prabath Jayasuriya. Rishabh Pant was unbeaten on 27 at the close, giving India batting depth still in the shed as Day 2 was set to begin.

## Sri Lanka's Bowling Struggles

Sri Lanka's attack found little success on a placid Galle surface. Jayasuriya, who sent down 24 overs, was the only bowler among the home side's attack to pick up a wicket, while debutant off-spinner Keshara Nuwantha leaked 98 runs in his 21 overs. Pacers Asitha Fernando and Lahiru Kumara also struggled to find purchase from the surface, leaving Sri Lanka needing early inroads on Day 2 to prevent India from building a first-innings total that could put the match out of reach.

## What the Delay Means for the Contest

With India already well placed and the surface at Galle traditionally offering more turn as a Test wears on, any time lost to rain works broadly in the batting side's favour, allowing the pitch to deteriorate further before Sri Lanka gets a chance to bowl on it. The match is the first of a two-Test series and forms part of the ongoing World Test Championship cycle, giving both sides an early opportunity to pick up points. It also marks an early assignment for Gill in his new role as India's full-time red-ball captain. Should the weather clear, the priority for India will be to bat on and push their total well past 300, while Sri Lanka will be hoping for early breakthroughs to keep the match competitive.
