India's parliamentary standing committee on information technology has given Meta chief executive Mark Zuckerberg three days to personally apologize over the temporary removal of a Facebook video featuring Prime Minister Narendra Modi, warning that failure to comply could trigger a review of the platform's legal protections in India.

## What Happened to the Video

The video in question showed the Prime Minister addressing students and members of Gen Z on issues related to examinations and the government's crackdown on paper leaks. According to the panel, the video was unavailable on Facebook for roughly five to six hours before being restored. Meta has already issued a statement attributing the removal to a technical glitch and offering an apology at the company level, but the parliamentary panel, chaired by BJP MP Nishikant Dubey, has said that response falls short of what it is demanding.

## Why the Panel Wants Zuckerberg Personally

The committee's letter specifically calls for an apology from Zuckerberg himself rather than a general corporate statement, arguing that the seriousness of removing content from a sitting Prime Minister warrants direct accountability from Meta's top leadership. The panel has set a three-day window for compliance, after which it says it may pursue a reconsideration of the safe harbour protections that Meta and other intermediaries currently enjoy under Section 79 of India's IT law.

## What Safe Harbour Protection Means Here

Safe harbour provisions generally shield platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp from being treated as the publisher of content posted by users, as long as they comply with due diligence requirements and government takedown requests. A formal move to reconsider or restrict that protection for Meta in India would be a significant escalation, since it could expose the company to greater legal liability for content moderation decisions and complicate its operations in one of its largest markets by user base.

## The Broader Pattern

This is not the first friction point between Meta and Indian authorities over content involving the Prime Minister or elections. The company has previously issued apologies to the Indian government after controversies involving public remarks made by Zuckerberg about India's national elections, suggesting a recurring sensitivity around how global platforms handle political content in the country. Each episode has tended to sharpen scrutiny of how much discretion foreign platforms should have over content moderation decisions that touch Indian political figures.

## What Happens if the Deadline Passes

It remains unclear what concrete regulatory steps the government would take if Zuckerberg does not respond within the stipulated window, since altering safe harbour protections typically requires a more formal legal and legislative process rather than a unilateral committee decision. Even so, the threat itself signals how seriously Indian lawmakers are treating platform accountability heading into a period of heightened scrutiny of Big Tech companies operating in the country. Meta has not yet indicated publicly whether Zuckerberg intends to issue the personal statement the panel is demanding.
