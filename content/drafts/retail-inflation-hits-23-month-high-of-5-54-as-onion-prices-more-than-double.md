India's consumer price inflation rose to 5.54% in September, its highest level in 23 months, according to data released by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation on Monday evening. The reading was up from 4.83% in August and marks the sixth consecutive monthly increase since March.

It is also the first time headline inflation has gone above 5% under the new CPI series, which uses 2024 as its base year. Because of the change in base, the numbers can't be compared directly with readings from the older series.

## Food is doing most of the damage

The Consumer Food Price Index rose 7.74% year on year in September, up sharply from 5.97% a month earlier. Vegetables drove much of the increase. Onion prices were 108% higher than a year ago, more than double August's year-on-year rise of about 49%. Ginger, garlic and lemons also showed steep annual increases.

The broader food and beverages group in the CPI basket, which includes items beyond the core food index, rose 7.36%.

## Villages feel it more than cities

The rural-urban gap widened. Rural retail inflation rose to 6.08% from 5.24%, putting it above the 6% upper limit of the Reserve Bank of India's tolerance band. Urban inflation climbed to 4.88% from 4.31%. Food inflation was also higher in rural areas, at roughly 8%, against about 7.2% in cities.

This matters because food takes up a larger share of household spending in rural India, so price shocks there hit budgets harder and quickly change how people expect prices to move.

## Services and transport add pressure

Price rises were not limited to the kitchen. Restaurant and accommodation services rose 9.36%, a jump that some reports link to higher energy costs as the West Asia conflict keeps crude oil expensive. Goods transport services rose 14.73%, and the personal care, social protection and miscellaneous services group recorded the highest division-level inflation at 13.75%.

There were pockets of relief. Health costs rose just 1.40%, and housing and utilities 2.74%.

## A test for the RBI's tightening stance

The data comes less than a week after the RBI [raised its repo rate to 5.5%](/business/rbi-raises-repo-rate-to-5-5-in-first-hike-since-2023-shifts-to-calibrated-tighten), its first hike since 2023, and moved to what it called calibrated tightening. September's figures broadly support the central bank's concern that price pressures are building across the economy rather than in a single category.

Headline inflation is still below the 6% ceiling. But a steady six-month climb, a rural reading already above it and costlier crude increase the chances of further policy action if the trend continues into the festive season. Bond markets were already uneasy, with 10-year government bond yields rising on Monday ahead of the release.
