Gold pushed further into record territory on Friday even as India's equity benchmarks traded in a narrow, directionless band, highlighting a split mood between safe-haven and risk assets in domestic markets.

## Bullion's Latest Leg Up

October gold futures on the Multi Commodity Exchange opened at roughly Rs 1,60,630 per 10 grams, up about Rs 1,205 from the previous close, before easing back somewhat as the session progressed. Silver moved in tandem, with the September contract opening near Rs 2,44,988 per kilogram, a gain of about Rs 1,745 over its prior close. Retail gold rates in several cities crossed the Rs 1.60 lakh per 10 grams mark for 24-karat gold during the day. Internationally, gold traded near $4,590 an ounce on Comex and silver hovered around $69 an ounce, both firmer on the day.

Several forces are feeding the rally. A softer US dollar and falling Treasury yields have made non-yielding bullion more attractive, while continued central-bank buying, particularly from China, has added a steady bid under prices. Rising Middle East tensions -- with crude oil pushing toward $94 a barrel after Washington signalled broader sanctions pressure on Iran -- have also stoked inflation worries that typically favour gold as a hedge. Traders noted this marks bullion's third consecutive weekly gain in the global market.

## Equities Stuck in Neutral

Indian stock benchmarks told a very different story. The BSE Sensex opened around 163 points higher at roughly 77,701, and the Nifty 50 gained about 52 points to open near 24,284, extending Thursday's rebound. By early afternoon, however, most of those gains had evaporated: the Sensex was up only about 22 points near 77,559 and the Nifty was essentially flat, adding just a few points near 24,235. IT and oil-and-gas stocks were among the laggards weighing on sentiment, while a handful of counters, including Power Grid and HDFC Life, bucked the trend to post gains.

Foreign institutional investors remained net sellers of Indian equities on Thursday, offloading shares worth roughly Rs 583 crore, while domestic institutional investors continued to absorb the supply, buying close to Rs 3,538 crore worth of stock -- a pattern that has persisted through much of the recent choppy stretch for the market.

## What It Means for Investors

The divergence captures a market caught between conflicting signals: elevated geopolitical risk and firm crude prices are pushing investors toward gold even as mixed global cues and sector-specific weakness keep equity gains capped. For household investors, the gold rally reinforces a theme many wealth managers have flagged through the year -- that a modest bullion allocation has meaningfully cushioned portfolios during a period when equity benchmarks have struggled to sustain rallies. Whether that pattern holds into the festive season, when Indian gold demand typically climbs further, will be closely watched by both jewellers and investors.
