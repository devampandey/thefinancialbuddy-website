Israel's Housing Ministry has issued a tender inviting construction bids for more than 1,200 housing units in the E1 area near Jerusalem, a long-frozen settlement project that critics say would sever the West Bank in two and make a contiguous Palestinian state effectively impossible.

## What The Tender Covers

The E1 zone sits between Jerusalem and the settlement of Maale Adumim. Development there has been diplomatically radioactive for more than two decades because it would create an unbroken chain of Israeli settlements separating the northern and southern West Bank, while also cutting East Jerusalem off from the rest of Palestinian-administered territory. Officials in Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's government have pushed the project forward as part of a broader acceleration of settlement approvals this year, even as several Western governments have imposed targeted sanctions on individuals and entities tied to the settler movement. The bidding window for the tender closes roughly a week before Israel's national elections at the end of October, adding a domestic political dimension to the timing.

## International Reaction

The move drew swift condemnation abroad. Palestinian Authority officials and the Palestinian ambassador to the United Nations, Riyad Mansour, described the decision as dangerous and irresponsible, warning it undermines any remaining prospect of a negotiated two-state outcome. In London, British officials signaled that the UK views the move as unacceptable, with reports indicating the government is weighing further diplomatic and economic responses. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar pushed back sharply against European criticism, rejecting what he characterized as foreign lecturing over where Israelis may build within what he called their historic homeland.

## Why E1 Matters So Much

Successive Israeli governments have floated E1 development since the early 2000s, only to shelve it each time under heavy diplomatic pressure from Washington and European capitals. Analysts have long flagged the corridor as one of the single most consequential pieces of land in the conflict precisely because building there would be difficult, if not impossible, to reverse. Once housing units and connecting infrastructure exist on the ground, they argue, any future map for a Palestinian state would have to route around a solid bloc of Israeli settlement rather than through contiguous Palestinian territory. That is why this tender, more than many other individual settlement approvals, is being read internationally as a marker of intent about the shape of any eventual peace framework.

## The Broader Context

The tender lands amid an already volatile period in the region, with Gaza ceasefire arrangements still fragile and separate tensions simmering over Iran's nuclear programme and the Strait of Hormuz. Diplomats say the E1 decision complicates parallel efforts by US and regional mediators to stabilise the broader Middle East picture, since it hands opponents of normalisation talks a concrete grievance to point to. Whether the tender translates into actual construction will depend partly on legal challenges and partly on whether international pressure, including any new sanctions from European governments, is enough to slow the process the way it has in the past.
