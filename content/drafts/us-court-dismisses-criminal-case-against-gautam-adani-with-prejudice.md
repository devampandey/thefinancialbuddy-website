A US federal judge has dismissed criminal charges against Indian billionaire Gautam Adani, his nephew Sagar Adani and fellow group executive Vneet Jaain, closing out one of the most consequential legal challenges the conglomerate has faced in recent years and sending Adani Group stocks higher in early trade on Tuesday.

The dismissal, issued by the US District Court for the Eastern District of New York, was granted "with prejudice," meaning the case cannot be reopened or refiled based on the same allegations. Adani Green Energy informed Indian stock exchanges that the order was passed following a motion by the US Department of Justice, which had itself sought the dismissal after what it described as a fresh review of the case.

## Background of the Case

The charges stemmed from a November 2024 indictment, in which federal prosecutors alleged that Gautam Adani and seven other defendants had promised more than $250 million in bribes to Indian government officials to help secure solar energy contracts expected to generate roughly $2 billion in profit over two decades. Prosecutors had also alleged that the arrangement was concealed from US investors while the group was raising capital in American markets. The Adani Group has consistently denied any wrongdoing throughout the proceedings.

The Justice Department had first signalled in May that it intended to stop pursuing the case. Before signing off on the dismissal, however, the presiding judge pressed prosecutors to explain their reasoning, reportedly calling an earlier justification "bland and conclusory." In subsequent filings, a senior Justice Department official described the case as primarily foreign in nature, difficult to prove, and no longer consistent with the department's current enforcement priorities. The judge also directly asked Gautam Adani whether anything had been promised in exchange for the case being dropped; in a sworn declaration, Adani stated he was not aware of any such arrangement, while acknowledging an earlier pledge to invest $10 billion in the United States.

## A Separate Settlement

The criminal case is distinct from a separate matter resolved earlier this year, in which the US Treasury Department reached a $275 million settlement with Adani Enterprises over alleged breaches of sanctions on Iran. That settlement remains in place and was not affected by Tuesday's court order.

## Market and Company Reaction

Shares of Adani Green Energy, Adani Power and Adani Enterprises were among the early gainers on Tuesday, even as the broader market opened weaker, as investors treated the dismissal as removing a significant legal overhang that has weighed on sentiment toward the group since the charges were first unsealed. In a post on social media, Gautam Adani said he welcomed the court's decision "with humility and deep respect for the judicial process," thanking those who had stood by the group through the proceedings and reiterating a commitment to continued investment in India.

## Why It Matters

For a conglomerate that has spent nearly two years contending with the reputational and financing overhang of an active US criminal case, alongside a separate short-seller controversy in 2023, the dismissal marks a notable turning point. It removes a persistent source of uncertainty for investors and lenders and may ease some of the caution that global institutions have shown toward Adani Group paper and equity since the allegations first surfaced, though the group's broader scrutiny from regulators and rating agencies is likely to continue independently of this outcome.
