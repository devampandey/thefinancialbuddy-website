California state senator Aisha Wahab has taken a narrow lead in the special runoff election for the state's 14th congressional district, according to unofficial results released late Tuesday by the Alameda County Registrar of Voters. With ballots still being counted, Wahab held roughly 51% of the vote to Melissa Hernandez's 49%, a margin of just over two percentage points.

## An All-Democrat Contest

Because the 14th district leans heavily Democratic, the runoff came down to a contest between two candidates from the same party rather than a traditional partisan showdown. Wahab, a sitting state senator known for progressive policy positions, faced off against Hernandez in a race that both national and local outlets described as tightly fought through election night, with the lead changing narrowly as returns came in.

The seat became vacant after Eric Swalwell, who had represented the district for over a decade, resigned in April following allegations of sexual misconduct. His departure triggered the special election process, with Tuesday's runoff serving as the decisive round after neither candidate cleared a majority in the initial special election.

## What The Result Signals

Local election officials cautioned that Tuesday night's tallies remain unofficial, with mail-in and provisional ballots still to be fully processed in the coming days. Even so, the margin narrowed just enough over the course of the evening that most outlets tracking the race characterized Wahab as the likely winner heading into final certification.

The race has been closely watched as a proxy battle over the direction of California's Democratic base ahead of the broader 2026 midterm cycle, with Wahab generally viewed as occupying the more left-leaning lane of the party and Hernandez positioning herself as a more centrist alternative. Turnout in special elections of this kind tends to run lower than in general elections, and analysts noted the tight margin reflects a genuinely divided electorate within the district rather than a decisive ideological mandate either way.

## Broader Political Context

The outcome adds one more data point to a 2026 election cycle already marked by a wave of congressional vacancies and special elections across multiple states, as both parties test messaging and turnout strategies ahead of November's midterms, where control of the House and Senate will be at stake. Whoever is ultimately certified as the winner will serve out the remainder of Swalwell's term before facing voters again in the regular midterm election later this year.

Final certification of the results is expected once all outstanding ballots are counted, a process that typically takes several days to a couple of weeks under California's elections timeline.
