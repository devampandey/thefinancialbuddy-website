The inquiry committee report into the allegations against former Delhi High Court judge Justice Yashwant Varma was tabled in both the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha on Wednesday, bringing a formal end to a process that began after unaccounted cash was allegedly discovered at his official residence in March 2025. According to the day's list of business, the Secretary-General laid before Parliament both volumes of the report, in English and Hindi, along with the oral and documentary evidence recorded during the inquiry.

## How the controversy began

The episode traces back to March 14, 2025, when a fire broke out at Justice Varma's official residence in Lutyens' Delhi while he was still sitting as a judge of the Delhi High Court. Justice Varma was not present at the time. Fire tenders responding to the blaze allegedly found semi-burnt wads of currency notes on the premises, triggering a controversy that quickly escalated into calls for his removal from judicial office.

In the aftermath, Justice Varma was transferred out of the Delhi High Court to the Allahabad High Court, where he took oath in April 2025. A separate Supreme Court-appointed panel of judges had earlier examined the allegations, questioning more than 55 witnesses. That panel found substance in the claims about burnt and unburnt cash at the residence and recommended Justice Varma's removal on grounds of misconduct.

## The parliamentary process

Following that indictment, 146 members of the Lok Sabha submitted a formal notice of motion seeking Justice Varma's removal under the Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968. Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla constituted a three-member inquiry committee on August 15, 2025, to examine the charges through the constitutional removal process, which requires Parliament itself to weigh the evidence before any motion for removal can be moved and voted upon.

The committee's report, tabled on Wednesday, is the product of that year-long process. Its tabling clears the way for the next stage of proceedings, in which Parliament can debate a formal motion for Justice Varma's removal, should MPs choose to pursue one. Justice Varma resigned from his position as a judge of the Allahabad High Court earlier this year, a move that came after the panel's earlier findings but before Wednesday's report was formally placed before the two Houses.

## A parallel legal track

The parliamentary process has run alongside separate legal efforts. Days before the report was tabled, the Supreme Court on Friday declined to entertain a plea seeking criminal action against Justice Varma over the alleged recovery of burnt cash, leaving the constitutional and parliamentary route as the primary mechanism through which the allegations are being addressed.

The case has been closely watched as a test of India's rarely used judicial removal process, which requires evidence gathered by an inquiry committee to be placed before Parliament before any impeachment motion can proceed. With the report now formally tabled, attention turns to whether the government and opposition will move to debate a removal motion during the ongoing Monsoon Session, or whether Justice Varma's earlier resignation from the bench will be treated as having settled the matter in practice.
