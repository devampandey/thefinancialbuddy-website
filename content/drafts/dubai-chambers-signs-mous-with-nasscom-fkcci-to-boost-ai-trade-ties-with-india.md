Dubai Chambers signed two Memorandums of Understanding in Bengaluru on Thursday, one with the National Association of Software and Service Companies and another with the Federation of Karnataka Chambers of Commerce and Industry, aimed at deepening collaboration on agentic artificial intelligence and strengthening trade and investment ties between Dubai and India.

## What Was Signed

The visit was led by Mohammad Ali Rashed Lootah, President and CEO of Dubai Chambers, who held a series of meetings with Indian business organisations and multinational companies operating out of Bengaluru during the trip. The agreement with NASSCOM sets up a framework for cooperation specifically on agentic AI and deeptech, sectors where Indian software and services firms have been racing to build capability. Under the pact, Dubai Chambers has committed to supporting market access for Indian companies looking to establish or expand a presence in Dubai, alongside broader knowledge-sharing, ecosystem engagement and joint innovation initiatives. The separate MoU with FKCCI is more operational in nature, establishing what the two sides are calling a Virtual Dubai Desk, a single point of contact intended to guide Karnataka-based companies through market entry, regulatory orientation and expansion support when they look to set up in the emirate.

## Why Bengaluru, Why Now

The choice of Bengaluru is not incidental. The city remains India's largest technology and software services hub, home to a dense cluster of the companies NASSCOM represents and a natural target for any Gulf economy trying to plug into India's AI talent pool. The timing also lines up with Dubai's own domestic push: the emirate launched an initiative in May 2026 aimed at transitioning its private sector toward agentic AI adoption within two years, and partnerships like this one with NASSCOM are being positioned as a way to import expertise and talent to support that transition rather than build it from scratch domestically.

## The Bigger Trade Picture

Dubai and the wider UAE have been steadily deepening commercial ties with India over the past several years, building on the India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement that has already pushed bilateral non-oil trade higher. Agreements like Thursday's MoUs tend to function less as immediate deal announcements and more as institutional plumbing, formal channels that individual Indian companies can subsequently use to navigate Dubai's regulatory and business environment with less friction, and vice versa for Gulf firms eyeing India. For NASSCOM member companies specifically, easier market access into Dubai could matter as a base for serving Middle East and North Africa clients without having to negotiate entry terms individually.

## What To Watch Next

The practical impact of the MoUs will depend on how quickly the Virtual Dubai Desk becomes operational and how many companies actually use the NASSCOM-Dubai Chambers channel to expand. Neither agreement carries a disclosed financial commitment, and both are structured as cooperation frameworks rather than binding investment pledges, so the near-term signal is more about institutional intent than an immediate capital flow into either market.
