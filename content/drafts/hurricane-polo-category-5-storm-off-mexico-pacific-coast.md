Hurricane Polo has rapidly intensified into a Category 5 storm over the eastern Pacific, becoming one of the most powerful hurricanes recorded anywhere in the Western Hemisphere this year and putting Mexico's southwestern coast on high alert.

## From Tropical Storm to Category 5 in a Day

The US National Hurricane Center said Polo strengthened explosively over roughly 24 hours, reaching maximum sustained winds of about 165 mph and a central pressure near 920 millibars, readings that place it among the strongest storms ever tracked in the region. Forecasters described Polo as an extremely dangerous major hurricane.

As of early Wednesday, the storm was centered about 165 miles off Zihuatanejo, a resort city in the Mexican state of Guerrero, and had been largely stationary over open water before forecasters expected it to make a sharp turn toward the northwest through Wednesday and Thursday, bringing its core closer to the southwestern Mexican coastline.

## Guerrero and Michoacan Brace for Impact

Mexican authorities, including President Claudia Sheinbaum, said current forecasts suggest Polo is unlikely to make a direct landfall, but officials are not taking chances given the storm's size and strength. Guerrero and Michoacan states are expected to see 4 to 8 inches of rainfall as the hurricane passes offshore, with isolated pockets receiving even more. That level of rain raises the risk of flash flooding and landslides in coastal and hillside communities, many of which are still recovering from previous storm seasons.

Civil protection agencies in both states have activated emergency protocols, and fishing communities along the coast have been advised to stay off the water as swells and dangerous rip currents build well ahead of the storm's closest approach.

## Why This Storm Stands Out

Meteorologists tracking Polo noted that its jump from a weaker tropical system to Category 5 strength in about a day qualifies as rapid intensification, a phenomenon that has become more common and more extreme in recent Pacific and Atlantic hurricane seasons as ocean temperatures rise. Such rapid strengthening leaves coastal communities with a narrower window to prepare, which is one reason forecasters are urging residents in the storm's path to finalize evacuation and safety plans well before conditions deteriorate.

Polo is expected to remain a powerful major hurricane over the next few days even as it eventually begins to weaken, with forecasters cautioning that any wobble closer to shore could sharply increase the danger to coastal Guerrero and Michoacan.

## What Comes Next

The National Hurricane Center is continuing to issue updated advisories as Polo's exact track becomes clearer, and Mexican officials say they are monitoring the storm around the clock. For now, the priority for coastal residents remains preparing for heavy rain, flooding and dangerous surf rather than a direct hit, though forecasters caution that hurricane tracks can shift with little notice.
