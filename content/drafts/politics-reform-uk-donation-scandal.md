Reform UK was plunged into a fresh political crisis on Friday after two of party leader Nigel Farage's most senior aides stepped down following an undercover investigation that alleged breaches of Britain's electoral funding laws.

## What the Investigation Found

James Orr, the party's head of policy, and Dan Jukes, a close adviser to Farage, both relinquished their positions pending an internal inquiry. The resignations came after Channel 4 News broadcast a half-hour investigative report, produced with the group Verbatim, built around hidden-camera footage of the two men discussing funding arrangements with undercover reporters posing as a wealthy American financier and his UK-based son.

Under British electoral law, political parties are barred from accepting donations or research funding from foreign entities or individuals not registered on the UK electoral roll. The recordings reportedly show the pair coordinating the commissioning of three opinion polls, together costing roughly £32,500, purportedly financed by the undercover "donor." Reform UK said both aides had stepped aside so that an independent internal investigation could proceed without prejudice.

## Farage Pushes Back, Rivals Escalate

Farage has firmly denied any wrongdoing, describing the sting as a politically motivated hoax and characterising it as a foreign-funded entrapment operation orchestrated by climate activists. Party chairman Lee Anderson went further, telling broadcasters that neither aide would be allowed to return to the party regardless of the investigation's outcome, framing the swift separations as evidence the party takes the allegations seriously.

Opposition parties were quick to seize on the controversy. Labour and the Liberal Democrats have referred the matter to the Metropolitan Police, which says it is reviewing the material. Labour's party chair called on authorities to establish transparently whether campaign finance rules were breached, arguing the allegations reach the top of Reform's operation. Electoral watchdogs are examining the disclosures alongside existing parliamentary scrutiny of party financing more broadly.

## Timing Complicates Reform's Conference Push

The scandal has overshadowed Reform UK's two-day annual conference in Birmingham, diverting attention from the party's policy rollout and fundraising claims at a moment when it has been positioning itself as a serious contender ahead of the next general election. Reform has built momentum in recent polling on the strength of its insurgent, anti-establishment message, making any suggestion of rule-breaking around donations a particularly awkward story for a party that has campaigned heavily on standards in public life.

The affair also lands amid broader scrutiny of how political parties across the UK finance themselves, with foreign-donor rules a recurring flashpoint in British politics. How the internal investigation unfolds, and whether police or electoral regulators pursue formal action, will shape whether the episode remains a personnel matter or escalates into a more serious legal and reputational challenge for Reform UK heading into a critical electoral period.
