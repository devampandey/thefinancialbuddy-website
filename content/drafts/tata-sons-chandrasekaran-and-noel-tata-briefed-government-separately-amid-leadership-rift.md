Tensions at the top of the Tata Group reportedly reached the highest levels of the Indian government well before outgoing Tata Sons chairman N Chandrasekaran publicly announced his decision to step down. According to a report attributed to the Times of India, Chandrasekaran and Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata separately briefed senior government officials about their differences over the conglomerate's strategy, governance and leadership direction.

The government reportedly did not initiate these conversations. Instead, both leaders approached officials on their own to raise concerns, and the government listened without formally intervening or taking sides in the internal dispute, according to the report.

## What the Discussions Covered

The briefings are said to have touched on disagreements over new business ventures, governance practices, and uncertainty surrounding Chandrasekaran's future at the helm of Tata Sons. Specific concerns raised reportedly included certain leadership appointments as well as ongoing losses at group businesses such as Air India, Tata's digital platforms, and its semiconductor ambitions.

Tata Group has made substantial investments across aviation, semiconductors, electronics manufacturing and digital services in recent years. Air India has required significant capital infusion since the group regained control of the airline in 2022, while Tata Electronics is building a semiconductor fabrication facility in Gujarat in partnership with Taiwan's Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corp. The group's digital arm, anchored by the Tata Neu platform, has also involved heavy spending, making the performance of these newer businesses a central point of the internal debate.

## Succession Comes Into Focus

Chandrasekaran announced on August 12 that he would not seek another term once his current tenure concludes on February 20, 2027. He has led Tata Sons since 2017, steering the group through its expansion into aviation, semiconductors, electronics and digital businesses. His planned exit has thrust the group's unusual governance structure back into the spotlight: Tata Trusts collectively control roughly 66 percent of Tata Sons, giving the philanthropic trusts significant sway over the holding company even as its chairman manages day-to-day operations.

Noel Tata took over as chairman of Tata Trusts following the death of Ratan Tata in October 2024. Observers have drawn parallels between the current friction and the group's earlier, more public falling-out with former chairman Cyrus Mistry, which ended in his removal in 2016 and years of subsequent legal battles.

## The IPO Question

Another significant point of contention involves a long-discussed public listing of Tata Sons itself. Tata Trusts are said to have reservations about such a listing, given that it could reshape the holding company's ownership structure and dilute the trusts' influence over the broader group. Noel Tata is reported to have met senior Reserve Bank of India officials in June to discuss the matter.

## What Comes Next

With Chandrasekaran's tenure now on a fixed clock, the Tata Group faces the twin task of managing an orderly leadership transition while resolving disagreements over capital allocation and the future ownership structure of its flagship holding company. The developments come just ahead of the group's Tata Sons annual general meeting, which is scheduled for August 18 and reportedly faces its own procedural hurdles linked to an ongoing regulatory dispute involving the Sir Ratan Tata Trust.
