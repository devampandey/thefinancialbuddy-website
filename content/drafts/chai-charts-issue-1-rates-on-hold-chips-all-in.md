Welcome to the very first cup of Chai & Charts — this week, the RBI played it safe, the market didn't, and a hospital chain out-debuted everyone's expectations.

## This Week in a Nutshell

- **RBI holds the repo rate at 5.25%**, keeping its neutral stance while flagging West Asia-linked disruption to trade and sentiment — a "wait and watch" verdict that markets took in stride.
- **Sensex and Nifty logged a strong weekly gain**, helped along by crude oil sliding below $80 a barrel on hopes of a US-Iran diplomatic thaw.
- **Manipal Health Enterprises jumped 11% on its stock market debut**, defying a grey market that had been pricing in a muted listing.
- **Bharti Airtel's Q1 FY27 profit jumped 43%**, powered by rising ARPU and strength in its Homes and enterprise businesses.
- **The government opened the LIC OFS**, offering up to a 6.5% stake worth roughly Rs 31,000 crore at a floor price of Rs 382 a share.
- **Cabinet cleared a Rs 1.9 lakh crore push for semiconductors and mobile manufacturing**, deepening the bet on India's chip ecosystem under Semicon India 2.0.
- **Manufacturing PMI slipped to a near five-year low of 53.5**, a reminder that softer domestic demand is offsetting export strength.
- **Karnataka's Labour Department summoned TCS** over roughly 12,000 planned job cuts, after a union complaint pushed the matter into the open.

## Markets This Week

It was a week where the headline number undersold the drama. The Sensex closed at 78,581 and the Nifty at 24,624, but both benchmarks were up more than 2.5% for the week — Nifty added roughly 617 points, its best weekly showing in a while — as easing crude prices took the edge off inflation worries. The RBI's rate hold on verdict day (August 5) was largely priced in, so the bigger swing factor was oil: Brent's slide below $80 on US-Iran talk optimism gave cyclicals and rate-sensitive names room to run. Gold held just under its recent highs near Rs 1,43,900 per 10 grams for 24-karat, having touched a fresh peak earlier in the week on a weaker dollar, while the rupee stayed rangebound between roughly 94.9 and 95.8 to the dollar, settling near 95.16. It wasn't a one-way week, though — a new closing auction mechanism and the LIC stake sale briefly knocked indices off their rally on Tuesday before buyers stepped back in.

## Cheers, Fears & Jeers

**Cheers:** Nykaa's Q1 FY27 net profit tripled to Rs 79.76 crore, riding beauty and fashion GMV growth and quick-commerce expansion — a rare quarter where a new-age consumer name delivered on both revenue and margin.

**Fears:** Manufacturing PMI cooling to its weakest reading since 2021, paired with TCS's 12,000-odd job cuts drawing state government scrutiny, points to a labour market and factory floor that are both losing a step even as headline indices climb.

**Jeers:** Indian startup funding fell 67% to just $662 million in July, a sharp reversal after June's surge, with both large rounds and overall deal count scarce — a sobering data point for anyone hoping the funding winter was fully behind us.

## Person/Story of the Week

Prashant Kishor's Jan Suraaj party broke through in Bihar this week, with its founder winning the Bankipur bypoll by roughly 19,000 votes — snapping a BJP stronghold and giving the poll strategist-turned-politician his party's first electoral victory. It's a small win in seat count but a large one in narrative terms, arriving just ahead of the 2027 UP polls cycle and giving Kishor's outsider project its first real proof of concept at the ballot box.

## The Lighter Side

Fresh off her maiden BWF Super 300 title in Taipei, 17-year-old Tanvi Sharma heads into the Korea Masters as the face of India's badminton challenge this week — a reminder that while Glasgow 2026 just wrapped with India's 39-medal campaign, the next generation is already stacking up moments of its own.
