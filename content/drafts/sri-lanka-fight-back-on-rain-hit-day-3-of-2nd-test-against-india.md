Sri Lanka put up a spirited display on the third day of the second Test against India at the Sinhalese Sports Club in Colombo on Tuesday, closing on 265 for 8 in 83.4 overs before rain brought an early end to play, with the hosts still trailing India by 238 runs. The result leaves the match finely poised heading into what should be a decisive fourth day, with India needing quick breakthroughs to press home their advantage.

## Sooriyabandara Shines On Debut

The standout performance of the day came from debutant Pasindu Sooriyabandara, who anchored the Sri Lankan innings with a patient knock of 80 off 133 balls before eventually falling. His innings was built on discipline against a probing Indian attack, and he found a productive partner in Kamindu Mendis, with the pair adding 87 runs for one of the more substantial stands of the innings before India found a breakthrough just before the lunch interval.

The morning session largely belonged to the home side as Sooriyabandara and Mendis worked to blunt India's bowling attack and rebuild after early setbacks. However, the afternoon session saw the momentum shift, with Ravindra Jadeja and Prasidh Krishna striking at regular intervals to peg the Sri Lankan innings back and prevent the hosts from building a platform for a substantial total.

## Match Context

India go into this Test with significant momentum after a dominant performance in the series opener at Galle, where they beat Sri Lanka by 165 runs to move 1-0 up in the two-match series. That victory was built on strong contributions from the bat and ball, with India carrying a first-innings lead of close to 178 runs in that game before closing it out comfortably.

With Sri Lanka currently 238 runs adrift and still two wickets away from being bowled out, India will be eyeing an opportunity to wrap up the tail quickly once play resumes, potentially setting up a chance to enforce further pressure depending on how the match situation develops. The weather, however, remains a factor to watch, with the early stumps on day three raising the possibility of further interruptions affecting the remaining days of the contest.

## What's Next

The two sides will resume on day four with Sri Lanka looking to extend their lower-order resistance and trim the deficit as much as possible, while India's bowlers — led by the returns Jadeja and Krishna have already shown — will be aiming to close out the innings without further delay. Depending on how quickly the innings wraps up, the match could open up either as a testing chase for Sri Lanka or a further opportunity for India to consolidate their position in the series with a clean sweep on the horizon.

Fans and followers of the series will be watching closely to see whether Sri Lanka's lower order can extend Tuesday's fightback into a genuine rearguard effort, or whether India's bowlers can force an early breakthrough and take firm control of the contest.
