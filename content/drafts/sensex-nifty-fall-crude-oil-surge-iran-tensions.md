Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply lower on Wednesday, extending a run of nervous sessions as crude oil prices pushed toward the $100-a-barrel mark on renewed fears of supply disruption from the Middle East. Within the first half hour of trade, the Sensex had shed close to 580 points, or roughly 0.8 per cent, slipping under the 75,000 level, while the Nifty50 lost more than 130 points to trade near 23,500.

## What is driving the selloff

The immediate trigger for the decline is the continued escalation between Iran and the United States, which has kept crude markets on edge for days. Reports of fresh strikes on Saudi Arabian oil infrastructure, alongside American action against tankers believed to be linked to Iranian oil exports, have stoked concern that a meaningful share of Gulf crude supply could be disrupted heading into the winter demand season. Adding to the unease, Iran has said it intends to set up a new shipping corridor with Oman inside the Strait of Hormuz, requiring vessels to coordinate with Tehran before entering a newly designated zone — a move markets are reading as a signal of Iran's willingness to assert control over one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints.

Brent crude traded near three-month highs through the session, and the rise in landed fuel costs has direct implications for India, which imports the vast majority of its crude requirements. A sustained rally in oil prices tends to widen the current account deficit, add to inflationary pressure, and squeeze margins for fuel-intensive sectors, all of which weighed on sentiment on Dalal Street on Wednesday.

## Sectoral moves

The pain was not evenly spread. Information technology and metal counters bore the brunt of the selling, with heavyweight IT names among the top laggards on the Sensex as investors rotated out of export-oriented and rate-sensitive stocks. Pharmaceutical and chemical stocks, by contrast, held up comparatively well, cushioning some of the index-level damage. Among individual gainers, infrastructure major Larsen & Toubro, along with Sun Pharma and NTPC, bucked the broader trend to trade higher in early deals.

Broader market indices mirrored the weakness on the benchmarks, with the Nifty MidCap and Nifty SmallCap both trading lower, suggesting the selloff was not confined to large-caps alone.

## Currency under pressure too

The rupee also felt the heat, opening a few paise weaker against the US dollar compared with Tuesday's close, as importers stepped up dollar demand to cover costlier oil purchases. A weaker rupee and firmer crude tend to reinforce each other in periods of geopolitical stress, and traders will be watching closely for any signs of intervention or stabilising flows through the remainder of the week.

## What investors are watching

With Middle East tensions showing no clear signs of easing, market participants are likely to stay cautious in the near term. Analysts tracking the situation say the key variables to watch are the trajectory of Brent crude, any further escalation around the Strait of Hormuz, and incoming signals from the US Federal Reserve on the path of interest rates, all of which will shape how Indian equities trade in the sessions ahead.
