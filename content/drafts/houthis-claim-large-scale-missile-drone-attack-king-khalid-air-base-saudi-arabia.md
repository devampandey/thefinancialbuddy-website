Yemen's Houthi movement said on Monday that it had carried out a large-scale missile and drone attack on King Khalid Air Base in the southern Saudi city of Khamis Mushait, marking a sharp escalation in the group's confrontation with Riyadh even as diplomats scrambled elsewhere in the region to contain a widening energy and security crisis.

## What The Houthis Say Happened

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said fighters fired dozens of ballistic missiles and drones at the base, claiming hits on aircraft hangars, radar installations, runways and ammunition depots. He described the damage as significant, though the claims could not be independently verified. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm an attack or acknowledge the scale of damage the Houthis described, a pattern that has repeated through earlier rounds of strikes this year.

## Saudi Response

Saudi Arabia's Civil Defence agency issued an alert for residents of Khamis Mushait shortly after the claimed strike, urging people to avoid gathering in open areas or filming military activity, before later announcing that the immediate danger had passed. The kingdom has faced a string of similar alerts across its southern provinces in recent days as cross-border exchanges with the Houthis have intensified.

## Why This Is Happening Now

The Houthis say the attack was retaliation for Saudi air strikes across several Yemeni provinces, which they allege have numbered more than 300 sorties by fighter jets over the past five days alone. The group has warned that continued Saudi operations in Yemen will be met with what it called broader and more severe responses. The exchange comes against the backdrop of a months-long standoff between Washington and Tehran that has drawn in the wider Gulf, with Saudi Arabia recently forced to shut a key east-west oil pipeline after drone attacks elsewhere in the kingdom.

## Part Of A Wider Regional Unravelling

The claimed strike lands at a particularly volatile moment. Over the past week, Houthi forces have also moved to consolidate control over Yemen's Red Sea coast, including the strategic port of Mocha near the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a chokepoint for global shipping second in importance only to the Strait of Hormuz. Regional analysts say the twin pressure points give Iran-aligned forces unusual leverage over global energy and container traffic at a time when Gulf capitals are already anxious about disruptions further north in the Strait of Hormuz. Saudi Arabia, already grappling with the fallout from its pipeline shutdown, now faces the prospect of sustained pressure on two fronts: its own southern border and the broader shipping lanes its economy depends on.

## What To Watch Next

Whether Saudi Arabia responds with fresh air strikes of its own, and whether Riyadh chooses to publicly confirm or deny the extent of damage at King Khalid Air Base, will shape the next phase of this exchange. For now, neither side shows signs of de-escalating, and the claimed attack adds another flashpoint to a Gulf region already contending with blocked pipelines, disputed shipping corridors and stalled diplomacy.
