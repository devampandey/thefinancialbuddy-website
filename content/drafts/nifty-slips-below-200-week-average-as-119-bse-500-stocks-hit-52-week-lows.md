On paper, Friday looked like a mild day for Indian equities. The BSE Sensex slipped 125.56 points, or 0.17%, to close at 73,123.83, and the NSE Nifty 50 lost 87.65 points, or 0.39%, to settle at exactly 22,600. Both benchmarks have now fallen for four sessions in a row.

The headline numbers hide what happened in the rest of the market. Of the 2,871 stocks that traded, only 425 ended higher while 2,408 closed lower. The Nifty Smallcap 100 fell 2.94% and the Nifty Midcap 100 dropped 2.21%. In the BSE 500, 119 companies hit fresh 52-week lows and only two touched 52-week highs. For a day when the Sensex barely moved, that is a lopsided result.

## A level that chart-watchers had been guarding

The closing figure matters to technical analysts. At 22,600 the Nifty is now below its 200-week moving average on the weekly chart and below its 20-day average on the daily chart. Long-term investors often watch the 200-week line as a rough marker of whether a market is still in a long uptrend.

Vatsal Bhuva, technical analyst at LKP Securities, said the pattern of lower highs is still in place, which suggests the recent bounce was a relief rally and not a turn in trend. He named 22,500 as immediate support and 22,200 as the level that really counts. A clear break below 22,200 could set off another round of selling, he said. Resistance sits at 22,700 to 22,800, and he added that Brent crude staying above $110 a barrel would keep pressure on sentiment.

Fear gauges agreed. India VIX, which tracks expected volatility over the next month, rose 6.3% to 16.76.

## Why the big indices held up better

The gap between the Sensex and the broader market came from a late recovery in heavyweight stocks. Earlier in the day, as Brent climbed toward $114, the Sensex had fallen more than 1,200 points from its morning high (we covered that intraday slide in [our earlier report](/markets/brent-at-114-flips-sensex-into-a-1-279-point-slide-as-metals-and-hdfc-bank-drag)). Large caps won back most of that ground in the last hours of trade. Smaller stocks did not.

Eternal rose 4.3%, Shree Cement gained 1.8% and Apollo Hospitals added 1.7%, which helped soften the benchmark losses. The broader Nifty 500 still fell 1.2% to 21,427.60, about three times the Nifty 50's drop.

Vinod Nair, head of research at Geojit Investments, said the much sharper fall in mid and small caps reflects worries about valuations and traders unwinding leveraged positions. Expensive crude and weak global markets, including the AI-driven sell-off in technology shares, have added to the pressure.

## Where the selling hit hardest

Metals had the worst day among sectoral indices, with the Nifty Metal index down more than 3% as aluminium prices fell and investors priced in higher global supply. Fertiliser stocks fell 2.9% as a group.

Among individual names, Swiggy dropped 16.8% to a 52-week low of Rs 176 after its second-quarter results, and Jammu & Kashmir Bank fell 16%. Adani Energy Solutions lost 5.8% and Adani Green Energy fell 5.2%. Hindustan Zinc also declined after silver prices eased on reports that the government will allow 6,500 tonnes of silver imports over six months to meet festive demand. Readers tracking bullion can follow the daily [silver rate](/tools/silver-rate).

## What to watch next week

Three things will likely decide whether the 22,200 floor holds. The first is crude, since Brent has climbed sharply this month on attacks against Gulf energy infrastructure. The second is the run of September-quarter earnings, starting with how investors react on Monday to Infosys's results and its cut to full-year revenue guidance. The third is breadth. A market where four of every five stocks fall while the index barely moves is not stable. Either large caps give way and catch up with the decline, or buyers return to smaller stocks.

Analysts expect stock-specific moves to dominate for now, with earnings visibility and reasonable valuations separating winners from losers.
