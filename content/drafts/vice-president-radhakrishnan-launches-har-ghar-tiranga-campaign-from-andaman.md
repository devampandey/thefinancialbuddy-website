Vice President C P Radhakrishnan on Sunday launched this year's edition of the Har Ghar Tiranga Abhiyaan from Sri Vijaya Puram (Port Blair) in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, hoisting the national flag at Flag Point to mark August Kranti Diwas, the anniversary of the 1942 Quit India Movement.

## A Site With Historical Weight

The choice of venue was deliberate. It was at this very location in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands that Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose, leading the Azad Hind Fauj, hoisted the tricolour in 1943 and declared the islands free from British colonial rule — one of the earliest moments in which an Indian flag flew over Indian soil claimed as independent territory, years before formal independence in 1947. Referencing that history, the Vice President said "Bharat is one and will always remain one," framing the campaign as a continuation of the spirit of unity and sacrifice associated with the freedom struggle.

## What the Campaign Involves

The Har Ghar Tiranga Abhiyaan is a central government initiative, now in its fifth year, that encourages citizens across the country to hoist or display the national flag at their homes, offices and public institutions in the run-up to Independence Day on August 15. First launched in 2022 as part of the Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav commemorations marking 75 years of independence, the campaign has since become an annual fixture on the government's civic calendar, with state governments, municipal bodies and educational institutions typically organising flag distribution drives and community events through the first half of August.

By launching this year's campaign from the Andaman and Nicobar Islands rather than the national capital, the government appears to be using the symbolism of the archipelago's wartime history to anchor the messaging around national unity. In his remarks, the Vice President underlined themes of patriotism, sacrifice and collective identity, tying the flag-hoisting exercise to the broader idea of citizens actively participating in commemorating the country's freedom movement rather than treating it as a passive holiday.

## Broader Context

The launch comes at a time when the monsoon parliamentary session has been dominating national political headlines, with the government pushing through several legislative measures in recent days. The Vice President's Andaman visit and the accompanying campaign launch offered a comparatively low-key, ceremonial counterpoint to that legislative activity, focused on civic messaging rather than policy.

Official events of this kind typically see participation from local administration officials, defence personnel stationed in the islands, and school students, and are usually followed in the coming days by similar flag-hoisting ceremonies organised by state governments and local bodies across India as August 15 approaches. No further policy announcements were made alongside the launch.
