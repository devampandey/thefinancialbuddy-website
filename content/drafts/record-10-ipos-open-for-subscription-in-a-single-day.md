India's primary market notched a rare milestone on Wednesday, with as many as ten initial public offerings opening for subscription on the same day, one of the busiest single days for new share sales in nearly three decades. Collectively, the issues are seeking to raise close to Rs 6,950 crore from investors, underscoring the sheer scale of activity in India's IPO pipeline even as the secondary market navigates a volatile patch driven by rising crude oil prices.

Among the most closely watched of the ten offerings is Asset Reconstruction Company (India) Ltd, popularly known as Arcil, which opened its Rs 733 crore issue on Wednesday after raising about Rs 220 crore from anchor investors including Goldman Sachs India Asset Management, WhiteOak Capital, Tata Mutual Fund, Aditya Birla Sun Life Mutual Fund and Bajaj Life Insurance. The issue, structured entirely as an offer for sale of existing shares, carries a price band of Rs 132-139 and will remain open through Friday, September 11, with listing expected later this month.

## A crowded calendar of debutants

Also opening Wednesday was the IPO of rental marketplace Rentomojo, priced in a band of Rs 384-404 per share, alongside offerings from companies spanning payments, engineering and infrastructure segments, including Manipal Payment & Identity Solutions and Karamtara Engineering. The simultaneous listings reflect merchant bankers' efforts to push deals through before the year winds down, taking advantage of what has so far been resilient appetite among both institutional and retail investors for new-economy and financial-services issuances.

## Why so many issuers are rushing to market

Bankers tracking the primary market said companies and their advisers have been keen to complete fundraising before any prolonged bout of volatility, of the kind triggered by Wednesday's spike in crude oil prices, has a chance to dent investor sentiment or valuations. With more than a dozen offerings estimated to be in the market this week alone seeking a combined tens of billions of rupees, bankers describe the current window as one of the most active stretches for Indian IPOs in recent years, spanning sectors from asset reconstruction and fintech to consumer and industrial businesses.

Retail participation has been a recurring feature of this IPO wave, with several recent listings drawing outsized demand from individual investors even when institutional appetite has been more measured. Analysts caution, however, that the packed calendar means investors will need to be selective, weighing valuations and business fundamentals carefully rather than subscribing indiscriminately across every issue hitting the market this week.
