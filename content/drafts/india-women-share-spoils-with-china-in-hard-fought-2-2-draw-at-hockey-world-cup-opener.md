India's women's hockey team opened its FIH Hockey World Cup 2026 campaign with a hard-fought 2-2 draw against Olympic silver medallists China on Sunday, sharing the points after a fluctuating contest at the Wagener Hockey Stadium in Amstelveen, the Netherlands.

## India Twice Take the Lead

The Indian side made the brighter start, going ahead in the eighth minute when forward Navneet Kaur found the corner of the goal from inside the circle to put the Women in Blue 1-0 up. China hit back before the end of the opening quarter, earning a penalty stroke that they converted after an unsuccessful Indian video referral, levelling the score at 1-1.

India regained the advantage early in the second quarter through a penalty-corner routine, with a deflection looping the ball into the Chinese goal to make it 2-1. That slender lead held all the way to half-time, with India's defence weathering a spell of sustained Chinese pressure in the process.

## China Fight Back Again

The second half saw China come out with renewed intensity, and their persistence paid off six minutes before the end of the third quarter, when a sharp finish from the edge of the penalty circle beat the Indian goalkeeper to restore parity at 2-2. Both sides pushed for a winner through a tense final quarter — China even earned a late penalty corner — but neither team could find the decisive goal, and the match ended level.

## Squad and Standings

India fielded Savita in goal behind a lineup featuring Shilpi Dabas, Jyoti, Navneet Kaur, Sushila Chanu, Sunelita Toppo, captain Salima Tete, Neha, Rutuja Pisal, Sakshi Rana and Deepika. The draw leaves both India and China with a single point apiece from their opening Pool D fixture. India will look to build momentum when they take on South Africa in their next group match at the same venue.

## Part of a Busy World Cup Weekend for India

The result comes a day after India's men's team opened their own World Cup campaign in style, beating Wales 3-1 in Amstelveen, with captain Harmanpreet Singh among the scorers. Together, the results underline India's active push across both the men's and women's tournaments as the country looks to bolster its standing in international hockey after years of steady investment in the sport at the junior and senior levels.

For the women's team, Sunday's draw against a strong Chinese side that reached the Olympic final is likely to be viewed as a solid, if incomplete, start — a reminder of the squad's ability to trouble higher-ranked opposition, but also of the fine margins that will decide how far this campaign can go.
