The Union Cabinet on Wednesday approved a productivity-linked bonus equal to 78 days' wages for 11,40,770 non-gazetted railway employees, a payout that will cost the exchequer ₹2,009.61 crore. Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced the decision after the Cabinet meeting, describing it as recognition of the workforce's role in the national transporter's performance.

The money is timed to reach employees before Durga Puja and Dussehra, as it does every year.

## Who gets it and how much

The bonus covers Group C staff across the railway system, including track maintainers, loco pilots, train managers (formerly called guards), station masters, supervisors, technicians and their helpers, pointsmen and ministerial staff.

The maximum any single employee can receive is ₹17,951. That ceiling has stayed unchanged for years because the calculation uses a notional wage cap of ₹7,000 a month rather than an employee's actual pay. Seventy-eight days at that capped rate works out to just under ₹18,000, which means most eligible workers receive a similar amount regardless of seniority.

## How this year compares

The 78-day formula is now a long-standing annual practice, so the more telling numbers are the outlay and the headcount. Last year, the Cabinet cleared about ₹1,866 crore for roughly 10.9 lakh employees. This year's bill is about 8% higher, mainly because around 50,000 more staff qualify.

The figure is still slightly below the ₹2,029 crore approved in 2024, when the bonus covered about 11.72 lakh employees. The year-to-year swings largely reflect changes in the number of eligible non-gazetted staff rather than any change in the formula itself.

## The performance numbers behind the payout

The government linked the bonus to operating results for 2025-26. Indian Railways handled a record 1,670 million tonnes of freight during the year and carried close to 7.41 billion passengers. Both figures are up from the previous year, when freight loading stood at about 1,615 million tonnes and passenger journeys at around 7.3 billion.

Freight is the more significant of the two for the railways' finances, since goods traffic cross-subsidises passenger fares. Steady growth in loading also matters for the wider economy, as rail moves a large share of India's coal, cement, steel and food grain.

## Why it matters

For more than 11 lakh families, the bonus is a meaningful festive-season cash boost at a time when household budgets are under pressure. Retail inflation recently touched a 23-month high, driven by a sharp rise in onion and other food prices, and wholesale inflation for September came in at 11.39%. A lump sum of up to ₹17,951 arriving before the festivals is likely to flow quickly into consumer spending.

For the government, the decision is routine but politically visible. Railway employees form one of the largest single workforces in the country, and the annual bonus announcement is closely watched by staff unions. With the payment cleared well ahead of Durga Puja, the focus now shifts to how quickly the zonal railways credit the money to employees' accounts.
