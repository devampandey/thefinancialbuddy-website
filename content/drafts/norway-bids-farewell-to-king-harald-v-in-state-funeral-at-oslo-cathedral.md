Norway held a state funeral for King Harald V on Wednesday, drawing European royalty, heads of state and dignitaries to Oslo to bid farewell to a monarch who reigned for 35 years. Harald, who had become Europe's oldest reigning monarch before his death on August 28 at age 89, was remembered as a modernizing figure who steered the Norwegian royal house through decades of social change while resisting the trend among some aging European monarchs of stepping aside.

The ceremony began with a procession carrying the king's coffin from the Royal Palace along Karl Johans gate to Oslo Cathedral, where Crown Prince Haakon, now expected to formally ascend the throne, led senior royals, foreign guests and representatives of Norway's parliament into the service. The funeral was conducted by the Church of Norway's presiding bishop, with eulogies delivered by the speaker of parliament and the prime minister, and a candle-lighting ceremony involving representatives of different faiths underscored the country's growing religious diversity.

At the start of the service, Norway observed a nationwide minute of silence, with church bells tolling and public transport pausing in tribute, a gesture organizers said reflected the affection in which the king was held by ordinary Norwegians. After the cathedral service, a smaller procession was set to accompany the coffin to the Royal Mausoleum at Akershus Fortress for a private interment attended by family members and visiting royal houses.

## World leaders in attendance

Among the foreign dignitaries in Oslo was Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, who arrived a day ahead of the funeral for separate talks with Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store on air defense cooperation and winter support for Ukraine, before joining the ceremony to pay his respects. Sweden's King Carl Gustaf and Queen Silvia led a large Scandinavian royal delegation, alongside Crown Princess Victoria, Prince Carl Philip and Princess Madeleine, reflecting the close ties between the Nordic royal houses. The United States sent Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs Allison Hooker to represent Washington, while a broad roster of European royals and heads of state also travelled to Oslo for the occasion.

## A reign defined by continuity

Harald ascended to the throne in 1991 following the death of his father, Olav V, and became known for a relatively informal style that included attending public events without heavy security and speaking candidly about inclusivity in Norwegian society. He had undergone heart valve surgery in recent years and had been hospitalized intermittently before his death, but continued to carry out ceremonial duties for as long as his health allowed. His passing sets in motion a period of official mourning in Norway even as the country continues to play a prominent role in supporting Ukraine militarily and financially amid the ongoing war with Russia.

With Haakon now stepping into the role of head of state, attention turns to how the incoming king will balance the ceremonial demands of the monarchy with Norway's expanding diplomatic footprint, including its position as one of Kyiv's most significant European backers.
