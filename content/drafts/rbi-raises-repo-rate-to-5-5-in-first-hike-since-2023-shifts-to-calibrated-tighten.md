The Reserve Bank of India raised its key lending rate on Wednesday for the first time since February 2023, ending a long stretch in which borrowers had either enjoyed cuts or a steady rate. The six-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) voted unanimously to lift the repo rate by 25 basis points, from 5.25% to 5.50%.

The bigger signal came with the change in stance. The committee dropped its "neutral" position in favour of "calibrated tightening", and Governor Sanjay Malhotra made it plain that the next move can only be another hike or a pause. A rate cut, he indicated, is not on the table in the near term.

## Why the RBI moved now

Malhotra said that, on the available data, inflation and its outlook were no longer as benign as they were a year ago. The backdrop has changed sharply. Brent crude has been trading above $100 a barrel as the war involving Iran keeps shipping through the Strait of Hormuz under threat, and the rupee has slipped past 96 to the US dollar. Both feed directly into India's import bill and, eventually, into retail prices.

The central bank's new projections show the pressure building over the next few quarters. Consumer price inflation for the full financial year 2026-27 is now forecast at 5.2%. The quarter-by-quarter path is steeper than the annual figure suggests:

- July-September 2026: 4.9%
- October-December 2026: 6.0%
- January-March 2027: 5.7%
- April-June 2027: 5.6%

A 6% print in the December quarter would put inflation at the top of the RBI's tolerance band, and the projections do not show it returning to the 4% target within the forecast window.

## Growth forecast goes up, not down

Unusually for a tightening decision, the RBI also raised its growth estimate. Real GDP growth for FY27 is now projected at 7.1%, 40 basis points higher than before. The quarterly forecasts are 7.2% for July-September, 6.9% for October-December and 6.8% for January-March. Malhotra described economic momentum as strong and broad-based, which gives the central bank room to focus on prices without worrying as much about choking off activity.

The Governor also noted that foreign portfolio investors have pulled a net $10.3 billion out of Indian markets this year up to October 5, and announced that the RBI will set up a consultative committee for financial markets to engage more formally with market participants.

## How markets reacted

The hike was widely expected, so the reaction was measured. The Nifty 50 had been down about 0.7% before the announcement and pared some of its losses once the higher growth forecast came through, trading around 22,700 by late morning. Rate-sensitive auto and realty stocks were among the weaker pockets, while public sector bank shares gained around 1%, since lenders with loans linked to the repo rate tend to see a short-term lift in margins. The yield on India's 10-year government bond jumped 8 basis points to 7.27%.

## What it means for borrowers

For anyone with a floating-rate home or car loan linked to the repo rate, a 25 basis point increase will usually show up as a higher EMI or a longer tenure at the next reset. Because the RBI has signalled that more hikes are possible, borrowers may want to check how much room their budget has. Some economists believe the cycle has further to run. Elara Capital's Garima Kapoor said she sees a likelihood of another 50 basis points of hikes, pointing to commodity price pressures and a tightening global rate environment. The next data point that could shift the outlook is October's consumer inflation reading, the first to fall inside the quarter where the RBI expects inflation to peak.
