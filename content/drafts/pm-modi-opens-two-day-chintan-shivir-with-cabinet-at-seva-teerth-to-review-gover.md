Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Wednesday began a two-day "Chintan Shivir," or brainstorming retreat, with Union Cabinet ministers at Seva Teerth in New Delhi, a governance review that has fuelled speculation over a possible reshuffle of the Council of Ministers even as no such change has been officially announced.

## What's Happening

The complex at Seva Teerth, which houses the Prime Minister's Office, the National Security Council Secretariat and the Cabinet Secretariat, was formally inaugurated earlier this year and is hosting the retreat for the first time at this scale. On the opening day, every Cabinet minister is presenting an account of the work carried out by their respective ministry over the past year, along with a self-analysed report card assessing progress against targets and operational outcomes.

The second day, September 24, is expected to focus on a detailed review of those presentations, along with a broader discussion of policy implementation, ongoing government schemes, structural reforms and inter-ministerial coordination. Officials say the sessions are also meant to identify priorities for the next phase of governance, framed around the government's long-term Viksit Bharat 2047 vision for a developed India.

## Behind the Format

Ahead of the retreat, Modi had reportedly formed several eight-member ministerial groups tasked with deliberating on themes such as self-improvement, public communication and administrative efficiency, a structure similar to a Chintan Shivir held in October 2021. Ministers were asked to prepare their self-assessments in advance, and the retreat format is designed to surface both successes and gaps in execution directly from the ministers responsible, rather than through a top-down review alone.

## The Reshuffle Question

Political circles have read the timing and scale of the exercise as a signal that changes to the Union Council of Ministers could follow, particularly given the performance-review format of the sessions. However, government sources have stopped short of confirming any reshuffle, describing the Shivir instead as a governance and performance review exercise. Any changes to ministerial portfolios, if they come, are likely to be announced separately rather than during the retreat itself.

## Why It Matters

Chintan Shivirs of this kind have historically served as a checkpoint for the government to recalibrate priorities roughly midway through a term, and this year's edition comes as the administration looks to sharpen delivery on flagship programmes ahead of the next major policy and budget cycles. For ministries and the bureaucracy, the exercise sets the tone for how performance will be measured and communicated over the coming months, even if no immediate personnel changes are announced this week.
