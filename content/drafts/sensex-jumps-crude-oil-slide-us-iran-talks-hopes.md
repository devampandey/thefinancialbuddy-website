Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply higher on Monday, with the BSE Sensex jumping nearly 789 points and the Nifty 50 crossing the 24,550 mark, as a steep overnight slide in crude oil prices lifted sentiment across the market. The rally was driven largely by news that the United States would hold talks with Iran rather than pursue fresh military action, easing fears of a wider conflict in West Asia that had been weighing on global risk appetite in recent sessions.

## What Moved the Market

The Sensex opened at 78,883.34, up 788.7 points, or roughly 1 percent, from its previous close of 78,094.64. The Nifty 50 added 189.1 points to open at 24,572.70. By mid-morning, gains had moderated slightly, with the Nifty trading around 24,568.75, up 0.76 percent, and the Sensex near 78,666.94, up 0.73 percent, as some of the initial enthusiasm cooled.

The trigger was a sharp drop in crude oil prices, with Brent futures falling more than 5 percent to around $83 a barrel after US President Donald Trump indicated that talks with Iran were set to take place, raising hopes that military escalation could be avoided. For India, which imports the vast majority of its crude requirements, a sustained decline in oil prices is broadly positive: it eases pressure on the import bill, supports the rupee, and can help contain inflation, all of which feed into corporate margins for energy-intensive sectors.

## Sector and Stock Moves

The rally was broad-based but led by a handful of names. InterGlobe Aviation, which operates IndiGo, was among the top gainers, rising over 3 percent, as lower fuel costs directly benefit airline margins. ITC, Bajaj Finance, Infosys and Tata Steel also featured among the Nifty's gainers in early trade. Not every stock joined the rally, however; Muthoot Finance shares fell sharply, down more than 14 percent, underscoring that the broader positive mood did not lift every corner of the market equally.

## Institutional Flows and the Backdrop

Institutional activity offered additional support. Domestic institutional investors turned net buyers, picking up equities worth roughly Rs 2,260 crore, while foreign institutional investors also remained on the buying side, adding a smaller Rs 277 crore worth of shares in the prior session. That combination of steady domestic buying and modest foreign inflows has helped cushion Indian markets even as global crude and geopolitical headlines have swung sentiment from one session to the next.

The rally also comes as the Reserve Bank of India's rate-setting committee began its scheduled deliberations Monday, with a policy announcement due later in the week. While Monday's gains were driven primarily by the oil price move rather than rate expectations, traders will be watching closely for any signals on the central bank's read on inflation now that energy costs appear to be easing.

## What It Means for Investors

Monday's session was a reminder of how sensitive Indian markets remain to global crude oil swings and geopolitical headlines out of West Asia. A sustained de-escalation between the US and Iran, if it holds, would be a genuine tailwind for a net oil-importing economy like India. At the same time, the sharp divergence between broad index gains and steep individual stock declines, such as Muthoot Finance's fall, is a useful reminder that index-level optimism does not always translate evenly across sectors, and investors would do well to look past the headline numbers to what is actually driving moves in the stocks they hold.
