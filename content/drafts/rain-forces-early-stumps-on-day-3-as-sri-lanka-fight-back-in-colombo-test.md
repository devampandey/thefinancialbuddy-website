Sri Lanka showed some fight on the third day of the second Test against India in Colombo, but rain and fading light once again intervened to cut the day's play short, with the hosts closing on 265 for 8 in 83.4 overs at the Sinhalese Sports Club.

Chasing down India's mammoth first-innings total of 503 for 9 declared, Sri Lanka still trail by 238 runs with only two second-innings wickets in hand heading into Day 4. The weather has been a recurring disruption throughout this Test, with a rain delay of more than two hours also affecting play on the previous day, and forecasts pointing to a continued chance of showers in Colombo.

## Sooriyabandara and Dinusha Provide Resistance

The day's most notable feature was the resilience shown by Sri Lanka's middle order after a shaky start. Debutant concussion substitute Pasindu Sooriyabandara compiled a composed 80 off 133 balls before eventually falling to a delivery from Ravindra Jadeja, giving the hosts some much-needed stability in what had looked like a lopsided contest. At the other end, Sonal Dinusha continued the good form he showed in the earlier Galle Test, remaining unbeaten on 85 and recording his third consecutive fifty-plus score across as many innings in the series.

India's bowling effort was led by Prasidh Krishna, who picked up multiple wickets through the day and kept probing at Sri Lanka's fragile top order, while Manav Suthar also chipped in with breakthroughs earlier in the innings. Despite the resistance from the middle order, India's bowlers ensured Sri Lanka never fully escaped the pressure created by the size of the first-innings deficit.

## A Dominant Position Built Over Two Days

India's position in this match was built on standout centuries earlier in the game. Devdutt Padikkal's hundred anchored the innings on Day 1, before Dhruv Jurel's unbeaten 115, supported by a quick-fire 63 from Rishabh Pant, propelled India past the 500-run mark on Day 2. That total has left Sri Lanka fighting an uphill battle simply to avoid an innings defeat, let alone level the two-match series after already trailing following the Galle Test.

With Sri Lanka two wickets away from being bowled out and still needing 238 runs just to make India bat again, the hosts face a difficult final stretch when play resumes. India will be eager to wrap up the innings quickly on Day 4 and enforce further pressure, while Sri Lanka's lower order will need to dig in if the tourists are to be denied a series sweep. Bad light and rain remain the other opponent this Test has had to contend with, and another interrupted day cannot be ruled out as the match heads towards its conclusion.
