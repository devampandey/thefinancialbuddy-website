Hindustan Unilever (HUL) gave traders two very different readings of the same quarterly report on Wednesday. Right after its September-quarter numbers, the stock rose about 2.3% to an intraday high of Rs 1,861 on the NSE. Within hours it had reversed and dropped to Rs 1,765.50, more than 5% below that high and only a few rupees above the six-year low of about Rs 1,758 it hit on Tuesday.

By 12:10 pm the shares had settled around Rs 1,803, down about 0.8%, on roughly 19 lakh shares traded on the NSE. That compared with a 0.4% fall in the Nifty 50, which was trading near 22,655 at the time after a crude-driven sell-off earlier in the session.

## Two numbers pulling in opposite directions

The early buying was about the top line. HUL's underlying sales growth (USG) was 15%, its fastest in 14 quarters, and underlying volume growth (UVG) was a solid 7%. Consolidated revenue from operations rose nearly 15% to a little over Rs 18,000 crore. For a company whose volumes have stalled for much of the last two years, a 7% volume print is the number the market had been waiting for.

The selling was about what happened below the top line. Consolidated net profit rose just 3.9% to Rs 2,790 crore, from Rs 2,685 crore a year earlier. EBITDA grew 8% to Rs 4,089 crore, about half the pace of sales, and the EBITDA margin narrowed 140 basis points to 22.6% from 24%. HUL said the margin is within its guided range. Still, sales growing 15% while profit grows 4% is the kind of gap that makes investors ask whether the volume recovery is being bought with heavier promotions, higher ad spending or input costs the company has chosen not to pass on.

## Segment mix

Home care and beauty and wellness grew revenue in double digits. Personal care and foods grew around 8% each. The company declared an interim dividend of Rs 19 per share, with November 12 as the record date and payment due on November 25.

CEO and managing director Priya Nair described consumer demand as resilient and reiterated the company's focus on volume-led profit growth, pointing to investment in brands and in its distribution network.

## Why the reaction matters

HUL has been one of the weakest large-cap stocks of 2026, down roughly 24% for the calendar year before these results. Stocks that have fallen that far often bounce on any sign of better demand. Wednesday's failed rally suggests investors want proof that growth can turn into earnings before they pay up for the recovery.

The broader market didn't help. FMCG and IT were among the weakest sectors as the Sensex at one point fell close to 1,000 points with Brent crude above $115 a barrel. Rising oil prices raise HUL's costs directly, through packaging and the crude-linked chemicals used in its detergents. That links the stock's margin worries to the same global factor that was hitting the whole index.

## The level to watch

The six-year low around Rs 1,758 is now the line traders are watching. Wednesday's dip stopped just above it. A close below that level would put the stock at its lowest since 2020. Holding it, after a quarter with the strongest sales growth in more than three years, would give buyers a clear support level to build on.
