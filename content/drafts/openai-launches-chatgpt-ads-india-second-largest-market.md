OpenAI has started showing advertisements inside ChatGPT for users in India, marking one of the company's most significant monetisation moves in what it has repeatedly described as one of its largest and most active markets globally.

The ads are being rolled out to users on ChatGPT's free tier as well as its Go plan, which costs roughly Rs 399, or about $4, per month in India. Subscribers on the higher-priced Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Education tiers will continue to see no advertising, preserving an ad-free experience for paying premium users.

## How the Rollout Works

OpenAI said it is launching the India ad program with around 50 brand partners at the outset, working alongside global advertising agencies WPP and Omnicom to manage the initial campaigns. A self-serve ad manager is scheduled to open on September 4, after which marketers will be able to run their own campaigns directly, with a daily minimum budget requirement of roughly Rs 725, or about $7.60.

According to the company, ads will appear beneath ChatGPT's responses rather than alongside or within them, and will carry clear labelling to distinguish sponsored content from the assistant's own output.

## OpenAI's Assurances on Answer Independence

The company has been keen to address concerns that advertising could compromise the neutrality of ChatGPT's responses. OpenAI's India leadership has stated that advertisers will not gain access to user conversations, that ads will not influence how ChatGPT answers questions, and that users will retain control over how their data is used for personalisation. The company has described "answer independence" as non-negotiable to its overall product philosophy, a message aimed at reassuring both users and regulators as it expands advertising into new markets.

## Why India, and Why Now

India has emerged as one of ChatGPT's largest user bases worldwide, and OpenAI has been steadily deepening its investment in the market, including offering free access to ChatGPT Go for extended periods and expanding its local leadership team. Introducing advertising in India ahead of many other markets reflects both the country's scale and OpenAI's need to diversify revenue streams as it works toward a planned initial public offering in 2027.

Building a meaningful advertising business is widely seen as central to that IPO narrative, giving the company a second major revenue stream beyond subscriptions as it continues to spend heavily on compute infrastructure and model development.

## What It Means for Advertisers and Users

For Indian advertisers, the launch opens a new large-scale digital channel at a moment when platforms like Google and Meta already dominate the digital ad market. For users on the free and Go tiers, the shift means their experience will now include sponsored content for the first time, a trade-off OpenAI is betting will be acceptable in exchange for keeping access affordable or free. How Indian users respond to ads embedded in an AI assistant, rather than a traditional search or social feed, will be closely watched as other AI companies weigh similar monetisation paths.
