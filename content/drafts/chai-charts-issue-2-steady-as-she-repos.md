The RBI kept its hand steady on rates this week, but everything around it — earnings, PMI, foreign flows, and a fair bit of politics — moved plenty.

## This Week in a Nutshell

- **RBI held the repo rate at 5.25%**, flagging West Asia disruption as a risk to watch. Markets liked the "no surprises" verdict anyway: Sensex jumped 626 points and Nifty topped 24,650 on decision day as crude slipped below $80 a barrel.
- **Q1 FY27 earnings season ran hot.** SBI's profit rose 10% to Rs. 21,121 crore, Bharti Airtel jumped 43%, Trent grew 22%, and Nykaa's profit nearly tripled to Rs. 79.76 crore — a solid batch of prints from banking to retail to beauty.
- **IPO demand stayed frenzied.** MV Electrosystems' issue was oversubscribed almost 200 times, Juniper Green Energy and Leap India both drew heavy bidding, and Manipal Health Enterprises turned a muted opening into an 11% debut-day pop.
- **Foreign investors have now pulled a cumulative $58 billion from Indian equities over 22 months**, even as domestic buyers kept indices grinding higher — a quiet tug-of-war beneath the headline numbers.
- **Manufacturing PMI slipped to a near five-year low**, a soft patch in domestic demand that sits uneasily next to a market near record levels.
- **Gold hit fresh highs in India** on a weaker dollar, and exchanges rolled out a new closing-auction mechanism the same week.
- **Parliament stayed busy**, passing bills recognising digital banking records as legal evidence, the Taxation and Other Laws Amendment Bill, and a bill expanding the Supreme Court to 38 judges — against a backdrop of bypoll upsets in Bihar and Madhya Pradesh.
- **TCS drew scrutiny from Karnataka's labour department** over roughly 12,000 job cuts, a reminder that IT's growth headlines don't always extend to headcount.

## Markets This Week

It was a week that leaned positive even with a bumpy finish. The Sensex closed Friday at 78,499.17 and the Nifty 50 at 24,570.65 — both down on the day (financials dragged, Bajaj Finance led losses) but still up on the week from last Friday's 78,094.64 and 24,383.60, a gain of roughly half a percent for the Sensex and three-quarters of a percent for the Nifty. The real fireworks came mid-week, when the RBI's steady-rate verdict and a retreat in crude oil sent the Sensex up 626 points in a single session. Gold and silver both had a strong week — gold's 24-karat rate in India climbed to roughly Rs. 1,49,730 per 10 grams, up over 4% from last week, while silver surged nearly 10% to around Rs. 2,39,900 per kilogram, both riding a weaker dollar. The rupee, meanwhile, held its ground, firming marginally against the dollar to close near 95.16.

## Cheers, Fears & Jeers

**Cheers:** Q1 earnings season delivered across the board — SBI, Airtel, Trent, and Nykaa all beat expectations, and Manipal Health's turnaround from a muted opening to an 11% debut pop suggests investor appetite for new listings is still healthy despite a crowded IPO calendar.

**Fears:** Manufacturing PMI sliding to a near five-year low and $58 billion in cumulative FII outflows over 22 months point to fragility beneath the market's cheerful surface — domestic flows have papered over the gap so far, but that's not a permanent fix.

**Jeers:** TCS's roughly 12,000 job cuts drawing a formal summons from Karnataka's labour department was this week's reminder that "resilient IT sector" headlines don't always translate to job security on the ground.

## Person/Story of the Week

Prashant Kishor's Jan Suraaj party pulled off its first real electoral breakthrough this week, winning the Bankipur bypoll in what had long been considered BJP territory. For a strategist who spent nearly two decades shaping other politicians' campaigns, it's the clearest sign yet that his own political project has legs. One bypoll doesn't make a movement, but it's the kind of result that changes how seriously rivals — and voters — take what comes next.

## The Lighter Side

Badminton had a genuinely good week for India. Tanvi Sharma stormed through the Korea Masters draw to claim her maiden title, while Ashmita Chaliha upset a top seed to set up an all-Indian semifinal. Add in India's 39-medal closing tally at the 2026 Commonwealth Games — handed over to Ahmedabad, host for 2030 — and it was a week worth cheering beyond the ticker.
