Red Bull can keep calling its product an energy drink in India, at least for now. The Delhi High Court on Tuesday quashed an order from the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) that had told the Austrian beverage maker to drop the description from its cans.

Justice Amit Mahajan did not rule on whether the label is scientifically or legally appropriate. Instead, the court found that the regulator had issued its direction without giving Red Bull any opportunity to put forward its side, a basic requirement of fair procedure.

## A Procedural Win, Not a Final Verdict

The distinction matters. Because the order was set aside on natural-justice grounds, FSSAI is free to revisit the issue after hearing the company. A government official told Reuters that the regulator plans to appeal the decision, citing public health concerns.

The signal was visible a day earlier. When the matter came up on Monday, the judge asked whether Red Bull had received a show-cause notice or a hearing before the order was passed, and remarked that an order issued without notice could not stand. The court sought instructions from the regulator before ruling on Tuesday.

## How the Dispute Began

FSSAI issued the direction on June 30, 2026, telling makers of high-caffeine beverages sold as energy drinks to stop using that description. A follow-up communication on July 17 asked food safety officers across states and Union Territories to act against products that did not comply.

Red Bull challenged both. Its India unit argued that the sudden ban created serious regulatory uncertainty and hurt its current and planned investments in the country. The company pointed to a March 2024 FSSAI advisory, which had said the term was allowed for products licensed under the relevant caffeinated beverage categories, and to a Union Health Ministry press release from April 2024. Red Bull also argued that the regulator had changed the rules on labelling without changing the underlying product standard.

## Why the Wider Industry Is Watching

Red Bull was the only company to go to court, but it is far from the only one affected. Pepsi, Monster Beverage and Mukesh Ambani's Reliance had all privately objected to the move, according to Reuters, worried that losing the category label would weaken brands they have spent years building.

The stakes are significant. India's energy drink market is expected to be worth about $1.6 billion by 2028. Much of its growth came after Pepsi launched Sting in 2017, with its Rs 20 plastic bottles becoming popular among teenagers and in rural markets, according to consumer research firm Euromonitor.

## The Health Question Has Not Gone Away

Regulators in several countries have grown more cautious about energy drinks because of their caffeine, sugar and taurine content, which research has linked to problems such as high blood pressure and heart issues. England, for instance, will bar sales of such drinks to under-16s from April next year.

The ruling also lands during a broader push on food labelling in India. The Supreme Court has been pressing the government over the timeline for front-of-pack warning labels on packaged foods, a debate that has already drawn pushback from large food companies.

For beverage makers, Tuesday's order buys time rather than certainty. If FSSAI restarts the process with a proper hearing, or wins on appeal, the energy drink label could come back under threat.
