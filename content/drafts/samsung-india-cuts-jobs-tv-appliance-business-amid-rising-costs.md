Samsung India has begun laying off executives across its television and home appliance businesses, according to multiple reports on Tuesday, as the electronics giant undertakes a cost-rationalisation drive amid rising operating expenses, softer consumer demand and broader organisational restructuring.

## Scope of the Layoffs

Reports indicate that around 80 to 100 executives have been asked to leave so far, cutting across levels that include directors, team leaders, branch managers and area managers. The job cuts are reportedly being carried out in phases, with termination letters issued over the past several days, and some affected employees said to have been asked to exit immediately without serving out their notice period. Samsung is said to be offering a severance package comprising three months' salary plus an additional month's pay for every completed year of service. When approached for comment, Samsung declined to address the reports.

## What's Driving the Cuts

The restructuring comes as consumer electronics companies globally contend with softening demand and mounting cost pressures. Higher memory chip prices, elevated raw material costs and the depreciation of the Indian rupee have all added to operating expenses for manufacturers with significant India exposure. Compounding the pressure, India's smartphone shipments have declined by roughly 12 per cent year-on-year, and some industry analysts expect the broader Indian smartphone market to contract by around 13 per cent for 2026 as a whole, though they anticipate the second half of the year to outperform the first as festive-season demand kicks in.

Samsung had also been planning to merge its television and home appliance sales teams as part of the same restructuring push, but that integration has reportedly been deferred until the December quarter, suggesting the company is proceeding cautiously with organisational changes even as it moves ahead with the layoffs.

## A Profitable Business Under Pressure

Notably, the job cuts come despite Samsung India remaining a substantial and profitable operation. The company reported revenue of more than Rs 1.1 lakh crore and a net profit of Rs 11,287 crore for FY25, underscoring that the layoffs are being driven by cost discipline and margin protection rather than outright financial distress.

## Why It Matters for India's Electronics Market

The move reflects a broader recalibration under way across India's consumer electronics sector, where companies are having to balance rising input costs against a demand environment that has been inconsistent through much of the year. For an industry gearing up for its most important selling season around Diwali, the timing of Samsung's restructuring is notable: the company appears to be trimming overhead even as it prepares to compete for festive-season market share, a balancing act that will test how effectively it can sustain sales momentum with a leaner organisational structure. How rivals in the television and appliance space respond to Samsung's cost actions in the coming weeks could offer an early signal of how the wider industry is bracing for margin pressure heading into 2027.
