India's men's hockey team booked a place in the semi-finals of the Asian Champions Trophy on Friday night, beating Japan 4-1 at the Olympian Surjit Singh Hockey Stadium in Jalandhar. It was the defending champions' third win in three matches, and the night belonged to forward Abhishek, who scored twice on his 150th international appearance and was named player of the match.

The scoreline makes it look easy. For three quarters it was anything but.

## Japan made India work

Japan pressed high from the opening whistle and won a penalty corner in the second minute. India struck first in the 11th minute, when Sukhjeet Singh turned sharply on the left of the circle and cut the ball back for Abhishek to finish.

Japan were level before the quarter ended. A drag-flick from captain Raiki Fujishima struck defender Sanjay on the goal line, and the umpires awarded a penalty stroke. India's review failed, and Shota Yamada converted to make it 1-1.

The second and third quarters were a stalemate. Japan held more of the ball before half-time and defended compactly, denying India space inside the circle. Goalkeeper Krishan Bahadur Pathak had to rush off his line to stop one Japanese attack, and a backhand from Angad Bir Singh and a Shilanand Lakra tomahawk were the best India could produce.

## Three goals in nine minutes

The match turned in the 47th minute when India finally earned their first penalty corner of the night and captain Harmanpreet Singh drilled it in. It was his fifth goal of the tournament, and every one has come from a penalty corner, a reminder of how much India's scoring still leans on set pieces.

Four minutes later, Dilpreet Singh found Sanjay on the left and the defender squared it for Abhishek's second. Vishnu Kant Singh, one of two players brought into the side along with Angad Bir Singh, sealed it in the 56th minute. He controlled a long aerial ball from Harmanpreet outside the circle, drove in, and flicked past the goalkeeper. Mandeep Singh and Nilakanta Sharma were rested for the match.

Abhishek said head coach Craig Fulton had asked the team at half-time to put Japan under more pressure, and the plan in the final quarter was to press higher and control possession.

## Malaysia next, with first place at stake

India now have nine points from three games and a goal difference of +19, after opening with a 13-0 rout of Bangladesh and a 5-2 win over China. Malaysia are level on nine points after beating Bangladesh 6-1 earlier on Friday, but trail on goal difference at +8. Japan are third on six points, and South Korea picked up their first win by edging China 2-1.

The two unbeaten sides meet on Sunday, November 1, at 7 pm IST in Jalandhar, with top spot in the standings on the line. Harmanpreet was named to lead this squad earlier this month ([see our squad coverage](/sports/harmanpreet-to-lead-india-at-asian-champions-trophy-in-punjab-as-pathak-returns)). The concern for Fulton is that India needed until the final quarter to break down a well-organised Japan side. Semi-final opponents are unlikely to give them that much time.
