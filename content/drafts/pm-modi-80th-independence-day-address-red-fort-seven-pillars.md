Prime Minister Narendra Modi addressed the nation from the ramparts of the Red Fort in Delhi on Saturday morning, marking India's 80th Independence Day and delivering his 13th consecutive speech from the historic site since taking office. The ceremony began with Modi's arrival at around 7:13 AM, a Guard of Honour inspection by a tri-service contingent, and the unfurling of the Tricolour at approximately 7:30 AM, accompanied by a 21-gun salute. In a first for the occasion, the national song "Vande Mataram" was sung from the ramparts to mark 150 years since its composition by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay.

## Seven Pillars of Development

The centerpiece of the address was what Modi described as "Shakti ki Sapt Dhara," or seven streams of strength, framed as the government's forward-looking development roadmap. The seven focus areas named were manufacturing, agriculture and food processing, technology and innovation, Gati-Shakti infrastructure planning, Raksha Shakti (defence capability), the green and blue economy, and India's soft power on the global stage. This year's broader theme, "Yuva Shakti for Viksit Bharat@2047," placed young Indians at the centre of the government's long-term vision for a developed nation by the 100th year of independence.

## Semiconductor Manufacturing Gets a Fresh Push

Modi used the platform to reiterate India's ambitions in chip manufacturing, telling the gathering that the world would come to a standstill without semiconductors and that India was moving steadily toward self-reliance in the sector. He noted that three semiconductor plants in the country have already begun exporting chips, and said five to eight more facilities are expected to come up over the next seven to eight years. The remarks build on the Union Cabinet's approval last month of Semicon 2.0, a program worth roughly Rs 1.27 lakh crore intended to build an end-to-end domestic ecosystem spanning chip design, manufacturing equipment, new fabrication plants, advanced packaging and specialised talent development.

## A Bid for the 2036 Olympics

On the sporting front, Modi reaffirmed India's intent to bid for the 2036 Olympic Games, calling the country a strong contender to host the event. He announced a nationwide talent identification drive aimed at children between the ages of 5 and 15, intended to build a long-term pipeline of athletes. The announcement ties into the government's existing Target Olympic Podium Scheme, first launched in 2014 to support elite athletes, and comes as India continues to position sport as a component of its broader soft-power ambitions.

## Context and Reception

This year's Independence Day address carries added symbolic weight as India marks its 80th year of independence, and coverage across national outlets emphasised the speech's dual focus on economic self-reliance and youth engagement. Security in Delhi was reported to be tight around the Red Fort, national capital landmarks and the airport ahead of the celebrations, consistent with the scale of the annual event. Modi's address is watched closely each year as a signal of near-term policy priorities, and this year's emphasis on manufacturing, chips and infrastructure suggests these areas are likely to feature prominently in government messaging and budgetary focus over the coming year.

The seven-pillar framing and the semiconductor commitments in particular are likely to draw attention from industry watchers, given the scale of capital already committed to India's chip ambitions and the government's stated timeline of under a decade to significantly expand domestic fabrication capacity.
