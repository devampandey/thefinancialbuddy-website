Crude oil prices extended their slide into a second session on Wednesday, and Indian equity benchmarks opened firmer, as fresh signs of progress in talks between Iran and Oman over the Strait of Hormuz fuelled hopes that one of the world's most important oil chokepoints could soon see disrupted traffic ease.

Brent crude futures fell $1.78, or about 2%, to $86.80 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude dropped $1.49, or 1.8%, to $80.87, building on losses of more than 3% in the previous session. The declines came after Iran said it had resumed talks with Oman on managing transit through the strait, which carries roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil and liquefied natural gas under normal conditions but has seen traffic largely paralysed since the region's war broke out in February.

## What's Driving the Move

Market participants pointed directly to the diplomatic track as the trigger for the latest leg of the sell-off. "The market continues to react to developments surrounding navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, and hopes for progress in talks between Iran and Oman have triggered selling," said Mitsuru Muraishi, an analyst at Fujitomi Securities, who also cautioned that lingering uncertainty over the outlook was prompting some bargain buying and could keep prices range-bound in the near term.

Adding to the bearish tone for oil, the American Petroleum Institute reported that US crude inventories rose by about 4.2 million barrels in the week ended August 21 — well above the roughly 600,000-barrel build that analysts polled by Reuters had expected. Official inventory data from the US Energy Information Administration was due later in the day. The market's reaction came even as a fresh reminder of the underlying risk emerged Tuesday, when an oil tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile and disabled near the entrance to the strait off Oman's coast, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency.

## Indian Markets Track the Global Mood

The retreat in crude, a key input cost for oil-importing India, helped lift sentiment in domestic equities at the open. The Sensex rose over 150 points in early trade while the Nifty50 hovered around the 24,300-24,330 band, with gains led by banking and select PSU names. Stocks including ICICI Bank, Bajaj Finserv, Asian Paints and Eternal featured among the early gainers on the 30-share index, while the broader Nifty Midcap and Smallcap indices also traded higher.

The move mirrored a broadly positive session across Asia, with Japan's Nikkei climbing around 0.5%, China's Shanghai Composite up about 0.5%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng gaining nearly 1%, and South Korea's Kospi advancing over 1%, as lower crude prices offered relief to import-dependent economies across the region.

## The Bigger Picture

For India specifically, cheaper crude has an outsized impact given the country imports the vast majority of its oil needs, feeding through to the trade deficit, inflation expectations and, by extension, sentiment in rate-sensitive sectors. Even so, traders cautioned that the rally in risk assets remains contingent on the Iran-Oman talks translating into an actual, durable reopening of shipping lanes rather than just a diplomatic framework — meaning volatility around Hormuz-linked headlines is likely to persist in the sessions ahead.
