India's consumer protection regulator has given app makers a clear warning about how they ask users for money. The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has fined ANI Technologies, the company behind Ola, Rs 10 lakh over the app's advance tipping feature. It has also ordered Ola to remove any prompt, message or screen design that pushes riders to pay extra before a ride is confirmed. The order is dated November 10 and came to light through a Bar and Bench report.

## What the feature did

While a rider waited for a driver to accept a booking, the Ola app showed a message suggesting that adding a tip might help them get a ride faster. Suggested amounts initially ranged from Rs 10 to Rs 40, and the cap later rose. The CCPA found the app allowed tips of up to Rs 499. The case began with a consumer complaint received in May 2025.

The regulator found that the practice was a misleading advertisement, an unfair trade practice and an unfair contract term under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Ola must submit a compliance report within 15 days.

## Ola's defence and why it failed

Ola argued that the payment was voluntary, went entirely to the driver with no platform cut, and helped drivers justify accepting trips they would otherwise skip, such as those with long pickup distances. It also said that adding a tip did not guarantee a faster match.

The CCPA was not persuaded. It said Ola's own data contradicted the claim that tips had no effect on driver engagement or ride assignment. It also drew a basic distinction: a tip normally rewards a service already delivered, while Ola was asking for money before any driver had even agreed to provide one. Seen that way, the advance tip worked less like gratitude and more like a price increase presented as optional.

## A dark pattern, even if most users ignore it

The most important part of the order for the wider tech industry is the dark pattern finding. The CCPA classified the interface as confirm shaming, one of the manipulative designs banned under the Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023. Confirm shaming uses wording that makes users feel they are hurting themselves by declining an option.

Ola argued that only a small share of customers were affected. The regulator rejected that, noting that more than one lakh ride requests in a single city involved the feature. It said a design remains a dark pattern even if it does not work on every consumer, which sets a low bar for future cases. A platform cannot defend a manipulative prompt simply because most users ignore it.

## Why it matters beyond Ola

Advance tipping became a flashpoint for ride-hailing apps in 2025, when the consumer affairs minister publicly questioned the practice and the CCPA issued a notice to Uber over a similar feature. Thursday's order is one of the clearest penalties yet under the dark pattern rules, and its reasoning reaches well beyond ride-hailing. Food delivery, quick commerce and travel apps all use nudges at checkout, from pre-ticked add-ons to guilt-laden opt-out buttons.

The fine is small for a company of Ola's size. The precedent is what counts: the regulator has shown it will look at a platform's own data, rather than its stated intent, when deciding whether an interface misleads users.
