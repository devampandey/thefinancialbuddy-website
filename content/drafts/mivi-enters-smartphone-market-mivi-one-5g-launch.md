Mivi, the Indian brand best known for its earbuds and Bluetooth speakers, stepped into an entirely new category on Friday, launching its first smartphone, the Mivi One 5G, and setting up a direct entry into India's fiercely contested budget handset market.

## What The Phone Offers

The Mivi One 5G is positioned squarely at price-conscious buyers looking for a capable 5G device without spending much beyond entry-level territory. The phone is built around a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 chipset, runs a near-stock version of Android with minimal bloatware, and packs a 6000mAh battery aimed at users who prioritise all-day usage over flagship-level cameras or displays.

The device will be sold in three memory configurations: 4GB+128GB, 6GB+128GB and 8GB+128GB, priced at Rs 14,999, Rs 15,999 and Rs 16,999 respectively at regular pricing. To coincide with the launch, Mivi is offering a special introductory price tied to Flipkart's Big Billion Days event, cutting the entry variant down to Rs 11,999, with the 6GB and 8GB versions available at Rs 12,999 and Rs 13,999. Sales are set to begin on October 8.

## Why An Audio Brand Is Making Phones

Mivi built its reputation over the past several years in headphones, earbuds and portable speakers, competing against both established audio names and a wave of newer domestic brands. Moving into smartphones is a significant strategic shift, one that puts the company in direct competition with a segment dominated by Chinese brands such as Xiaomi, Realme and Vivo's budget sub-brands, alongside domestic and international players all chasing the same price-sensitive Indian buyer.

The rationale for such a move typically comes down to category adjacency and distribution leverage: a brand with existing recognition among value-conscious electronics buyers, and an established retail and e-commerce presence, can attempt to cross-sell into a much larger addressable market. India's smartphone segment priced under Rs 15,000 remains one of the highest-volume bands in the country, driven by first-time 5G upgraders and users replacing ageing 4G devices.

## The Competitive Reality

Entering the budget smartphone race is not without risk. The segment runs on thin margins, rapid product cycles and intense price competition, and it has proven difficult territory for newer or non-traditional entrants without deep manufacturing and after-sales service infrastructure already in place. Mivi's ability to sustain the Mivi One 5G beyond a splashy launch price will depend on how it handles service networks, software updates and inventory at scale, areas where established handset makers already have years of operational experience.

## What To Watch

The exclusive tie-up with Flipkart for the initial sale suggests Mivi is leaning on e-commerce reach rather than building out retail distribution from scratch, a strategy several other brands have used to test demand before expanding to offline channels. Whether the Mivi One 5G finds traction will offer an early signal on whether accessory-first Indian brands can successfully cross over into full-fledged smartphone manufacturing, a leap that has proven difficult for many companies that have attempted it before.
