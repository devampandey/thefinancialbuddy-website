The initial public offering of the National Stock Exchange of India Ltd raced toward full subscription on the second day of bidding on Friday, with the issue booked 92% by early afternoon as institutional investors piled into what ranks among the largest public offers in Indian capital market history.

Bids had come in for over 8.16 crore shares against the 8.86 crore shares on offer, according to data available with the exchanges as of 1 pm. The pace marked a sharp pickup from the opening day, when the issue closed at just 43% subscription, and from the early hours of Friday, when the tally stood at 51% before climbing steadily through the morning.

## Institutional Demand Leads the Charge

Qualified institutional buyers, whose participation is typically viewed as a bellwether for an issue's quality, had subscribed 1.24 times their allotted quota by early afternoon, up sharply from a modest 19% a day earlier. Non-institutional investors booked 1.20 times their portion, while the employee reserved category was subscribed 1.26 times. Retail individual investors lagged relatively, with their segment subscribed 61%, though bidding for this category typically accelerates closer to an issue's close.

The Rs 22,561.57-crore offer is structured entirely as an offer for sale, meaning existing shareholders are diluting their holdings rather than the exchange raising fresh capital. The price band has been fixed at Rs 1,700 to Rs 1,785 per share, and retail investors can apply for a minimum lot of eight equity shares and in multiples thereafter.

## Why The Issue Matters

NSE holds a commanding, near-monopoly position across India's cash equity and futures and options segments, a scale that has fuelled investor enthusiasm despite the exchange's already-substantial valuation. The company's financials underline that dominance: revenue rose from Rs 14,780 crore in FY24 to Rs 16,601 crore in FY26, while net profit touched Rs 10,302 crore for the year, with the first quarter of FY27 alone contributing over Rs 3,120 crore in profit.

Brokerages have broadly turned constructive on the offer. Several domestic research desks have cited the exchange's structural growth potential and dominant market position as reasons to subscribe, even as some investors weigh the rich valuation implied by the price band against the scarcity value of owning a piece of India's primary stock exchange.

## What Happens Next

The issue remains open for bidding through Monday, September 21. The basis of allotment is expected to be finalised on September 22, with refunds and demat credit to follow before the stock's tentative listing on the BSE on September 24. Grey market activity around the issue has also picked up in tandem with the subscription numbers, though such unofficial indicators tend to be volatile in the days before a listing.

For an issue of this size and profile, the final subscription tally when bidding closes on Monday will be closely watched as a gauge of broader investor appetite for large-cap primary offerings at a time when the IPO calendar remains unusually crowded.
