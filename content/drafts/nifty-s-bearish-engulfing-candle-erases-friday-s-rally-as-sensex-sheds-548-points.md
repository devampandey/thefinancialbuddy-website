For chart watchers, Monday's session said more than the headline loss. The Nifty 50 printed a long bearish candle that completely swallowed Friday's long bullish one, a pattern technical analysts call a bearish engulfing. In plain terms, nearly all of the ground bulls won in [Friday's 879-point rally](/markets/sensex-nifty-snap-eight-week-losing-streak-as-friday-rally-adds-879-points) was handed back within a single day.

The BSE Sensex ended 547.65 points, or 0.76%, lower at 71,924.68. The Nifty 50 closed down 157.10 points, or 0.70%, at 22,363.35. Both indices had opened in the green. At its worst the Sensex was more than 1,200 points below its intraday high, so the close was a partial recovery from the lows.

## The level everyone is now watching: 22,200

Nagaraj Shetti, senior technical research analyst at HDFC Securities, said the reversal shows the recent bounce off support lacked strength. He expects the index to retest its recent swing low near 22,180 in the short term. A decisive break below the 22,200 zone, he warned, could drag the Nifty toward 21,700. On the upside, he sees heavy resistance around 22,600.

That leaves the benchmark in a fairly narrow band. About 160 points separate Monday's close from the support analysts are watching, while the nearest meaningful resistance is roughly 240 points above it.

## A split market under the surface

The index loss hid a very different picture lower down the market-cap ladder. The Nifty Smallcap 100 rose 1.42%, and the Nifty Midcap 100 edged up 0.08%. Media and chemical stocks led the sectoral gainers, with auto, realty and metal names also holding up relatively well.

Most of the damage came from heavyweight sectors. Nifty FMCG and Nifty PSU Bank were the weakest sectoral indices, and IT and private banks also lost ground. Apollo Hospitals, ITC and Tech Mahindra were the biggest drags on the Nifty 50.

The contrast suggests traders were still willing to take selective bets in smaller stocks while trimming exposure to large, index-heavy names where foreign investors hold big positions.

## Why the early optimism faded

Vikram Kasat of PL Capital pointed to several pressures arriving at once: rising 10-year government bond yields, continued selling by foreign portfolio investors, renewed tension in the Middle East, soft global cues and profit-taking after Friday's jump. Market participants were also cautious ahead of September retail inflation data, due after the close.

The morning had offered some support. The rupee strengthened sharply after the Reserve Bank's weekend measures ([covered here](/markets/rupee-jumps-over-1-percent-as-rbi-oil-window-and-hedging-curbs-cut-dollar-demand)), and crude prices eased. Neither was enough to keep buyers in the large caps once selling picked up through the afternoon.

## What it means for investors

Kasat's advice was to stick with fundamentally strong companies and spread fresh investments over time until earnings visibility and market breadth improve. With the Q2 earnings season now under way and inflation data adding to rate worries, the 22,200 mark is likely to be the key reference point for the rest of the week. If it holds, Monday may turn out to be a pause. If it breaks, chartists expect a deeper slide.
