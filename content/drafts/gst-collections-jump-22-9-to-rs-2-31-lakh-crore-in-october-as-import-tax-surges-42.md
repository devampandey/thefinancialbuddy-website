India's goods and services tax (GST) collections posted their fastest growth in a long while in October. Gross collections reached Rs 2,31,290 crore, 22.9% more than the Rs 1,88,125 crore collected in October 2025, according to provisional figures released by the Finance Ministry on Sunday. After refunds, net revenue came to Rs 2,03,356 crore, up 25.6% from a year earlier.

The headline number is strong. How it was built matters more.

## Imports did the heavy lifting

Tax collected on imports rose 42.2% to Rs 72,246 crore, from Rs 50,796 crore a year ago. Gross domestic revenue grew a far more modest 15.8%, to Rs 1,59,044 crore from Rs 1,37,328 crore. Imports account for less than a third of the total, but they supplied roughly half of the Rs 43,000 crore increase.

Integrated GST, charged on imports and on trade between states, came to Rs 1,37,015 crore in total. Of that, Rs 72,246 crore came from imports and Rs 64,769 crore from domestic transactions. Central GST stood at Rs 42,935 crore and State GST at Rs 51,340 crore.

Import-linked tax tracks the rupee value of what India buys abroad, so it rises when import bills swell, not only when the economy is doing well. With Brent crude trading above $110 a barrel for much of October and the rupee under pressure, a larger import bill would push this line up whatever happened to domestic demand. The net figure on the customs side makes the point even more sharply: after refunds, import-related GST rose 56.2% to Rs 58,319 crore.

## The domestic picture is solid, not spectacular

Net domestic GST revenue grew 16.4% to about Rs 1.45 lakh crore. That is healthy growth, helped by festive buying ahead of Dhanteras and Diwali, which fall in early November this year.

The base also matters. October 2025 was the first full month after the September 2025 rationalisation of GST rates, which cut tax on a wide range of everyday goods. A softer comparison month flatters this year's growth rate.

## States: the east and north lead

Among large states, Maharashtra collected Rs 35,150 crore in domestic GST, up 13%. Karnataka grew 18% to Rs 15,922 crore, Gujarat 16% to Rs 13,516 crore and Haryana 17% to Rs 11,529 crore. The fastest growth came from smaller and poorer states: Assam's collections rose 47% to Rs 1,934 crore, and Uttar Pradesh and Bihar each grew 24%.

## Refunds stayed restrained

Refunds rose 6.5% to Rs 27,934 crore. Domestic refunds increased 9.8% to Rs 14,007 crore and import-related refunds 3.4% to Rs 13,927 crore. Refunds growing much more slowly than gross collections is the main reason net revenue grew faster than gross revenue this month.

## The year-to-date view is more sober

Across April to October, gross GST collections totalled Rs 14,77,569 crore, up 13.2% from Rs 13,05,213 crore in the same period last year. Net revenue for the seven months was Rs 12,69,486 crore, up 12.6%. Those figures are a better guide to the underlying trend than a single month that combined a weak base, festive demand and an inflated import bill.

For the Centre, the October figure is welcome relief at a time when higher oil prices are straining the budget elsewhere. Whether the pace holds will become clearer in November's data, which will capture the Diwali sales period itself and show whether import-led tax growth eases if crude prices settle.
