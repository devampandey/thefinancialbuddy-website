A sudden and powerful flash flood tore through Nepal's Rasuwa district near the border with China on Wednesday morning, killing dozens of people and leaving hundreds more unaccounted for in one of the country's most serious natural disasters in recent years.

## What happened

The flood surged down the Bhote Koshi River at around 9 am local time, sweeping away bridges, vehicles, roads and hydropower infrastructure before spreading into the neighbouring Nuwakot and Dhading districts. Preliminary assessments from Nepali authorities suggest the disaster may have been triggered by an earthquake-linked avalanche of ice and rock that temporarily blocked the river upstream, before the buildup gave way and sent a wall of water and debris downstream without warning.

Confirmed death tolls have varied and continued to climb through the day as rescue teams reached affected areas, with local police in Bagmati province reporting figures rising through the day as search operations expanded. Nepali officials have cautioned that the final toll is likely to rise further given the scale of destruction and the number of people still unaccounted for.

## Hundreds missing, including Indian nationals

Nepali authorities said close to 300 foreign nationals were unaccounted for in the immediate aftermath, a list that includes roughly 105 Indian citizens along with Nepali workers and travellers from Malaysia, Britain and the United States. Among the missing are dozens of Nepal Police and Armed Police Force personnel who were stationed in the border town of Timure, as well as staff from the local customs office there, underscoring how the flood struck a heavily trafficked trade and tourism corridor.

## Rescue operations underway

Nepal's army has mobilised helicopters alongside privately operated aircraft to pull survivors from the debris-choked riverbanks, with dozens of people confirmed rescued so far. Search and rescue teams are working through difficult terrain complicated by damaged roads and continuing instability along the riverbanks, and officials have signalled that operations will continue for several days as the scale of the disaster becomes clearer.

## India's response

India's prime minister spoke with Nepal's leadership on Wednesday to convey condolences and offer humanitarian assistance, with New Delhi indicating it is preparing to send relief material, including medicines and rations, aboard Indian Air Force aircraft under the government's disaster relief framework. The Indian Air Force has kept transport aircraft on standby, and officials have said deployment of National Disaster Response Force personnel is under consideration to support Nepal's own rescue effort. The Indian embassy in Kathmandu has also shared emergency contact numbers for citizens seeking information about missing relatives.

The Rasuwa flood adds to a difficult monsoon season for Nepal's Himalayan region, which has faced repeated flash floods and landslides in recent years linked to glacial melt and unstable terrain along its northern border.
