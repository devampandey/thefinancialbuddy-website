Russia launched one of its heaviest combined missile-and-drone assaults on Kyiv in recent weeks overnight into Thursday, killing at least 12 people and wounding dozens more as explosions rocked the Ukrainian capital for hours, according to Ukrainian authorities and multiple international news agencies.

## Attack Unfolds Across the Capital

The barrage began shortly after midnight local time, with ballistic missiles, Kalibr cruise missiles and jet-powered drones striking more than a dozen locations across Kyiv's Darnytskyi, Sviatoshynskyi and Solomianskyi districts. Emergency crews worked through the night pulling survivors from rubble as air raid sirens stayed active for hours.

City officials said residential buildings, a children's hospital, a school and several warehouses were hit. In the Solomianskyi district, the upper floors of a nine-story apartment block were destroyed and caught fire, while windows were blown out and vehicles set ablaze on the grounds of the children's hospital. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said parts of the city lost power as energy infrastructure was affected.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russian forces had "prepared for a long time" and combined multiple types of munitions, including cruise missiles, ballistic weapons and drones, to maximize damage to civilian infrastructure. Outside the capital, Russian strikes also hit gas infrastructure in the Chernihiv region and a border crossing with Moldova in the Odesa region, according to Zelensky.

## Toll Rises as Rescue Operations Continue

Casualty figures climbed through the morning as emergency services updated their assessments. Ukrainian officials said at least 12 to 13 people were killed and roughly 33 were injured, with rescue workers continuing to search damaged buildings for survivors. Kyiv's military administration said residential buildings, a medical facility and an educational institution had all sustained damage, with people reported trapped in a school shelter before being freed by emergency crews.

## Air Defense Shortages Cited

The attack underscored what Ukrainian officials describe as a chronic shortage of interceptor missiles needed to counter Russia's ballistic missile attacks. Zelensky has said in recent weeks that Ukraine currently has only about 10% of the Patriot interceptors it needs from the United States and other allies, even as Russia has ramped up the pace of its ballistic missile launches compared with the previous year.

## Spillover Beyond Ukraine's Borders

The scale of the overnight assault had ripple effects beyond Ukraine. Romania's defense ministry said a Russian drone crashed into an uninhabited area near its border with Ukraine, the latest in a series of drone incursions into Romanian airspace during the war. Poland said it launched preventive military aviation operations to safeguard its airspace while the attack on Kyiv was underway.

Russia's defense ministry, for its part, said it had carried out a "massive strike using high-precision weapons" against what it described as military facilities and transport and logistics infrastructure in the Kyiv region.

## Broader Context

The strike came days after Ukraine launched one of its own largest drone attacks of the war on Russian territory, with Moscow's state media describing it as the biggest since the start of the year. The exchange of large-scale strikes reflects an intensifying pattern in the conflict, with both sides stepping up long-range attacks even as diplomatic efforts to end the war remain stalled.
