Hyderabad-based hyperscale data centre operator CtrlS Datacenters has raised Rs 250 crore in fresh capital, with Zerodha co-founder Nikhil Kamath contributing around Rs 200 crore and entrepreneur Sreeram Reddy Vanga putting in the remaining roughly Rs 50 crore. The investment adds two prominent individual backers to a company that has increasingly positioned itself at the centre of India's build-out of AI and cloud infrastructure.

## Where the Money Goes

CtrlS said the fresh capital will support its ongoing infrastructure expansion and capacity build-out as it works to meet rising demand from enterprises and hyperscale cloud providers. Founded by Sridhar Pinnapureddy, the company currently operates 19 data centres spread across nine key markets in India, with more than 370 megawatts of live capacity and roughly 4.4 gigawatts of additional projects at various stages of execution. That pipeline underscores the scale at which Indian data centre operators are now planning, as demand from artificial intelligence workloads, cloud migration and digital public infrastructure accelerates.

Pinnapureddy said the partnership reflected an alignment in long-term vision around where India's digital economy is headed and the scale of infrastructure it will require, adding that the backing gives the company more room to expand quickly. Kamath, in comments accompanying the announcement, pointed to India's data centre sector as critical infrastructure for the next decade, arguing that every major technology shift ahead, from AI to cloud computing, ultimately depends on the availability of this kind of capacity.

## Part of a Bigger Fundraising Run

Wednesday's investment comes just months after CtrlS raised a far larger sum of roughly Rs 7,000 crore from the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board in June, a deal that valued the company at about $4.7 billion. Taken together, the two rounds point to CtrlS aggressively building out its balance sheet and capacity at a time when data centre operators across India are racing to secure land, power and capital ahead of an expected surge in AI-related compute demand.

Vanga, the second investor in Wednesday's round, said he was drawn to the opportunity given India's growing ambitions in AI and cloud computing, and cited his long-standing familiarity with Pinnapureddy's track record in building out the company's infrastructure over the years.

## Why It Matters

India's data centre sector has emerged as one of the more closely watched corners of the country's digital economy, drawing capital from sovereign wealth funds, private equity majors and, increasingly, high-profile individual investors. Power availability and land access remain persistent bottlenecks for the industry, even as demand from both domestic enterprises and global hyperscalers continues to climb. CtrlS's ability to draw repeat, high-quality capital in quick succession suggests investors continue to see the segment as a durable long-term bet, even amid broader volatility in Indian equity markets tied to global crude prices and geopolitical uncertainty.

For CtrlS, the latest round adds star power to its capital base while reinforcing its position among the country's largest hyperscale data centre operators, as competition intensifies for a slice of India's fast-growing AI and cloud infrastructure market.
