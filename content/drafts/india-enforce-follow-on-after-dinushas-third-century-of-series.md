Day four of the second Test between India and Sri Lanka at the Sinhalese Sports Club in Colombo delivered another twist, with Sri Lankan left-hander Sonal Dinusha continuing his remarkable run against India before India's bowlers wrapped up the innings and immediately sent the hosts back out to bat again.

## Dinusha's Hundred

Resuming the morning at 265 for 8, still 238 runs behind India's first-innings total, Sri Lanka leaned once more on Dinusha, who brought up his century with a powerful pull shot off pace bowler Prasidh Krishna. The hundred was his third in as many innings against India this series, following scores of 100 and 84 in the first Test at Galle, an extraordinary sequence for a batter playing his first two Tests against the visitors. Dinusha's innings finally ended shortly after, with Sri Lanka bowled out for 290 as Prasidh Krishna and left-arm spinner Manav Suthar shared the bulk of the wickets, taking three apiece to clean up the tail.

## The Follow-On Decision

With a first-innings lead comfortably in excess of 200 runs, India's captain Shubman Gill opted to enforce the follow-on rather than bat again, asking Sri Lanka to go straight back out under overcast Colombo skies. The decision paid an early dividend when Mohammed Siraj struck in his opening spell, removing Lahiru Udara cheaply to leave the hosts reeling at 5 for 1.

Sri Lanka's middle order responded with some resistance, as Kamil Mishara and debutant opener Pasindu Sooriyabandara — who had already impressed with a maiden fifty on day three — put on a counterattacking stand of 58 runs to steady the innings. The partnership was eventually broken by Ravindra Jadeja, who had Mishara caught for 32, triggering another mini-collapse as India's spin and pace combination continued to chip away. Sooriyabandara remained unbeaten on 35 at the lunch interval, still fighting to keep Sri Lanka's hopes of saving the match alive.

## Series Context

India go into the fourth day firmly in control, having already wrapped up the first Test in Galle and now holding a commanding position in the second. A win in Colombo would give Gill's side an unassailable 2-0 lead in the series with one Test to play. For Sri Lanka, the priority has shifted from competing for a result to simply surviving the remaining sessions, with rain having interrupted play on both the second and third days and forecasts suggesting further disruption could yet play a role in how the match unfolds.

For Dinusha, the series has already been a breakout moment regardless of the eventual result, with his sequence of scores marking him out as one of the more promising batting talents to emerge from Sri Lanka's often-changing top order in recent seasons.
