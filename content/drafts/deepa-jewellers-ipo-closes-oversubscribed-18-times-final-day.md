The initial public offering of Deepa Jewellers Limited closed for subscription on Thursday, capping a three-day bidding window with a sharp late surge in demand that left the mainboard issue oversubscribed by close to eighteen times overall.

## Issue Details

The IPO comprised a fresh issue of shares worth Rs 250 crore alongside an offer for sale of Rs 209.72 crore from existing shareholders, taking the total issue size to approximately Rs 459.72 crore. Shares were offered in a price band of Rs 168 to Rs 177, with a minimum lot size of 84 shares, meaning retail investors needed to commit roughly Rs 14,868 at the top end of the band to place a single bid. Bidding opened on September 1, with the anchor investor portion having been subscribed a day earlier on August 31.

## How the Subscription Built Up

The offering saw a gradual build in demand over its three-day run rather than an immediate rush. Early data on the second day showed subscription levels in the low single digits, before demand accelerated meaningfully as the issue entered its final day. By early afternoon on the closing day, cumulative subscription had reached 17.59 times the shares on offer, with the non-institutional investor category leading the charge at nearly 48 times its allotted quota, retail investors coming in at just over 12 times, and qualified institutional buyers at a more modest 4.28 times. The sharp skew toward non-institutional and retail demand, relative to comparatively muted institutional participation for much of the bidding window, is a pattern increasingly common in smaller mainboard jewellery and consumer issues, where high-net-worth individual investors often place large last-day bets anticipating listing gains.

## Grey Market Signal

Ahead of the issue's close, shares of Deepa Jewellers were commanding a grey market premium of around Rs 28 over the upper end of the price band in unofficial trading, suggesting investors were pricing in a moderate listing pop rather than a blockbuster debut. Grey market premiums are unregulated and can move quickly in either direction before listing, so the figure should be read as a sentiment indicator rather than a reliable forecast of the eventual listing price.

## What Happens Next

With bidding now closed, the basis of allotment is expected to be finalised on September 4, giving successful and unsuccessful bidders clarity within roughly a day of the issue closing. Shares are tentatively scheduled to list on both the BSE and the NSE on September 8. The strong final-day subscription numbers place Deepa Jewellers among a cluster of mid-sized mainboard IPOs that have drawn healthy retail interest even as broader secondary market indices have struggled through a rough patch this week, a divergence that suggests primary market appetite in India has, for now, held up better than sentiment in the secondary market.
