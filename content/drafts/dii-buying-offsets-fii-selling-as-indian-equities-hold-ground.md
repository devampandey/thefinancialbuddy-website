Indian equity markets saw a familiar tug-of-war play out on Thursday, as domestic institutional investors (DIIs) stepped in with net purchases of Rs 4,353.10 crore in the cash segment, comfortably offsetting net selling of Rs 510.70 crore by foreign institutional investors (FIIs).

## A Day of Diverging Flows

The contrast in institutional behaviour was stark. While FIIs continued to trim their exposure to Indian stocks, domestic funds — spanning mutual funds, insurance companies, banks and pension funds — used the opportunity to add to their positions at a pace roughly eight times larger than the foreign outflow. The net effect helped cushion the market from steeper losses during a session that saw the benchmark indices trade in a volatile band through the day.

The Sensex ultimately closed higher, supported in part by this domestic buying, while the Nifty slipped modestly as select heavyweight sectors, including metals, cement and IT, weighed on sentiment even as other pockets of the market advanced.

## Why the Divergence Matters

The pattern of FII selling met by DII buying has become a recurring feature of Indian markets through much of the year, as global funds recalibrate their India exposure against a backdrop of elevated crude oil prices, geopolitical uncertainty tied to the Iran-linked disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, and shifting expectations around US interest rate policy. Domestic institutions, buoyed by steady inflows from retail investors through systematic investment plans, have increasingly served as a stabilising counterweight during periods of foreign outflows.

Currency markets reflected some of this same tension, with the rupee hovering near the 95.3-95.4 per dollar mark, pressured by elevated global oil prices even as broader risk sentiment stayed relatively contained.

## Stock-Specific Moves Add Colour

Individual stock moves on Thursday illustrated how the broader flow dynamics played out at the company level. Godrej Industries shares initially fell before staging a sharp reversal to close higher after its June-quarter results showed a profit decline alongside strong revenue and margin growth — the kind of nuanced result that often prompts institutional investors to look past headline numbers. Auto and select consumer names also saw active trading interest as investors positioned around the ongoing Q1 FY27 earnings season, which has brought a wave of quarterly results from Indian companies through the first half of August.

## The Bigger Picture

For market watchers, the key takeaway from Thursday's flow data is less about the size of any single day's numbers and more about the underlying trend: domestic capital has continued to provide a durable floor under Indian equities even during stretches of foreign investor caution. Whether that pattern holds through the rest of August will depend heavily on how global crude prices, the Federal Reserve's rate trajectory, and geopolitical developments evolve in the coming weeks — all factors that continue to shape how much conviction foreign investors are willing to put behind India as an investment destination relative to other emerging markets.
