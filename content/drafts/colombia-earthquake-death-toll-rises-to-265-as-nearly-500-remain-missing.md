The human toll from Colombia's devastating earthquake continued to climb on Thursday, with President Abelardo de la Espriella confirming that 265 people have died and 496 remain missing, three days after a 7.4-magnitude quake tore through the western part of the country.

The earthquake struck on Monday with an epicenter near San Jose del Palmar, sending tremors through several major cities and triggering building collapses across the region. According to the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD), roughly 25,872 families — an estimated 53,816 people — have been affected by the disaster, with 3,494 people reported injured.

## Rescue Effort Enters Critical Phase

Rescue teams, working alongside international aid missions, have pulled more than 340 people alive from the rubble since the quake struck, according to disaster response officials. However, with each passing day reducing the odds of finding further survivors, authorities have described the search-and-rescue operation as entering its "final phase."

The scale of the destruction has tested Colombia's newly formed government under President de la Espriella, who took office earlier this year. His administration has mobilized emergency response units, military personnel and volunteer teams to the hardest-hit areas, even as aftershocks continue to complicate recovery work and threaten already-weakened structures.

## International Response

Colombia's earthquake has drawn a wave of international assistance, with foreign search-and-rescue teams and medical units arriving to support the domestic effort. Aid coordination has focused on reaching remote and mountainous areas of western Colombia that were hit hardest but remain difficult for rescue convoys to access.

The death toll has risen sharply since the earthquake first struck — from an initial count of around 20 confirmed dead on the day of the quake, to more than 130 by Monday night, past 200 by Tuesday, and now 265 as of Wednesday's update from the president. Officials have cautioned that the toll is likely to rise further as rescue teams reach areas that were previously cut off by landslides and damaged infrastructure.

## Humanitarian Needs Mount

Beyond the search for survivors, the disaster has created an urgent humanitarian crisis for tens of thousands of displaced families. Temporary shelters have been set up in affected cities, and the government has pledged emergency financial assistance to households that lost homes or family members. Damage assessments of critical infrastructure, including roads, hospitals and water systems, are still underway, and officials say a fuller picture of the economic cost of the disaster will emerge only once rescue operations conclude.

The earthquake ranks among the deadliest natural disasters to hit Colombia in recent years, and its aftermath is expected to remain a dominant national story in the country for weeks as reconstruction efforts begin alongside the ongoing search for the missing.
