Indian equity benchmarks closed lower for a second consecutive session on Tuesday, as renewed military tension between the United States and Iran and a fresh spike in crude oil prices outweighed an otherwise encouraging set of domestic growth numbers. The BSE Sensex slipped 12.99 points, or 0.02 percent, to end at 76,944.28, while the NSE Nifty50 shed 24.60 points, or 0.1 percent, to settle at 24,055.80. Earlier in the session losses were sharper, with the Nifty briefly dipping below the 24,000 mark before recovering some ground into the close.

## Banks, autos and pharma drag; FMCG and IT hold up

The pressure was concentrated in rate-sensitive and cyclical sectors. Nifty Healthcare, Auto, Realty and Pharma were among the day's worst performers, while defensive pockets such as FMCG and IT attracted buying interest as investors rotated away from sectors seen as most exposed to global uncertainty. Shriram Finance, Maruti Suzuki and InterGlobe Aviation were among the biggest drags on the Nifty50, while broader markets fared worse than headline indices, with the Nifty Midcap 100 down 1.39 percent and the Nifty Smallcap 100 off 0.23 percent.

## Oil and geopolitics back in the driver's seat

The immediate trigger for the day's weakness was the escalating standoff in the Strait of Hormuz, after the United States confirmed fresh strikes on Iranian military targets, reviving fears of a broader Gulf conflict that could disrupt oil supply. Crude prices had already been climbing through the past two sessions, and India, as one of the world's largest oil importers, is particularly sensitive to such moves given their knock-on effects on the currency, inflation and the current account. Elevated US Treasury yields added to the cautious mood, making dollar assets relatively more attractive and fuelling renewed foreign portfolio outflows from Indian equities.

## GDP beat provides only partial cushion

The market's muted reaction came despite data released a day earlier showing India's economy grew 7.8 percent in the June quarter of the current financial year, a stronger print than most economists had pencilled in and a sign that domestic consumption and investment activity remain resilient even as global headwinds mount. Market strategists noted that while the GDP numbers underline the durability of India's growth story, investors are currently more focused on near-term risks from energy markets and global monetary policy than on the medium-term growth outlook.

## What analysts are watching next

Technical analysts flagged the 23,950 level on the Nifty as a key support zone; a decisive break below that could open the door to a further slide toward 23,800, while a recovery above the 24,170–24,200 band would be needed to meaningfully improve sentiment. With crude prices, the trajectory of the Iran conflict, and signals from the US Federal Reserve all in play simultaneously, traders expect volatility to persist in the sessions ahead, even as India's underlying macro fundamentals remain comparatively strong.
