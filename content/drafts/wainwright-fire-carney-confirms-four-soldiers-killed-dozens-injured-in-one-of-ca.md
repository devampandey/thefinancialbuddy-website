Four Canadian service members were killed and dozens more were seriously injured in a fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta, Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed on Sunday. He called it one of the worst peacetime tragedies the Canadian military has suffered on home soil. Speaking on Parliament Hill in Ottawa, Carney said the families of the dead had been informed. He described the situation as still developing.

Flags on federal buildings across Canada will be lowered to half-staff. Carney said he had directed senior officials to give a detailed public briefing in Wainwright later on Sunday about how the disaster unfolded.

## What Is Known So Far

The military says the fire started inside the base's training area at about 4 pm local time on Saturday. It then spread quickly beyond the area's boundaries, driven by strong winds. Early reports described an explosion as well as a fire. Defence Minister David McGuinty had confirmed on Saturday night that there were deaths and injuries, but officials did not give figures until families had been notified. McGuinty called the situation very active and delicate and said he could not recall another incident like it.

The Department of National Defence has said there is no sign the incident was linked to terrorism. The cause is still under investigation, and officials have not said what kind of training was taking place when the fire started. No evacuation order was issued for residents of the nearby town.

## Hospitals Under Strain

The number of casualties quickly overwhelmed local medical services. Health facilities near the base struggled to cope with the volume of patients, and injured personnel were sent to hospitals across the region, including in Edmonton. Photographs from the Wainwright Health Centre on Saturday evening showed paramedics and military personnel moving the injured into ambulances while helicopters arrived.

## Why Wainwright Matters

CFB Wainwright, about 200 km southeast of Edmonton, is one of the Canadian Army's largest and busiest training bases. Its huge training area is used for brigade-level exercises, live-fire drills and pre-deployment preparation,. An accident on this scale at a central training site is likely to raise questions about range safety procedures and about how wildfire risk is managed during exercises in high winds.

The base has seen fatal training accidents before, including a 2014 incident that killed a senior officer and a 2020 live-fire shooting. Those were single-casualty events. A fire that kills four and seriously injures dozens is on a different scale entirely.

## What Comes Next

The briefing in Wainwright should give the first official account of the sequence of events, the number of people still in hospital and the scope of the investigation. The Canadian military usually convenes a board of inquiry after serious incidents like this. Premiers and other officials across the country have sent condolences and offered support.

The names of the four service members have not yet been released.
