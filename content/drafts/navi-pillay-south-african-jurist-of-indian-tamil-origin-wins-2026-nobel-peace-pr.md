The 2026 Nobel Peace Prize has gone to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, the South African jurist and former United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, in a choice the Norwegian Nobel Committee framed as a defence of international law at a moment when its institutions are under strain.

The announcement was made in Oslo on Friday. This year's prize is the 125th in the award's history, and the committee said Pillay was being recognised for her efforts to promote peace and international law, highlighting her role in making sure war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide are prosecuted.

## Why the committee chose her

Committee chair Jorgen Watne Frydnes used the announcement to deliver a pointed message. He described the system of international law as being under heavy pressure, with its institutions facing open attack, and said judges are the ones who hold the line between order and chaos. The committee's statement argued that, with more wars and conflicts under way than the world has seen in a long time, international law can no longer be treated as an optional add-on to peace and security.

In an unusual moment, Frydnes said the committee had not been able to reach Pillay before going public, and appealed from the podium for her to answer if she saw a call from a Norwegian number.

## A career spanning apartheid courts to global tribunals

Pillay, who is 85, is a South African of Indian Tamil descent. She began her legal career representing anti-apartheid activists, including working as part of the defence for Nelson Mandela, himself a Nobel Peace laureate.

She went on to serve as a judge on the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda, which handed down landmark rulings on genocide, and later as a judge of the International Criminal Court. From 2008 to 2014 she was the UN's top human rights official. In recent years she chaired a UN commission of inquiry that concluded Israel had committed genocide against Palestinians in Gaza, a finding Israel rejects.

## The wider context

The committee received nominations for 287 candidates this year. Other names reported to be in contention included emergency response groups in Sudan, advocates for children in Ukraine and two international courts.

The award is also likely to be read against US President Donald Trump's repeated public claims that he deserves the prize. Last year's winner, Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado, later handed Trump her medal as a symbolic gesture, though the committee has said the honour cannot be transferred.

The prize carries an award of 12 million Swedish kronor, about $1.2 million, and will be presented in Oslo on December 10. The Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, the last of this year's awards, will be announced on Monday. Earlier this week, the physics prize went to Francis Halzen for the IceCube neutrino discovery, which we covered [here](/world/francis-halzen-wins-2026-nobel-prize-in-physics-for-icecube-s-discovery-of-cosmi).

For India, the award carries an added resonance: Pillay is among the most prominent figures of the Indian diaspora in South Africa, a community whose own history is bound up with the struggle against apartheid.
