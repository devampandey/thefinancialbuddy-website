Indian equity benchmarks extended their losing streak into a second consecutive session on Tuesday, with the BSE Sensex and NSE Nifty both closing at their lowest levels in nearly three months as rising crude oil prices rattled investor sentiment.

The Sensex ended the day down 555.23 points, or 0.73 per cent, at 75,577.58, while the Nifty 50 fell 144.05 points, or 0.61 per cent, to settle at 23,635.10. Both indices opened weak and stayed under pressure through the session, with a brief attempt at recovery in the afternoon failing to hold.

## Oil Prices Behind The Selloff

The primary trigger for the day's decline was a fresh spike in global crude prices. Brent crude climbed toward $99 a barrel after reports emerged that Yemen-based Houthi rebels had targeted multiple facilities belonging to Saudi Aramco, reviving concerns about supply disruptions from the region. Since India imports the vast majority of its crude requirement, any sustained rise in oil prices tends to weigh on corporate margins, the rupee, and the broader market mood, and Tuesday's session was no exception.

The uptick in oil prices came against the backdrop of already elevated Middle East tensions this year, with the ongoing conflict involving Iran and shipping disruptions near the Strait of Hormuz having kept energy markets on edge for months. Dow futures were also trading lower by around 390 points during the Indian session, reflecting a broader risk-off mood in global markets.

## Sectoral And Broader Market Trends

Within the Nifty 50, SBI Life Insurance, ICICI Bank and Axis Bank were among the top losers, with private banking and financial services stocks bearing the brunt of the selling. Sector-wise, Nifty Private Bank and Nifty Financial Services indices declined the most, while Nifty Pharma and Nifty Healthcare bucked the trend to end higher. The defence index was a standout gainer, rallying more than two per cent even as the broader market struggled.

Broader markets held up relatively better than the frontline indices. The Nifty MidCap 100 and Nifty SmallCap 100 indices rose 0.21 per cent and 0.17 per cent respectively, helped by an even advance-decline ratio, even though heavy activity in the primary market continued to draw liquidity away from secondary trading.

## Liquidity Conditions Also In Focus

Investors were also tracking domestic liquidity conditions after the Reserve Bank of India withdrew more than ₹6 lakh crore of excess liquidity from the banking system a day earlier. Tighter systemic liquidity, combined with the external pressure from oil prices, added to the cautious tone on Dalal Street.

## What Analysts Are Watching

Market technicians noted that the Nifty has now formed a bearish pattern on daily charts after the latest fall, with immediate resistance seen around the 23,750 mark. A break below current levels could see the index retest the 23,500-23,450 zone in the near term, according to commentary from brokerage desks tracking the session. Conversely, a decisive move back above resistance could open room for a further pullback toward higher levels.

With crude prices, geopolitical developments and domestic liquidity all in play simultaneously, market participants are likely to stay watchful heading into the coming sessions, particularly given the packed primary market calendar this month.
