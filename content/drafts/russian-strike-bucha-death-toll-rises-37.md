The death toll from a Russian strike on the Bucha district of Ukraine's Kyiv region climbed to 37 on Saturday, regional officials said, as rescue teams continued combing through the wreckage of a warehouse compound hit in an overnight attack.

Timur Tkachenko, head of the Kyiv Regional State Administration, said on Telegram that the toll had risen through the day from an earlier count of 27, with work still ongoing at the site of what he called a tragedy. More than 40 people were reported wounded, and officials said around 400 residents had been evacuated from the area as fires continued to burn.

## What happened

According to Ukrainian officials, the strike hit a warehouse in the village of Myla in the Bucha district, close to the capital, late on Friday evening. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the initial strike was followed by a large secondary detonation, which authorities believe worsened the casualty toll and the scale of destruction. At least 50 residential buildings were reported damaged, with the blaze spreading to nearby homes and a restaurant.

Ukraine's Prosecutor General's Office has opened a pre-trial investigation into possible negligence over storage conditions at the warehouse. Zelenskyy said existing regulations bar the storage of ammunition and other hazardous materials near homes and residential areas, suggesting the scale of the secondary blast may be linked to what was being kept on site.

Sunday, August 30, has been declared a day of mourning across the Kyiv region.

## Part of a wider barrage

The Bucha district strike was among a series of Russian attacks reported across Ukraine through Friday night and into Saturday. Ukrainian authorities said Russian forces also struck Zaporizhzhia twice within a single day, damaging residential buildings and injuring at least one person, while a separate attack on Pavlohrad in the Dnipropetrovsk region sparked a fire and left two people injured. Kherson was hit by drone strikes that killed one person and injured two others, and a large-scale drone barrage caused a blackout in the Mykolaiv region that left roughly 300,000 customers without power.

Ukraine's air force said its defenses had neutralized 233 Russian drones overnight, underscoring the scale of the aerial assault. Kyiv also reported continuing casualties from the barrage, including a child injured in a drone strike on the capital's Obolon district and a toddler who later died from injuries sustained in an earlier strike.

## International reaction

The scale of the attack drew swift reaction from Western capitals. Finland's government said it stood with the people of Ukraine in the aftermath of the strike, while Estonia's foreign minister called for increased international pressure on Moscow. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte discussed the escalation with Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk, with both sides emphasizing the need for stronger air defense support for Ukraine as Russian strikes continue to intensify.

The attack comes as the war grinds on with no resolution in sight, more than four and a half years after Russia's full-scale invasion began. Ukrainian officials have repeatedly warned that Russian strikes on civilian and residential areas have escalated in recent months even as diplomatic efforts to end the conflict continue on and off in the background.
