Air India has named Tewolde Gebremariam, the long-serving former chief executive of Ethiopian Airlines, as its new Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director. He takes over from Campbell Wilson, who is exiting the carrier, marking one of the most closely watched leadership transitions in Indian aviation since the Tata Group took over the airline.

## Who Is the New Chief

Gebremariam built his reputation over more than three decades at Ethiopian Airlines, which he led through a period widely regarded as one of the most successful transformations among African and emerging-market carriers. Under his stewardship, Ethiopian Airlines grew into one of the continent's largest and most profitable airlines, expanding its route network, fleet and cargo operations while several peer carriers across the region struggled financially. That track record of scaling an airline profitably in a challenging operating environment appears to be a central reason the Tata Group has turned to him for Air India's next phase.

## Why the Change Matters

Air India has been in the middle of a multi-year turnaround since its return to Tata ownership, involving fleet modernisation, a major aircraft order, service upgrades and the merger of Vistara and AirAsia India into the Air India group. Campbell Wilson, a former Singapore Airlines and Scoot executive, was brought in to lead that overhaul and oversaw much of the integration work and early stages of the fleet renewal programme. His departure comes at a stage where the airline is shifting focus from integration toward consistent operational execution and profitability, and the incoming CEO's international network-carrier experience is likely to be tested on exactly those fronts.

The leadership change also lands at a time when Indian aviation continues to see strong passenger growth alongside intense cost pressure from fuel prices, aircraft delivery delays and competitive fare wars with rivals such as IndiGo. Managing that combination while continuing to integrate Air India's various erstwhile airline brands into a single cohesive operation will likely be the new CEO's immediate priority.

## What Comes Next

Analysts and industry watchers will be looking for early signals on whether Gebremariam intends to continue the current fleet and network expansion strategy largely unchanged, or whether he brings a materially different approach drawn from his experience scaling Ethiopian Airlines' hub-and-spoke international model. Given Air India's ambitions to grow its long-haul international footprint and compete more directly with Gulf and Southeast Asian carriers for transit traffic, his background running a successful international hub carrier could prove particularly relevant.

For now, the Tata Group's decision to bring in an outside aviation veteran with deep international experience, rather than promote from within, suggests the group continues to view Air India's turnaround as a work in progress requiring fresh strategic input at the top. Investors and employees alike will be watching the transition closely over the coming months for early indications of strategic continuity or change.
