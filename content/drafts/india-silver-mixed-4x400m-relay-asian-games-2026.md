India's mixed 4x400m relay team won silver at the Asian Games 2026 on Thursday, extending the country's remarkable run of podium finishes in the event to three consecutive editions of the Games.

## A Tight Finish Behind Bahrain

The Indian quartet of M.R. Poovamma, Vishal T.K., Vithya Ramraj and Manu T.S. clocked 3 minutes 14.46 seconds in the final, finishing second behind Bahrain, who won gold in a Games-record time of 3:12.87. The margin for silver was razor thin: Vietnam crossed the line in 3:14.54, just 0.08 seconds behind the Indian team, narrowly missing out on a medal. Hosts Japan finished fourth in 3:15.52.

The mixed relay format, which requires each team to field two men and two women running in any order, has become one of the more closely watched middle-distance events at recent Asian Games, rewarding squad depth across both genders rather than individual star power alone. India's quartet managed their baton exchanges cleanly under pressure, holding off a fast-closing Vietnamese team in the final leg to secure the silver.

## A Consistent Medal Event For India

Thursday's result extends a streak that goes back to the event's introduction at the continental level. India won gold in the mixed 4x400m relay at the 2018 Asian Games in Jakarta, followed that up with silver at Hangzhou in 2023, and has now added a third straight medal at Aichi-Nagoya in 2026. Few Indian athletics events have delivered that kind of sustained podium consistency across three editions, making the mixed relay one of the more reliable medal sources for the country's track and field programme heading into each Games cycle.

The result also came on a broader day of athletics medals for India at the 2026 Games, with the country's track and field contingent adding to its overall tally through the mixed relay alongside other individual events held on the same day, as India's athletes pushed for a strong finish in the discipline that has traditionally been dominated by East and West Asian powers such as Bahrain, Japan and China.

## What It Means For India's Campaign

With athletics historically one of India's largest medal-contributing disciplines at the Asian Games, a podium finish in a marquee relay event like this carries weight beyond the medal tally alone. It reflects continued strength in India's quarter-mile running depth, a discipline that has produced Asian and Commonwealth-level performers over the past decade and continues to feed talent into both the men's and women's individual 400m events as well as the mixed and traditional relay squads.

For the Indian contingent at Aichi-Nagoya, the mixed relay silver adds to a growing medal count across track and field, rowing and combat sports disciplines, as the team looks to build on its performance from the previous edition in Hangzhou heading into the Games' closing days.
