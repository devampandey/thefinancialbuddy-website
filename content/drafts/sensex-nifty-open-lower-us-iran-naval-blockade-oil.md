Indian equity benchmarks opened lower on Friday, extending a cautious tone in early trade after the United States signalled it intends to keep up its naval blockade near the Strait of Hormuz indefinitely, a stance that kept crude oil prices elevated and stoked fresh worries about imported inflation.

## How the market moved

The BSE Sensex opened around 301 points, or 0.39 percent, lower at 77,778.95, and extended its decline to roughly 311 points by mid-morning, trading near 77,768. The Nifty 50 slipped about 71 to 74 points, or close to 0.3 percent, to hover around the 24,323-24,325 mark. Ahead of the open, GIFT Nifty futures had already flagged a weak start, quoted around 24,438, down roughly 30 points from the prior session.

Among the session's early laggards were Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles, Max Healthcare Institute and Hindalco Industries, all featuring among the top losers on the Nifty 50 in the opening stretch of trade.

## What is driving the move

The proximate trigger was Washington's reiterated stance that it would continue its naval blockade tied to the Strait of Hormuz for an indefinite period, a signal that dampened hopes of a near-term easing in the months-long standoff between the US and Iran. While Brent crude had snapped a six-session rally in the prior session on demand concerns, prices steadied and stayed elevated near $85-87 a barrel on Friday as the blockade rhetoric persisted, keeping alive concerns that costlier crude imports could add to India's inflationary pressures given the country's heavy reliance on oil imports.

The domestic market's soft start came even as global cues were mixed to modestly positive. Asia-Pacific markets traded unevenly, with Japan's Nikkei 225 and South Korea's Kospi both firmer by roughly 0.7-0.9 percent on the back of a softer US Producer Price Index reading that eased near-term worries about further rate hikes, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng slipped around half a percent. Overnight on Wall Street, the S&P 500 closed at a fresh record high, up 0.65 percent, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average and Nasdaq Composite also ending higher.

## What investors are watching

Friday's session carries added significance beyond the geopolitical overhang. It is a heavy day for corporate earnings, with results due from a long list of companies including Ashok Leyland, Voltas, NMDC, Alkem Laboratories, Bharat Dynamics, Patanjali Foods and Cochin Shipyard, among dozens of others reporting first-quarter numbers. The primary market is also active, with EPC firm Technocraft Ventures and supply-chain player LEAP India both set to make their stock exchange debuts.

For now, the tug-of-war between resilient global risk appetite, reflected in Wall Street's record highs, and the drag from elevated crude and unresolved Middle East tensions appears to be tilting sentiment cautiously negative in Indian trade, with investors likely to stay watchful of any further escalation around the Strait of Hormuz through the rest of the session.
