Uttar Pradesh's 2027 Assembly election has a new contender. On Thursday, AIMIM president Asaduddin Owaisi and Azad Samaj Party (Kanshi Ram) chief Chandrashekhar Azad held a joint press conference in Lucknow and launched a front called the Samajik Parivartan Morcha, or Social Change Front. Owaisi announced that Azad, the Lok Sabha MP from Nagina, will be the alliance's chief ministerial candidate.

## A Dalit-Muslim pitch

The arithmetic behind the front is easy to read. Muslims make up roughly 19% of Uttar Pradesh's population and Scheduled Castes about 21%. Azad's politics are rooted in Dalit mobilisation in western UP. Owaisi's party has spent years trying to build a base among Muslim voters outside Hyderabad. Together they are pitching to Dalits, Muslims, backward classes and other groups they say are under-represented in the state's politics, with western UP and the Rohilkhand belt expected to be their main focus.

Azad said there were no differences between the partners and that the front would contest all 403 Assembly seats together. He also insisted the morcha is not a third front. The leaders did not announce a seat-sharing formula, which will be the alliance's first real test.

## What the two leaders said

Owaisi presented the front as a force strong enough to stop the BJP from winning a third straight term in Lucknow. He said its campaign would centre on social change, unemployment and farm distress. He also announced a large public rally on the 26th, which reports place on November 26.

Azad spent much of the press conference attacking the BJP government in Uttar Pradesh. He said the state had done little for marginalised communities in nine years and listed concerns ranging from violence against Dalits and backward groups to women's safety and joblessness. He spoke about his own 16 months in jail and said cases against him had not been withdrawn even as cases against the chief minister had been. He also warned against any attempt to weaken the reservation system. Azad invited the Samajwadi Party and the Congress to join the fight against the BJP.

## Why the SP should pay attention

The more immediate pressure may fall on the Samajwadi Party rather than the BJP. Akhilesh Yadav's strategy rests on what he calls PDA, short for Pichde (backward classes), Dalit and Alpsankhyak (minorities), the coalition that helped the SP-Congress alliance do well in UP in the 2024 Lok Sabha polls. A front that targets Dalit and Muslim voters directly could split some of that vote in close contests.

Akhilesh played the announcement down, saying UP's voters have already made up their minds to back the SP in 2027. The BJP said every party is free to contest and questioned Owaisi's approach to politics. Owaisi had earlier tried to join the SP-Congress opposition alliance and was turned away.

## The track record

Neither party has won a seat in a UP Assembly election. In 2022, AIMIM contested 95 seats and won about 0.49% of the statewide vote. The Azad Samaj Party contested more than 100 seats that year without a win. What has changed since then is Azad's 2024 victory in Nagina, which gave him a national platform and showed he can win in western UP on his own terms.

The election is still more than a year away, but the new front adds a fresh variable to a contest that had been shaping up as a straight BJP-versus-SP fight. Seat-sharing talks and the November 26 rally will give the first indication of how much ground the two parties can cover together.
