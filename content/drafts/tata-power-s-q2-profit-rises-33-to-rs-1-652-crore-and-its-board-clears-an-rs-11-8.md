Tata Power closed out its September quarter with its strongest profit print in some time, and paired it with one of its largest single project approvals for energy storage. The company reported its results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 on Wednesday evening, and the board signed off on Rs 11,871 crore for a pumped hydro storage plant in Maharashtra in the same meeting.

## The numbers

Tata Power's reported profit after tax for Q2 FY27 came in at **Rs 1,652 crore**, up 33% from Rs 1,245 crore in the same quarter last year. On the narrower measure of consolidated profit attributable to the company's own shareholders, the figure was about Rs 1,366 crore, up roughly 49% from Rs 919 crore. The gap between the two measures is mainly the share of profit that belongs to minority partners in Tata Power's subsidiaries and joint ventures.

Revenue from operations rose about 26% to roughly Rs 19,636 crore, from Rs 15,545 crore a year earlier. The quarter also marks a clear step up from Q1 FY27, when the company reported a profit of about Rs 1,401 crore.

For the first half of the year, from April to September, net profit stood at Rs 3,053 crore, up 22%, on revenue of Rs 38,784 crore, up 17%. Over the same six months, Tata Power added 765 MW of renewable capacity.

## Why the storage approval matters

The headline decision beyond the numbers is the **Shirwata pumped storage project** in Maharashtra. The plant will have a capacity of 1,800 MW and will be funded with 75% debt and 25% equity. Its zero date, the formal start of the construction clock, is January 1, 2027, with 51 months allowed for completion, which points to commissioning around early 2031.

Pumped storage works like a giant water battery. Water is pumped uphill when power is cheap and plentiful, typically from solar plants in the afternoon, and released through turbines when demand peaks in the evening. As India adds more solar and wind, grid operators need this kind of storage to keep supply steady after sunset. Management also said it is studying the large battery energy storage tenders announced in recent weeks, with a focus on sites where it already has enough land.

## Other moving parts

Several other updates came with the results:

- **Mundra:** The imported-coal plant in Gujarat is running at full capacity under Section 11 directions until the end of December. A supplementary power purchase agreement with Gujarat has been signed and filed with the regulator, and Rajasthan and Haryana are expected to sign before year-end.
- **Solar manufacturing:** Tata Power produced 0.9 GW of solar cells and 1 GW of modules in the quarter. Its planned ingot and wafer facility at Gopalpur in Odisha has all approvals, with site work starting this month and completion targeted for March 2028.
- **Legal dispute:** In its long-running dispute involving Kleros Capital, the company has appealed to the Singapore Court of Appeal, with a hearing listed for January 19, 2027. Management said no provision is needed until the appeal is decided.

## What to watch

The profit growth shows the payoff from Tata Power's shift towards renewables, rooftop solar and manufacturing. The risks are execution and funding. Large projects like Shirwata take years to build, and the debt-heavy structure means interest costs will matter. Investors will also watch whether Mundra's power purchase deals with Rajasthan and Haryana are signed on time, since that affects how the plant runs once the current directions lapse.
