Sri Lanka staged a determined fightback on Day 4 of the second and final Test against India at the Sinhalese Sports Club Ground in Colombo, closing the day at 229 for 6 in their follow-on innings, just 16 runs behind India's massive first-innings total.

## How the day unfolded

Batting again after being asked to follow on, Sri Lanka's top order came under pressure early before Pasindu Sooriyabandara anchored the innings with a fighting 92 before being stumped, narrowly missing out on what would have been his maiden Test century. He found a valuable partner in Kamindu Mendis, who chipped in with a well-made 55, as the pair steadied the innings through the middle session and denied India the quick breakthroughs they were chasing. Left-arm spinner Manav Suthar was among the Indian bowlers who made early inroads, keeping the pressure on through tight, disciplined spells.

The match remains finely poised heading into the final day, with India needing to close out the remaining Sri Lankan wickets quickly to set up a target, or force an outright win, on a pitch that has offered turn and assistance to the bowlers as the game has progressed.

## The context

India had earlier declared their first innings on a commanding 503 for 9, built around a series of substantial individual contributions, before bowling Sri Lanka out for 290 in their first essay at the crease and enforcing the follow-on. That decision to bat again immediately, rather than take a lead into a fresh Indian second innings, reflected India's confidence in closing out the match inside five days on a surface expected to deteriorate further as the Test wears on.

The result of this match carries series-defining weight. India go into the Colombo Test with a 1-0 lead, having beaten Sri Lanka by a commanding 165 runs in the series opener at Galle. A win or draw here would secure India the series, while Sri Lanka need victory to level things up and take the contest to a decider, a scenario that has kept crowds and broadcasters closely tuned in to every session of play.

## What to watch on Day 5

With Sri Lanka still 16 runs adrift and four second-innings wickets remaining, the final day promises a compelling contest between India's bowling attack, likely to lean heavily on spin as the pitch wears, and a Sri Lankan middle and lower order desperate to either wipe out the deficit and build a lead, or bat out time to salvage a draw. India's team management will be weighing up field placements and bowling changes carefully in the morning session, aware that early wickets could open up a clear path to a series-clinching victory well before the scheduled close of play.
