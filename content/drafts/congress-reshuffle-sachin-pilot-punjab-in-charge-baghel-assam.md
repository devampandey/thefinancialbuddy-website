The Indian National Congress on Saturday announced a sweeping reshuffle of its state-level leadership, reassigning general secretary in-charge roles across several states in a move widely read as an attempt to steady organisational troubles ahead of a busy electoral calendar. The announcement was made by AICC General Secretary K.C. Venugopal, and it immediately became one of the day's most discussed political developments.

## Pilot Moves to Punjab, Baghel Heads to Assam

The most closely watched change sends Sachin Pilot, the former Rajasthan deputy chief minister, to Punjab as the party's new general secretary in-charge. He replaces Bhupesh Baghel, the former Chhattisgarh chief minister, who has now been handed responsibility for Assam. Political observers noted that Baghel's exit from Punjab follows a stretch of visible infighting within the state unit, and several reports suggested the high command wanted a fresh face to manage the state as it looks ahead to future polls.

Pilot has been a prominent, if occasionally restive, figure within the party's central leadership circle for years, and his elevation to a politically sensitive state like Punjab will be seen as both a vote of confidence and a test of his troubleshooting abilities.

## Wider Rejig Across States

The changes were not limited to Punjab and Assam. Randeep Singh Surjewala, another senior party troubleshooter, was given additional charge of Gujarat while continuing to oversee Karnataka, effectively expanding his footprint within the organisation. Sukhdeo Bhagat was named in-charge of Chhattisgarh, the state Baghel once led as chief minister. Dr. Sirivella Prasad was appointed to oversee Maharashtra, while P.V. Mohan takes charge of Andhra Pradesh.

Taken together, the reshuffle touches several states where the Congress faces distinct challenges, from managing coalition dynamics to rebuilding organisational strength after recent electoral setbacks. By spreading responsibilities among a mix of experienced state-level leaders and central troubleshooters, the party leadership appears to be betting on a combination of regional familiarity and central oversight to steady its state units.

## Reading the Signal

Reshuffles of this kind are routine in Indian political parties, but the timing and scale of Saturday's changes suggest the Congress leadership is trying to get ahead of organisational friction before it hardens into a larger crisis. Punjab, in particular, has seen persistent tension within the state unit, and installing a leader with Pilot's profile signals the high command's intent to manage the situation directly rather than let it simmer.

Whether the new appointees can deliver tangible improvements in organisational cohesion and electoral readiness remains to be seen. For now, the changes give the party a fresh lineup of state managers heading into what is expected to be an eventful period on the political calendar, with implications for how the Congress positions itself in Punjab, Assam, Gujarat, Karnataka, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh alike.
