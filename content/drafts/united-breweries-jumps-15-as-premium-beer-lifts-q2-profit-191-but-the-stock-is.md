United Breweries (UBL), the maker of Kingfisher, gave investors one of the few bright spots on a bruising Wednesday for Indian equities. Its shares jumped as much as 15% to an intraday high of about Rs 1,317 from Tuesday's close of Rs 1,143.60 after the company reported that September-quarter profit had nearly tripled. The rally came while the Sensex was down more than 1% as crude oil climbed past $115 a barrel.

Early buying faded through the morning. By around noon the stock was up a little under 7% at roughly Rs 1,222, still a clear outperformer but well off the peak.

## The numbers behind the jump

For the July-September quarter of FY27, UBL's consolidated net profit rose about 191% to Rs 134.8 crore, from Rs 46.3 crore a year earlier. Net sales grew 24% to around Rs 2,537 crore. Revenue including excise duty came to roughly Rs 4,600 crore. Earnings per share rose to Rs 5.14 from Rs 1.78.

Operating profit grew faster than revenue. EBITDA more than doubled, and the EBITDA margin widened by 480 basis points to 11.9%. The company credited operating leverage and tighter cost control.

Volume did most of the work. Sell-in volumes rose 16%, and UBL said trade inventory ended the quarter below last year's level. That suggests the growth reflects consumer demand rather than stock pushed into distributors. The company also cited structural price increases, a better geographic mix and market-share gains in profitable states, led by Karnataka.

## Premiumisation is the real story

The headline profit number partly reflects a weak comparison: the September 2025 quarter was a poor one for UBL, with profit down sharply from the year before. The more durable signal is the mix shift.

Premium volumes grew 38% in the quarter, an acceleration of 31 percentage points over the June quarter. Heineken Silver volumes rose 67% and Kingfisher Ultra rose 44%. Premium brands earn higher margins per case, so a rising share of them in the mix lifts profitability even when overall volume growth is moderate. That is why margins expanded so much faster than sales.

## Why the stock is still cheap relative to its past

Even after Wednesday's move, UBL is about 30% below where it traded a year ago, and well short of its 52-week high of Rs 1,844. Two things explain the gap.

First, the quarter was weaker than the previous one. Profit fell about 19% and sales about 22% from the June quarter. Part of that is seasonal, since beer demand peaks in summer, but it shows the business is still prone to large swings from quarter to quarter.

Second, UBL said it plans to raise commercial spending in the December quarter to chase further share gains. Higher marketing and trade spending could eat into the margin gains made this quarter, so investors will watch whether the 11.9% margin holds.

On the charts, technical analysts noted that Wednesday's move lifted the stock above a short-term falling trendline after months of decline, with momentum indicators coming out of oversold territory. They also cautioned that the broader trend remains weak and flagged Rs 1,300 and Rs 1,400 as the next resistance levels if the recovery holds.

## What to watch

The key questions now are whether premium growth in the high-30s can last once the weak base effect fades, how heavily the planned Q3 spending weighs on margins, and whether state-level excise and pricing policy, a constant risk for alcohol makers in India, stays favourable through the festive and wedding season.
