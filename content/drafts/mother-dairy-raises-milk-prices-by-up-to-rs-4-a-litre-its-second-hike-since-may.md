Mother Dairy, the biggest milk supplier in Delhi-NCR, will raise the price of its milk by Rs 2 to Rs 4 per litre from Thursday, November 12, 2026. The steepest increase is on full cream milk, which goes from Rs 72 to Rs 76 a litre. It is the company's second price revision in six months, after an increase on May 14, and it comes days after Karnataka's Nandini and Gujarat's Amul raised prices on milk and dairy products. For households in the capital region, where Mother Dairy sells about 35 lakh litres a day, this is a direct and immediate rise in a daily expense. For the wider economy, it adds to food inflation at a time when consumer prices are already rising faster than they have in almost two years.

## The new prices

The revised rates in Delhi-NCR are:

- Full cream milk (pouch): Rs 76 a litre, up from Rs 72 (about 5.6%)
- Cow milk: Rs 65, up from Rs 62 (about 4.8%)
- Toned milk (pouch): Rs 62, up from Rs 60 (about 3.3%)
- Toned milk (bulk vended): Rs 60, up from Rs 58 (about 3.4%)
- Double toned milk: Rs 56, up from Rs 54 (about 3.7%)

The pattern matters. The richer variants that cost the most went up the most, both in rupees and in percentage terms. Double toned and bulk vended milk, which lower-income households tend to buy, rose by Rs 2.

## Why prices are going up

The company did not comment officially, but people familiar with the decision said the price Mother Dairy pays farmers for raw milk has risen and the increase is being passed on to buyers. Mother Dairy, a subsidiary of the National Dairy Development Board, says it hands 75-80% of what it earns from milk sales back to farmers. That structure means when procurement prices rise, retail prices tend to follow closely, because there is little margin in the middle to absorb the cost.

The company sells more than 50 lakh litres of milk a day across its markets and reported turnover of Rs 20,300 crore last financial year, up 17%.

## Part of a wider dairy price cycle

This is not an isolated move. On October 29, Karnataka raised the price of Nandini milk by Rs 8 a litre, and curd, butter and ghee were made costlier with it. Amul then raised prices for ghee and butter, and by early November Nandini had followed with further increases on ghee, butter and paneer. Mother Dairy's revision now carries that pressure into liquid milk in the country's largest urban market.

When the three biggest cooperative brands in their regions all move in the same direction within a few weeks, it usually points to a cost problem at the farm level, not a pricing decision by one company.

## What this means

For a household, the arithmetic is simple. A family that buys one litre of full cream milk a day will spend about Rs 120 more each month, or roughly Rs 1,460 a year. Families using toned milk will pay about Rs 60 more a month. That is not large on its own, but milk is bought every day, has few substitutes, and takes up a bigger share of spending in lower-income homes. If you are already reworking your monthly budget for higher food costs, our [budget calculator](/tools/budget-calculator) can help you see where the extra spending fits.

For inflation, the timing is unhelpful. Retail inflation rose to 5.54% in September, a 23-month high, with food inflation at 7.74%. Milk carries one of the largest weights of any single food item in the consumer price index, so a broad rise in dairy prices tends to show up in the data and, more importantly, tends to stay. Unlike vegetables, which can swing back down within weeks, milk prices rarely fall once they are raised. That makes this kind of increase more worrying for the Reserve Bank of India, which raised its repo rate in October, than a one-off spike in onion or tomato prices.

There are some things we do not know. The company has not said how much procurement prices have risen, or why. It is also unclear whether rival suppliers in Delhi-NCR, including Amul, will match the increase in liquid milk; if they do, the effect on prices will be wider. And Mother Dairy's revision covers only Delhi-NCR for now.

Our view: this is a cost-driven increase that consumers will have little choice but to absorb, and it strengthens the case that food inflation will stay sticky through the winter. For farmers, higher procurement prices are good news. For households and the RBI, it is one more reason not to expect quick relief on prices.

What to watch next: the October retail inflation data, whether Amul and other brands raise liquid milk prices in north India, and whether Mother Dairy extends the revision to its other markets.
