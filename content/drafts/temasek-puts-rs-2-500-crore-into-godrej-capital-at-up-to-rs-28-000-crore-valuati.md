Godrej group's lending business has found a heavyweight backer. On Thursday, Godrej Industries said its financial services arm, Godrej Capital, will receive Rs 2,500 crore from Jongsong Investments, an affiliate of Singapore state investor Temasek. The deal values Godrej Capital at up to Rs 28,000 crore and leaves the Godrej group in control of the company.

The market welcomed the news. Shares of Godrej Industries rose as much as 9.7% to an intraday high of Rs 1,062.90 on the NSE. Around 1 pm the stock was still up about 9%, giving the company a market value of close to Rs 35,000 crore.

## What Godrej Capital does

Godrej Capital is a relatively young non-bank lender, but it has grown quickly. Its assets under management stood at about Rs 35,000 crore as of October 31, 2026, and it operates in more than 100 cities. Its loan book spans home loans, loans against property, construction finance for developers, working capital and supply chain finance for small businesses, gold loans and personal loans.

The group has set a much bigger target. Chairperson Pirojsha Godrej said the aim is to turn Godrej Capital into a Rs 1 lakh crore AUM business by 2031, close to triple its current size in about five years.

## Why the capital matters

Growing a loan book that fast takes equity. Lenders must hold capital against the loans they write, so a non-bank finance company expanding at this pace either keeps going back to its parent for money or brings in outside investors. Reports on the deal describe the Temasek money as primary capital that goes into Godrej Capital itself, which strengthens its balance sheet and capital ratios without adding leverage at the parent.

Managing Director and CEO Manish Shah said the funds will be used to speed up growth, extend the company's reach across India, deepen its products for businesses and individuals, and invest in technology.

For Godrej Industries shareholders, the valuation is the key number. A third-party price of up to Rs 28,000 crore for the lending arm gives investors a market reference for an asset that had been hard to value inside a holding company, which helps explain the sharp move in the stock.

## Temasek's India bet

The deal adds to Temasek's long list of investments in Indian financial services. Global long-term investors have been backing Indian non-bank lenders that focus on retail and small-business credit, where demand remains strong and banks do not reach every borrower. It also builds on an older relationship: Temasek has invested in Godrej group companies before.

The Godrej group has spent the past few years building Godrej Capital into one of its main growth engines, alongside consumer goods, real estate and agri-businesses. Reports did not say when the transaction is expected to close or which regulatory approvals it needs.

## What to watch

The questions now are execution and asset quality. Tripling a loan book in five years in segments such as small-business credit and personal loans requires careful underwriting, especially as regulators keep a close watch on unsecured lending. Investors will look to Godrej Capital's upcoming quarterly numbers for signs that growth is not coming at the cost of credit quality.
