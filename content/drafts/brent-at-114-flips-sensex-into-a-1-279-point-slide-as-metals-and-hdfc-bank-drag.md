For about forty minutes on Friday, it looked as if Indian equities had found a floor. Then the oil market took over. Brent crude climbed to $114.70 a barrel, its highest level since May 4, and the BSE Sensex gave up an opening rally to slide as much as 1,279 points, or 1.7%, from its day's high of 73,761.51 to an intraday low of 72,487.

The NSE Nifty 50 mirrored the move, dropping about 380 points from a high near 22,787 to 22,407. At 10:45 am the Sensex was 433 points lower at 72,813 and the Nifty was down 198 points at 22,489. If the losses hold, it will be the fourth consecutive losing session for both benchmarks, which have each shed roughly 3% over that stretch. Thursday's close was 73,249.39 for the Sensex and 22,687.65 for the Nifty.

## Oil is now the whole story

The trigger this time sits in Saudi Arabia rather than the Strait of Hormuz alone. Houthi strikes on Saudi targets this week, including an attack near King Fahd International Airport in Dammam and a drone aimed at the Shaybah oilfield, have pushed traders to price in risk to the kingdom's own energy infrastructure. Reports that Saudi Aramco told remaining staff to leave its Dhahran headquarters and work remotely added to the unease.

The scale of the move matters for India, which imports most of its crude. Brent has risen about 63.5% from its July 2 low of $70.14 and is now within $12 of the record $126.40 it hit on April 30. Analysts warned that at these levels oil stops being a passing sentiment shock and starts feeding into inflation expectations, household spending and corporate margins, which makes a broad recovery in stocks hard without a sustained cooling in energy prices.

## Who did the damage

Two heavyweights carried a large share of the fall. HDFC Bank and Reliance Industries together accounted for about 15% of the Nifty's decline. In percentage terms, metal stocks were hit hardest: Hindalco and Tata Steel fell about 3% each, and the Nifty Metal index was down more than 3% in late-morning trade. HDFC Life, Jio Financial Services, Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles, Titan, Adani Enterprises and NTPC each lost over 2%.

The selling also reached the riskiest corner of the market. The Nifty Microcap 250 index slipped about 2%, and the India VIX, the market's fear gauge, rose more than 4%.

## The one pocket of strength

Technology shares went the other way. TCS gained more than 2% and Infosys rose over 1% ahead of its July-September results, due later on Friday. IT companies earn mostly in dollars, so a weaker rupee driven by higher oil import bills tends to support their earnings, which explains why they have become a defensive bet in this sell-off.

## Levels traders are watching

On the charts, the Nifty has slipped below a support band of about 22,700 to 22,750 that held earlier in the week. HDFC Securities' Nagaraj Shetti said the daily candle resembles a doji, a pattern that sometimes signals a reversal after a long decline, but he added that the broader trend stays down and that further weakness could drag the index towards 22,200 to 22,300 next week.

For investors, the takeaway is that the direction of crude, more than domestic earnings, is setting the market's mood right now. Infosys's numbers and guidance after market hours will show whether the IT trade can keep holding up the index on days like this one.
