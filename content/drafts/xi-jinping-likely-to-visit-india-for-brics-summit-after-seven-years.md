Chinese President Xi Jinping is likely to travel to New Delhi next month for the BRICS summit, according to a Reuters report citing people familiar with the matter. If confirmed, it would mark Xi's first visit to India in seven years, a gap that has spanned some of the most difficult years in the two countries' modern relationship.

India is set to host the summit on September 12 and 13 as the current chair of the bloc. Sources told Reuters that Xi could arrive with a delegation of roughly 400 officials, more than double the size of the group that accompanied him on his last visit in 2019. One Indian official said Chinese counterparts were already working through logistics such as hotel arrangements and security protocols, though the final travel plans and any bilateral agenda have not been locked in.

## Border Talks Underway in Beijing

The prospect of Xi's visit comes as the two sides continue working through their long-standing border dispute. India's National Security Adviser Ajit Doval was in Beijing this week for talks with Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi, the two officials designated to lead the boundary negotiations mechanism established in 2003 to address the roughly 3,488-kilometre frontier. Doval also met Chinese Vice President Han Zheng ahead of those discussions, with both sides emphasising the need to maintain peace along the border while expanding cooperation on economic and multilateral issues.

## A Slow Thaw Since Galwan

Relations between India and China deteriorated sharply after the deadly 2020 clash in the Galwan Valley, which killed 20 Indian soldiers and four Chinese soldiers and triggered a four-year military standoff along the Line of Actual Control. The two militaries reached a disengagement agreement in October 2024, and Prime Minister Narendra Modi travelled to China last year in his first visit to the country in seven years. Since then, ties have improved only gradually, with both governments treading carefully around unresolved issues, including the status of Arunachal Pradesh.

Xi's possible attendance at the BRICS summit would be read as a further, more visible sign of that thaw. Notably, Xi skipped the 2025 BRICS summit held in Brazil, so his presence in New Delhi would carry symbolic weight beyond the summit's formal agenda. Officials from both governments are expected to use the sidelines of the gathering to explore ways of deepening engagement even as they continue to manage friction over the border and regional security matters.

China's foreign ministry, for its part, has been circumspect in public, saying only that Beijing values BRICS cooperation and supports India's role as host this year, while declining to confirm details of Xi's travel. India's foreign ministry has similarly not issued an official comment on the matter, leaving the visit in the realm of expectation rather than confirmation for now.

The BRICS summit itself brings together leaders from Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa and the bloc's newer members at a moment when the grouping has been positioning itself as a counterweight to Western-led economic institutions. A high-profile Xi-Modi engagement on Indian soil, even a brief one, would be closely watched well beyond the region for what it signals about the durability of the India-China reset.
