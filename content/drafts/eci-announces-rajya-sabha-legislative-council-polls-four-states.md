The Election Commission of India on Tuesday announced the schedule for a fresh round of indirect elections, covering Rajya Sabha seats from three states and 28 Legislative Council seats spread across four others. The announcement sets in motion a compressed election calendar running through late October.

For the Rajya Sabha, the Commission notified elections to fill vacant seats from Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal. These seats are elected by the members of the respective state legislative assemblies rather than by direct public vote, a routine but closely watched process given how Rajya Sabha composition shapes the arithmetic of India's upper house of Parliament.

Alongside this, the Commission announced biennial elections to 28 Legislative Council seats that have fallen vacant across four states. Uttar Pradesh accounts for the largest share with 11 seats, split between five Graduates' constituencies and six Teachers' constituencies. Bihar follows with eight seats, evenly divided between four Graduates' and four Teachers' constituencies. Maharashtra has five seats up for election, three from Graduates' constituencies and two from Teachers' constituencies, while Karnataka rounds out the list with four seats, split evenly between the two categories.

Graduates' and Teachers' constituencies are a distinctive feature of India's state legislative councils, where voting rights are restricted to registered graduates and teachers within the constituency rather than the general electorate. These seats often carry outsized influence in state politics because of their more educated, issue-driven voter base, and contests for them are frequently fought hard by regional parties and student or teacher unions with deep roots in the constituency.

The Commission has laid out a clear timeline for the process. The official notification for the biennial council elections will be issued on September 29, after which candidates have until October 6 to file their nominations. Scrutiny of nominations will follow on October 7, with October 9 set as the last date for candidates to withdraw from the contest. Polling across all the seats, both Rajya Sabha and Legislative Council, will take place on October 23 between 8 am and 4 pm, and votes will be counted on October 27.

The schedule gives political parties roughly a month to finalise candidates and organise campaigns, particularly for the Graduates' and Teachers' constituency seats, which tend to see intense localised campaigning rather than the mass rallies associated with general elections. With Uttar Pradesh and Bihar together accounting for more than two-thirds of the Legislative Council seats up for grabs, the outcome will be watched closely as an early indicator of political mood in both states ahead of larger electoral contests down the line.

The Commission's announcement is a routine administrative exercise required whenever legislative council or Rajya Sabha seats fall vacant, whether through retirement, resignation or the end of a member's term, and does not by itself signal any change to the broader political calendar in the states involved.
