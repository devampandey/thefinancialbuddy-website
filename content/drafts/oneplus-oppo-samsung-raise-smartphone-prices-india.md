Smartphone buyers in India are facing yet another round of price increases, with OnePlus, OPPO and Samsung all raising prices across multiple models as manufacturers grapple with rising memory and component costs at a time when overall demand in the domestic market is cooling. The latest revisions, most of which took effect this week, range from roughly Rs 1,000 to Rs 5,000 depending on the device and variant.

## A Pattern of Repeated Hikes

The increases are notable less for their size than for their frequency. OnePlus has now raised prices for the fifth time in nine months, with its latest revision communicated to retail partners in mid-August and implemented shortly after. Since December last year, the company has adjusted prices on eight models in its lineup, with individual increases ranging from single digits to over a quarter of the original price. Samsung, meanwhile, has revised prices on its budget-focused Galaxy M and F series smartphones for the ninth time in eight months, with select models seeing increases of roughly Rs 1,000. OPPO has also raised prices across its Reno and A-series smartphones, with one model seeing an increase of Rs 5,000 at the top end of its variant lineup.

Industry analysts say the repeated nature of these revisions reflects genuine cost pressure rather than one-off adjustments. Smartphone manufacturers rely heavily on dynamic random-access memory and NAND flash storage, both of which have seen sharp price increases over the past year as demand from artificial intelligence infrastructure and data centre buildouts has surged. With AI and cloud companies able to absorb higher input costs more easily than consumer electronics makers, smartphone brands are increasingly finding themselves competing for the same components at a disadvantage.

## Retailers Push Back

The price increases have also stirred friction with India's organised retail trade. The All India Mobile Retailers Association has criticised OnePlus's broader retail strategy, noting that the company scaled back its general-trade operations earlier this year in a move that affected tens of thousands of retailers who had previously sourced its devices through regional distributors. The retailer body has sought government intervention, arguing the shift amounts to unfair trade practice, even as OnePlus maintains that its pricing and distribution decisions reflect ordinary business and market considerations.

## A Market Already Under Pressure

The price hikes land at a difficult moment for India's smartphone industry. Shipments in the country declined by roughly 10 to 11 per cent year-on-year in the second quarter of the year, as rising prices and weaker affordability weighed on consumer upgrade cycles. Analysts note that brands with stronger premium positioning and better resale value, such as Samsung, may have more room to absorb higher costs without significantly denting demand, while manufacturers more reliant on price-sensitive, mid-range buyers face a tougher balancing act.

The timing is particularly sensitive with the festive season approaching, a period when Indian smartphone brands typically lean on discounts and promotional pricing to drive volumes. With component costs showing no signs of easing, manufacturers may have considerably less room to offer the kind of aggressive festive discounts that have driven sales in previous years, leaving both brands and consumers to navigate a more expensive upgrade cycle through the rest of 2026.
