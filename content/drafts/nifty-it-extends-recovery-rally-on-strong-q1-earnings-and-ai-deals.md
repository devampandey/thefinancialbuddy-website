Technology stocks have quietly become one of the standout stories of India's equity market over the past month. After a rough start to 2026 that left the Nifty IT index down as much as 29 percent for the calendar year, the sector has staged a sharp turnaround, gaining nearly 16 percent in July alone and extending those gains into early August, with the index adding a further 2 percent or so in Monday's session even as broader indices traded more cautiously.

## From 52-Week Lows to Sustained Recovery

The scale of the reversal is notable. Nifty IT touched a 52-week low of roughly 25,699 on July 1, and has since recovered more than 21 percent from that trough. Heavyweights Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, Coforge, and LTIMindtree have led the climb, with the index rising for multiple consecutive sessions as investor sentiment toward the sector improved. The move stands in sharp contrast to the mood earlier in the year, when concerns about slowing global technology spending had weighed heavily on export-oriented IT names.

## Earnings and Deal Wins Behind the Rally

Two forces appear to be driving the recovery. First, Q1 FY27 earnings from India's major IT services firms came in stronger than the market had braced for, easing fears that enterprise clients were pulling back sharply on technology budgets. Second, a wave of large deal announcements has reinforced confidence in order pipelines. Tata Consultancy Services shares alone jumped more than 6 percent on news of a significant multi-year agreement with Swiss engineering group ABB to modernise its global network operations using artificial intelligence tools, a deal that investors read as evidence that AI is becoming a source of new business for Indian IT vendors rather than purely a threat to their existing services model.

## A Rotation Story as Much as a Fundamentals Story

Part of the rally also reflects positioning rather than pure fundamentals. Investors who had grown wary of stretched valuations and heavy capital spending among global semiconductor and AI infrastructure names appear to have rotated some of that money into what now looks like a comparatively cheaper software services trade. Easing worries about excessive AI-related spending, combined with expectations that the US Federal Reserve could begin cutting interest rates later in the year, have made technology stocks more attractive, since lower rates increase the present value of companies' future earnings. A relatively weak rupee has added a further tailwind for export-driven IT revenues.

## What to Watch From Here

For market participants, the key question is whether this recovery reflects a genuine turn in the technology spending cycle or a shorter-term rotation that could reverse if global rate expectations or AI spending sentiment shift again. The sector's steep decline earlier in the year was itself driven by concerns that have not fully disappeared, only eased. With the Reserve Bank of India's rate decision due later this week and US Fed policy signals still evolving, IT stocks are likely to stay sensitive to global cues even as domestic earnings momentum provides support.
