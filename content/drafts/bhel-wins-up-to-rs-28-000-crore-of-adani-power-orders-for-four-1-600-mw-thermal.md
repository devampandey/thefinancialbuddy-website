Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd (BHEL) has secured orders from Adani Power for four coal-fired power projects of 2x800 MW each, the state-owned engineering company told stock exchanges on Wednesday. Each contract is valued between Rs 5,000 crore and Rs 7,000 crore excluding GST, putting the combined package at roughly Rs 20,000 crore to Rs 28,000 crore.

BHEL's scope covers the core of each plant: boilers, turbines and generators along with their auxiliaries, plus agreed services. Together, the four projects add up to 6,400 MW of new thermal capacity. The company said the orders are not related-party transactions.

## A large order on top of a record backlog

At the end of September, BHEL's outstanding order book stood at about Rs 2.58 lakh crore, the largest in its history. The Adani package alone would add between 8% and 11% to that figure, depending on where the final contract values land. Thermal power still makes up the bulk of the backlog, but the company has been building other pipelines too, including about Rs 16,400 crore in transportation, Rs 13,600 crore in transmission, Rs 12,800 crore in nuclear and Rs 7,300 crore in defence.

The orders fit a wider pattern. After years of stagnant coal-plant ordering, India's utilities have returned to thermal capacity to meet rising peak power demand, and private generators such as Adani Power have been placing large repeat orders. For BHEL, which faces few domestic rivals for supercritical 800 MW sets, the revival has turned a long-weak order pipeline into a multi-year execution challenge.

## Market reaction

BHEL shares rose as much as 6% to Rs 423.10 on the BSE in early trade on Wednesday on heavy volumes, well ahead of a modest gain in the Sensex. The stock remains below its record high of Rs 463.60, hit on October 14. Adani Power shares edged up about 1% to around Rs 197.

## Execution is the real test

The bigger question for investors is not winning orders but delivering them on time and at a profit. BHEL added 2,260 MW of capacity in the first half of FY27 against a full-year target of 4,790 MW, which means the second half has to run noticeably faster. Receivable days improved to 94 from 120 a year earlier, a sign that cash collection is getting tighter.

Brokerages remain split on valuation. Geojit upgraded the stock to buy last week with a target of Rs 453, arguing that operating leverage could lift return on equity from about 6% in FY26 to over 15% by FY28. ICICI Securities has a buy rating with a fair value of Rs 605. At the other end, Macquarie is neutral with a Rs 420 target, and Kotak Institutional Equities has a sell call with a target of just Rs 160, reflecting concern that the share price already assumes years of smooth execution.

For a company with a history of project delays and cost overruns, Wednesday's orders strengthen revenue visibility well into the next decade. Whether that turns into sustained earnings growth depends on margins holding up as raw material costs and project timelines are tested.
