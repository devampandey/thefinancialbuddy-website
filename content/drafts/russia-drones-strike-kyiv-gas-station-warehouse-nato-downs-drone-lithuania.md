Russian forces launched a fresh wave of drone strikes on Kyiv overnight into Tuesday, hitting a gas station in the capital's Darnytskyi district and warehouse premises in the Obolonskyi district, Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said. One man was hospitalised in serious condition after the strike on the fuel station, while authorities said details of the damage in Obolonskyi were still being confirmed.

The attack was part of a broader pattern of nightly drone assaults that have targeted Kyiv's energy and civilian infrastructure in recent weeks, part of what Ukrainian officials describe as a deliberate campaign to wear down morale and strain the power grid ahead of the coming winter. Emergency crews were dispatched to both strike sites soon after the attacks were reported.

## NATO scrambles over Lithuania

In a separate but related episode overnight, a drone that crossed into Lithuanian airspace from neighbouring Belarus was shot down by NATO fighter jets. The aircraft was tracked flying over Lentvaris, near the capital Vilnius, before being intercepted close to the city of Kaunas, according to Vilmantas Vitkauskas, head of Lithuania's National Crisis Management Center.

Lithuanian President Gitanas Nauseda thanked allied forces for their "swift response" in a social media post, calling the incident a reminder of the risks NATO's eastern members face as Russia's war against Ukraine continues into its fifth year. Lithuania, a NATO and European Union member bordering both Belarus and Russia's Kaliningrad exclave, has repeatedly reported stray drones and airspace violations linked to the conflict.

Poland's military also said it activated aircraft over its own airspace as a precautionary measure during the night's drone activity targeting Ukraine, a step it has taken on several previous occasions when Russian strikes edged close to the Polish border. The Polish Operational Command said the preventive measures concluded a few hours after being triggered, with no reported incursion into Polish territory this time.

## A war still reshaping the region

The overnight incidents underscore how, even as diplomatic channels intermittently discuss limited ceasefires on energy infrastructure, the war continues to generate near-daily friction along NATO's borders. Ukrainian and allied officials have repeatedly flagged the risk of stray Russian drones drifting into neighbouring airspace, a concern that has occasionally strained relations between Moscow and NATO members such as Poland, Romania and the Baltic states.

For Kyiv residents, the strikes are part of a now-familiar routine of overnight air raid sirens, drone intercepts and morning damage assessments. Ukrainian officials have not yet said whether the attacks on the capital were linked to any broader escalation or were part of the routine tempo of strikes that has persisted through the summer and into autumn.

Neither Russian nor Belarusian authorities had issued an official statement on the Lithuanian drone incident at the time of publication. The Kremlin has previously described such incursions as accidental, while Kyiv and its allies argue they reflect a wider Russian tactic of testing NATO's air defences.
