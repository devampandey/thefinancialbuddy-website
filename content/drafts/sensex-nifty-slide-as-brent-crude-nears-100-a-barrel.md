Indian equity benchmarks fell sharply in Wednesday's session as global crude oil prices surged toward the psychologically important $100-a-barrel mark, reviving worries about inflation, the current account deficit and corporate margins across energy-intensive sectors. By midday trade, the BSE Sensex was down more than 570 points, or about 0.76%, to around 75,004, while the NSE Nifty50 slipped nearly 139 points, or 0.59%, to near 23,496.

The sell-off tracked a broader risk-off mood in global markets after Brent crude briefly touched the $100 level for the first time in weeks, before paring some of the gains, as renewed strikes across the Middle East stoked fears of prolonged disruption to oil supplies. Traders pointed to attacks on energy infrastructure in the Gulf region as the immediate trigger, adding to weeks of elevated crude prices driven by the broader conflict involving Iran.

## What is driving the market's nerves

For India, which imports the vast majority of its crude requirements, a sustained move toward $100 oil carries outsized consequences: a wider trade and current account deficit, added pressure on the rupee, and the risk of imported inflation feeding through to fuel and transport costs just as the festive season approaches. The rupee has been trading under pressure in recent sessions, hovering close to record lows against the dollar, compounding investor unease.

Rate-sensitive and oil-linked stocks bore the brunt of Wednesday's decline, with technology heavyweights among the top laggards on the Nifty50 even as select defensive names such as pharmaceuticals and select metals stocks managed to buck the trend. Sun Pharma, Adani Ports, Tata Steel, Trent and Bharat Electronics were among the gainers on the Sensex, while IT bellwethers led the losses as investors rotated away from richly valued growth stocks amid the uncertain macro backdrop.

## Outlook remains clouded

Market participants said sentiment is likely to stay volatile in the near term, with crude prices, the trajectory of the Middle East conflict, and incoming signals from the US Federal Reserve on interest rates all likely to dictate the direction of Indian equities. Gift Nifty futures had already signaled a weak start to Wednesday's session, opening lower ahead of the cash market open, reflecting the cautious mood carried over from overnight moves in oil and global equities.

Analysts noted that while Indian corporate earnings and domestic institutional buying have provided some cushion against foreign portfolio outflows in recent months, a prolonged spike in oil prices would test that resilience, particularly for sectors such as aviation, paints, tyres and consumer durables that are directly exposed to crude-linked input costs.
