Bengaluru-based Ultraviolette Automotive has raised $85 million in a Series E funding round led by Yali Capital and TDK Ventures, the electric motorcycle maker announced, as it looks to scale manufacturing and push further into international markets.

## Who's Backing the Round

The round drew participation from Lip-Bu Tan, chairman of Walden International, who has also joined Ultraviolette as an advisor, alongside the company's existing investor base. The company did not disclose a valuation for the round.

## Where the Money Is Going

Ultraviolette said the fresh capital will go toward scaling production of its current line-up, developing new electric vehicle platforms, and deepening its presence in international markets. The company currently sells its F77 motorcycle and X-47 Crossover across India and 20 European countries, and has two new products in the pipeline: the Tesseract electric scooter and the Shockwave enduro motorcycle.

The funding follows Ultraviolette's earlier announcement of a new manufacturing facility designed for an annual production capacity of up to 500,000 units, a sharp step-up from its current scale and a signal of how aggressively the company is betting on both domestic and export demand.

## The Global Push

International sales currently account for roughly 15% of Ultraviolette's overall volumes, and the company said it expects that share to climb to around 25% as its overseas operations mature. The company is targeting an entry into the US market in 2027, alongside further expansion across Latin America and Southeast Asia, positioning itself as a India-made performance-EV brand competing on a global stage rather than one confined to the domestic two-wheeler market.

## Why It Matters

The round lands at a moment when India's electric two-wheeler segment is maturing beyond early-stage subsidy-driven growth into a phase where companies are being judged on manufacturing scale, product breadth and their ability to compete internationally. A large, late-stage round for a performance-EV maker — rather than a mass-market scooter brand — also signals investor appetite for premium, export-oriented plays out of India's EV sector, a segment that has drawn comparisons to how Indian IT and pharma companies built global businesses from domestic manufacturing bases.
