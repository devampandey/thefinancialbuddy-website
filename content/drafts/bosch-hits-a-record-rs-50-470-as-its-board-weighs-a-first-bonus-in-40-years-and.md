Shares of Bosch Ltd, the Indian arm of the German engineering group, climbed to a record high on Tuesday after the company said its board would consider splitting its shares and issuing bonus shares. The stock rose as much as 5.37% to Rs 50,470 on the BSE, against Monday's close of Rs 47,896, taking the company's market value to about Rs 1.46 lakh crore.

The new peak beat the previous high of Rs 49,629.50 set on September 1. By late morning the stock was still about 4% higher near Rs 49,790, outpacing a Sensex that was up around 1% at the time.

## What the board will consider

In an exchange filing after market hours on Monday, Bosch said its board, when it meets on November 6, will also take up two proposals: a sub-division of its Rs 10 face-value shares, and an issue of bonus shares. Both would need shareholder approval. The company has not yet disclosed a split ratio, a bonus ratio or record dates.

The same meeting had already been scheduled to approve results for the July-September quarter, so investors will get the earnings and the capital decisions on the same day.

The rarity of the move is part of the excitement. Bosch last issued bonus shares in 1986, and its only previous split came in 2004, when the face value was cut from Rs 100 to Rs 10. A fresh bonus would be the first in about 40 years.

## Why a split matters, and why it does not

A stock split or bonus issue does not change what a company is worth. If Bosch splits each share into ten, a shareholder ends up with ten times as many shares, each priced at roughly a tenth of the old level. The total value of the holding stays the same.

What changes is access. At around Rs 50,000 per share, Bosch is one of the most expensive stocks by share price on Dalal Street, which puts even a single share beyond the reach of many small investors and makes trading less fluid. Companies that split often see more retail participation and narrower bid-ask spreads. Announcements like this also tend to be read as a signal that management is confident about the business, which helps explain the jump.

## The rally before the news

Tuesday's gain caps a strong run. The stock has risen about 76% from Rs 28,650 on March 30 and is up roughly 38% so far this year, including about 11% in the past month.

Part of that reflects expectations for the quarter ahead. ICICI Securities expects Bosch's September-quarter revenue to grow about 50% from a year earlier, helped by the consolidation of the recently acquired RBIC business, which it estimates added about Rs 1,250 crore in the quarter, along with steady growth in the automotive segment and a recovery in non-mobility businesses. The brokerage also expects the operating margin to widen by about 190 basis points, thanks to a richer mix and better operating leverage, partly offset by cost inflation.

## The risk for latecomers

With the stock at a record and a major announcement already priced in, Friday's board meeting carries a "sell the news" risk. If the quarterly numbers fall short of the 50% revenue growth the market is expecting, the split alone may not be enough to hold the stock at these levels. Investors buying now are effectively betting on the earnings, not the corporate action.
