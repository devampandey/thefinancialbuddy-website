Gaurav Munjal, the co-founder who built Unacademy into one of the best-known names of India's edtech boom, is stepping back from running the company. In a LinkedIn post on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, he said he would move into the role of executive chairman, with Akhilendra Kumar, Unacademy's chief business officer, taking over as chief executive.

The handover is the clearest sign yet of how much Unacademy has changed since it was sold. It comes roughly two months after upGrad, the education group led by Ronnie Screwvala, completed its acquisition of the test-prep platform in September.

## A founder steps aside, but not away

Munjal has been careful to frame the move as a change of title rather than an exit. According to his post, as reported by Inc42 and NewsBytes, he plans to stay closely involved in day-to-day operations and to work alongside Kumar on business reviews and major decisions. He also said he would help with upGrad's preparations for a stock market listing, and he publicly expressed full confidence in his successor.

That distinction matters. Founder departures after an acquisition often signal friction, but in this case the founder is staying inside the tent, with a formal seat at the top and a stated role in the parent company's next big step. The company has not yet said when the change takes effect.

It is also not the first time Munjal's role has been in the spotlight. In May 2025, several media outlets reported that he was set to give up the CEO post as Unacademy weighed a costlier push into offline coaching centres. This time, the announcement came from Munjal himself and names a successor from within the leadership team.

## What upGrad paid, and what it says about edtech

The sale price is a reminder of how far edtech valuations have fallen. upGrad bought Unacademy in an all-stock deal valued at just over $200 million, with Unacademy's shareholders receiving upGrad shares in exchange. That is about 94% below the roughly $3.4 billion valuation Unacademy commanded at its 2021 peak, when pandemic-era online learning drew record venture funding into Indian education startups.

Under the deal, Unacademy's brands and businesses were kept intact rather than folded into upGrad. Reports on the leadership change say that under Kumar, the company will prioritise profitable growth and tighter execution, while continuing to move towards online learning products and franchise-led models instead of capital-heavy expansion.

## Why it matters

For students and parents, little is likely to change in the short term, since the Unacademy brand and its courses remain in place. For the wider startup ecosystem, the story is a useful marker of where Indian edtech now stands: consolidation, a focus on cash discipline, and founders taking supervisory roles while operators run the business.

The bigger question is upGrad's possible IPO. A cleaner management structure at its largest recent acquisition, with a founder publicly backing the new CEO, is the kind of signal a company preparing for public investors would want to send. How quickly Kumar can show steady, profitable growth at Unacademy will shape how that listing story is received.

What to watch next: the effective date of the transition, any further senior changes at Unacademy, and filings or timelines linked to upGrad's planned public offering.
