The number that set the tone for Wednesday's session did not come from the stock market at all. Around midday, the commerce ministry reported that wholesale inflation climbed to 11.39% in September from 9.92% in August, the highest reading in the revised WPI series. Bond yields moved higher on the data, and by the closing bell the Nifty had surrendered a good part of the previous day's 1.6% rally.

The Sensex ended 251.89 points, or 0.34%, lower at 72,781.89. The Nifty 50 dropped 99.25 points, or 0.44%, to settle at 22,622.70. The broader market was weaker as well, with the Nifty Midcap 100 slipping 0.08% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 losing 0.32%.

## Why a wholesale number mattered so much

Wholesale prices are an early signal of costs that eventually reach consumers. The September jump was driven mainly by fuel and power, where annual inflation hit 25.85%, as the oil shock linked to the conflict in West Asia continued to feed through. Food and manufactured goods also rose faster than in August.

For equity investors, the worry is what this means for interest rates. The Reserve Bank of India has already turned towards tightening, and a sharp rise in input costs strengthens the case for keeping policy tight for longer. Higher yields hurt sectors that depend on cheap borrowing, and that is where the selling concentrated. Analysts at Geojit Investments pointed to autos, private banks and metals as the segments most affected by the inflation data.

Lenders and financiers took the biggest hits among large caps. Shriram Finance, HDFC Life Insurance and Bajaj Finance were the top Nifty losers, while Bajaj Finance, Mahindra & Mahindra, Bajaj Finserv, Eternal and ICICI Bank weighed most on the Sensex.

## Pharma, not banks, was the weakest sector

On a sectoral basis, the Nifty Pharma index fell 1.3%, making it the worst performer of the day, and the healthcare index followed it lower. The drop extended weakness in drugmakers that was visible from the morning session.

At the other end, two defensive pockets held firm. The Nifty IT and Nifty FMCG indices each gained more than 1%. Infosys, Tech Mahindra and Tata Consultancy Services were among the top Sensex gainers, along with Hindustan Unilever, NTPC and Reliance Industries. Tech Mahindra's rise came a day before its September-quarter results, where its board is also set to consider a bonus issue.

## A market stuck in a range

The day's move left the Nifty inside a band it has been trading in for 11 sessions. SBI Securities noted that the index remains below its 20-day and 50-day exponential moving averages, though momentum indicators show selling pressure easing. It sees 22,480 to 22,500 as immediate support and 22,770 to 22,800 as the first hurdle on the upside. A clean break of either side would likely decide the next directional move.

Some factors cushioned the fall. Crude prices eased during the day, and the rupee had strengthened after the RBI's recent currency measures. Talk of a possible review of long-term capital gains tax and the securities transaction tax also continued to support sentiment.

## What comes next

Traders were also cautious ahead of the US consumer price data due after the Indian close, given its bearing on Federal Reserve policy and global risk appetite. At home, the September-quarter earnings season moves into a busier phase, with Tech Mahindra among the companies reporting on Thursday.
