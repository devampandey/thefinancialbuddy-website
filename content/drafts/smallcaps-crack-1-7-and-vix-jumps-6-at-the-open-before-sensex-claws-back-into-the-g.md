The first hour of trade on Monday was a reminder that the headline indices tell only part of the story. While the Sensex and Nifty 50 opened with modest losses, the selling in smaller stocks was far more aggressive, and a key gauge of expected volatility spiked before buyers stepped in.

## The damage was in the broader market

Within minutes of the opening bell, the Nifty Smallcap 100 had fallen 1.69% and the Nifty Midcap 100 was down 1.23%, roughly two to three times the decline in the benchmarks. The India VIX, which tracks expected swings in the Nifty over the next month, rose about 6%, a sign that traders were paying up for protection.

The benchmarks themselves opened soft. The Sensex started at 72,990.04 against Friday's close of 73,123.83 and slid as much as 0.83% to an intraday low of 72,514.57. The Nifty 50 opened at 22,524.40 and touched 22,387, down 0.94% at the worst point. At the open, every sectoral index except pharma traded lower, with the Nifty Auto, FMCG, IT and PSU Bank indices each down more than 1%.

Several mid-sized names marked fresh 52-week lows in the morning, including Swiggy, Sundaram Finance, NHPC, Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders and L&T Finance. That cluster, spanning consumer internet, lending, power and defence, points to broad de-risking rather than a single-sector problem.

## Crude set the tone, then eased

The backdrop was oil. Brent crude settled at $116.72 a barrel on Friday after a 6% weekly gain, driven by attacks on shipping and energy infrastructure in the Gulf. On Monday it eased by about 1.6% in Asian trade to near $114.9, as overbought conditions prompted some profit-taking. For India, which imports most of its crude, every sustained move above $110 feeds into concerns about inflation, the fiscal deficit and the rupee. Geojit's V K Vijayakumar said crude at these levels would cap any rally from the 22,600 area on the Nifty.

## The recovery, and who led it

As oil cooled, the benchmarks reversed. By about 10:20 am, the Sensex was up roughly 0.5%, more than 300 points above Friday's close, and the Nifty was back above 22,600 with a gain of about 0.46%. That was a swing of close to 1,000 points on the Sensex from its morning low.

Metals and a handful of results-driven stocks did the lifting. BSE Ltd was the top Nifty gainer, up about 3.75%, followed by JSW Steel at about 3.2% and Adani Ports at 1.7%. Kotak Mahindra Bank was also among the early gainers. IT stayed a drag, with Infosys sliding after cutting its growth guidance.

## Why the split matters

The pattern of the morning, a sharp fall in small and mid caps alongside a contained dip in large caps, has become familiar this month. Foreign investors have sold heavily in October, and smaller stocks with thinner liquidity are bearing the brunt whenever risk appetite fades. A recovery led by a few large names does not by itself signal that the pressure on the broader market has lifted. For that, traders will want to see smallcap indices recover their opening losses and the VIX settle back down.
