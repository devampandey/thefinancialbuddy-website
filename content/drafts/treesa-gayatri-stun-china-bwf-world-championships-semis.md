India's Treesa Jolly and Gayatri Gopichand pulled off one of the standout wins of their careers on Friday, coming from a game down to beat world number four Chinese pair Jia Yi Fan and Zhang Shu Xian in the women's doubles quarterfinals of the BWF World Championships in New Delhi, sealing India's first medal in the event since 2011.

## How the Match Unfolded

The unseeded Indian duo, who arrived at the tournament having lost all three of their previous meetings with the Chinese pair, started slowly and fell behind early, at one stage trailing 1-6. They clawed back to 8-9 with a string of successive points but ultimately dropped the opening game 16-21 as Jia and Zhang held their nerve in the closing stretch.

The second game followed a similar arc in reverse. China raced to an early lead, moving 10-7 ahead, before Treesa and Gayatri mounted a spirited comeback, drawing level and then pulling clear to win 21-15 and force a decider.

It was in the third game that the Indian pair produced their most complete performance of the match. After an even start at 4-4, Treesa found the breakthrough with a powerful smash that triggered a run of seven straight points, swinging the match from 4-all to a commanding 11-5 lead at the interval. China briefly threatened a fightback after the change of ends, but Treesa and Gayatri responded with another burst of points and did not relinquish control again, eventually closing out the match 21-13 after roughly one hour and nine minutes on court.

## What the Win Means

The victory sends Treesa and Gayatri into the semifinals, and under the World Championships format, reaching the last four alone guarantees at least a bronze medal. That makes them only the second Indian pair ever to win a women's doubles medal at the tournament, following Jwala Gutta and Ashwini Ponnappa's bronze in London back in 2011 -- a 15-year gap now finally broken.

The result caps a rapid rise for a pairing that had broken into the world's top 10 before a training injury to Treesa derailed their momentum earlier this year. Their return through the Indonesia Open, the US Open and a title-winning run at the Philippine International Challenge set the stage for this run on home soil, where a supportive New Delhi crowd has been a factor throughout the week.

## The Bigger Picture for India

The result arrived on a day that otherwise saw India's singles campaign end, with both PV Sindhu and Ayush Shetty exiting in the round of 16 a day earlier. That leaves the country's medal hopes resting squarely on its doubles pairs. Men's doubles duo Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty, two-time World Championships bronze medallists, were also readying for their own quarterfinal against China's third seeds later in the day, meaning India could yet double its medal tally from the tournament depending on that result.

For Treesa and Gayatri, the achievement already stands as a landmark moment in Indian women's doubles badminton, and their semifinal opponent will be decided by the other half of the draw.
