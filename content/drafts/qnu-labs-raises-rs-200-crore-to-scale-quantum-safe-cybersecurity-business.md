QNu Labs, a Bengaluru-based deep-tech cybersecurity company building quantum-safe security solutions, has raised Rs 200 crore in a Series A1 funding round led by the National Quantum Mission and Speciale Invest, with participation from Sony Innovation Fund, Gaja Capital and Artha Ventures, among other investors. The round takes the company's total capital raised to Rs 375 crore and marks one of the larger funding events in India's still-nascent quantum technology sector.

Founded in 2016 after being incubated at IIT Madras, QNu Labs was started by Srinivasa Rao Aluri and Sunil Gupta and has spent nearly a decade building quantum-safe encryption technology aimed at protecting critical infrastructure, government systems and the banking, financial services and insurance sector from future quantum-enabled cyberattacks. The company says its products are already deployed across these sectors both in India and internationally.

## Where the Money Will Go

The fresh capital will be used to accelerate research and development, deepen the company's technology stack, and scale its sales and go-to-market efforts across India and global markets. A portion of the funding will also support QNu's contribution to a government-backed project to build the technical backbone of India's Quantum Secure and Sensing Networks, a national initiative aimed at securing digital infrastructure against emerging quantum computing threats. The company said it plans to extend its offerings into quantum sensing and artificial intelligence as part of this expansion.

Company executives pointed to growing global urgency around the shift to quantum-safe infrastructure, noting that roughly 80 percent of the new round came from investors outside India, which they described as a sign of rising international confidence in India's quantum technology capabilities. India's National Quantum Mission has set an aggressive 2029 timeline for sectors to begin migrating to quantum-resistant cryptographic systems, a shift driven by concerns that sufficiently advanced quantum computers could eventually break widely used encryption standards.

## Part of a Broader Global Race

The investment reflects a broader global race among governments and enterprises to prepare for the eventual arrival of quantum computers capable of undermining current encryption methods, a transition that cybersecurity experts have described as one of the most significant technology migrations in decades. Investors in the round said QNu's decade of groundwork, combined with existing deployments in sensitive sectors, positioned the company to move from proving its technology to scaling adoption globally.

The round adds to a string of recent funding activity in India's cybersecurity and deep-tech startup ecosystem, coming as global spending on quantum-safe migration is expected to accelerate over the next several years. For QNu Labs, the raise is expected to support both its domestic expansion and efforts to compete for cybersecurity contracts in international markets where quantum-safe standards are beginning to shape procurement decisions.
