Saudi Arabia's government issued its sharpest warning yet to Yemen's Houthi movement on Tuesday, with Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan declaring that the kingdom "will not hesitate" to defend itself after a wave of missile and drone strikes wounded at least 73 civilians, including women and children, across four southern cities.

## Attacks Hit Aramco Sites and an Air Base

The Saudi-led coalition supporting Yemen's internationally recognised government said the Iran-aligned Houthi militia struck civilian and economic sites in Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan and Najran, sparking fires at Saudi Aramco energy facilities and temporarily halting operations at several locations. The Houthis' military spokesperson described the assault as a "broad operation," claiming it involved dozens of ballistic missiles and drones aimed at Aramco infrastructure and King Khalid air base.

Saudi authorities said crews were still working to contain the resulting blazes and had not yet detailed the extent of the damage. The Houthis, in turn, said the strikes were retaliation for more than 120 Saudi-led air raids on Yemeni territory over the preceding three days, and warned of "harsher and wider" attacks if the campaign against them continues.

## Diplomatic Fallout and a Firm Warning From Moscow

Speaking at a joint press conference with Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov in Moscow, Prince Faisal accused the Houthis of pursuing "a series of provocations" that place their own narrow interests above those of the Yemeni people. He said Riyadh remains committed to diplomacy but "will not hesitate to protect our interests."

The attacks drew swift condemnation from across the Gulf and beyond. The UAE called the strikes a "flagrant violation" of Saudi sovereignty, while the Gulf Co-operation Council's secretary general said the assault revealed the group's "malicious intentions." Pakistan, which maintains a joint defence pact with Saudi Arabia, offered what Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif called "unwavering solidarity," pledging support for the kingdom's sovereignty and territorial integrity.

## Oil Markets Feel the Strain

The escalation pushed Brent crude prices toward the $100-a-barrel mark, compounding pressure on energy markets that have been on edge for months amid the wider confrontation between the United States, Israel and Iran. Analysts have noted that the Houthi-Saudi flare-up adds a fresh layer of risk to a region already grappling with disrupted shipping lanes and intermittent strikes near the Strait of Hormuz.

## A Deepening Humanitarian Toll

The renewed hostilities come against the backdrop of intensifying fighting inside Yemen itself. The International Committee of the Red Cross warned that the escalation risked a broader regional conflict with potentially catastrophic consequences for millions of Yemenis who already depend on humanitarian aid. Aid officials say roughly 18,500 people have been displaced from Taiz and Hodeidah since fighting flared last week, with families arriving at makeshift shelters "with nothing," according to the International Organization for Migration's mission chief in Yemen.

A fragile truce reached in 2022 had significantly reduced hostilities between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis, but tensions have climbed sharply in recent weeks, with Tuesday's attack following an earlier strike in early August that wounded 11 civilians in Najran province. With both sides now trading threats of further escalation, the coming days are likely to determine whether the truce can hold or whether the conflict widens further, with implications for global energy markets that extend well beyond the Arabian Peninsula.
