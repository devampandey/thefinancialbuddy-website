Garware Hi-Tech Films delivered the best quarter in its history and raised its full-year profitability guidance. Its shares still went sharply the wrong way on Thursday.

The stock opened about 2 per cent higher and touched an intraday high of Rs 6,886.65, roughly 3.4 per cent above Wednesday's close. The early optimism did not last. Selling built through the morning, and the shares fell as much as 13.9 per cent to Rs 5,750 on the NSE, a swing of more than Rs 1,100 per share from the day's high. The broader market offered no cushion: the Sensex was down around 500 points by midday.

## The numbers on paper

For the September quarter of FY27, Garware reported consolidated net profit of about Rs 129.4 crore, up 42 per cent from roughly Rs 91 crore a year earlier. Revenue rose 22 per cent to a record Rs 695.5 crore, and EBITDA margin came in at 27.2 per cent.

Management also raised its FY27 EBITDA margin guidance to 27 per cent, give or take two percentage points, and reaffirmed a full-year revenue target of more than Rs 2,500 crore. On the operating side, the company signed a memorandum of understanding with US specialty chemicals firm Lubrizol to develop its paint protection film (PPF) business, said a new TPU line is due for commissioning in the current quarter, and expects a Rs 191 crore solar control film line to begin commercial production in the first half of FY28.

## Why a strong quarter can still disappoint

There was no single negative disclosure in the results to explain the fall, which makes the stock's own history the more useful lens.

**Expectations were already high.** Garware's shares had gained roughly 90 per cent over the past year before Thursday's session. A stock priced for continued outperformance needs more than a good quarter; it needs a quarter that beats what the market had already assumed. Revenue growth of 22 per cent is strong, but merely reaffirming the Rs 2,500 crore full-year target, rather than raising it, may have looked conservative to investors hoping for an upgrade.

**The margin guidance raise is narrower than it looks.** Lifting the central guidance to 27 per cent is positive, but the quarter's 27.2 per cent margin already sits at that level. In effect, management is guiding to roughly the current run-rate rather than signalling further expansion, and the two-point band either side leaves room for a softer second half.

**Tariff tailwinds may be one-off.** Part of the improvement in the export business has come from lower US duties on imported films after the tariff was cut sharply earlier this year. Gains tied to a policy change tend to be valued less generously than organic demand, because investors cannot be sure they will persist.

**Market mood.** Small-cap stocks have been under steady pressure this month, with the Sensex trading close to its 52-week low. In that environment, investors sitting on large gains often use good news as an exit point, and Garware had already slipped about 16 per cent over the week before results.

## What to watch

The next markers are the commissioning of the TPU line in the December quarter and any detail on the Lubrizol tie-up's commercial terms. Both bear directly on whether PPF, the company's highest-value product line, can keep growing faster than the overall business. Until then, Thursday's move is a reminder that in a nervous market, a record quarter is not the same thing as a surprise.
