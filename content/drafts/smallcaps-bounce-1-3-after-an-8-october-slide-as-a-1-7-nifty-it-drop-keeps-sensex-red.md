Tuesday's opening hour on Dalal Street showed two very different markets. The headline indices dipped, with the Nifty 50 opening at 22,708.80, down 0.19%, and the Sensex at 73,484.38, down 0.16%. Below the surface, though, smaller stocks were rising. The Nifty Smallcap 100 gained about 1.3% in early trade, while the Nifty IT index fell about 1.7%, the worst sector on the board.

The Sensex dropped as much as 325 points to around 73,281 shortly after the open, and the Nifty touched 22,671. Both recovered part of that loss by mid-morning, with the Nifty trading back near 22,750 and the Sensex down only around 100 points.

## Why smallcaps are getting a breather

The bounce in smaller stocks comes after a rough month. The Nifty Smallcap 250 lost about 7.1% last week alone and roughly 8.5% across October. Much of that selling has been linked to leverage. Outstanding margin trading facility positions, where brokers lend investors money to buy shares, fell to about Rs 1.54 lakh crore on October 22 from around Rs 1.60 lakh crore in September. An estimated 46% of that book, or about Rs 72,000 crore, is tied to smallcap stocks. When prices fall, margin calls force sales, and those sales push prices down further.

A 1.3% rise in one morning does not mean that cycle is over. It does suggest that some of the forced selling may have run its course for now, and that bargain hunters are stepping in after Monday's session, when the Sensex and Nifty each closed about 0.7% higher at their intraday highs. The Nifty Midcap 100 was nearly flat, so the move was concentrated in the smallest and most beaten-down names.

## IT drags the benchmarks

Technology stocks were the main reason the headline indices stayed in the red. Infosys and TCS were among the biggest Sensex losers, along with Hindustan Unilever and Asian Paints. Infosys extended its slide after falling nearly 4% on Monday over a cut to its FY27 revenue growth guidance. Nifty Oil and Gas was the second-weakest sector, down about 0.8%, while FMCG and private banks also slipped.

On the other side, Bharat Electronics was the top Sensex gainer at the open, followed by Maruti Suzuki, Eternal, Power Grid and NTPC. BEL's move follows Monday's approval by the Defence Acquisition Council of about Rs 2.12 lakh crore of procurement proposals for the Army, Navy and Air Force, with roughly 82% of the value expected to go to Indian suppliers. Brokerage Antique named HAL, BEL, Solar Industries and Astra Microwave as its top defence picks after the approvals.

Tata Consumer Products rose about 4% after a September quarter that matched estimates, with investors focusing on its margin recovery.

## The flows and global backdrop

Institutional data from Monday showed the usual split. Foreign investors sold a net Rs 1,462 crore of Indian shares, while domestic institutions bought a net Rs 2,610 crore. Crude remains the main worry. Brent was near $110 a barrel early on Tuesday, a level that keeps pressure on India's import bill and the rupee, which was trading around 95.2 to the dollar.

Wall Street had closed higher overnight, led by the Nasdaq, and Japan's Nikkei rose more than 2% in early trade. That support was offset by caution ahead of interest rate decisions this week from the US Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan. HDFC Bank, the heaviest stock in the Nifty, is also under a new chief executive from today, with Anup Bagchi taking over from Sashidhar Jagdishan.

For investors, the takeaway from the first hour is that the index level is hiding the real story. A quiet day for the Nifty can still be a big day for smallcap portfolios, in either direction.
