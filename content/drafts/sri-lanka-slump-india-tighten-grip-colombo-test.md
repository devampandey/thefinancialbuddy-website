India continued to dominate the second Test against Sri Lanka on Tuesday, reducing the hosts to 137 for 5 during the third day's play at the Sinhalese Sports Club Ground in Colombo, a venue where turn has increasingly troubled the home batting lineup.

## A Commanding First-Innings Total

India's position of strength was built over the first two days of the match. Devdutt Padikkal struck his second consecutive Test century to anchor the innings on day one, with India closing that day at 300 for 5. On day two, the innings was carried further by wicketkeeper-batter Dhruv Jurel, whose unbeaten century underpinned a productive stand with Rishabh Pant. India eventually declared at 503 for 9, handing themselves a first-innings lead of close to 500 runs before play had even reached its halfway point.

Late on day two, India's bowlers struck twice in quick succession as fading light closed in, removing Sri Lankan openers cheaply and leaving the hosts 8 for 2 overnight.

## Spinners Take Control on Day Three

Resuming under pressure, Sri Lanka lost opener Kamil Mishara for 19 in the first session of day three, extending India's early breakthroughs from the previous evening. The hosts found some resistance through the middle order, with Pasindu Sooriyabandara playing a composed innings and Kamindu Mendis offering support, briefly steadying the innings and pushing the total past the three-figure mark.

That recovery proved short-lived. India's spin attack, led by left-arm option Manav Suthar alongside the experienced Ravindra Jadeja, exploited a surface that has increasingly assisted turn as the match has progressed. Suthar picked up his second wicket of the innings to trigger another slide, leaving Sri Lanka at 137 for 5 and still trailing India's first-innings total by a wide margin.

## Series Context

India travelled into this match already 1-0 up in the two-Test series after a comfortable win in the series opener, meaning a strong result in Colombo would secure an away series victory. With a lead of nearly 500 runs and Sri Lanka now five wickets down well short of even an innings-and-follow-on threshold, India are firmly in control of the contest with two full days of play still to come.

The turning pitch at the SSC has played a significant role in shaping the match, with both teams' spin options finding purchase as the surface has deteriorated through the middle days of the Test. Barring a substantial rearguard effort from Sri Lanka's remaining batters, India appear well placed to enforce further pressure and push for an outright result inside four days.

Play was continuing on day three at the time of writing, with Sri Lanka's tail still to negotiate a testing final session against India's spin-heavy attack.
