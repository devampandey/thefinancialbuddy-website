Fighting between Yemen's Houthi movement and Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces sharply escalated overnight, with Houthi fighters claiming to have shot down a Saudi F-15 fighter jet and reporting some 40 Saudi airstrikes across Yemen in the span of a single day, according to AFP wire reporting corroborated by multiple international outlets.

## Clashes near the Saudi border

Yemeni government forces were locked in active combat with Houthi fighters near Al-Labanat camp, close to the Saudi border, with footage showing armoured vehicles and troops pushing across desert terrain under machine-gun fire. Military sources told AFP that capturing the camp would open a path toward Al-Hazm, the capital of Al-Jawf governorate roughly 50 kilometres away. The fighting marks one of the most intense rounds of the renewed conflict since it flared up again in July, when Houthi forces launched a large-scale offensive against government positions after Yemen's civil war had been largely dormant since 2022.

## Escalating claims from both sides

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said Saudi warplanes carried out 40 airstrikes over the previous 24 hours targeting the provinces of Taiz, Marib and Hodeidah, and separately claimed that more than 450 Saudi strikes had been launched over the preceding week. The group also said it had shot down a Saudi fighter jet, releasing images through its media arm showing wreckage it identified as belonging to an F-15. In response, the Houthis said they had targeted a Saudi air base and a state oil facility in the Red Sea port city of Yanbu.

## Backdrop: the Mecca accusation

The latest flare-up follows a tense standoff after Saudi authorities accused the Houthis of directing a drone toward Mecca, Islam's holiest city, an incident Riyadh called a cowardly terrorist attack. The Houthis rejected the accusation as a fabricated claim, insisting their targets remain Saudi military and oil infrastructure well away from religious sites. Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry reaffirmed its right to defend the two holy mosques and pilgrims, while the Saudi-led coalition warned it would not hesitate to take deterrent measures against what it called the Terrorist Houthi Militia.

The accusation triggered a wave of condemnation from across the Muslim world, with the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation calling the alleged attack heinous and the Arab League describing it as a matter of extreme gravity. Egypt, Lebanon, Syria, Pakistan and Turkey also voiced concern.

## Wider regional stakes

The renewed fighting has drawn in the kingdom directly after the Houthis declared a maritime blockade on Saudi vessels in the Red Sea and seized control of Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline along with the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a critical route for Saudi oil exports. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi jointly called for free navigation through the waterway, which also feeds into the Suez Canal, a major revenue source for Egypt. Reports indicate the crown prince personally sought direct US military intervention from President Donald Trump but was turned down, though a US task force is reportedly assisting Saudi forces with intelligence-sharing.

The United Nations says the renewed conflict has displaced more than 100,000 people, adding new humanitarian strain to a war that had shown signs of easing in recent years. With both sides trading escalating claims and Red Sea shipping increasingly at risk, the coming days are likely to determine whether the fighting spreads further or de-escalates.
