Opposition MPs staged a pointed demonstration outside Parliament this week, turning donation boxes into props to symbolize what they allege was the theft of donations collected at the Ram Temple in Ayodhya. The protest, held near the Makar Dwar entrance of the Parliament complex, drew participation from a wide cross-section of opposition parties, signaling that the issue has moved beyond a single party's talking point into a broader coalition grievance.

## Who Joined the Protest

Congress general secretary Priyanka Gandhi Vadra was among the most visible faces at the demonstration, joined by Samajwadi Party leaders Dimple Yadav and Dharmendra Yadav, Trinamool Congress MP Mahua Moitra, Jharkhand Mukti Morcha's Mahua Maji, and RSP's N K Premchandran, among others. Several MPs placed cash into donation boxes set up at the protest site, a visual meant to dramatize the alleged misappropriation of temple funds and draw media attention to the demand for answers.

## Two Grievances, One Protest

The demonstration combined two distinct threads of opposition anger. The first centers on unresolved questions about alleged theft of donations at the Ram Temple, a matter that opposition leaders say the government has been slow to address publicly. The second concerns what protesting MPs describe as excessive police action against students who had separately taken to the streets. Congress general secretary (organisation) K C Venugopal sharpened the criticism, saying that as Parliament entered the third week of its session, the opposition was still waiting for Union Home Minister Amit Shah to respond directly on the alleged assault on peaceful student protesters.

## Parliament Under Strain

The protest adds to what has already been a turbulent session, with repeated adjournments and floor disruptions over unrelated matters, including a separate confrontation that led to the suspension of an opposition legislator in a state assembly. Taken together, these episodes point to a Parliament session where opposition parties are using every available platform, from floor protests to complex-wide demonstrations, to keep pressure on the treasury benches ahead of a politically significant stretch of the calendar.

## Why the Timing Matters

The protest also coincided with a closely watched meeting of the ruling National Democratic Alliance's parliamentary party, underscoring how contested this period has become. Political observers have noted that early August carries particular symbolic weight for the ruling party given past milestones associated with the date, which may partly explain why the opposition chose this window to escalate its messaging around temple donations and alleged police excesses.

## What Comes Next

As of now, the government has not issued a detailed, on-record response addressing the specific allegations raised by the protesting MPs. Whether the issue leads to a formal parliamentary debate, a government statement, or simply fades amid the session's other controversies will likely depend on how much sustained pressure the opposition can maintain in the coming days. For now, the episode reflects the broader adversarial tone that has characterized this session, with both sides trading accusations rather than engaging on substantive policy debate.
