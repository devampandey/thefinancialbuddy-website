Gaza City saw one of its most violent days in weeks on Tuesday after Israeli forces carried out a covert raid to detain a senior Hamas security official, followed by a separate series of strikes that killed several Palestinians, including children, according to multiple international outlets and Gaza health officials.

## What Happened

Reports from the region indicate that an Israeli special forces unit entered Gaza City in a bid to capture a man identified as the head of Hamas' internal security service. Once the operation was discovered, Israeli aircraft carried out strikes to cover the unit's withdrawal, killing at least three Palestinians, two of them children, according to medics cited by international wire services.

In a separate and apparently unconnected incident hours later, a heavier round of Israeli bombardment hit the al-Katiba area in western Gaza City, killing at least six civilians, including three children, and wounding several others, according to local health officials. The Israeli military separately said it had struck a Hamas commander in the Jabalia area of northern Gaza, alleging he had been planning attacks on Israeli troops and working to rebuild the group's military capabilities in violation of the ceasefire.

## Ceasefire Under Strain

The strikes come nearly eleven months after a US-brokered ceasefire took effect in October 2025, halting the large-scale fighting that had devastated the Gaza Strip. That truce has held in broad terms but has not stopped near-daily Israeli strikes, which Israel says target militants who continue plotting attacks or rebuilding weapons capacity in violation of the agreement's terms.

According to Gaza's Health Ministry, more than 1,300 people have been killed and over 4,000 wounded in Gaza since the ceasefire formally began on October 11, 2025 — a toll that has fuelled growing criticism from rights groups and mediating governments about how durable the arrangement really is.

## Why It Matters

Tuesday's events add to a pattern that has repeated through the second half of 2026: periods of relative calm in Gaza punctuated by targeted Israeli operations that Palestinian officials describe as ceasefire violations and Israel frames as necessary counterterrorism action. Each episode complicates efforts by US, Qatari and Egyptian mediators to move the fragile truce toward a more durable political settlement, and keeps alive investor and market concerns about wider instability in the region, which has already been a factor behind elevated crude oil prices this year.

For India and other energy-importing economies, continued volatility in the Middle East remains a variable to watch, given its bearing on crude prices, shipping costs through regional chokepoints, and broader risk sentiment in global markets. Diplomats from the ceasefire's guarantor countries are expected to continue pressing both sides to avoid escalation that could unravel nearly a year of fragile de-escalation.

No official Israeli government statement beyond the military's operational account had been issued at the time of writing, and Hamas had not released a formal response to Tuesday's incidents.
