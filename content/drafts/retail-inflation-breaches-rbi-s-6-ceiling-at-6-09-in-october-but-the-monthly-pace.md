India's retail inflation rose to 6.09% in October, up from 5.54% in September, according to data the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation released on Thursday evening. That puts consumer price growth above the 6% upper limit of the Reserve Bank of India's tolerance band for the first time under the new CPI series. The reading was also a little higher than the 5.93% economists polled by Reuters had expected. It lands three weeks before the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee meets on December 2-4, and it makes a second straight rate hike look very likely.

A month ago we reported that [September inflation had reached a 23-month high of 5.54%](/business/retail-inflation-hits-23-month-high-of-5-54-as-onion-prices-more-than-double). October's number is not just higher. It shows where the pressure is coming from and gives a first hint that it may be close to peaking.

## Food is still doing most of the work

The consumer food price index rose 8.94% from a year earlier, up from 7.74% in September. The wider food and beverages group, which makes up 36.75% of the CPI basket, rose 8.51% against 7.36% the month before. Onions remain the clearest example. Their prices were 116% higher than in October 2025, after rising 108% in September. Garlic and ginger have also become much more expensive.

The pressure is not confined to food, though. ICRA chief economist Aditi Nayar noted that inflation picked up in 11 of the 12 CPI divisions. That suggests price pressure is spreading beyond the kitchen, with expensive crude oil since the West Asia conflict a likely contributor through transport and services costs.

## Rural households are hit harder

Rural inflation rose to 6.63% from 6.08%, while urban inflation rose to 5.41% from 4.88%. The gap between the two has widened almost every month this year. In January, rural inflation was 2.73%, slightly below urban inflation at 2.75%. It has been higher in every month since February.

This matters because food takes up a bigger share of a rural family's budget. When vegetable and milk prices go up, rural households have fewer ways to cut back. A sustained rise also tends to shape what people expect prices to do next, which is exactly what a central bank wants to avoid.

## The month-on-month numbers tell a different story

The headline figure hides an important detail. Prices rose 0.51% between September and October, compared with 0.69% between August and September. That is the fourth straight month in which the monthly increase has slowed, down from a peak of 1.03% in June.

So why is the annual rate still going up? The main reason is the base. In October 2025 the CPI index barely moved from September. Even a smaller price increase this October therefore pushes the year-on-year comparison higher. Put simply, prices are still rising, just less quickly than they were in the middle of the year. The 6.09% figure partly reflects how calm prices were a year ago.

## The RBI is already tightening

The RBI raised its repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on October 7, its first increase since February 2023. It also changed its stance from neutral to calibrated tightening ([our report on that decision](/business/rbi-raises-repo-rate-to-5-5-in-first-hike-since-2023-shifts-to-calibrated-tighten)). In its own projections, the central bank expected inflation to average 6.0% in the October-December quarter. October's reading is close to that path, not far above it.

Under India's inflation-targeting law, one month above 6% is not a formal failure. The RBI misses its mandate only if average inflation stays above 6% for three consecutive quarters. Even so, a print outside the band weakens the case for waiting.

## What this means

For borrowers, the message is simple: plan for higher EMIs. ICRA's Nayar said a further 25 basis point hike in December looks almost certain, and she expects CPI inflation to stay above 6% for the next couple of months. Last month SBI Chairman CS Setty went further and suggested a 50 basis point move was possible. Anyone with a floating-rate home or car loan linked to the repo rate should expect their rate to reset upward within a quarter of any December hike. Our [EMI calculator](/tools/emi-calculator) can show what an extra 0.25 to 0.5 percentage point would mean for your monthly payment.

For savers, higher policy rates usually lead to better fixed deposit rates, but with a lag. With inflation at about 6%, many deposits currently earn little or nothing after inflation and tax.

In our view, the slowing monthly numbers make a 25 basis point hike more likely than 50. The RBI does not need to shock the system if price momentum is already cooling. But we see three reasons the risks still lean towards more tightening:

- **Food supply.** Nayar flagged a weak monsoon and poorly refilled reservoirs as risks to the rabi crop. She also expects food and beverages inflation to stay above 8% in November, with milk, sugar and onions all pushing upward. Mother Dairy raised milk prices by up to Rs 4 a litre this week.
- **Crude oil.** Oil prices have eased in recent sessions. Nayar said their path will decide how much more tightening is needed in 2027. A renewed flare-up in West Asia would quickly undo that relief.
- **Growth gives the RBI room.** GDP growth is expected to stay above 7% in the September quarter. With the economy that strong, the RBI has less reason to hold back on rate hikes.

Several things are still unclear. We do not yet know how much of the food spike is a one-season supply problem and how much is persistent. We also do not know how quickly higher rates will slow consumer credit. The next signals to watch are November's wholesale price data, the RBI's December projections, and whether the monthly CPI increase keeps slowing. If November's monthly gain falls below October's 0.51%, the case for a pause after December gets much stronger.
