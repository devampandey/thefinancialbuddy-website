The Nifty 50 lost its grip on the 23,000 mark within minutes of the opening bell on Monday, a level that chart watchers had flagged all weekend as the line the bulls needed to hold. By mid-morning the index had fallen as much as 284 points, or 1.22 per cent, to 22,856, while the BSE Sensex dropped as much as 900 points to 72,995.

The trigger this time was not a domestic data miss or a disappointing earnings print. It came from two numbers far from Dalal Street: Brent crude at roughly $106 a barrel and the US 10-year Treasury yield at 5.2 per cent.

## Why a bond market in New York matters in Mumbai

Brent futures climbed about 2.2 per cent to $106.6 a barrel on Monday, while US WTI rose to $93.76, after President Donald Trump rejected Iran's latest offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days. Tehran has said it will stick to its conditions, leaving oil traders pricing in a longer disruption.

Higher oil feeds straight into inflation expectations, and that is pushing global bond yields higher. The rate-sensitive US two-year yield rose five basis points to 4.90 per cent, and the 10-year added four basis points to 5.20 per cent. When a US government bond pays more than 5 per cent with little risk, the case for foreign funds to hold volatile emerging-market equities such as India's weakens.

That is already showing up in the flow data. Foreign portfolio investors, who were net buyers in July and August, have sold Indian shares worth Rs 17,131 crore so far in September, taking their year-to-date selling to about Rs 2.41 lakh crore, according to depository data. Domestic institutions continue to absorb much of that supply, but not enough to stop the slide.

## A broad sell-off, with one lonely gainer

Every sectoral index opened in the red. Private banks were the weakest pocket, and the Nifty Midcap and Smallcap indices each fell about 1 per cent, showing that the selling went beyond index heavyweights.

On the Sensex, Kotak Mahindra Bank, Bajaj Finance, HDFC Bank, BEL, Hindustan Unilever and Bajaj Finserv each fell more than 1 per cent in early trade. TCS was briefly the only gainer in the 30-share pack. Among Nifty stocks, Hindalco and Max Healthcare were among the biggest early losers.

The rupee opened 4 paise weaker at 95.86 against the US dollar, compared with Friday's close of 95.82. For the world's third-largest crude importer, costlier oil and a softer currency are a painful combination, since they widen the import bill and the current account deficit together.

## The levels traders are watching

The fall extends a rough stretch for Indian equities. Last week the Nifty logged its longest run of weekly declines since the Covid-19 crash in 2020.

Technical analysts had described the setup going into Monday as extremely weak, with 23,000 as the key support. A decisive close below it, they warned, could open the way to the 22,800-22,700 zone in the coming weeks. On the upside, the gap left between roughly 23,280 and 23,350 is expected to cap any bounce in the near term.

Volatility could rise further on Tuesday, when the monthly Nifty and Bank Nifty derivatives contracts expire. Market strategists say that as long as Brent stays above $100 and US yields hold above 5 per cent, the pressure on Indian stocks is unlikely to ease quickly. The next real test will be whether Washington and Tehran resume talks this week, as Trump has suggested they might.
