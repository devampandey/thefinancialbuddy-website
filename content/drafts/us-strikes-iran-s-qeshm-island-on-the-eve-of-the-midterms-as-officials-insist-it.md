The United States carried out strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, a strategically placed island in the Strait of Hormuz, on Monday, according to US officials. The attack was first reported by the Wall Street Journal and later confirmed by officials speaking to several news organisations. It came only a day before Americans vote in the midterm elections and hours after President Donald Trump suggested he was not planning new attacks on Iran.

Officials were careful to frame the action narrowly. One described the strikes as limited and said they did not mark a resumption of major combat operations. Another called them defensive measures taken after Iranian forces fired surface-to-air missiles at US assets. Some reports said the trigger was a missile launched from the island toward an American aircraft. The Pentagon referred questions to US Central Command, which did not immediately comment.

Iranian state media had earlier reported several explosions on the island. The Revolutionary Guards claimed to have shot down two US drones south of Qeshm, a claim that has not been independently verified.

## A quiet campaign comes into view

The more significant detail may be the context. According to the Journal's reporting, Monday's attack was the latest in a series of smaller US strikes carried out over recent weeks and deliberately kept low-profile, so as not to signal an intention to restart the wider military campaign against Iran. The two countries have been in conflict since late February, though large-scale fighting has largely paused for months.

That suggests Washington is trying to do two things at once: keep Iran from threatening ships in the Strait of Hormuz while keeping the door open to diplomacy. Shipping through the strait, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil, has been repeatedly disrupted this year, and protecting commercial traffic has become a central US aim.

## Mixed signals from Washington

The timing sits awkwardly with Trump's own comments. Asked on Monday whether he was considering striking Iran after the midterms, he said no, and told reporters that the US had already won the war with Iran. He also said an agreement with Tehran could happen. Only weeks ago he had hinted at renewed attacks after the vote, as [we reported in September](/world/trump-rejects-irans-seven-day-ceasefire-proposal-eyes-strikes-after-midterms). Qatari mediators have also been shuttling to Tehran with proposals to end the conflict.

For Iran, the strikes complicate any argument that talks are worth pursuing while American munitions are still falling on its territory, even in limited numbers. How Tehran responds, whether through its own limited action in the Gulf or through the negotiating channel, will shape the next phase.

## Why markets noticed

Oil had fallen about 7% on Monday as hopes of a diplomatic breakthrough grew. Reports of the Qeshm strikes reversed a small part of that in early Asian trade, with Brent crude futures rising about 0.5% to around $105 a barrel and US crude near $97. The muted move shows that traders, for now, are taking officials at their word that this is a contained action rather than the start of a new round of war. Any sign that Iran plans to retaliate against shipping would test that view quickly, with direct consequences for fuel-importing economies such as India.
