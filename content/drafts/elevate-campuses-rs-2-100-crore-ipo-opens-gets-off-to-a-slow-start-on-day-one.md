Elevate Campuses, an education-infrastructure platform backed by Hillhouse Investment, opened its Rs 2,100-crore initial public offering on Wednesday, but the issue got off to a muted start, subscribed just 0.21 times by the end of the first day of bidding.

## Day One Numbers

According to exchange data, the retail portion of the issue was subscribed 0.14 times, qualified institutional buyers booked 0.19 times their allotted quota, and non-institutional investors led demand relatively at 0.32 times. The book-built issue comprises a fresh issue of over 5.8 crore equity shares, with the price band fixed at Rs 343 to Rs 362 per share and a lot size of 41 shares. The three-day issue closes on September 25, with listing on both the BSE and NSE expected on September 30.

## What the Company Does

Elevate Campuses owns, operates and manages on-campus student accommodation for higher education institutions, alongside a portfolio of K-12 school assets, with operations spanning both India and the United Arab Emirates. The business sits at the intersection of real estate and education services, a category that has drawn growing investor interest as India's higher-education enrolment and demand for organised student housing have expanded in recent years.

## Why the Slow Start

A sub-1x subscription figure on day one is not unusual for mainboard issues, particularly those in newer or less-understood business categories like education infrastructure, where investors often wait for anchor and institutional demand signals before committing retail money in the final days of bidding. The muted opening also comes at a time when investor bidding capital has been stretched thin by a wave of large IPOs hitting the market in the same week, with capital getting split across multiple simultaneous offers rather than concentrated in any single issue.

## Why It Matters

How Elevate Campuses' book fills over its remaining two days of bidding will be watched as a signal of investor appetite for niche, asset-backed business models beyond the technology and consumer-facing names that have dominated recent IPO activity. A stronger finish in the final day of bidding, common for many Indian IPOs as institutional and retail investors act closer to the deadline, would suggest confidence in the underlying real estate and education infrastructure story; a continued weak book would raise questions about how the issue is priced relative to investor appetite for the sector.
