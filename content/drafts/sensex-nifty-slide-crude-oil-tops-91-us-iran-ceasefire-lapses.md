Indian equity benchmarks ended lower on Tuesday, dragged down by a fresh spike in crude oil prices after the ceasefire between the United States and Iran lapsed without an extension. The BSE Sensex settled around 77,377, down roughly 351 points or 0.45 percent from Monday's close of 77,728, while the NSE Nifty 50 slipped below the 24,250 mark after opening near 24,224, itself about 64 points lower than the previous session.

The sell-off traced back to developments in West Asia. The 60-day ceasefire window between Washington and Tehran expired with no fresh agreement in place, and reports suggested Iran could adopt a more offensive posture going forward. Prospects of a full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical corridor for global energy shipments, weakened further as a result. Brent crude climbed past $91 a barrel, touching levels last seen in late July, while US benchmark WTI crude moved above $85. For India, which imports the bulk of its crude requirements, sustained strength in oil prices raises concerns about the import bill, inflation trajectory and corporate margins, particularly for energy-intensive and transport-linked sectors.

Beyond the oil-driven anxiety, a mix of other factors compounded the pressure on domestic equities. Foreign institutional investors continued to sell into the Indian market, adding to the cautious mood. The rupee weakened against the dollar, and a rise in global bond yields further dented risk appetite among investors. IT stocks were among the prominent laggards through the session as global growth worries weighed on the export-oriented sector, while stocks such as Colgate-Palmolive and Groww also featured among names pressuring the midcap index.

Market breadth told a mixed story through the day. In early trade, advancing stocks on the NSE outnumbered decliners, with more than 1,400 stocks in the green against about 1,150 in the red, suggesting broader participation even as headline indices struggled. However, within the Nifty 50 basket itself, breadth was distinctly negative, with far more constituents declining than advancing, underlining that the drag was concentrated in heavyweight names tied to global cues and crude sensitivity.

Even amid the broader weakness, corporate and primary market activity kept investors occupied. Attention remained on the eagerly awaited initial public offering of the National Stock Exchange, which is being watched closely as one of the most significant listings in the Indian market's recent history. ICICI Bank's dollar bond issuance also drew interest from investors tracking corporate fundraising activity overseas. On the IPO front, subscription figures for the Lalithaa Jewellery Mart public issue were closely tracked, while infrastructure company Ceigall India's fresh order wins offered a pocket of positive news for sector-specific investors.

Analysts tracking the session pointed to the combination of geopolitical uncertainty and energy price volatility as the dominant theme, cautioning that markets are likely to stay choppy until there is more clarity on whether Washington and Tehran can arrive at a fresh arrangement, or whether tensions around the Strait of Hormuz escalate further. Until then, crude oil price movements are expected to remain the key swing factor for domestic equity sentiment in the sessions ahead.
