India's men's hockey team turned their second Pool A fixture at the Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya into a goal-scoring exhibition on Tuesday, dismantling Sri Lanka 16-1 in a contest that stopped being competitive inside the first quarter.

Mandeep Singh got things moving in the fifth minute. Jugraj Singh doubled the lead five minutes later, and from there the scoreline simply accumulated. Sri Lanka's only reply came through Madushan Herath in the 34th minute, by which point the result had long been settled.

## The Scoresheet

Jugraj finished as the leading scorer with four goals. Abhishek and Dilpreet Singh each completed hat-tricks. Captain Harmanpreet Singh added a brace, keeping his drag-flick rhythm ticking over without needing to take on a heavy workload. Manpreet Singh, Mandeep Singh, Vivek Sagar Prasad and Sukhjeet Singh contributed one apiece.

Eight different names on the scoresheet is the detail worth noting. Lopsided pool matches against lower-ranked opposition tend to produce inflated numbers for one or two forwards. This one spread the goals across the squad, including from midfield, which is a more useful indicator of how the side is functioning than the final margin is.

## What a 16-1 Win Actually Tells You

Not a great deal about India's medal prospects, in honesty. Sri Lanka are not among the continent's serious hockey nations, and the gap in this fixture was always going to be wide. The tournament's real tests lie ahead, against the teams that have historically contested Asian Games gold with India.

What the match did offer was a low-risk environment to rotate personnel, give penalty-corner specialists reps without burning them out, and bank a substantial goal difference. In a pool format where goal difference can decide a semifinal pairing, sixteen goals in one afternoon is not a trivial asset.

Harmanpreet's restrained outing is arguably the most encouraging part of it. A captain who takes two chances in a sixteen-goal rout is a captain being managed carefully for the knockout stage.

## A Strong Day for India in Aichi-Nagoya

The hockey rout capped a productive Day 3 for the Indian contingent. India's medal tally reached seven, comprising one gold, four silver and two bronze, with the gold arriving earlier in the day from the women's cricket team.

Elsewhere on Tuesday, India's men's badminton team assured themselves of a medal by beating hosts Japan 3-0 in the quarterfinals, with Lakshya Sen, the pairing of Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty, and Ayush Shetty all winning their rubbers. In wushu, Naorem Roshibina Devi beat Macau's Chu Man Sin 5-0 in the women's 60kg sanda quarterfinal to guarantee herself a third consecutive Asian Games medal.

The men's hockey side now turns to the sterner part of its pool campaign, where the margins will be considerably narrower and the value of Tuesday's goal difference may yet become apparent.
