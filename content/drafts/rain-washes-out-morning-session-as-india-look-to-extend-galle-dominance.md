Heavy showers over the Galle International Stadium washed out the opening session of Day 2 in the first Test between India and Sri Lanka on Sunday, denying fans a ball of action through the morning as India looked to press home the advantage built on the opening day.

## Rain has the final say through the morning

Play had been scheduled to begin 15 minutes early, at 9:45 am local time, to help make up for overs lost to weather on the first day. Instead, persistent rain kept the entire square under covers from the early hours. The Board of Control for Cricket in India confirmed on social media that the start had been pushed back and asked fans to stay tuned for updates. A brief lull around mid-morning allowed the ground staff, more than a hundred strong, to begin peeling back the covers and attacking the wet patches, but a fresh band of showers rolled in soon after and ended any hope of an early resumption. With the forecast for the rest of the day offering little comfort, the crew faced a race against time to dry the outfield and get the surface playable before further overs were lost.

## India's platform from Day 1

The washout leaves India exactly where they finished Saturday's play: 288 for 2 in 73 overs, a position built almost entirely around a maiden Test hundred from Devdutt Padikkal. Batting at the crucial number three slot, the left-hander ended a two-decade-long wait, in terms of matches, for a century from that position, finishing the day unbeaten on 131 off 178 balls in an innings built on patience as much as stroke-play. He found a willing partner in vice-captain KL Rahul, who played a composed 77 before cramps in his forearm forced him to retire hurt, leaving the pair to add a substantial stand for the second wicket. It was not entirely serene at the top, though: opener Yashasvi Jaiswal was run out for 32 in a mix-up that ended a 47-run opening partnership, and captain Shubman Gill fell for 16 soon after. Rishabh Pant was the man at the crease alongside Padikkal when the umpires finally called a halt for the day, unbeaten on 27.

## Why this Test carries extra weight

Beyond the scoreline, the match holds historical significance on both sides. For India, it marks the 600th Test in the side's history, a landmark that added extra context to Gill's decision to bat first after winning the toss. For Galle as a venue, the match is the 50th Test staged at the picturesque coastal ground, one of world cricket's most storied settings. Sri Lanka's bowling attack, spearheaded by left-arm spinner Prabath Jayasuriya, managed to extract occasional turn and bounce from the surface but could not sustain the pressure needed to break through consistently on the opening day.

## What happens when play resumes

Once conditions allow, the second new ball will be due within a handful of overs, handing Sri Lanka a fresh chance to disrupt India's control. Until then, both sets of support staff, and supporters, are left watching the skies over Galle, with the tourists eyeing a total large enough to put the hosts under serious pressure once the ball finally starts moving again in this rain-hit contest.
