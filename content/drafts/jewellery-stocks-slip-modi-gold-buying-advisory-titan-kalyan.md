Shares of India's listed jewellery companies came under selling pressure in early trade on Tuesday after Prime Minister Narendra Modi renewed his call for citizens to avoid buying gold unless absolutely necessary, a message that rattled sentiment across the sector even as broader markets navigated a mixed session.

## What Modi Said

Posting on social media, Modi reiterated that gold purchases should be limited to essential needs, linking the appeal to his broader push for domestic self-reliance and reduced dependence on imported commodities. He connected the message to India's aspirations of positioning itself as a developed economy by the time of its 100th year of independence. This marks the second time this year Modi has made such an appeal, having made a similar request in May urging citizens to pause discretionary gold buying.

## Immediate Market Reaction

The comments triggered swift selling in jewellery counters. Titan Company, which runs India's largest organised jewellery retail chain, slipped nearly 2% in early trade. Kalyan Jewellers India saw a sharper decline, falling as much as 5.8% intraday, while Thangamayil Jewellery and newly listed BlueStone Jewellery and Lifestyle also traded lower, each shedding more than 1.5%.

The reaction reflects how sensitive listed jewellery retailers remain to any signal — official or otherwise — that could dent discretionary gold demand, which forms the core of their revenue base. India remains one of the world's largest gold consumers, with the metal holding deep cultural significance around weddings and festivals, making any government messaging around consumption patterns closely watched by investors in the space.

## Gold Prices Also Under Pressure

The advisory coincided with a dip in international gold prices, with spot gold slipping below the $4,400-per-ounce mark, down more than 1% on the day. While the fall in gold prices is being driven by a mix of global factors, including shifting expectations around interest rates, the domestic policy messaging added an extra layer of pressure on sentiment for India-linked gold and jewellery stocks specifically.

## The Bigger Picture

Gold and jewellery remain a meaningful line item in India's import bill, and repeated government messaging around restrained consumption is widely read by analysts as part of a broader effort to manage the current account and encourage savings to flow into more productive domestic assets. For jewellery retailers, however, the near-term effect has typically been short bursts of stock price volatility rather than a lasting shift in consumer behaviour, given gold's entrenched role in Indian households.

Investors in the sector will now watch upcoming festive and wedding-season demand — traditionally the strongest period for gold and jewellery sales in India — to gauge whether the Prime Minister's renewed appeal translates into any meaningful dent in consumption, or whether the sector's underlying demand drivers prove more resilient than the day's stock price moves suggest.
