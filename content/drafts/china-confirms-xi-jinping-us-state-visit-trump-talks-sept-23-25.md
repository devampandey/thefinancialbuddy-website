Beijing confirmed on Monday that President Xi Jinping will travel to the United States this week for a state visit and talks with President Donald Trump, marking Xi's first trip to Washington in 11 years and his second face-to-face meeting with Trump this year.

Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said Xi would be in the US from September 23 to 25, at Trump's invitation, and that the two leaders would hold an in-depth exchange of views on "major issues concerning China-US relations and world peace and development." Guo added that Beijing wants to use the visit to strengthen dialogue and cooperation with Washington while "properly managing differences" between the two countries.

## First Washington Trip Since 2015

The visit will be Xi's first state trip to the American capital since September 2015, during the Obama administration, and comes just months after Trump travelled to Beijing in May for his own state visit. According to a US official, Trump will personally welcome Xi at Joint Base Andrews on Wednesday, with formal talks between the two leaders following on Thursday.

Speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One over the weekend, Trump said he expected to discuss "almost everything" with Xi during the visit, underscoring the breadth of issues on the table between the world's two largest economies.

## Trade Truce Under Strain

The meeting comes as both governments try to shore up a fragile tariff truce that has held for nearly a year without translating into a lasting trade agreement. Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng, Beijing's top trade negotiator, was already in Washington ahead of Xi's visit for meetings with US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, with the two sides reportedly discussing the creation of a dedicated communication channel to manage concerns around artificial intelligence.

Taiwan remains another point of friction that is likely to feature prominently in the talks, alongside broader technology restrictions and the threat of renewed tariff escalation. Trump's ongoing standoff with Iran also looms over the relationship, with Washington pressing Beijing to curb any dealings that could help Tehran evade sanctions, following reports that China had supplied Iran with intelligence used in a strike on US troops earlier this year.

## Backdrop Of A Packed Diplomatic Week

Xi's visit lands in the middle of a particularly heavy week on the world stage, with the United Nations General Assembly's high-level session getting underway in New York and roughly 130 heads of state and government expected to attend. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky is also set to meet Trump in New York this week to discuss the war with Russia, while Washington separately weighs new sanctions against the International Criminal Court.

For India and other economies closely tied to both Washington and Beijing, the outcome of the Trump-Xi talks will be watched for signs of whether the current trade détente holds or gives way to fresh disruption, particularly given the knock-on effects tariff swings have had on global supply chains and commodity markets in recent years.
