Shares of Tempsens Instruments (India) drew a rush of investor demand on the opening day of its initial public offering on Thursday, with the ₹650 crore issue getting fully subscribed within an hour of bidding starting and closing the day subscribed 5.30 times overall.

## How Day One Played Out

According to NSE data, the issue had already crossed full subscription by around 11 am, with bids received for over 2.1 crore shares against roughly 1.52 crore shares on offer, translating to about 1.38 times subscription within the first couple of hours. Demand continued to build steadily through the trading day, and by 4 pm the issue stood subscribed 5.30 times across investor categories. The offering opened for bidding on August 20 and will remain open through August 24, with the price band for the issue fixed between ₹285 and ₹300 per share. The minimum lot size for retail investors is 50 shares, working out to roughly ₹15,000 at the upper end of the price band. Allotment is expected around August 25, with listing on the BSE and NSE tentatively scheduled for August 28.

## What Tempsens Instruments Does

Incorporated in 1990, Tempsens Instruments describes itself as a leading player in thermal engineering and specialised cable manufacturing, designing and producing custom temperature sensors, industrial heating solutions and engineered cables used across process industries such as steel, cement, power and petrochemicals. The company has positioned itself around a roughly 10.5 percent domestic market share and what analysts describe as a high-barrier niche business, given the technical specifications and long qualification cycles typically required by industrial clients before they switch suppliers. That combination of niche positioning and a domestic manufacturing base appears to have been a factor behind the early wave of institutional and retail interest.

## Why The Demand

Brokerages tracking the issue have broadly favoured a subscribe rating, pointing to the company's established market position in a specialised segment with relatively few large organised competitors, alongside steady demand from India's ongoing industrial capital expenditure cycle. The grey market premium on the issue had also been climbing in the days before listing, which market watchers often treat as an informal signal of the kind of listing-day pop investors are pricing in, though such unofficial indicators can move quickly and are not a guaranteed predictor of actual listing performance.

## What Investors Are Watching Next

With the issue already several times oversubscribed on day one, attention now shifts to how demand holds up through the remaining bidding days and into the retail and institutional categories individually, since strong early anchor and qualified institutional buyer interest often sets the tone for the rest of the book-build. Investors who missed out on Thursday's allocation will be watching the subscription figures over the following days, while those who did apply now wait for the August 25 allotment date to find out whether they have secured shares ahead of the planned listing.
