Shares of L&T Technology Services (LTTS) were among the biggest movers on Dalal Street on Wednesday morning, rising between 10% and 12% in early trade, with one report putting the high at about Rs 4,015. It was the market's first chance to react to the engineering services company's September-quarter numbers, since Indian exchanges were shut on Tuesday for Dussehra.

The rally came alongside a broader move in technology stocks, with the Nifty IT index up around 2%. But LTTS's jump was several times bigger than the sector's, which says the market saw something specific in the results.

## What Beat Expectations

Revenue for the quarter ended September 30 rose 12.1% from a year earlier to Rs 3,040.4 crore. More importantly for analysts, sequential growth in constant currency came in at 2.7%, ahead of Motilal Oswal's estimate of 2.2%. In a services sector where many peers are reporting low single-digit growth, that gap is meaningful.

Profitability also improved. EBIT margin expanded to 16.3%, slightly above the mid-16% level the company has been targeting. Large deal wins during the quarter totalled about $203 million, and the company said its deal pipeline was more than 25% larger than a year ago.

## Read the Profit Number Carefully

The headline profit figure deserves a closer look. Net profit was Rs 548.2 crore, up about 67% from Rs 328.7 crore a year earlier. That jump, however, includes a one-time gain of roughly Rs 165 crore from selling the company's Smart World and Communication business.

Strip that out and profit from continuing operations was Rs 379.6 crore, up 19.7% from Rs 317.2 crore. That is still a solid result, and arguably the more useful number for anyone valuing the business, because a divestment gain will not repeat next quarter. Investors who anchor on the 67% figure risk overestimating the company's underlying earnings power.

## Why the Market Cares About Engineering Services

LTTS sells engineering and research-and-development services, designing software for vehicles, medical devices, industrial plants and telecom equipment, rather than running back-office IT for banks or retailers. That niche has held up better than traditional IT outsourcing, where clients have cut discretionary spending. Demand for software-defined vehicles and factory automation has stayed relatively firm, while large IT firms have been struggling to grow at all.

The sale of the Smart World and Communication unit also sharpens that focus, leaving the company more concentrated in the segments where it has been winning work.

## Brokerages Are Not Fully Convinced

Analyst reaction was positive but cautious. JPMorgan and Nomura raised their price targets but kept neutral ratings, suggesting they see the quarter as encouraging rather than a turning point. After a double-digit single-day move, the stock's valuation now leaves less room for error if the second half of the year disappoints.

## Dividend and Next Steps

The board declared an interim dividend of Rs 20 per share, with October 27 set as the record date. Shareholders who hold the stock on that date will be eligible.

The next checkpoints are whether the deal pipeline converts into revenue over the December and March quarters, and whether margins can hold above 16% once the one-off support from the business sale is gone.
