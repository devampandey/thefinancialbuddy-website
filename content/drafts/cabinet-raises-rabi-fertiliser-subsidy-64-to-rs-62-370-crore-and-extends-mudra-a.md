The Union Cabinet chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi took two sets of decisions on Wednesday that point in the same direction: the government is choosing to absorb higher costs so that farmers and small borrowers do not have to. One is a sharp increase in fertiliser support for the winter crop season. The other renews the credit guarantee schemes behind collateral-free loans for micro businesses and first-time entrepreneurs.

## Fertiliser support jumps by Rs 24,418 crore

The Cabinet approved nutrient-based subsidy (NBS) rates for phosphatic and potassic (P&K) fertilisers for **rabi 2026-27**, with a tentative budget of **Rs 62,370.02 crore**. That is about Rs 24,417.73 crore, or roughly 64%, more than the Rs 37,952.29 crore set aside for rabi 2025-26. The rates apply from October 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027, which covers the sowing of wheat, mustard, gram and other winter crops.

The subsidy covers di-ammonium phosphate (DAP) and the NPKS complex fertilisers, 28 grades in all, that farmers buy at controlled prices. The NBS system has been in place since April 2010. Under it, the government pays companies a fixed amount per kilogram of nutrient, and the retail price stays affordable.

The government said the increase reflects recent trends in international prices of fertilisers and raw materials such as urea, DAP, muriate of potash and sulphur. Global fertiliser markets have been disrupted since the conflict involving Iran began in February, and the Strait of Hormuz has been a key route for both energy and fertiliser cargoes. India imports a large part of its DAP and almost all its potash, so when world prices rise, either the subsidy goes up or farm-gate prices do. The Cabinet has chosen the first. Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, briefing reporters, said domestic manufacturing would increase and farmers would not face any additional burden.

The scale of the jump stands out. The kharif 2026 subsidy approved in April was Rs 41,534 crore. The rabi figure is now about 50% higher than that, which shows how quickly input costs climbed over the summer and autumn.

## Mudra guarantees extended, with a lower fee

The Cabinet also extended the **Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU)** until March 31, 2031, with a corpus of **Rs 5,000 crore**. The fund guarantees loans under the Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana, so banks bear less risk when they lend to micro enterprises without collateral. The government expects it to support about Rs 12.6 lakh crore of Mudra loans between FY27 and FY31.

The annual guarantee fee that lenders pay drops from 1% to 0.75%, which should make banks more willing to lend. Coverage extends to Mudra loans of up to Rs 20 lakh, Jan Dhan overdrafts of up to Rs 10,000, and self-help group loans in the Rs 10-20 lakh range. Since the scheme began in April 2015, more than 60 crore Mudra loans worth about Rs 43.34 lakh crore have been sanctioned.

Alongside it, the **Stand-Up India** scheme was extended to March 2031 with a Rs 4,111 crore guarantee corpus. It offers loans of Rs 10 lakh to Rs 1 crore for new enterprises set up by women and Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe entrepreneurs, and aims to reach at least 2.5 lakh more beneficiaries.

## Why it matters

Both decisions lock in spending for the five-year cycle of the Sixteenth Finance Commission, which gives lenders and fertiliser makers some certainty. The fiscal cost is the open question. A subsidy bill that grows 64% in one season will put pressure on the government's deficit targets if global prices stay high. That makes the fertiliser outlay as much a bet on the Gulf crisis easing as it is a farm policy decision.
