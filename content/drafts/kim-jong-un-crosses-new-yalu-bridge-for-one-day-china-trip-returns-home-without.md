North Korean leader Kim Jong Un returned home on Saturday evening after an unannounced one-day visit to the Chinese border city of Dandong, his first trip to China in more than a year. China's state news agency Xinhua said he was seen off at the New Yalu River Bridge by Wang Huning, the fourth-ranked member of the Communist Party leadership, with no mention of any meeting with President Xi Jinping.

## A short trip across a long-delayed bridge

South Korea's Unification Ministry first flagged the crossing in the morning, saying Kim appeared to have entered Dandong over the New Yalu River Bridge. The four-lane, roughly 3 km crossing was completed in 2014 with Chinese money but sat unused for 12 years because North Korea had not built connecting roads or customs facilities. It finally opened to traffic around October 15.

Korean media reported that Kim travelled by private train to Sinuiju on the North Korean side, switched to a car to cross the bridge, and used the same route home. The bridge carries road traffic only, so it is meant to handle container trucks and heavy cargo rather than rail freight.

Neither Pyongyang nor Beijing announced the visit in advance. Japanese media reported traffic restrictions in parts of Dandong and hotel rooms being taken off sale, and Japanese television aired footage of what appeared to be Kim's motorcade.

## Who he met, and who he didn't

According to Xinhua, Kim laid flowers at Dandong's memorial to Chinese soldiers who fought in the Korean War and toured a local car factory. Wang passed on Xi's greetings, and both sides spoke of widening personnel exchanges, economic ties and strategic coordination. Foreign Minister Wang Yi also attended.

Accounts of the meetings differed during the day. A South Korean lawmaker on the parliament's intelligence committee said there were signs Kim had met Premier Li Qiang, but the Chinese readout did not mention Li.

Xi's absence has a simple explanation. He is in Beijing ahead of the party's Fifth Plenary Session, due to run from October 26 to 29, and meeting Kim would have meant either Xi travelling to the border or Kim extending his trip to the capital.

## Why the timing matters

The trip came on the eve of October 25, the date China marks as its entry into the Korean War in 1950, which may explain the choice of a war memorial for the main public event.

It also comes about a month before the APEC summit in Shenzhen. South Korea's spy agency has told lawmakers there is roughly a 50% chance that Kim meets US President Donald Trump on the sidelines. Analysts quoted by AFP suggested Kim may have wanted Beijing's support before any such meeting, or to push China to deliver faster on commitments made when Xi visited Pyongyang in June.

The visit also follows a period in which Kim leaned heavily towards Moscow, sending troops and weapons to support Russia's war in Ukraine. A day trip to a border city lets Pyongyang show it still values ties with its largest trading partner without the cost of a full state visit.

## What to watch

The bigger signal will be trade. Chinese analysts say the bridge removes a long-standing logistics bottleneck, which could lift cross-border cargo despite UN sanctions. Also worth watching is whether North Korean state media report the trip on Sunday, and how Seoul reads it. South Korea's military has just proposed talks with the North to clarify the border after a mine blast inside the DMZ, an offer Pyongyang has not yet answered.
