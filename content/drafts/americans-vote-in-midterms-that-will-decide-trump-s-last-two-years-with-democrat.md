Polling stations opened across the United States on Tuesday in midterm elections that will determine how much room Donald Trump has to govern for the rest of his second term. The first polls opened in Vermont at 5 am Eastern time, 3:30 pm in India, and by early evening in India in-person voting was under way in most states, including the battlegrounds of Georgia, Pennsylvania, Michigan and North Carolina.

## What is on the ballot

All 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 of the Senate's 100 seats are being contested, along with governorships in 36 states. Republicans currently control both chambers. Democrats need a net gain of three House seats and four Senate seats to take majorities.

Turnout before election day was already heavy. The University of Florida's Election Lab counted more than 57 million early ballots, putting the election on course to exceed participation in the last midterm in 2022. Ohio reported early voting above its own 2022 record.

## The state of the race

Public polling has pointed to a difficult night for the president's party. One widely followed polling aggregator gives Democrats a very high chance of winning the House and about an 80% chance of taking the Senate, though US polls have repeatedly underestimated Trump-aligned candidates in past cycles.

The Senate map is where the surprises may come. Democrats are competitive in several states Trump won comfortably in 2024, including Texas, where Democrat James Talarico has narrowly led Republican Ken Paxton in most recent polls, and Kansas, which has not elected a Democratic senator in almost a century. Republicans hope to flip seats in Georgia, where Jon Ossoff faces Mike Collins, as well as New Hampshire and Michigan. The Michigan contest was shaken on Sunday when Democratic Representative Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over her party's nominee, Abdul El-Sayed.

Voters have ranked the economy and healthcare as their top concerns, with the war with Iran close behind. Petrol prices near $5 a gallon in parts of the Midwest have become a liability for Republicans, and Trump told supporters on Monday that the president's party historically struggles in midterms while calling this vote the most important of his lifetime.

## Fights over the count

The run-up to voting was marked by legal battles over federal involvement in elections that states administer. The US Supreme Court on Monday rejected an emergency request from the Trump administration to sideline thousands of California ballots it said may have been cast by non-citizens, the latest in a string of losses for the effort. Further court challenges could stretch into the counting period.

The vote also comes a day after US forces carried out what officials called limited defensive strikes on Iranian targets around the Strait of Hormuz, while mediators continue to work on a framework that would reopen the waterway.

## When results arrive, and why India is watching

The first polls close at 6 pm Eastern time in parts of Indiana and Kentucky, which is 4:30 am Wednesday in India. Most states close an hour or two later, and Alaska is last at about 11:30 am Indian time. Close races could take days to call.

For Indian investors, the timing puts the outcome squarely inside Wednesday's trading session. A divided Congress would make it harder for the White House to pass spending and tax measures, while the result will also shape how much political cover Trump has for decisions on tariffs and on the Iran conflict that has kept crude oil above $100 a barrel for much of the year.
