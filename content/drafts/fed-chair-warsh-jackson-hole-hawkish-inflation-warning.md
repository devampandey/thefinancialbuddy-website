Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh used his first keynote address at the Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium on Friday to deliver a message investors were not fully prepared for: inflation is still running too high, and the central bank may need to raise interest rates rather than cut them further in the months ahead.

Speaking in Wyoming, Warsh said he was "impressed" by the underlying strength of the US economy but flagged that price pressures had not eased the way policymakers had hoped. He framed price stability as the Fed's predominant focus going forward, arguing that financial conditions are not currently restrictive enough to bring inflation convincingly back to target.

## A Different Tone Than Markets Expected

Going into the speech, traders had priced in a reasonable chance that Warsh, seen as more dovish than his predecessor at times, would use the platform to open the door to further easing. Instead, he set a high bar for the Fed to declare victory on inflation and avoided committing to a fixed reaction function, choosing instead to lay out his broader philosophy on central bank governance.

That ambiguity itself moved markets. Short-term Treasury yields rose as investors recalibrated the odds of a rate increase later in the year, with several analysts now pointing to a possible hike by October or December rather than the rate cuts some had been betting on earlier in the summer.

## Wall Street's Choppy Reaction

US equities whipsawed through the session as investors digested the remarks. The Dow Jones Industrial Average and S&P 500 initially dipped as the hawkish framing sank in, before paring losses and turning higher again later in the day as some investors concluded the Fed was simply preserving flexibility rather than signaling an imminent move. The Nasdaq Composite saw similar volatility, swinging between gains and losses.

Bond markets were more consistent in their reaction, with yields on shorter-dated Treasurys ticking up as traders raised the probability of tighter policy. Prediction markets tracking the odds of a September hike also moved higher following the speech, though most economists still see a hike, if it comes, as more likely in the final quarter of the year.

## Why It Matters Beyond the US

Jackson Hole speeches carry outsized weight because they often set the tone for global monetary policy expectations heading into the final stretch of the year. A more hawkish Fed typically strengthens the dollar, pressures emerging-market currencies and can dent risk appetite in equity markets worldwide, including in Asia, where investors were already navigating a mixed session ahead of the speech.

Analysts will now be watching upcoming inflation data and the Fed's September meeting closely for signs of whether Warsh's rhetoric translates into an actual policy shift, or whether it was primarily an exercise in keeping markets guessing at a pivotal moment for his young tenure as chair.
