On Tuesday morning the question for Indian equities was whether the Nifty could get through the 23,500 to 23,600 zone that had capped earlier bounces. [As we noted at the open](/markets/nifty-pushes-into-the-23-500-23-600-wall-as-india-shrugs-off-asia-s-ai-sell-off), the index walked straight into that band in the first half hour. By the closing bell it was not just through it but more than 200 points beyond it.

The Nifty 50 settled at 23,810.85, up 370.05 points or 1.58%. The Sensex finished at 76,917.85, up 1,225.07 points or 1.62%. That follows Monday's gain of 1,233 points, so the 30-share index has added roughly 2,450 points in two sessions. The combined market value of BSE-listed companies rose by about Rs 4.74 lakh crore on the day.

## Why the close matters more than the gain

A 1.6% day is large, but the more useful signal for traders is where the index finished. Resistance zones are only meaningful if the market respects them, and on Tuesday it did not. The Nifty spent the morning near 23,600 and then kept grinding higher through the afternoon instead of fading, which is the opposite of what happened on several failed rallies in October.

The earlier ceiling now becomes the first area to watch on any pullback. If the index can hold above roughly 23,600 in the coming sessions, the breakout looks genuine. A quick slide back below it would suggest Tuesday was another relief rally driven by short covering. Monday's close of 23,440.80 is the next reference point below that.

## Consumer stocks did the heavy lifting

The rally had a clear leader. The Nifty FMCG index jumped 3.65% to 45,580, its best showing among the sectors by a wide margin. Nestle India rose 4.47% and Tata Consumer Products gained 4.29%. Asian Paints climbed about 4%, helped by the same logic: cheaper crude lowers the cost of the petroleum-based raw materials that paint makers and packaged-goods companies buy.

Brent crude settled at $104.77 a barrel on Monday, down 7.6% on hopes of a US-Iran settlement that would reopen shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. For a country that imports most of its oil, a move of that size changes the inflation and margin outlook quickly, and investors rotated into the companies most exposed to that relief.

Realty rose 2.33% and private banks 1.98%, while auto and financial services each gained close to 2%. Ten of 13 major sector indices ended higher. BSE Ltd was the best Nifty performer, up about 5%, on the same day its larger rival NSE was due to report quarterly numbers after the close.

## Where the market said no

Not everything participated. The Nifty PSU Bank index fell 1.27%, dragged by State Bank of India, which slipped 1.70% to Rs 970.25 after reporting its September-quarter results during market hours. Power Grid was the weakest Nifty stock, down 3.04%, and ONGC, Adani Enterprises and Tata Steel also ended lower. Metal and energy indices finished slightly in the red, the natural losers when crude and commodity prices ease.

## The overnight risk

Provisional data showed foreign investors bought a net Rs 3,114.68 crore of Indian shares on Monday, a second day of buying, and the rupee firmed to about 94.74 per dollar. Whether that support continues may depend on events outside India. Americans are voting in midterm elections today, and results will arrive during Wednesday's Indian trading session, alongside any movement in the US-Iran talks. A market that has just cleared a technical hurdle will face its first test against those headlines.
