India's passenger vehicle makers reported one of their strongest months on record for October, with three of the four biggest companies posting all-time highs as festive-season buying ran ahead of Diwali. Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai Motor India and Mahindra & Mahindra each set new monthly records, while Tata Motors posted growth of more than 20%.

The numbers are wholesale dispatches from factories to dealers, not retail registrations, so they partly reflect companies stocking showrooms for the peak buying window. Even so, the scale of the increase across almost every major brand points to firm underlying demand.

## Maruti's best month ever

Maruti Suzuki's total dispatches rose 25% year-on-year to about 2.76 lakh units, the highest monthly figure in the company's history. Domestic passenger vehicle dispatches grew more than 20% to around 2.12 lakh units. Including the Super Carry light commercial vehicle and supplies to other manufacturers, domestic dispatches rose about 19% to 2.26 lakh units.

For the market leader, a 20%-plus domestic jump is significant because its sheer size usually keeps growth rates modest. The gap between its total and domestic numbers also suggests exports remained a meaningful contributor.

## Hyundai and Mahindra hit new highs

Hyundai Motor India recorded its highest monthly sales since it began operations in India, with total volumes up 17.7% to 82,299 units. Domestic sales rose 16.7% to a record 62,799 units, and exports climbed 21.1% to 19,500. The company is counting on its newly launched Bayon to keep that momentum going at home and in more than 50 export markets.

Mahindra & Mahindra sold 73,338 SUVs in the domestic market, its best month for utility vehicles, and total vehicle sales including commercial vehicles and three-wheelers rose 9% to 1,31,109 units. Exports surged 77% to 7,102 units. Its domestic SUV growth was slower than its peers in percentage terms, at about 2%, which shows the company was already running at a high base a year ago. Mahindra also pointed to early demand for its first CNG model, the XUV 3XO.

## Tata's EV push

Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles reported total sales of 75,426 units, up 23% from 61,295 a year earlier. Domestic sales grew 21% to 73,894, and international business jumped to 1,532 units from just 161. The standout number was electric vehicles: Tata sold 16,244 EVs, up about 75% from 9,286 a year ago, meaning more than one in five of its cars sold in October was electric.

Among smaller players, JSW MG Motor India's dispatches rose 19% to 8,013 units, helped by new launches including the Hector Tomahawk EV.

## Why it matters

The October surge comes despite a difficult backdrop. Fuel prices have been elevated because of the US-Iran conflict and pressure on crude supplies, and borrowing costs have been rising. Strong car demand in that environment suggests household spending on big-ticket items has held up through the festive period.

The figures also matter for auto stocks when markets reopen on Monday. Investors will now watch two things: whether retail registrations data confirms that these dispatches turned into actual sales, and whether demand holds after Diwali, when dealer inventory built up in October needs to be cleared.
