State Bank of India reported a standalone net profit of Rs 23,038 crore for the September quarter on Tuesday, up 14.3% from Rs 20,160 crore a year earlier. Consolidated profit, which includes subsidiaries such as SBI Card, SBI Life and the mutual fund business, rose to Rs 25,361 crore from Rs 21,137 crore.

The headline number is strong, but it needs some unpacking. A large part of the increase came from a one-time gain rather than from day-to-day banking.

## The one-off in the numbers

SBI sold a 6.3% stake in SBI Funds Management, the company that runs SBI Mutual Fund, through that unit's IPO during the quarter. The sale produced a pre-tax exceptional gain of Rs 7,288 crore. The bank also sold small stakes in the National Stock Exchange (0.65%) and the asset reconstruction company ARCIL (3.37%), but the roughly Rs 2,491 crore of gains from those deals went straight into capital reserves rather than through the profit and loss account.

Put the Rs 7,288 crore gain next to the Rs 2,878 crore year-on-year rise in profit and the picture changes. Even after allowing for tax, the stake sale is bigger than the entire increase in earnings. In fairness, the comparison quarter was not clean either: a year ago SBI booked a gain of roughly Rs 4,600 crore from selling part of its Yes Bank holding. Both quarters carry one-offs, but this year's is larger.

A better guide to the core business is operating profit, which rose 7.3% to Rs 34,226 crore. That is respectable growth for a bank of SBI's size, though well short of the 14% headline.

## The core business is in good shape

The underlying operating trends were solid. Net interest income, the gap between what the bank earns on loans and pays on deposits, grew about 14.7% from a year ago, ahead of the 11% to 13% that analysts had pencilled in. The domestic net interest margin improved to 3.11%, up 11 basis points from the June quarter, while the whole-bank margin stood at 2.94%.

Loan growth was the standout. Gross advances rose 17.84% to about Rs 52 lakh crore, with SME loans up nearly 22% and farm loans up almost 23%. Retail personal loans grew a slower 14.5%. Deposits grew 12.27% to about Rs 63 lakh crore.

That gap between loan and deposit growth is worth watching. When loans grow five to six points faster than deposits for long, banks usually have to pay more for funds. SBI's share of low-cost current and savings deposits (CASA) slipped to 38.32%, down 131 basis points from a year ago, as customers shifted money into term deposits, which grew 16.7%.

## Asset quality at a two-decade best

Bad loans kept shrinking. The gross non-performing asset ratio fell 34 basis points from a year ago to 1.39%, and the net NPA ratio dropped to 0.36%. The bank said these are its lowest levels in more than 20 years. Credit cost, a measure of fresh provisions against loans, fell to 0.22% for the quarter. Capital adequacy stood at 16.09%, leaving room to keep lending at this pace.

## Points to watch

SBI holds a 10.78% stake in Yes Bank and said it is still assessing what recent Supreme Court rulings on Yes Bank's written-off AT-1 bonds could mean for that investment. It has not made a specific impairment provision yet. That is a loose end investors will want clarity on.

SBI shares had risen about 1% to near Rs 1,000 ahead of the results as the broader market rallied. The market's verdict in the coming sessions will likely depend less on the headline profit and more on whether margins can hold up while loans continue to outpace deposits.
