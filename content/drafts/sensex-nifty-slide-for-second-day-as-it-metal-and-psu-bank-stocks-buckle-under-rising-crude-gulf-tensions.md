Indian benchmark indices extended their decline into a second straight session on Monday, with the Sensex shedding 382.62 points, or 0.5 per cent, to close at 76,132.81, while the Nifty50 fell 118.55 points, also down 0.5 per cent, to settle at 23,779.15. The losses came as IT, metal, PSU bank, realty and media stocks all fell sharply, more than offsetting gains in pharma and healthcare counters.

The retreat resumed after a brief one-day pause in the market's recent losing streak, with traders pointing to a mix of rising crude oil prices, persistent tension around the Strait of Hormuz, and lingering worries about global monetary tightening as the key drags on sentiment. Brent crude has stayed elevated in recent sessions amid the ongoing US-Iran standoff, a dynamic that continues to weigh on import-dependent sectors of the Indian economy and feeds into broader risk-off positioning across emerging markets.

## Sector Moves and Top Laggards

Information technology stocks were among the session's worst performers, with the Nifty IT index tumbling more than 2 per cent as Infosys featured among the top losers on the Nifty50. Insurance-linked counters also came under pressure, with SBI Life Insurance and HDFC Life Insurance both ranking among the index's weakest performers. Metal, PSU bank and media shares similarly ended in the red, reflecting broad-based selling rather than weakness confined to any single sector. Barring Nifty Pharma, every major sectoral index on the NSE closed lower for the day.

In the broader market, the picture was more mixed: the Nifty Midcap index slipped 0.46 per cent, while the Nifty Smallcap index eked out a marginal gain of 0.02 per cent, pointing to some resilience in smaller-cap names even as large-caps struggled.

## What Analysts Are Watching

Market strategists flagged the 23,720-23,680 zone on the Nifty as an important support level to watch in the sessions ahead, noting that a decisive break below that range could open the door to further declines toward 23,550. On the upside, the 23,870-23,900 band is seen as an immediate hurdle for the index to reclaim. Some analysts also cautioned that the recent rally in small- and mid-cap stocks, powered by stronger earnings and value buying after last year's sell-off, is beginning to look stretched, leaving that segment of the market more exposed to any fresh bout of global volatility or weak macroeconomic data.

## The Bigger Picture

Monday's losses underline how closely Dalal Street's near-term direction remains tied to developments well outside India's borders, particularly the trajectory of the US-Iran conflict and its knock-on effect on energy prices. With crude oil costs a major input for Indian industry and a key driver of the country's import bill, sustained strength in oil prices tends to pressure both corporate margins and broader macroeconomic indicators such as inflation and the currency, a dynamic that traders expect to keep dictating the market's mood in the sessions ahead.
