India's startup funding environment cooled noticeably in July, with companies raising a combined $662 million across 85 deals, a steep 67 percent drop from the roughly $2 billion recorded in June. The June figure had been inflated by one outsized transaction, Meta's investment in fintech firm CRED, which meant July's decline looks sharper in percentage terms than the underlying trend might suggest. Compared with July of last year, funding was actually up about 11 percent, a reminder that month-to-month swings in India's startup capital markets can be misleading without longer-term context.

## Large Deals Are Scarce

The clearest signal from July's numbers is the near-total absence of mega-rounds. Only one funding event crossed the $100 million mark: a $130 million Series C raise by AI-focused startup Emergent, which also became India's seventh unicorn of the year in the process. Below that threshold, growth and late-stage funding was led by a trio of $40 million rounds involving Raghu Vamsi Aerospace Group, Veriqus, and BusinessNext, while EV and mobility-adjacent player Neo Group secured $36.3 million in its own Series C. Early-stage startups, by contrast, held up relatively well, accounting for 38 percent of total capital raised for the month even as growth-stage capital thinned out.

## Deal Count Also Slipped

Beyond the headline dollar figure, the number of deals fell too, dropping from 105 transactions in the same month last year to 85 in July. That decline in deal count, alongside the concentration of capital in a handful of rounds, points to investors becoming more selective rather than stepping away from the market entirely. Sectors drawing continued interest included artificial intelligence, electric vehicles, and fintech, according to industry trackers, even as overall risk appetite for early and mid-sized cheques appears to have narrowed.

## Layoffs Have Slowed, Even as Funding Tightens

One notable counterpoint to the funding slowdown is that startup layoffs, which had been a persistent headline through the first half of the year, essentially paused in July. Indian startups have cut more than 4,500 jobs over the past eight months as tighter capital availability, regulatory changes, and investor pressure for profitability reshaped hiring plans, but no major layoff announcements were reported last month. That suggests companies may have already right-sized their teams to match the tighter funding backdrop, rather than layoffs being purely reactive to July's specific numbers.

## What It Means Going Forward

Taken together, July's data paints a picture of an ecosystem that is uneven rather than in retreat: overall funding remains dependent on isolated large rounds rather than a broad base of mid-sized deals, but early-stage activity and year-on-year growth suggest investor interest has not disappeared. For founders, the message is that capital is available for standout businesses and specific themes such as AI and EVs, but the days of easy, broad-based fundraising remain some way off. Whether August brings a rebound or a further slowdown will offer a clearer signal on whether June's surge or July's pullback better represents the current state of India's startup capital markets.
