Parliament's Monsoon Session came to a close on Thursday as both the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha were adjourned sine die, bringing down the curtain on a session marked by repeated Opposition protests and frequent adjournments. The session, which convened on July 20, ultimately passed 11 of the 12 bills introduced, even as disruptions ate into scheduled business hours on multiple days.

## A Session Defined by Disruption

The Monsoon Session was dominated by Opposition anger over the police crackdown on students protesting the NEET paper leak at Jantar Mantar on July 20, as well as allegations of theft of donations at the Ram temple in Ayodhya. Both issues triggered slogan-shouting and walkouts that forced repeated adjournments in both Houses through the six-week session.

In the Lok Sabha, Speaker Om Birla brought proceedings to a close on Thursday shortly after the House assembled, following the customary playing of the national song. Notably, Birla did not deliver the valedictory address in which the Speaker typically summarises the session's productivity, a departure that underscored how disrupted the sitting had been. Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Home Minister Amit Shah and Defence Minister Rajnath Singh were present as the House met briefly, even as Opposition members raised slogans against the government one final time before adjournment.

Union Home Minister Amit Shah had said on Wednesday that he was open to a full debate on the students' protest issue and would respond to every question raised by the Opposition, urging the Speaker to work with Opposition leaders to schedule time for the discussion. That discussion never materialised before the session ended.

## Mines and Minerals Bill Clears Rajya Sabha

In an unusual departure from its regular pre-lunch schedule, the Rajya Sabha dispensed with Zero Hour and Question Hour on Thursday to take up the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026, which was passed by voice vote just before the House was adjourned sine die. The bill was among the last pieces of legislation cleared before the session's close.

Rajya Sabha Chairman C.P. Radhakrishnan reviewed the House's performance over the session before formally adjourning proceedings. Like the Lok Sabha, the Upper House witnessed frequent disruptions over the same set of issues, even as members worked through a substantial legislative agenda alongside the protests.

## What Got Done, and What Didn't

Of the 12 bills introduced during the session, 11 were passed by both Houses, while the Indian Statistical Institute Bill, 2026, remains pending. The session's productivity numbers reflect a familiar pattern in recent years, where governments have leaned on their parliamentary majority to push through legislation even as floor time for debate shrinks amid Opposition protests.

For markets and industry watchers, the passage of the Mines and Minerals Amendment Bill is likely to draw the most attention, given its direct bearing on mining sector regulation and investment approvals. Details of the bill's specific provisions are expected to be notified in the coming weeks as the legislation moves toward presidential assent.

With the Monsoon Session now concluded, attention turns to the Winter Session later in the year, where the unresolved NEET protest debate and other pending legislative business are likely to resurface.
