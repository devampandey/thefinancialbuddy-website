Defending champions India booked their place in a fourth consecutive AHF Men's Junior Hockey Asia Cup final on Friday, beating Malaysia 3-1 in the first semi-final of the tournament in Moqi, China. The win keeps alive India's bid for a sixth overall title in the competition and sets up a summit clash against the winner of the second semi-final between Pakistan and South Korea.

## Ekka Leads From The Front

Captain Anmol Ekka was once again at the heart of India's victory, scoring twice from penalty corners to take his tournament goal tally to 16, extending his lead at the top of the competition's scoring charts. The Indian drag-flicker has been the standout performer of the tournament for the defending champions, converting set-piece opportunities into goals with consistency through the knockout rounds. His brace against Malaysia continued a run of form that has made him central to India's title defence.

Goalkeeper Vivek Lakra was named Player of the Match for a series of crucial saves that kept Malaysia at bay, including denying a penalty stroke at a pivotal moment in the contest. Lakra's shot-stopping proved decisive in preserving India's lead as Malaysia pushed for an equaliser in the closing stages, with the Indian defence holding firm to close out the win.

## A Dominant Campaign

India's run to the final has been built on a mix of attacking firepower and defensive discipline through the pool stage and knockouts. The team topped its group with a string of commanding wins, including a 15-0 rout of Chinese Taipei, before advancing to the semi-finals riding that momentum. The scale of the wins through the tournament has underlined India's status as the team to beat in age-group hockey in Asia, a reputation the current squad has looked to live up to under a demanding schedule of matches in China.

## Chasing A Sixth Title

India go into the final as the tournament's most successful side, having already won the competition five times, and a win on Sunday would extend that record to a sixth Asia Cup crown while also completing a fourth straight final appearance. The identity of India's final opponent will be decided by the outcome of the Pakistan-South Korea semi-final, with both teams likely to offer a different tactical challenge to the one posed by Malaysia.

For a young Indian side blending experienced age-group campaigners with newer faces, reaching the final again is being seen by followers of the sport as validation of the pipeline Indian hockey has built at the junior level in recent years, with several players from previous Junior Asia Cup-winning squads having gone on to represent the senior national team. A strong showing in the final would add further weight to that narrative as India looks to maintain its dominance in continental age-group hockey.
