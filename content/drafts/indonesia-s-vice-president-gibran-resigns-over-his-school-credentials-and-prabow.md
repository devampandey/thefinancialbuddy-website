Indonesia's vice-presidency is vacant. Gibran Rakabuming Raka, the 39-year-old son of former president Joko Widodo, walked into the parliamentary complex in Jakarta shortly before noon on Thursday and handed his resignation letter to the leadership of the People's Consultative Assembly (MPR). The meeting with MPR Speaker Ahmad Muzani lasted about 20 minutes. Reuters described it as the highest-profile resignation in Indonesia since Suharto left the presidency in 1998.

## What the letter says, and what it leaves out

Muzani told reporters that the letter states Gibran is stepping down at his own request and gives no specific reason. Gibran asked the assembly to process it immediately. A separate letter went to President Prabowo Subianto. According to senior law minister Yusril Ihza Mahendra, that one was five paragraphs long and cited personal reasons and what Gibran called growing public sentiment.

The resignation did not come out of nowhere. On November 10, State Secretary Prasetyo Hadi said Gibran had raised the idea of stepping down in a private meeting with Prabowo. Gibran said the same day that he still planned to take his case to the State Administrative Court and produce his education documents. By Thursday that route had been abandoned.

## The credentials dispute

The pressure stems from Gibran's educational qualifications. Rulings from the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court concluded that his overseas high school credentials did not meet Indonesia's legal requirements and should not have been accepted by election authorities when he ran in 2024. The Constitutional Court had rejected a bid to disqualify him outright on October 8, which left him in office. The political pressure kept building anyway.

On November 11, the Indonesian Democratic Party of Struggle (PDI-P) said its 110 lawmakers had agreed to back a parliamentary initiative that could lead to impeachment. Indonesian media reported that more than 120 legislators supported the move. Local outlets reported on Thursday that lawmakers had dropped the motion now that the office is empty.

Gibran's candidacy was controversial from the start. He became eligible for the 2024 ticket with Prabowo only after a court ruling changed the age rules for candidates, and critics accused his father of influencing that decision.

## How Indonesia replaces a vice-president

The constitution sets out a clear process. The MPR first holds a joint session to formally record the vacancy, then writes to the president. Prabowo must then nominate two candidates, and Antara reported that he has 14 days from the MPR's letter to do so. The assembly then has up to 60 days from the vacancy to elect one of the two. Muzani said preparations would begin straight away.

Prabowo's pick will shape his coalition for the rest of his term. The vice-presidency had been the clearest symbol of his alliance with Jokowi's political camp. Jokowi said on Thursday that he continues to support Prabowo's government, which looks like an attempt to calm talk of a rift between the two men.

## Markets and the wider backdrop

Investors reacted nervously. The rupiah fell about 0.6% to a record low of roughly 18,255 per US dollar on Thursday. Economists quoted by Antara warned that a long period of political uncertainty could weigh on Southeast Asia's largest economy.

The resignation also lands in the middle of another political storm. Indonesian police said they have enough initial evidence to pursue treason charges against four suspects over the wiretapping of Prabowo, other politicians, activists and journalists. Senior figures under investigation include former national police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo, who is widely seen as a Jokowi loyalist.

For now, the main question is who Prabowo nominates and whether the MPR can complete the process quickly enough to reassure markets that the government is stable.
