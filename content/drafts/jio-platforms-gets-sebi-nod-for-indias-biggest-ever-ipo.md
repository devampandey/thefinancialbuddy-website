Jio Platforms Limited has cleared a major regulatory milestone in its long-awaited journey to the stock market, with the Securities and Exchange Board of India issuing its final observations on the company's draft prospectus on Friday. The approval clears the way for what is widely expected to be the largest initial public offering in Indian history.

The Reliance Industries subsidiary filed its draft red herring prospectus on June 19, and the National Stock Exchange's offer-document portal has now updated the filing's status to "approved," confirming that the issue has cleared SEBI's regulatory scrutiny.

## Deal Structure and Size

Jio Platforms is planning a fresh issue of up to 27 crore equity shares of face value Rs 10 each, with no offer-for-sale component from existing shareholders. That means the entire proceeds raised will flow into the company rather than being used to let early investors cash out.

Market estimates suggest the offering could raise somewhere in the region of $3.8 billion to $4 billion, a figure that would comfortably eclipse the country's previous largest listings and mark one of the biggest public offerings anywhere in Asia this year. The final issue price will be determined through the standard book-building process once the company opens the offer to investors.

## Where the Money Is Going

According to the filing, a significant portion of the proceeds is earmarked to repay or prepay, in whole or in part, roughly Rs 27,500 crore of outstanding borrowings held by Reliance Jio Infocomm Limited, Jio Platforms' key operating subsidiary. Reducing debt on the telecom and digital services arm's books is expected to strengthen the group's balance sheet ahead of its next phase of expansion into 5G, cloud, and AI-linked services.

## Part of a Busy Day for India's IPO Market

Jio Platforms was not alone in getting the regulator's go-ahead on Friday. SEBI also cleared six other public offers on the same day, underscoring how active India's primary market has remained through 2026 even as global monetary policy uncertainty has kept broader equity markets choppy. Bankers and analysts have pointed to strong domestic liquidity and continued retail investor appetite as key reasons India's IPO pipeline has stayed full despite intermittent bouts of volatility.

## What Comes Next

With SEBI's observations in hand, Jio Platforms can now proceed toward filing its final prospectus, setting a price band, and opening the issue for subscription in the coming months. Given the scale of the offering, the listing is expected to draw intense interest from both domestic mutual funds and global institutional investors, and could prompt index inclusion discussions given the sheer size of the float once shares begin trading.

For India's capital markets more broadly, a successful Jio Platforms listing would cement 2026 as a landmark year for the country's IPO ecosystem, coming on the heels of several other large listings across sectors ranging from financial services to consumer technology.
