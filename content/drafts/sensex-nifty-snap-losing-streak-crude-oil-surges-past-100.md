India's benchmark equity indices ended Thursday's session higher, breaking a three-day losing streak, even as investors continued to grapple with crude oil prices above $100 a barrel and persistent uncertainty stemming from the escalating conflict in the Middle East.

The BSE Sensex climbed 138.36 points, or 0.19 per cent, to close at 74,902.59, while the NSE Nifty50 added 46.30 points, or 0.20 per cent, to settle at 23,477.80. The recovery came after the index had shed more than 800 points in the previous session alone, as rising geopolitical tensions and climbing energy costs weighed on sentiment through the first half of the week.

## Energy Stocks Provide The Cushion

Gains were relatively narrow but concentrated in stocks positioned to benefit from higher crude prices. Oil and gas producers led the rally, with ONGC advancing around 2 per cent and Oil India gaining roughly 2.4 per cent, as higher crude prices lifted the outlook for upstream earnings. Power Grid Corporation, Bharti Airtel and Tech Mahindra were also among the session's notable gainers, while HCL Technologies, Tata Steel and IndusInd Bank featured among the larger laggards.

Banking stocks provided broader support to the market, helping offset weakness in technology and select consumer names that remain more directly exposed to global demand and input-cost pressures. Market breadth, however, stayed mixed for much of the day, reflecting the tug-of-war between investors seeking to buy into the dip and those staying cautious given the unresolved geopolitical backdrop.

## Crude Above $100 Keeps Pressure On

The dominant theme for Indian markets this week has been the sharp rise in global crude prices, with Brent crossing the $100-a-barrel mark for the first time since May, driven by an intensifying standoff between the United States and Iran that has disrupted tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. Higher crude prices carry outsized significance for India, which imports the vast majority of its oil needs, feeding directly into concerns over the country's import bill, inflation trajectory, economic growth and corporate profit margins, particularly for oil marketing companies, aviation and other energy-intensive sectors.

Asian markets broadly mirrored the cautious mood, with most regional indices trading lower in sympathy with a weak session on Wall Street overnight, as global investors continue to price in the risk of a prolonged conflict in the Gulf. Domestic traders said the modest bounce in Indian indices reflected some relief buying and bargain-hunting after the recent slide, rather than any material improvement in the underlying geopolitical picture.

## What Investors Are Watching Next

Market participants said the near-term direction for Indian equities would likely continue to hinge on developments in the Gulf, including any further disruption to shipping routes or oil infrastructure, as well as incoming corporate earnings and central bank commentary both in India and globally. With crude prices elevated and volatility indices still running above recent averages, analysts cautioned that Thursday's gains should be read as a pause in the broader risk-off mood rather than a definitive turnaround, especially with the conflict in the Middle East showing no clear signs of resolution.
