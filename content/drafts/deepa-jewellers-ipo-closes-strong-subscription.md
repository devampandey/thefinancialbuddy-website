Deepa Jewellers Limited's Rs 460-crore initial public offering closed for subscription on Thursday, its final day of bidding, after drawing robust demand across investor categories through the three-day window.

By early afternoon on the closing day, the issue had been subscribed more than 17 times overall, with the non-institutional investor (NII) category leading demand at nearly 48 times its allotted quota. The retail investor portion was subscribed over 12 times, while the qualified institutional buyer (QIB) category, which typically fills up later in the bidding window, had crossed 4 times subscription.

## Issue details

The IPO carried a price band of Rs 168 to Rs 177 per share, with the entire offer structured to raise Rs 460 crore for the jewellery retailer. Grey market activity around the issue remained buoyant through the closing day, with premiums in the unofficial market indicating investors expect the stock to list at a modest gain over its upper price band once trading begins.

Deepa Jewellers plans to use the IPO proceeds to fund its business expansion plans, though the exact break-up of fund deployment follows the standard disclosures made in the company's offer documents filed with market regulator Sebi.

## What comes next

With the subscription window now closed, share allotment for the issue is scheduled to be finalised on Friday, September 4. Investors who receive an allotment will see the shares credited to their demat accounts ahead of the stock's debut on the NSE and BSE, with listing tentatively set for Tuesday, September 8.

## Why it matters

Deepa Jewellers' strong subscription numbers add to what has been an active year for India's IPO market, particularly in the jewellery and gems retail space, where several companies have tapped public markets in 2026 to fund expansion amid steady consumer demand for gold and jewellery products. The heavy NII and retail demand for this issue mirrors a broader pattern seen across recent mainboard IPOs, where high-net-worth individuals and retail investors have continued to chase listing gains even as institutional investors take a more measured, valuation-driven approach to primary market bets.

The company's upcoming listing will be closely watched as a gauge of investor appetite for smaller jewellery retailers looking to scale up through public capital, at a time when gold prices in India have remained elevated through much of the year.
