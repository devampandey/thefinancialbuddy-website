India's commanding position in the opening Test against Sri Lanka in Galle narrowed considerably on the fourth day, after the hosts' bowling attack dismissed the visitors for 193 in their second innings, setting up a target of 372 runs for Sri Lanka to chase down and level or win the contest.

India had entered Day 4 in firm control after posting 462 in their first innings and then bowling Sri Lanka out for 284 in 79.4 overs on Day 3, giving them a healthy first-innings lead of 178 runs. The expectation heading into the fourth day was that India would bat well into the session to extend the lead further and leave Sri Lanka chasing an imposing total on a wearing Galle surface known to assist spin as matches progress.

Instead, Sri Lanka's bowlers produced a disciplined and incisive display that turned the momentum of the match. Pace spearhead Asitha Fernando was the standout performer, finishing with four wickets in the innings, including the final wicket of Prasidh Krishna to wrap up the Indian innings. Supported by tight bowling at the other end, Sri Lanka picked up seven wickets across the session, reducing India from a position of relative comfort to being bowled out for 193. Wicketkeeper-batter Rishabh Pant offered the most resistance with a gritty half-century, but found little support from the rest of the order as wickets fell at regular intervals.

With India's second innings folding for 193, Sri Lanka were left needing 372 runs to complete a famous victory in the series opener — a target that would rank among the highest successful fourth-innings chases in Galle Test history if achieved. The playing conditions added another layer of drama to the contest, with rain interruptions during the day delaying the start of Sri Lanka's chase. Covers came off in time for the third session to begin at around 3:30 pm local time, after which openers Lahiru Udara and Nishan Fernando walked out to begin the run chase under pressure, knowing early wickets could hand the initiative straight back to India.

For India, the onus now shifts to their bowling attack to defend a target that, while substantial, is not entirely out of reach on a pitch that has already shown signs of wear. The tourists will be banking on their spinners to exploit the deteriorating surface in the same way Sri Lanka's attack did during India's second innings collapse. For Sri Lanka, openers Udara and Fernando will need to see off the new ball and lay a platform, given the history of collapses on used Galle pitches when chasing significant targets.

The result of this opening Test, part of India's tour of Sri Lanka and the ongoing World Test Championship cycle, is now finely poised heading into the concluding stages of the match, with both sides having genuine claims to victory depending on how the pitch behaves over the remaining play.
