SFC Environmental Technologies, the Navi Mumbai company that builds and operates wastewater and solid-waste treatment plants, made a better-than-expected stock market debut on Tuesday. Its shares opened at Rs 355.10 on the National Stock Exchange, 7.6% above the IPO price of Rs 330. On the BSE the stock started at Rs 354.05, up about 7.3%.

The real move came after the open. Buying picked up soon after listing and the shares climbed to Rs 405 on the NSE, about 24% above the issue price and 15.5% above where they had opened. For a retail investor who received one lot of 45 shares for Rs 14,850, that intraday level represented a paper gain of roughly Rs 3,375.

## A debut that beat the grey market

The listing was ahead of what the unofficial grey market had signalled. Premiums there had been tracking around 4.7% just before listing, well below the 7.6% opening gain and far below the intraday high.

That mirrors the shape of demand during the subscription window from October 27 to 29. The issue was subscribed 9.47 times overall, but the enthusiasm came mainly from large investors. Qualified institutional buyers bid for 18.81 times their quota and non-institutional investors 12.81 times, while the retail portion was covered only 2.71 times. When institutions are this keen and retail interest is moderate, the first-day trade often depends on whether institutions add more shares in the open market, which appears to be what happened.

## What investors are now paying

When the IPO opened, we noted it was [priced at about 21 times FY26 earnings](/ipo/sfc-environmental-opens-rs-668-crore-ipo-at-21x-earnings-but-a-rs-1-48-crore-q1-pr), with a post-issue value of around Rs 3,235 crore. At Rs 405 a share, the same share count implies a market value of nearly Rs 4,000 crore, or roughly 25 times the FY26 net profit of about Rs 160 crore.

That is a richer multiple, but not an extreme one for a company reporting operating margins of around 29% and a net margin close to 20%. Revenue grew from about Rs 658 crore in FY24 to about Rs 804 crore in FY26, a compound growth rate of a little over 10% a year. Profit growth was slower, from Rs 152 crore to Rs 160 crore between FY25 and FY26, as margins narrowed slightly.

## The concerns have not gone away

Two issues flagged at the IPO stage still apply. First, the June 2026 quarter produced a profit of just Rs 1.48 crore, a reminder that earnings in government-linked water projects bunch up late in the fiscal year. Investors will want the December and March quarters to show the full-year figure is repeatable.

Second, cash collection is slow. The company's cash conversion cycle stretched to about 242 days in FY26, largely because municipal and state clients take a long time to pay. The Rs 150 crore of fresh money raised in the IPO goes mainly to prepaying debt at the company and its subsidiary Vasudha Waste Treatment, and to working capital, which helps the balance sheet but does not fix the collection cycle. The larger Rs 518 crore offer-for-sale portion went to selling shareholders, not the company.

## How it compares

SFC's debut was far more modest than the 65% opening pop for [BLS Polymers](/ipo/bls-polymers-lists-at-a-65-premium-and-climbs-further-rewarding-a-241x-book), which listed the same morning after a much larger book. But SFC's steadier subscription and its climb after listing suggest buyers are pricing in the order book rather than chasing a short-term frenzy. The next test will be the company's first results as a listed firm.
