The United States has issued a fresh travel warning for Iran, urging American citizens not to travel to the country "for any reason" and telling those already there to leave immediately, as tensions across West Asia show signs of sharp escalation.

The alert, issued by the US Virtual Embassy in Iran on September 21, places Iran at the State Department's Level 4 "Do Not Travel" category, its highest advisory tier. Officials said the regional security environment remained complex and warned that flight cancellations, airspace closures and other travel disruptions could occur with little notice.

## "Leave Iran now"

The security notice was blunt in its language, instructing Americans in the country to depart without delay and to enrol in the State Department's Smart Traveler Enrollment Program so they can receive real-time security updates and stay in contact with US diplomatic staff during an emergency. Travellers were also told to monitor developments at airports, airlines and land border crossings, since entry and exit procedures could change abruptly.

The advisory also carried practical guidance in case of an aerial attack, telling citizens to stay away from windows and glass doors, avoid debris and follow official communications closely. Americans were further urged to avoid demonstrations, large public gatherings and areas with a heavy police presence, and to keep a low profile near locations publicly associated with the United States.

## Houthi attacks widen the regional flashpoint

Washington specifically flagged the activities of Iranian-backed Houthi forces, which it said have targeted Saudi Arabia, including civilian airports, describing the military conflict as having "the potential to escalate rapidly." The warning comes days after Saudi authorities issued an emergency alert over a "hostile aerial threat," after a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh was intercepted by coalition air defences. Saudi officials also reported attempted strikes on infrastructure and civilian areas in Bish, Taif, Farasan and Yanbu.

Qatar has condemned the Houthi strikes on its neighbour, calling them a violation of Saudi sovereignty and international law, while the US Mission in Saudi Arabia has separately required government personnel to obtain special authorisation before travelling to Taif and Yanbu, citing the same security concerns.

## Part of a broader escalation

Monday's alert builds on a series of increasingly urgent US travel notices for the region over the past week, reflecting Washington's view that a broader confrontation involving Iran could be nearing a flashpoint. The State Department also warned that American diplomatic facilities — including ones outside the Middle East — have previously been targeted, and that Iran or groups aligned with Tehran could go after US interests, businesses and institutions overseas.

For companies and investors tracking West Asia, the advisory adds to a growing list of signals — including repeated warnings about the Strait of Hormuz and elevated military activity in the Gulf — that the region's security situation remains fluid. Iran, for its part, has previously warned of a "sustained response" if US military pressure continues, keeping alive the risk of further disruption to shipping, energy markets and regional air travel in the days ahead.
