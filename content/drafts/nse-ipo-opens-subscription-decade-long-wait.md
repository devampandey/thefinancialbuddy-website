The National Stock Exchange of India opened its long-awaited initial public offering for public subscription on Thursday, bringing to a head a listing saga that has stretched on for nearly a decade and stands as one of the most closely watched public offerings in Indian capital markets history.

## The offer at a glance

The IPO is priced in a band of Rs 1,700 to Rs 1,785 per share and is structured entirely as an offer for sale, meaning existing shareholders are divesting stakes rather than the exchange raising fresh capital. The issue size works out to approximately Rs 22,562 crore, with a minimum lot size of eight shares requiring an investment of roughly Rs 14,280 at the upper end of the price band. The issue is scheduled to close on September 21, with the exchange's own shares expected to begin trading around September 24. Grey market activity has hinted at a listing premium of around 9 percent over the upper price band.

## Nearly ten years in the making

NSE first filed its draft red herring prospectus in December 2016, seeking to raise around Rs 10,000 crore through an offer for sale. What followed was one of the longest regulatory delays in Indian corporate history, rooted largely in the co-location scandal that surfaced from 2015 whistleblower complaints alleging that certain brokers received preferential access to NSE's trading systems. The Securities and Exchange Board of India returned the exchange's draft prospectus in 2019, directing it to refile only after resolving governance concerns tied to the episode. The subsequent years brought further regulatory scrutiny, court proceedings and repeated rounds of clarification before Sebi finally issued its no-objection certificate this year, clearing the final hurdle to listing.

## Early demand looks tepid

Despite the anchor book for the issue having drawn what was described as unexpectedly large demand earlier this week, retail and institutional appetite on the opening day of the public issue appeared more muted. As of the morning session, the overall issue was subscribed only a fraction of a time over, with qualified institutional buyer demand yet to meaningfully show up, while non-institutional and retail investor categories had each been subscribed only in fractional multiples. Market watchers noted that large offer-for-sale issues of this size often see subscription build progressively through the final one or two days of bidding, particularly once institutional books close.

## Why this listing matters

NSE's dominant position in India's equity and derivatives trading makes its listing a bellwether event for the exchange-operator space, drawing natural comparisons with the strong post-listing performance of rival BSE in recent years. Beyond the exchange itself, the listing is being watched as a signal of Sebi's confidence in NSE's corporate governance overhaul since the co-location episode, and as a marker of how much appetite domestic and foreign investors retain for large offer-for-sale issues at a time when global rate uncertainty, highlighted by the US Federal Reserve's rate hike this week, has made investors more selective.
