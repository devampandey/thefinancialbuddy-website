India wrapped up a comprehensive 165-run victory over Sri Lanka on the fifth and final day of the first Test in Galle on Wednesday, taking a 1-0 lead in the series. Sri Lanka, resuming the day on 84 for 4 while chasing a daunting target of 372, could not survive the final session as India's bowlers cleaned up the tail to seal the win.

## How The Match Unfolded

India laid the platform for the win with a first-innings total of 462, built around a standout performance from opener Devdutt Padikkal, who followed his first-innings century with another composed knock in the second innings as India set Sri Lanka an imposing target. India's second innings closed at 193, with Rishabh Pant playing a typically aggressive hand of 66 off just 69 balls to help push the target beyond Sri Lanka's reach.

In reply, Sri Lanka were bowled out for 284 in their first innings before being set 372 to win. The hosts fought hard through the middle order in the second innings, with Dhananjaya de Silva and Sonal Dinusha steadying things on the fourth evening, but the gap always looked too wide to bridge. On the final day, India's bowling attack, led by spinner Manav Suthar, closed out the contest with the tail unable to extend the innings meaningfully.

## Standout Performers

Left-arm spinner Manav Suthar was the standout bowler of the match, finishing with a ten-wicket haul across both innings, a career-defining performance that underlined his growing role in India's spin attack away from home. Padikkal's twin centuries earned him the player-of-the-match honours for a match-defining contribution with the bat, while Pant's quickfire second-innings knock proved crucial in giving India enough runway to bowl Sri Lanka out. Mohammed Siraj and Prasidh Krishna also chipped in with important wickets to keep the pressure on throughout Sri Lanka's chase.

## What It Means For The Series

The win gives India a 1-0 lead heading into the second Test, which moves to the Sinhalese Sports Club in Colombo and is scheduled to run from August 23 to 27. A dominant all-round performance in Galle, spanning both bat and ball, will give the visiting side considerable momentum heading into the second leg of the tour, while Sri Lanka will need to regroup quickly, particularly with their batting unable to build partnerships long enough to challenge a stiff target.

For India, the result continues a strong run in overseas conditions this year and adds further weight to the squad's depth, with contributions coming from across the batting order and bowling attack rather than resting on any single individual performance.
