India's insurance regulator on Thursday proposed a sweeping overhaul of how insurers pay commissions to agents, brokers and digital distribution platforms, triggering one of the sharpest single-day stock routs the country's listed insurtech firms have seen.

## What IRDAI Is Proposing

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India released a consultation paper titled Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution, laying out proposed changes to commission structures, distribution expenses, market conduct practices and transparency norms across the sector.

Under the draft framework, distribution entities would receive nil commission on new-vehicle third-party insurance and a cap of 5 per cent on new-vehicle own-damage cover. Health insurance commissions would be capped at 15 per cent on first-time sales and 5 per cent on renewals. For banks and lending institutions selling insurance alongside loans, commissions would be capped between 2 and 5 per cent depending on the product category.

Life insurance faces the most structural change: the paper proposes capping first-year commissions between 5 and 20 per cent depending on a policy's tenure, and spreading payouts across multiple years rather than concentrating them in the first year, a practice regulators have long flagged as encouraging mis-selling.

## Markets React Sharply

The proposal landed hard on insurance distribution stocks. PB Fintech, which owns Policybazaar and runs largely on commission income from insurers, crashed as much as 36 per cent in its steepest single-day fall on record, erasing more than Rs 25,000 crore of market capitalisation. Turtlemint, another major insurance distribution platform, hit its 20 per cent lower circuit. Shares of insurers including HDFC Life and Max Financial Services also came under pressure as investors reassessed how the new caps would hit distribution-heavy business models.

Analysts said the scale of the reaction reflects how central commission income has become to platforms that built their growth on aggregating insurance sales online, rather than manufacturing policies themselves. A structural cut to commissions, particularly the near-total removal on third-party motor cover, strikes directly at a high-volume, low-margin business line many of these platforms have relied on to drive traffic and scale.

## What Happens Next

IRDAI has opened the consultation paper for public and industry feedback, with the window for stakeholder responses closing on October 25. Industry bodies representing brokers and insurtech platforms are expected to push back on the more aggressive caps, particularly the elimination of commissions on third-party motor policies, arguing it could reduce incentives to sell mandatory covers and push penetration lower rather than higher.

The regulator's broader intent, as outlined in the paper, is to shift the economics of insurance distribution away from commission-driven selling and toward outcomes that better serve policyholders, including greater transparency on how much of a premium goes toward commissions versus actual risk cover. For an industry that has expanded rapidly on the back of digital distribution over the past decade, Thursday's proposal marks the regulator's most direct attempt yet to rewrite those economics, and markets responded to the scale of that shift immediately.
