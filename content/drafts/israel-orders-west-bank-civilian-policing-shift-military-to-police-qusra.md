Israel's Defense Minister Israel Katz has ordered the military to draw up a plan to transfer civilian law-enforcement responsibilities in the occupied West Bank from the army to the Israel Police, a significant policy shift that comes amid mounting criticism of the military's handling of Jewish settlers who besieged a Palestinian family's home in the West Bank village of Qusra.

## The Qusra Standoff That Triggered the Order

The immediate trigger for the announcement was a days-long standoff in Qusra, where hardline settlers built encampments surrounding several isolated Palestinian houses on the town's outskirts, cutting off water and electricity and blockading access roads. The blockade reportedly trapped two Palestinian families along with a group of visiting Italian nationals inside the homes. Israeli troops were criticised, including by the United States, for failing to swiftly remove the settlers involved in the siege, with a senior American diplomat describing the episode as an "act of terror." Reports also indicated that Washington had privately expressed frustration with what it characterised as Israel allowing "anarchy" and rampant settler violence to persist in parts of the West Bank.

## Katz's Directive

Under the plan ordered by Katz, the Israel Police would establish a dedicated force to handle civilian matters across the West Bank and would be given the powers and funding needed to take on that role, effectively narrowing the army's day-to-day responsibilities in policing Israeli civilians in the territory. Katz was quoted defending the shift by arguing that the military's core mission should remain focused on combating Palestinian militancy rather than being diverted toward policing what he referred to as "hilltop youth" — a term used in Israel for young, hardline settlers involved in establishing unauthorised outposts and in attacks on Palestinian communities.

The move represents a notable, if implicit, acknowledgment of friction between the Israeli government and its own military over how aggressively soldiers should act against settler violence, an issue that has drawn scrutiny both domestically and from Israel's allies over the past year.

## Wider Context: Gaza Diplomacy and Regional Tensions

The West Bank policy shift comes as separate US-backed diplomatic efforts continue over Gaza. Reports indicate that senior White House adviser Jared Kushner is expected to travel to Israel and Egypt in the coming days alongside officials from the so-called Board of Peace to push forward elements of a Gaza plan that Washington announced in late July. That plan, according to earlier statements from President Donald Trump, envisions the disarmament of Hamas and other militant groups in the territory, though a senior Hamas figure has said any disarmament would be contingent on an Israeli withdrawal from Gaza and progress on reconstruction efforts. Israeli forces have also continued targeted operations in Gaza in recent days as part of the broader, unresolved conflict.

## What Comes Next

It remains unclear how quickly the West Bank policing transfer will be implemented, and Israeli defense officials have not detailed a specific timeline for standing up the new police force or transferring funding and legal authority from the military. The order is likely to face scrutiny both from settler advocacy groups, who may view it as a curb on military latitude in the territory, and from critics who argue that a police-led approach could struggle to rein in settler violence without stronger accountability measures. The episode in Qusra, and Israel's response to it, is likely to remain a point of diplomatic friction with Washington in the days ahead as the broader regional picture, spanning Gaza, the West Bank and wider Middle East tensions, continues to evolve.
